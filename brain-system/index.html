    About Brain System

    What you see

    A procedural human brain in MNI space and, in whole-body mode, a clinical figure built of stars that carries the brain's networks through the body: somatic, autonomic, enteric, neuroendocrine and neuroimmune. Cool-white stars in the brain, cord and ganglia are neural tissue. Each neural star stands for many neurons; hovering a structure shows "1 star ≈ N neurons" for the current quality tier. On the skin and on non-neural organs, stars mark tissue, not cells.

    • Body brightness is normalised for visibility.
    • Nerves, ganglia, adrenal glands, cranial-nerve nuclei and small nuclei are enlarged for legibility.
    • Sense organs, brainstem relays and some body coordinates are estimated positions.
    • Pelvic nerves and plexuses are schematic.
    • Hormone and cytokine glows show plasma level, not vessels.

    Signals travel as packets: population volleys at true conduction speed at the shown clock rate, not single spikes. Afferent packets, travelling toward the brain, always have the coolest colour; direction of travel, not colour alone, tells afferent from efferent. A disabled network fades to a grey ghost.

    In the orgasm scenario, warm amber lanterns are released dopamine in each terminal field (rat-calibrated model), and amber threads are the dopamine pathways. Faint tints show modulator tone on its source nucleus:

    • serotonin on the raphe
    • GABA on the RMTg
    • prolactin on the pituitary
    • opioid tone on the periaqueductal grey

    Switches, interventions and stimuli

    Body switches between Brain only and Whole body and turns each network on or off. Off means denervation for nerves and basal replacement for hormones; the rest of the model responds by its own dynamics. Interventions remove specific nerves or structures, one at a time. Stimulus delivers one sense at a site picked on the figure or chosen from the site list. Scenarios play scripted timelines; their outputs are a model run with a stated seed, not population means. In brain-only mode the body vitals read "—".

    Time

    Every model quantity runs in human time. The clock shows human time and the rate: ×1/100 means one screen second shows 10 ms. The clock slows by itself so that fast signals stay visible; Lock fixes the rate.

    The model

    Seven A-groups (A8–A14) feed seven model populations (A8 pooled with A9, A10 split into lateral and medial VTA), which drive extracellular dopamine in nine terminal fields, with Michaelis–Menten uptake, D1/D2 receptor binding, autoreceptors, release plasticity and a reward-prediction-error signal. Prolactin, GABA, endogenous opioids, serotonin and oxytocin act only as modulators of dopamine.

    Design and sources: research/ANALYSIS.md; dopamine equations: research/DOPAMINE.md.

    Caveats

    • Human dopamine during sex has never been measured.
    • Concentrations are calibrated on rat microdialysis and voltammetry.
    • Human imaging measures blood flow, not dopamine.
    • Prolactin: an established short-loop action on tuberoinfundibular neurons (rodent); a brake on mesolimbic dopamine is an opt-in hypothesis.
    • The oxytocin readout is a model proxy, not a measured level.
    • The efferent route of the inflammatory reflex is contested.
    • Model latencies are always shown next to their measured target range.

    Safety

    No part of the screen flashes more than 3 times per second, there are no red flashes, and large-area brightness changes are limited and smoothed. Stimuli are never drawn as flames or screen flashes: a light stimulus is a local glow in the eye. No sound is played. Reduced motion is respected: limb motion, breathing and gut movement, heartbeat pulses and auto-rotation stop.
