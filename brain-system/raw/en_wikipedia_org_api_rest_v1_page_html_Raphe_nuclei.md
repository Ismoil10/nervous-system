The **raphe nuclei** ([Greek](./Greek_language "Greek language"): ῥαφή, "seam")[[1]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Liddell_&_Scott-1) are a moderate-size cluster of [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") found in the [brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem"). They have [5-HT1 receptors](./5-HT1_receptor "5-HT1 receptor") which are coupled with [Gi/Go-protein](./Heterotrimeric_G_protein "Heterotrimeric G protein")-inhibiting [adenyl cyclase](./Adenyl_cyclase "Adenyl cyclase"). They function as [autoreceptors](./Autoreceptor "Autoreceptor") in the brain and decrease the release of [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"). The [anxiolytic](./Anxiolytic "Anxiolytic") drug [Buspirone](./Buspirone "Buspirone") acts as [partial agonist](./Partial_agonist "Partial agonist") against these receptors.[[2]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-2) [Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor](./Selective_serotonin_reuptake_inhibitor "Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor") (SSRI) [antidepressants](./Antidepressant "Antidepressant") are believed to act in these nuclei, as well as at their targets.[[3]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-3)

## Anatomy

The raphe nuclei are traditionally considered to be the medial portion of the [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation"), and
appear as a ridge of cells in the center and most medial portion of the [brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem").

In order from [caudal](./Caudal_(anatomical_term) "Caudal (anatomical term)") to [rostral](./Anatomical_terms_of_location#Directional_terms "Anatomical terms of location"), the raphe nuclei are known as the *[nucleus raphe obscurus](./Nucleus_raphe_obscurus "Nucleus raphe obscurus")*, the *[nucleus raphe pallidus](./Nucleus_raphe_pallidus "Nucleus raphe pallidus")*, the *[nucleus raphe magnus](./Nucleus_raphe_magnus "Nucleus raphe magnus")*, the *[nucleus raphe pontis](./Pontine_raphe_nucleus "Pontine raphe nucleus")*, the *[median raphe nucleus](./Median_raphe_nucleus "Median raphe nucleus")*, *[dorsal raphe nucleus](./Dorsal_raphe_nucleus "Dorsal raphe nucleus")*, *caudal linear nucleus*.[[4]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Törk-4) In the first systematic examination of the raphe nuclei, Taber *et al.*. (1960)[[5]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Taber_'60a-5) originally proposed the existence of two linear nuclei (nucleus linearis intermedius and nucleus linearis rostralis). This study was published before techniques enabling the visualization of serotonin or the enzymes participating in its synthesis had been developed, as first demonstrated by Dahlström and Fuxe in 1964.[[6]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-D&F-6) Later, it was revealed that of these two nuclei, only the former (nucleus linearis intermedius, now known as the caudal linear nucleus), proved to contain serotonin-producing neurons,[[7]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-7) though both of them contain dopaminergic neurons.[[8]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Ikemoto-8)

In some works (e.g.[[9]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Nieuwenhuys-9)), researchers have grouped the nuclei lineares into one nucleus, the ***nucleus linearis***, shrinking the number of raphe to seven, e.g.,
[NeuroNames](./NeuroNames "NeuroNames") makes the following ordering:[[10]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-10)

- Raphe nuclei of [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata")
  - [Nucleus raphe obscurus](./Nucleus_raphe_obscurus "Nucleus raphe obscurus")
  - [Nucleus raphe magnus](./Nucleus_raphe_magnus "Nucleus raphe magnus")
  - [Nucleus raphe pallidus](./Nucleus_raphe_pallidus "Nucleus raphe pallidus")
- Raphe nuclei of the [pontine](./Pons "Pons") reticular formation
  - [Nucleus raphe pontis](./Pontine_raphe_nucleus "Pontine raphe nucleus")
  - [Nucleus centralis inferior](./Inferior_central_nucleus "Inferior central nucleus")
- Raphe nuclei of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") reticular formation
  - [Nucleus centralis superior](./Superior_central_nucleus "Superior central nucleus") (*median raphe nucleus*)
  - [Nucleus raphe dorsalis](./Dorsal_raphe_nucleus "Dorsal raphe nucleus")

## Nomenclature

The Latin names commonly used for most of these nuclei are grammatically and orthographically incorrect. Latin grammar would require to use the [genitive case](./Genitive_case "Genitive case") *raphes* ('of the seam') instead of the [nominative case](./Nominative_case "Nominative case") *raphe* ('seam') in these Latin expressions. The main authority in anatomical names, [Terminologia Anatomica](./Terminologia_Anatomica "Terminologia Anatomica") uses for example *nucleus raphes magnus*[[11]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-FCAT-11) instead of the grammatically incorrect *nucleus raphe magnus*. The spelling *raphe/raphes* however can also be contested as numerous sources[[12]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Hyrtl1880-12)[[13]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Foster1891-13)[[14]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Triepel1910b-14) indicate that *raphe* is an incorrect Latin rendering of the Ancient Greek word ῥαφή as the initial letter rho with [rough breathing](./Rough_breathing "Rough breathing") (*spiritus asper*) is normally rendered as *rh* in Latin.[[12]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Hyrtl1880-12) The edition of the [Nomina Anatomica](./Nomina_Anatomica "Nomina Anatomica") that was ratified in [Jena](./Jena "Jena") in 1935 used *rhaphe* instead of *raphe*.[[15]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Kopsch-15)[[16]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-Stieve1949-16)

## Projections

These nuclei interact with almost every pertinent portion of the brain, but only a few of them have specifically independent interaction. These select nuclei are discussed as follows.

Overall, the caudal raphe nuclei, including the nucleus raphe magnus, nucleus raphe pallidus and nucleus raphe obscurus, all project towards the spinal cord and brain stem. The more-rostral nuclei, including the nucleus raphe pontis, nucleus centralis superior (also called median raphe nucleus, mRN) and nucleus raphe dorsalis (dRN) project towards the brain areas of higher function [[17]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-17)

The numerous projections from the mRN and dRN to key brain structures make serotonergic system fundamental in regulating brain homeostasis. However, studies also show feedback loops from numerous areas of the brain controlling the serotonergic neurons located in the [nucleus raphe dorsalis](./Nucleus_raphe_dorsalis "Nucleus raphe dorsalis"), including the orbital cortex, [cingulate cortex](./Cingulate_cortex "Cingulate cortex"), medial [preoptic area](./Preoptic_area "Preoptic area"), lateral [preoptic area](./Preoptic_area "Preoptic area"), and several areas of the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"). The connection between these areas, particularly between the [nucleus raphe dorsalis](./Nucleus_raphe_dorsalis "Nucleus raphe dorsalis") and the orbital cortices, is thought to have influences on [depression](./Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)") and [obsessive compulsive disorder](./Obsessive_compulsive_disorder "Obsessive compulsive disorder") prognosis.[[18]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-18)

## Function

The raphe nuclei have a vast impact upon the central nervous system.
Many of the neurons in the nuclei (but not the majority) are serotonergic; i.e., contain [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"), a type of [monoamine](./Monoamine "Monoamine") neurotransmitter and are modulated through fibrous pathways in the midbrain.[[19]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-19)

Projections from the raphe nuclei also terminate in the dorsal horn of spinal gray matter where they regulate the release of [enkephalins](./Enkephalins "Enkephalins"), which inhibit pain sensation.

The raphe nuclei provide feedback to the [suprachiasmatic nuclei](./Suprachiasmatic_nuclei "Suprachiasmatic nuclei") (SCN), thus contributing in circadian rhythms in animals. The SCN transmits to the raphe nuclei via the [dorsomedial hypothalamic nucleus](./Dorsomedial_hypothalamic_nucleus "Dorsomedial hypothalamic nucleus") altering serotonin levels for sleep/wake states. The raphe nuclei will then transmit feedback to the SCN about the animal's vigilance and levels of alertness. This reciprocal feedback between the two structures provides an adaptable yet stable basis of circadian rhythms.[[20]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-20)

### Thermoregulation

A large increase in sympathetic nerve activity was observed when an excitatory amino acid was injected into the [raphe pallidus](./Nucleus_raphe_pallidus "Nucleus raphe pallidus"), resulting in both brown adipose tissue (BAT) temperature and heart rate increasing. This suggests that activation of the raphe nucleus results in an increase in sympathetic activity to the BAT.[[21]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-pmid14596844-21)

The raphe pallidus wasn't switched off using 8-OH-DPAT, which in turn reduced body temperature due to a reduced response to cold. This suggests the importance of the raphe nucleus in responding appropriately to the cold.[[22]](./Raphe_nuclei#cite_note-22)
