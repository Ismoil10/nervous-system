The **visual system** is the [physiological](./Physiology "Physiology") basis of *[visual perception](./Visual_perception "Visual perception")* (the ability to [detect and process](./Perception "Perception") [light](./Light "Light")). The system detects, [transduces](./Phototransduction "Phototransduction") and interprets [information](./Information "Information") concerning [light](./Light "Light") within the [visible range](./Visible_range "Visible range") to construct an [image](./Imaging "Imaging") and build a [mental model](./Mental_model "Mental model") of the surrounding environment. The visual system is associated with the [eye](./Eye "Eye") and functionally divided into the [optical](./Optics "Optics") system (including [cornea](./Cornea "Cornea") and [lens](./Crystalline_lens "Crystalline lens")) and the [neural](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") system (including the [retina](./Retina "Retina") and [visual cortex](./Visual_cortex "Visual cortex")).

The visual system performs a number of complex tasks based on the *image forming* functionality of the eye, including the formation of monocular images, the neural mechanisms underlying [stereopsis](./Stereopsis "Stereopsis") and assessment of distances to ([depth perception](./Depth_perception "Depth perception")) and between objects, [motion perception](./Motion_perception "Motion perception"), [pattern recognition](./Pattern_recognition "Pattern recognition"), accurate [motor coordination](./Motor_coordination "Motor coordination") under visual guidance, and [colour vision](./Colour_vision "Colour vision"). Together, these facilitate higher order tasks, such as [object identification](./Object_recognition_(cognitive_science) "Object recognition (cognitive science)"). The [neuropsychological](./Neuropsychological "Neuropsychological") side of visual information processing is known as [visual perception](./Visual_perception "Visual perception"), an abnormality of which is called [visual impairment](./Visual_impairment "Visual impairment"), and a complete absence of which is called [blindness](./Blindness "Blindness"). The visual system also has several non-image forming visual functions, independent of visual perception, including the [pupillary light reflex](./Pupillary_light_reflex "Pupillary light reflex") and circadian [photoentrainment](./Entrainment_(chronobiology) "Entrainment (chronobiology)").

This article describes the human visual system, which is representative of [mammalian vision](./Mammalian_vision "Mammalian vision"), and to a lesser extent the [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") visual system.

### Optical

Together, the [cornea](./Cornea "Cornea") and [lens](./Lens_(anatomy) "Lens (anatomy)") refract light into a small image and shine it on the [retina](./Retina "Retina"). The retina [transduces](./Visual_phototransduction "Visual phototransduction") this image into electrical pulses using [rods](./Rods_(eye) "Rods (eye)") and [cones](./Cones_(eye) "Cones (eye)"). The [optic nerve](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") then carries these pulses through the [optic canal](./Optic_canal "Optic canal"). Upon reaching the [optic chiasm](./Optic_chiasm "Optic chiasm") the nerve fibers decussate (left becomes right). The fibers then branch and terminate in three places.[[1]](./Visual_system#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Visual_system#cite_note-2)[[3]](./Visual_system#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Visual_system#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Visual_system#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Visual_system#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Visual_system#cite_note-7)

### Neural

Most of the optic nerve fibers end in the [lateral geniculate nucleus](./Lateral_geniculate_nucleus "Lateral geniculate nucleus") (LGN). Before the LGN forwards the pulses to V1 of the visual cortex (primary) it gauges the range of objects and tags every major object with a velocity tag. These tags predict object movement.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The LGN also sends some fibers to V2 and V3.[[8]](./Visual_system#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Visual_system#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Visual_system#cite_note-10)[[11]](./Visual_system#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Visual_system#cite_note-12)

V1 performs edge-detection to understand spatial organization (initially, 40 milliseconds in, focusing on even small spatial and color changes. Then, 100 milliseconds in, upon receiving the translated LGN, V2, and V3 info, also begins focusing on global organization). V1 also creates a bottom-up [saliency map](./Saliency_map "Saliency map") to guide attention or [gaze shift](./Gaze_shift "Gaze shift").[[13]](./Visual_system#cite_note-13)

V2 both forwards (direct and via [pulvinar](./Pulvinar_nuclei "Pulvinar nuclei")) pulses to V1 and receives them. Pulvinar is responsible for [saccade](./Saccade "Saccade") and visual attention. V2 serves much the same function as V1, however, it also handles [illusory contours](./Illusory_contours "Illusory contours"), determining depth by comparing left and right pulses (2D images), and foreground distinguishment. V2 connects to V1 - V5.

V3 helps process '[global motion](./Global_motion "Global motion")' (direction and speed) of objects. V3 connects to V1 (weak), V2, and the [inferior temporal cortex](./Inferior_temporal_cortex "Inferior temporal cortex").[[14]](./Visual_system#cite_note-14)[[15]](./Visual_system#cite_note-15)

V4 recognizes simple shapes, and gets input from V1 (strong), V2, V3, LGN, and pulvinar.[[16]](./Visual_system#cite_note-16) V5's outputs include V4 and its surrounding area, and eye-movement motor cortices ([frontal eye-field](./Frontal_eye_fields "Frontal eye fields") and [lateral intraparietal area](./Lateral_intraparietal_cortex "Lateral intraparietal cortex")).

V5's functionality is similar to that of the other V's, however, it integrates local object motion into global motion on a complex level. V6 works in conjunction with V5 on motion analysis. V5 analyzes self-motion, whereas V6 analyzes motion of objects relative to the background. V6's primary input is V1, with V5 additions. V6 houses the [topographical map](./Topographic_map "Topographic map") for vision. V6 outputs to the region directly around it (V6A). V6A has direct connections to arm-moving cortices, including the [premotor cortex](./Premotor_cortex "Premotor cortex").[[17]](./Visual_system#cite_note-17)[[18]](./Visual_system#cite_note-18)

The [inferior temporal gyrus](./Inferior_temporal_gyrus "Inferior temporal gyrus") recognizes complex shapes, objects, and faces or, in conjunction with the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), creates new [memories](./Memories "Memories").[[19]](./Visual_system#cite_note-19) The [pretectal area](./Pretectal_area "Pretectal area") is seven unique [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)"). Anterior, posterior and medial pretectal nuclei inhibit pain (indirectly), aid in [REM](./Rapid_eye_movement_sleep "Rapid eye movement sleep"), and aid the [accommodation reflex](./Accommodation_reflex "Accommodation reflex"), respectively.[[20]](./Visual_system#cite_note-20) The [Edinger-Westphal nucleus](./Edinger–Westphal_nucleus "Edinger–Westphal nucleus") moderates [pupil dilation](./Pupil_dilation "Pupil dilation") and aids (since it provides parasympathetic fibers) in convergence of the eyes and lens adjustment.[[21]](./Visual_system#cite_note-21) Nuclei of the optic tract are involved in smooth pursuit eye movement and the accommodation reflex, as well as REM.

The [suprachiasmatic nucleus](./Suprachiasmatic_nucleus "Suprachiasmatic nucleus") is the region of the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") that halts production of [melatonin](./Melatonin "Melatonin") (indirectly) at first light.[[22]](./Visual_system#cite_note-22)

### Eye

Main articles: [Eye](./Eye "Eye") and [Anterior segment of eyeball](./Anterior_segment_of_eyeball "Anterior segment of eyeball")

Light entering the eye is [refracted](./Refracted "Refracted") as it passes through the [cornea](./Cornea "Cornea"). It then passes through the [pupil](./Pupil "Pupil") (controlled by the [iris](./Iris_(anatomy) "Iris (anatomy)")) and is further refracted by the [lens](./Lens_(vision) "Lens (vision)"). The cornea and lens act together as a compound lens to project an inverted image onto the retina.
