The **Golgi tendon organ** (**GTO**) (also known as **Golgi organ**, **tendon organ**, **neurotendinous organ** or **neurotendinous spindle**) is a skeletal muscle [stretch receptor](./Stretch_receptor "Stretch receptor") [proprioceptor](./Proprioceptor "Proprioceptor"). It is situated at the interface between a [muscle](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") and its [tendon](./Tendon "Tendon") known as the [musculotendinous junction](./Musculotendinous_junction "Musculotendinous junction").[[1]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-MacIntosh-1) It senses muscle tension (whereas [muscle spindles](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle") are responsible for detecting muscle length and changes in muscle length). It is innervated by [type Ib sensory nerve fibers](./Axon#Sensory "Axon").[[2]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-:02-2)

It represents the sensory leg of the [Golgi tendon reflex](./Golgi_tendon_reflex "Golgi tendon reflex") [arc](./Reflex_arc "Reflex arc").

The Golgi tendon organ is one of several [eponymous terms](./Camillo_Golgi#Eponyms "Camillo Golgi") named after the Italian physician [Camillo Golgi](./Camillo_Golgi "Camillo Golgi").

## Structure

The body of the Golgi tendon organ is made up of braided strands of [collagen](./Collagen "Collagen") (intrafusal fasciculi) that are less compact than elsewhere in the [tendon](./Tendon "Tendon") and are encapsulated.[[3]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Gray2011-3) The capsule is connected in [series](./Series_and_parallel_circuits "Series and parallel circuits") (along a single path) with a group of [muscle fibers](./Extrafusal_muscle_fiber "Extrafusal muscle fiber") (10-20 fibers[[4]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Purves2018-p201-202-4)) at one end, and merge into the tendon proper at the other. Each capsule is about 1 [mm](./Millimetre "Millimetre") long, has a [diameter](./Diameter "Diameter") of about 0.1 mm.[[5]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-PearsonGordon2013-p800-5)[[6]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Saladin2018-6)

One or more fast-conducting [type Ib sensory nerve fibers](./Axon#Sensory "Axon") penetrate the capsule and lose their medullary[*[clarification needed](./Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] sheaths, branch, intertwine with the collagen fibers, and terminate as flattened leaf-like endings between the collagen strands (see figure).[[5]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-PearsonGordon2013-p800-5)[[6]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Saladin2018-6)

## Function

Mammalian tendon organ showing typical position in a muscle (left), neuronal connections in spinal cord (middle) and expanded schematic (right). The tendon organ is a stretch receptor that signals the force developed by the muscle. The sensory endings of the Ib afferent are entwined amongst the musculotendinous strands of 10-20 extrafusal muscle fibers.[[A]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-8)[[4]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Purves2018-p201-202-4) See an [animated version](https://www.ualberta.ca/~aprochaz/research_interactive_receptor_model.html).

When the muscle generates force, the sensory terminals of the Ib afferent axons are compressed and become deformed which causes the opening of stretch-sensitive [cation](./Cation "Cation") channels, depolarizing the axon and causing it to fire [nerve impulses](./Action_potential "Action potential"). The action potential frequency encodes the [force](./Force "Force") being developed by the muscle fibers associated with the Golgi tendon organ. The average level of activity in a tendon organ population is representative of the whole muscle force.[[5]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-PearsonGordon2013-p800-5)[[8]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Prochazka-9)

### Integrative function

The Ib sensory [feedback](./Feedback "Feedback") generates [stretch reflexes](./Stretch_reflex "Stretch reflex") and supraspinal responses which control muscle contraction. Ib afferents [synapse](./Synapse "Synapse") with [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron") in the spinal cord that also project to the brain cerebellum and cerebral cortex. The [Golgi tendon reflex](./Golgi_tendon_reflex "Golgi tendon reflex") assists in regulating muscle contraction force. It is associated with the Ib. Tendon organs signal muscle force through the entire physiological range, not only at high strain.[[8]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Prochazka-9)[[9]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-10)

During locomotion, Ib input excites rather than inhibits motoneurons of the receptor-bearing muscles, and it affects the timing of the transitions between the stance and swing phases of locomotion.[[10]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-11) The switch to autogenic excitation is a form of positive feedback.[[11]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-12)

The ascending or [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") pathways to the [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum") are the dorsal and ventral [spinocerebellar tracts](./Spinocerebellar_tract "Spinocerebellar tract"). They are involved in the cerebellar regulation of [movement](./Human_musculoskeletal_system "Human musculoskeletal system").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## History

Until 1967, it was believed that Golgi tendon organs had a high threshold, only becoming active at high muscle forces. Consequently, it was thought that tendon organ input caused "weightlifting failure" through the [clasp-knife reflex](./Clasp-knife_response "Clasp-knife response"), which protected the muscle and tendons from excessive force. [*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] However, the underlying premise was shown to be incorrect by James Houk and Elwood Henneman in 1967.[[12]](./Golgi_tendon_organ#cite_note-Houk-13)
