**Group A nerve fibers** are one of the three classes of [nerve fiber](./Axon "Axon") as *generally classified* by [Erlanger](./Joseph_Erlanger "Joseph Erlanger") and [Gasser](./Herbert_Spencer_Gasser "Herbert Spencer Gasser"). The other two classes are the [group B nerve fibers](./Group_B_nerve_fiber "Group B nerve fiber"), and the [group C nerve fibers](./Group_C_nerve_fiber "Group C nerve fiber"). Group A are heavily [myelinated](./Myelin "Myelin"), group B are moderately [myelinated](./Myelin "Myelin"), and group C are unmyelinated.[[1]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-G_and_H-1)[[2]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Classification-2)

The other classification is a [sensory](./Axon#Sensory "Axon") grouping that uses the terms *[type Ia and type Ib](./Type_Ia_sensory_fiber "Type Ia sensory fiber")*, *[type II](./Type_II_sensory_fiber "Type II sensory fiber")*, *type III*, and *type IV*, sensory fibers.[[1]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-G_and_H-1)

## Types

There are four subdivisions of group A nerve fibers: alpha (α) Aα; beta (β) Aβ; , gamma (γ) Aγ, and delta (δ) Aδ. These subdivisions have different amounts of myelination and axon thickness and [therefore](./Cable_theory "Cable theory") transmit signals at [different speeds](./Nerve_conduction_velocity "Nerve conduction velocity"). Larger diameter axons and more myelin insulation lead to faster signal propagation.

Group A nerves are found in both motor and sensory pathways.[[2]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Classification-2)

Type Aα fibers include the *type Ia* and *type Ib* sensory fibers of the alternative classification system, and are the fibers from [muscle spindle](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle") endings and the [Golgi tendon](./Golgi_tendon "Golgi tendon"), respectively.[[1]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-G_and_H-1)

Type Aβ fibres, and type Aγ, are the *[type II](./Type_II_sensory_fiber "Type II sensory fiber")* [afferent fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") from [stretch receptors](./Stretch_receptor "Stretch receptor").[[1]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-G_and_H-1) Type Aβ fibres from the skin are mostly dedicated to touch. However a small fraction of these fast fibres, termed "ultrafast nociceptors", also transmit pain.[[7]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-NagiMarshall2019-7)

Type Aδ fibers are the [afferent fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") of [nociceptors](./Nociceptor "Nociceptor"). Aδ fibers carry information from peripheral mechanoreceptors and thermoreceptors to the dorsal horn of the spinal cord. This pathway describes the first-order neuron. Aδ fibers serve to receive and transmit information primarily relating to [acute pain](./Acute_pain "Acute pain") (sharp, immediate, and relatively short-lasting). This type of pain can result from several classifications of stimulants: temperature-induced, mechanical, and chemical. This can be part of a [withdrawal reflex](./Withdrawal_reflex "Withdrawal reflex")—initiated by the Aδ fibers in the [reflex arc](./Reflex_arc "Reflex arc") of activating withdrawal responses.[[8]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-9) These are the *type III* group. Aδ fibers carry cold, pressure, and acute pain signals; because they are thin (2–5 μm in diameter) and [myelinated](./Myelin "Myelin"), they send impulses faster than unmyelinated [C fibers](./C_fiber "C fiber"), but more slowly than other, more thickly myelinated group A nerve fibers. Their [conduction velocities](./Conduction_velocity "Conduction velocity") are moderate.[[10]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Sinauer-10)

Their [cell bodies](./Soma_(biology) "Soma (biology)") are located in the [dorsal root ganglia](./Dorsal_root_ganglion "Dorsal root ganglion") and axons are sent to the periphery to innervate target organs and are also sent through the dorsal roots to the spinal cord. Within the spinal cord the axons reach the [posterior grey column](./Posterior_grey_column "Posterior grey column") and terminate in Rexed laminae I to V.[[11]](./Group_A_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Basbaum-11)
