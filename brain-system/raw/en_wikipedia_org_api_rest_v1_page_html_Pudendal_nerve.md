The **pudendal nerve** is the main [nerve](./Nerve "Nerve") of the [perineum](./Perineum "Perineum").[[1]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Grant2013-1): 274 It is a [mixed (motor and sensory) nerve](./Mixed_nerve "Mixed nerve") and also conveys [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") fibers. It carries sensation from the [external genitalia](./External_genitalia "External genitalia") of both sexes and the skin around the [anus](./Human_anus "Human anus") and perineum, as well as the [motor](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron") supply to various pelvic muscles, including the [male](./External_sphincter_muscle_of_male_urethra "External sphincter muscle of male urethra") or [female external urethral sphincter](./External_sphincter_muscle_of_female_urethra "External sphincter muscle of female urethra") and the [external anal sphincter](./External_anal_sphincter "External anal sphincter").

If damaged, most commonly by childbirth, loss of sensation or [fecal incontinence](./Fecal_incontinence "Fecal incontinence") may result. The nerve may be temporarily anesthetized, called [pudendal anesthesia](./Pudendal_anesthesia "Pudendal anesthesia") or pudendal block.

The [pudendal canal](./Pudendal_canal "Pudendal canal") that carries the pudendal nerve is also known by the eponymous term "Alcock's canal", after [Benjamin Alcock](./Benjamin_Alcock "Benjamin Alcock"), an Irish anatomist who documented the canal in 1836.

## Structure

### Origin

Image showing the [greater sciatic foramen](./Greater_sciatic_foramen "Greater sciatic foramen") (large [foramen](./Foramen "Foramen")), and the [lesser sciatic foramen](./Lesser_sciatic_foramen "Lesser sciatic foramen"), separated by the [sacrospinous ligament](./Sacrospinous_ligament "Sacrospinous ligament"). The pudendal nerve exits the pelvis through the greater sciatic foramen, passes over the ligament, and then reenters the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen.

The pudendal nerve is paired, meaning there are two nerves, one on the left and one on the right side of the body. Each is formed as three roots immediately converge above the upper border of the [sacrotuberous ligament](./Sacrotuberous_ligament "Sacrotuberous ligament") and the [coccygeus muscle](./Coccygeus_muscle "Coccygeus muscle").[[2]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Grays-2) The three roots become two cords when the middle and lower root join to form the lower cord, and these in turn unite to form the pudendal nerve proper just proximal to the [sacrospinous ligament](./Sacrospinous_ligament "Sacrospinous ligament").[[3]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Shafik1995-3) The three roots are derived from the [ventral rami](./Ventral_rami "Ventral rami") of the 2nd, 3rd, and 4th [sacral spinal nerves](./Sacral_spinal_nerves "Sacral spinal nerves"), with the primary contribution coming from the 4th.[[2]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Grays-2)[[4]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Moore215-4): 215[[5]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Russell2006-5): 157

### Course and relations

The pudendal nerve passes between the [piriformis muscle](./Piriformis_muscle "Piriformis muscle") and [coccygeus](./Coccygeus "Coccygeus") (ischiococcygeus) muscles and leaves the pelvis through the lower part of the [greater sciatic foramen](./Greater_sciatic_foramen "Greater sciatic foramen").[[2]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Grays-2) It crosses over the lateral part of the [sacrospinous ligament](./Sacrospinous_ligament "Sacrospinous ligament") and reenters the pelvis through the [lesser sciatic foramen](./Lesser_sciatic_foramen "Lesser sciatic foramen"). After reentering the pelvis, it accompanies the [internal pudendal artery](./Internal_pudendal_artery "Internal pudendal artery") and [internal pudendal vein](./Internal_pudendal_vein "Internal pudendal vein") upwards and forwards along the lateral wall of the [ischiorectal fossa](./Ischiorectal_fossa "Ischiorectal fossa"), being contained in a sheath of the [obturator fascia](./Obturator_fascia "Obturator fascia") termed the [pudendal canal](./Pudendal_canal "Pudendal canal"), along with the internal pudendal blood vessels.[[6]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ASCRStextbook-6): 8

### Branches

Inside the pudendal canal, the nerve divides into branches, first giving off the [inferior rectal nerve](./Inferior_rectal_nerve "Inferior rectal nerve"), then the [perineal nerve](./Perineal_nerve "Perineal nerve"), before continuing as the [dorsal nerve of the penis](./Dorsal_nerve_of_the_penis "Dorsal nerve of the penis") (in males) or the [dorsal nerve of the clitoris](./Dorsal_nerve_of_the_clitoris "Dorsal nerve of the clitoris") (in females).[[6]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ASCRStextbook-6): 34

### Nucleus

The nerve is a major branch of the [sacral plexus](./Sacral_plexus "Sacral plexus"),[[7]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Brucker2013-7): 950 with fibers originating in [Onuf's nucleus](./Onuf's_nucleus "Onuf's nucleus") in the [sacral](./Sacrum "Sacrum") region of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[3]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Shafik1995-3)

### Variation

The pudendal nerve may vary in its origins. For example, the pudendal nerve may actually originate in the [sciatic nerve](./Sciatic_nerve "Sciatic nerve").[[8]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-8) Consequently, damage to the sciatic nerve can affect the pudendal nerve as well. Sometimes [dorsal rami](./Dorsal_ramus_of_spinal_nerve "Dorsal ramus of spinal nerve") of the first [sacral nerve](./Sacral_nerve "Sacral nerve") contribute fibers to the pudendal nerve, and even more rarely S5.[[3]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Shafik1995-3)

## Function

The pudendal nerve has both motor (control of muscles) and sensory functions. It also carries [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") autonomic fibers (but not [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") fibers).[[9]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Kaur2022-9)[[10]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-10): 1738

### Sensory

The pudendal nerve supplies sensation to the [penis](./Human_penis "Human penis") in males, and to the [clitoris](./Clitoris "Clitoris") in females, which travels through the branches of both the [dorsal nerve of the penis](./Dorsal_nerve_of_the_penis "Dorsal nerve of the penis") and the [dorsal nerve of the clitoris](./Dorsal_nerve_of_the_clitoris "Dorsal nerve of the clitoris").[[11]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-11): 422 The posterior [scrotum](./Scrotum "Scrotum") in males and the [labia majora](./Labia_majora "Labia majora") in females are also supplied, via the [posterior scrotal nerves](./Posterior_scrotal_nerves "Posterior scrotal nerves") (males) or [posterior labial nerves](./Posterior_labial_nerves "Posterior labial nerves") (females). The pudendal nerve is one of several nerves supplying sensation to these areas.[[12]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ORT2007-12) Branches also supply sensation to the [anal canal](./Anal_canal "Anal canal").[[6]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ASCRStextbook-6): 8 By providing sensation to the penis and the clitoris, the pudendal nerve is responsible for the [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") component of [penile erection](./Penile_erection "Penile erection") and [clitoral erection](./Clitoral_erection "Clitoral erection").[[13]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-13) : 147

### Motor

Branches [innervate](./Innervate "Innervate") muscles of the [perineum](./Perineum "Perineum") and the [pelvic floor](./Pelvic_floor "Pelvic floor"); namely, the [bulbospongiosus](./Bulbospongiosus_muscle "Bulbospongiosus muscle") and the [ischiocavernosus](./Ischiocavernosus_muscle "Ischiocavernosus muscle") muscles respectively[[12]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ORT2007-12), the [levator ani](./Levator_ani "Levator ani") muscle[[14]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-14) (including the [Iliococcygeus](./Iliococcygeus "Iliococcygeus"), [pubococcygeus](./Pubococcygeus "Pubococcygeus"), [puborectalis](./Puborectalis "Puborectalis") and either [pubovaginalis](./Pubovaginalis "Pubovaginalis") in females or [puboprostaticus](./Puboprostaticus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Puboprostaticus") in males)[[11]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-11): 422[[15]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-15) the [external anal sphincter](./External_anal_sphincter "External anal sphincter") (via the inferior anal branch),[[6]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-ASCRStextbook-6): 7 and [male](./External_sphincter_muscle_of_male_urethra "External sphincter muscle of male urethra") or [female external urethral sphincter](./External_sphincter_muscle_of_female_urethra "External sphincter muscle of female urethra").[[11]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-11): 424–425

As it functions to innervate the external urethral sphincter it is responsible for the tone of the sphincter mediated via [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine") release. This means that during periods of increased acetylcholine release the [skeletal muscle](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") in the external urethral sphincter contracts, causing urinary retention. Whereas in periods of decreased acetylcholine release the skeletal muscle in the external urethral sphincter relaxes, allowing voiding of the bladder to occur.[[16]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-16) (Unlike the internal sphincter muscle, the external sphincter is made of skeletal muscle, therefore it is under voluntary control of the [somatic nervous system](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system").)

It is also responsible for [ejaculation](./Ejaculation "Ejaculation").[[17]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-17)

## Clinical significance

The pudendal nerve may be tested by elicitation of the [anocutaneous reflex](./Anocutaneous_reflex "Anocutaneous reflex") ("anal wink").[[18]](./Pudendal_nerve#cite_note-Steele2020-18)
