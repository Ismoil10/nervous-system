The **paraventricular nucleus** (**PVN**) is a [nucleus](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") in the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), located next to the [third ventricle](./Third_ventricle "Third ventricle"). PVN [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") project to many different brain regions including the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary"), the [median eminence](./Median_eminence "Median eminence")  as well as the brainstem and spinal cord. Neurons which project to the posterior pituitary secrete [oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin"), whereas neurons that project to the median eminence release [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") (CRH), [thyrotropin-releasing hormone](./Thyrotropin-releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin-releasing hormone") (TRH) and other neuropeptides.[[1]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-1) CRH and TRH are secreted into the [hypophyseal portal system](./Hypophyseal_portal_system "Hypophyseal portal system"), and target effector endocrine cells in the [anterior pituitary](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary"). The PVN is thought to mediate many diverse functions, including [osmoregulation](./Osmoregulation "Osmoregulation"), [appetite](./Appetite "Appetite"), [wakefulness](./Wakefulness "Wakefulness"), [stress](./Stress_(biology) "Stress (biology)") responses, as well as the regulation of [social behavior](./Social_behavior "Social behavior").[[2]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-stuart-2)[[3]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-5)

## Location

The paraventricular nucleus lies adjacent to the [third ventricle](./Third_ventricle "Third ventricle"). It lies within the periventricular zone and is not to be confused with the [periventricular nucleus](./Periventricular_nucleus "Periventricular nucleus"), which occupies a more medial position, beneath the [third ventricle](./Third_ventricle "Third ventricle"). The PVN is highly vascularised and is protected by the [blood–brain barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier"), although its [neuroendocrine cells](./Neuroendocrine_cells "Neuroendocrine cells") extend to sites (in the [median eminence](./Median_eminence "Median eminence") and in the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary")) beyond the blood–brain barrier.[[6]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-:0-6) The PVN accounts for only about 1% of the brain volume. In the rat, the PVN consists of approximately 100,000 neurons located in a volume of about 0.5 cubic millimetre.[[7]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-7)

## Neurons

The PVN contains [magnocellular neurosecretory cells](./Magnocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Magnocellular neurosecretory cell") whose [axons](./Axon "Axon") extend into the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary"), [parvocellular neurosecretory cells](./Parvocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Parvocellular neurosecretory cell") that project to the [median eminence](./Median_eminence "Median eminence"), ultimately signalling to the [anterior pituitary](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary"), and several populations of other cells that project to many different brain regions including parvocellular preautonomic cells that project to the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[6]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-:0-6)

### Magnocellular neurosecretory neurons

Magnocellular neurons of the PVN and SON project to the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary")

The [magnocellular](./Magnocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Magnocellular neurosecretory cell") cells in the PVN elaborate and secrete two [peptide hormones](./Peptide_hormones "Peptide hormones"): [oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin").

These hormones are packaged into large vesicles, which are then transported down the [unmyelinated](./Myelin "Myelin") axons of the cells and released from neurosecretory nerve terminals residing in the posterior pituitary gland.[[8]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-8)

Similar magnocellular neurons are found in the [supraoptic nucleus](./Supraoptic_nucleus "Supraoptic nucleus") which also secrete vasopressin and oxytocin.[[9]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-9)

### Parvocellular neurosecretory neurons

The axons of the [parvocellular](./Parvocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Parvocellular neurosecretory cell") neurosecretory neurons of the PVN project to the median eminence, a neurohemal organ at the base of the brain, where their neurosecretory nerve terminals release their hormones at the primary capillary plexus of the [hypophyseal portal system](./Hypophyseal_portal_system "Hypophyseal portal system"). The median eminence contains fiber terminals from many hypothalamic neuroendocrine neurons, secreting different neurotransmitters or neuropeptides, including vasopressin, corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH), gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), dopamine (DA) and somatostatin (growth hormone release inhibiting hormone, GIH) into blood vessels in the hypophyseal portal system. The blood vessels carry the peptides to the anterior pituitary gland, where they regulate the secretion of hormones into the systemic circulation. The parvocellular neurosecretory cells include those that make:

- [Corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") (CRH), which regulates [ACTH](./ACTH "ACTH") secretion from the [anterior pituitary gland](./Anterior_pituitary_gland "Anterior pituitary gland")
- [Somatostatin](./Somatostatin "Somatostatin"), which regulates [growth hormone](./Growth_hormone "Growth hormone") secretion from the [anterior pituitary gland](./Anterior_pituitary_gland "Anterior pituitary gland")
- [Thyrotropin-releasing hormone](./Thyrotropin-releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin-releasing hormone") (TRH), which regulates [TSH](./Thyroid-stimulating_hormone "Thyroid-stimulating hormone") and [prolactin](./Prolactin "Prolactin") secretion

### Centrally-projecting neurons

As well as neuroendocrine neurons, the PVN contains [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron") and populations of neurons that project centrally (i.e., to other brain regions). The centrally-projecting neurons include

- Parvocellular oxytocin cells, which project mainly to the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). These neurons are thought to have a role in gastric reflexes and penile erection,[[10]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid11477488-10)[[11]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid16043278-11)
- Parvocellular vasopressin cells, which project to many points in the hypothalamus and [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system"), as well as to the brainstem and spinal cord (these are involved in blood pressure and temperature regulation), and [brown fat](./Brown_adipose_tissue "Brown adipose tissue") [thermogenesis](./Thermogenesis "Thermogenesis").
- Parvocellular CRH neurons, which are thought to be involved in stress-related behaviors.

## Afferent inputs

The PVN receives afferent inputs from many brain regions and different parts of the body, by hormonal control.[[2]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-stuart-2)

Among these, inputs from neurons in structures adjacent to the anterior wall of the third ventricle (the "AV3V region") carry information about the electrolyte composition of the blood, and about circulating concentrations of such hormones as [angiotensin](./Angiotensin "Angiotensin") and [relaxin](./Relaxin "Relaxin"), to regulate the magnocellular neurons.[[12]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid3044525-12)

Inputs from the brainstem (the [nucleus of the solitary tract](./Nucleus_of_the_solitary_tract "Nucleus of the solitary tract")) and the ventrolateral medulla carry information from the heart and [stomach](./Stomach "Stomach"). Inputs from the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") to the CRH neurones are important regulators of stress responses.

Inputs from [neuropeptide Y](./Neuropeptide_Y "Neuropeptide Y")-containing neurons in the [arcuate nucleus](./Arcuate_nucleus "Arcuate nucleus") coordinate metabolic regulation (via TRH secretion) with regulation of energy intake.[[13]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid16874931-13)[[14]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid16340035-14)[[15]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-pmid20074584-15) Specifically, the projections from the arcuate nucleus seem to exert their effect on appetite via [MC4R](./Melanocortin_4_receptor "Melanocortin 4 receptor")-expressing oxytocinergic cells of the PVN.[[16]](./Paraventricular_nucleus#cite_note-16)

Inputs from [suprachiasmatic nucleus](./Suprachiasmatic_nucleus "Suprachiasmatic nucleus") about levels of lighting (circadian rhythms).

Inputs from glucose sensors within the brain stimulate release of [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") and [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") from [parvocellular neurosecretory cells](./Parvocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Parvocellular neurosecretory cell").
