Neuromodulation

Regulation of neurons by neurotransmitters

For the therapy, see [Neuromodulation (medicine)](./Neuromodulation_(medicine) "Neuromodulation (medicine)"). For other uses, see [Neuromodulation (disambiguation)](./Neuromodulation_(disambiguation) "Neuromodulation (disambiguation)").

**Neuromodulation** is the [physiological](./Physiology "Physiology") process by which a given [neuron](./Neuron "Neuron") uses one or more chemicals to regulate diverse populations of neurons. Neuromodulators typically bind to [metabotropic](./Metabotropic_receptor "Metabotropic receptor"), [G-protein coupled receptors](./G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor") (GPCRs) to initiate a second messenger signaling cascade that induces a broad, long-lasting signal. This modulation can last for hundreds of milliseconds to several minutes. Some of the effects of neuromodulators include altering intrinsic firing activity,[[1]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-1) increasing or decreasing voltage-dependent currents,[[2]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-Harris-Warrick_2113–2128-2) altering synaptic efficacy, increasing bursting activity[[2]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-Harris-Warrick_2113–2128-2) and reconfiguring synaptic connectivity.[[3]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-3)

Illustration of the brain and spinal cord connecting to a muscle, illustrating the connection between the central and peripheral nervous system.

Major neuromodulators in the central nervous system include: [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine"), [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"), [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"), [histamine](./Histamine "Histamine"), [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine"), [nitric oxide](./Nitric_oxide "Nitric oxide"), and several [neuropeptides](./Neuropeptide "Neuropeptide"). [Cannabinoids](./Cannabinoids "Cannabinoids") can also be powerful CNS neuromodulators.[[4]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-6) Neuromodulators can be packaged into vesicles and released by neurons, secreted as hormones and delivered through the circulatory system.[[7]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-7) A neuromodulator can be conceptualized as a [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") that is not reabsorbed by the pre-synaptic neuron or broken down into a metabolite. Some neuromodulators end up spending a significant amount of time in the [cerebrospinal fluid](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid") (CSF), influencing (or "modulating") the activity of several other neurons in the [brain](./Brain "Brain").[[8]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-8)

## Neuromodulator systems

See also: [Neural pathways](./Neural_pathways "Neural pathways")

The major [neurotransmitter systems](./Neurotransmitter_systems "Neurotransmitter systems") are the [noradrenaline](./Noradrenaline "Noradrenaline") (norepinephrine) system, the [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine") system, the [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin") system, and the [cholinergic](./Cholinergic "Cholinergic") system. Drugs targeting the [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") of such systems affect the whole system, which explains the mode of action of many drugs.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Most other neurotransmitters, on the other hand, e.g. [glutamate](./Glutamate "Glutamate"), [GABA](./GABA "GABA") and [glycine](./Glycine "Glycine"), are used very generally throughout the central nervous system.

### Noradrenaline system

Further information: [Norepinephrine § Norepinephrine system](./Norepinephrine#Norepinephrine_system "Norepinephrine")

Skeletal formulae diagram of Noradrenaline

The noradrenaline system consists of around 15,000 neurons, primarily in the [locus coeruleus](./Locus_coeruleus "Locus coeruleus").[[12]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-SaraNeuron-12) This is diminutive compared to the more than 100 billion neurons in the brain. As with dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra, neurons in the locus coeruleus tend to be [melanin](./Melanin "Melanin")-pigmented. [Noradrenaline](./Noradrenaline "Noradrenaline") is released from the neurons, and acts on [adrenergic receptors](./Adrenergic_receptor "Adrenergic receptor"). Noradrenaline is often released steadily so that it can prepare the supporting glial cells for calibrated responses. Despite containing a relatively small number of neurons, when activated, the noradrenaline system plays major roles in the brain including involvement in suppression of the neuroinflammatory response, stimulation of neuronal plasticity through LTP, regulation of glutamate uptake by astrocytes and LTD, and consolidation of memory.[[13]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-pmid22717696-13)

### Dopamine system

Further information: [Dopamine § Functions in the brain](./Dopamine#Functions_in_the_brain "Dopamine")

The dopamine or dopaminergic system consists of several pathways, originating from the [ventral tegmentum](./Ventral_tegmentum "Ventral tegmentum") or [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra") as examples. It acts on [dopamine receptors](./Dopamine_receptor "Dopamine receptor").[[14]](./Neuromodulation#cite_note-pmid15177784-14)

Skeletal formulae diagram of Dopamine

[Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease") is at least in part related to dropping out of dopaminergic cells in [deep-brain nuclei](./Deep-brain_nuclei?action=edit&redlink=1 "Deep-brain nuclei"), primarily the melanin-pigmented neurons in the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra") but secondarily the noradrenergic neurons of the locus coeruleus. Treatments potentiating the effect of dopamine precursors have been proposed and effected, with moderate success.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Dopamine pharmacology

- [Cocaine](./Cocaine "Cocaine"), for example, blocks the [reuptake](./Reuptake "Reuptake") of [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine"), leaving these neurotransmitters in the [synaptic gap](./Synapse "Synapse") for longer.
- [AMPT](./AMPT "AMPT") prevents the conversion of tyrosine to [L-DOPA](./L-DOPA "L-DOPA"), the precursor to dopamine; [reserpine](./Reserpine "Reserpine") prevents dopamine storage within [vesicles](./Synaptic_vesicle "Synaptic vesicle"); and [deprenyl](./Deprenyl "Deprenyl") inhibits [monoamine oxidase](./Monoamine_oxidase "Monoamine oxidase") (MAO)-B and thus increases dopamine levels.
