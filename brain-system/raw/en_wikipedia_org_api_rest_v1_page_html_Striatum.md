The **striatum** (pl.: **striata**) is a cluster of interconnected [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") that make up the largest structure of the subcortical [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia").[[5]](./Striatum#cite_note-Bamford-5) The striatum is a critical component of the [motor](./Motor_system "Motor system") and [reward systems](./Reward_system "Reward system"); it receives [glutamatergic](./Glutamate_(neurotransmitter) "Glutamate (neurotransmitter)") and [dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") inputs from different sources; and serves as the primary input to the rest of the basal ganglia. Functionally, the striatum coordinates multiple aspects of [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition"), including both [motor and action planning](./Planning#Neurology "Planning"), [decision-making](./Decision-making "Decision-making"), [motivation](./Motivation "Motivation"), [reinforcement](./Reinforcement "Reinforcement"), and [reward](./Reward_system "Reward system") perception.[[2]](./Striatum#cite_note-YAGER2015-2)[[3]](./Striatum#cite_note-TAYLOR2013-3)[[4]](./Striatum#cite_note-FERRE2010-4) The striatum is made up of the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus"), the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen"), and the ventral striatum.[[6]](./Striatum#cite_note-6)

In [primates](./Primate "Primate"), the striatum is divided into the ventral striatum and the dorsal striatum, subdivisions that are based upon function and connections. The [ventral](./Anatomical_terms_of_location#Dorsal_and_ventral "Anatomical terms of location") striatum consists of the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and the [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle"). The [dorsal](./Anatomical_terms_of_location#Dorsal_and_ventral "Anatomical terms of location") striatum consists of the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen"). A [white matter](./White_matter "White matter") [nerve tract](./Nerve_tract "Nerve tract") (the [internal capsule](./Internal_capsule "Internal capsule")) in the dorsal striatum separates the caudate nucleus and the putamen.[[4]](./Striatum#cite_note-FERRE2010-4) Anatomically, the term *striatum* describes its striped (striated) appearance of grey-and-white matter.[[7]](./Striatum#cite_note-7)

## Nomenclature

The terms **striatum** and **neostriatum** refer to the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen").[[8]](./Striatum#cite_note-8) Two more terms have seen historic use, but are now antiquated: the term **corpus striatum** refers to both the neostriatum and the [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus"),[[9]](./Striatum#cite_note-9) while the term [**lentiform nucleus**](./Lentiform_nucleus "Lentiform nucleus") refers to the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen") and [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus"), excluding the striatum.[[10]](./Striatum#cite_note-Telford-10)

## Structure

The striatum is the largest structure of the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia"). The striatum is divided into two subdivisions, a ventral striatum and a dorsal striatum, based upon function and connections. It is also divisible into a matrix and embedded striosomes.

### Ventral striatum

The ventral striatum is composed of the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and the [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle").[[4]](./Striatum#cite_note-FERRE2010-4)[[11]](./Striatum#cite_note-pmid18047654-11) The nucleus accumbens is made up of the [nucleus accumbens core](./Nucleus_accumbens#Core "Nucleus accumbens") and the [nucleus accumbens shell](./Nucleus_accumbens#Shell "Nucleus accumbens"), which differ by neural populations. The olfactory tubercle receives input from the [olfactory bulb](./Olfactory_bulb "Olfactory bulb") but has not been shown to play a role in [processing smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction").[[11]](./Striatum#cite_note-pmid18047654-11) In non-primate species, the [islands of Calleja](./Islands_of_Calleja "Islands of Calleja") are included.[[12]](./Striatum#cite_note-:2-12) The ventral striatum is associated with the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system") and has been implicated as a vital part of the [circuitry](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit") for decision making and reward-related behavior.[[13]](./Striatum#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Striatum#cite_note-14)

### Dorsal striatum

The dorsal striatum is composed of the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen").
Primarily it mediates cognition and involves motor and executive function. The dorsal striatum can be further subdivided into the **dorsomedial striatum**, and the **dorsolateral striatum**. Both of these areas have different roles in the acquisition of learnt behaviour and skill formation.[[15]](./Striatum#cite_note-JoN2022-15) The dorsomedial region receives projections from the frontal and the parietal cortices. The dorsolateral region receives projections from the sensorimotor cortex.[[16]](./Striatum#cite_note-Macpherson2019-16)

#### Matrix and striosomes

[Neurochemistry](./Neurochemistry "Neurochemistry") studies have used [staining techniques](./Staining "Staining") on the striatum that have identified two distinct striatal compartments, the matrix, and the [striosome](./Striosome "Striosome") (or patch). The matrix is seen to be rich in [acetylcholinesterase](./Acetylcholinesterase "Acetylcholinesterase"), while the embedded striosomes are acetylcholinesterase-poor.[[17]](./Striatum#cite_note-Brimblecombe-17) The matrix forms the bulk of the striatum, and receives input from most areas of the cerebral cortex.[[18]](./Striatum#cite_note-Squire-18) Clusters of neurons in the matrix, called matrisomes receive a similar input. Their output goes to both regions of the globus pallidus and to the substantia nigra pars reticulata.[[18]](./Striatum#cite_note-Squire-18)

The striosomes receive input from the prefrontal cortex and give outputs to the substantia nigra pars compacta.[[18]](./Striatum#cite_note-Squire-18) There are more striosomes present in the dorsal striatum making up 10-15% of the striatal volume, than in the ventral striatum.[[17]](./Striatum#cite_note-Brimblecombe-17)

### Cell types

[Dendritic spines](./Dendritic_spine "Dendritic spine") on [medium spiny neuron](./Medium_spiny_neuron "Medium spiny neuron") of striatum

Types of cells in the striatum include:

- [Medium spiny neurons](./Medium_spiny_neurons "Medium spiny neurons") (MSNs), which are the principal neurons of the striatum.[[2]](./Striatum#cite_note-YAGER2015-2) They are [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") and, thus, are classified as inhibitory neurons. Medium spiny projection neurons comprise 95% of the total neuronal population of the human striatum.[[2]](./Striatum#cite_note-YAGER2015-2) Medium spiny neurons have two [characteristic types](./Phenotype "Phenotype"): [D1-type](./D1-type "D1-type") MSNs and [D2-type](./D2-type "D2-type") MSNs.[[2]](./Striatum#cite_note-YAGER2015-2)[[4]](./Striatum#cite_note-FERRE2010-4)[[19]](./Striatum#cite_note-MSN_40%_mixed-type_with_DRD1_and_DRD2-19) A subpopulation of MSNs contain both D1-type and D2-type receptors, with approximately 40% of striatal MSNs expressing both [DRD1](./DRD1 "DRD1") and [DRD2](./DRD2 "DRD2") [mRNA](./Messenger_RNA "Messenger RNA").[[2]](./Striatum#cite_note-YAGER2015-2)[[4]](./Striatum#cite_note-FERRE2010-4)[[19]](./Striatum#cite_note-MSN_40%_mixed-type_with_DRD1_and_DRD2-19)
- [Cholinergic](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine") [interneurons](./Interneurons "Interneurons") release acetylcholine, which has a variety of important effects in the striatum. In humans, other primates, and rodents, these interneurons respond to salient environmental stimuli with stereotyped responses that are temporally aligned with the responses of dopaminergic neurons of the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra").[[20]](./Striatum#cite_note-20)[[21]](./Striatum#cite_note-21) The large aspiny cholinergic interneurons themselves are affected by dopamine through [D5 dopamine receptors](./Dopamine_receptor_D5 "Dopamine receptor D5").[[22]](./Striatum#cite_note-22) Dopamine also directly controls communication between cholinergic interneurons.[[23]](./Striatum#cite_note-23)[[24]](./Striatum#cite_note-24)
- There are many types of GABAergic interneurons.[[25]](./Striatum#cite_note-Ibáñez-Sandoval_O._Front_Neuroanat_2010-25) The best known are [parvalbumin](./Parvalbumin "Parvalbumin") expressing interneurons, also known as [fast-spiking](./Action_potential "Action potential") interneurons, which participate in powerful [feedforward](./Feed_forward_(control) "Feed forward (control)") inhibition of principal neurons.[[26]](./Striatum#cite_note-26) Also, there are GABAergic interneurons that express [tyrosine hydroxylase](./Tyrosine_hydroxylase "Tyrosine hydroxylase"),[[27]](./Striatum#cite_note-27) [somatostatin](./Somatostatin "Somatostatin"), [nitric oxide synthase](./Nitric_oxide_synthase "Nitric oxide synthase") and [neuropeptide-y](./Neuropeptide_Y "Neuropeptide Y"). Recently, two types of neuropeptide-y expressing GABAergic interneurons have been described in detail,[[28]](./Striatum#cite_note-28) one of which translates synchronous activity of cholinergic interneurons into inhibition of principal neurons.[[29]](./Striatum#cite_note-29) These [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") of the striatum are not distributed evenly.[[25]](./Striatum#cite_note-Ibáñez-Sandoval_O._Front_Neuroanat_2010-25)
