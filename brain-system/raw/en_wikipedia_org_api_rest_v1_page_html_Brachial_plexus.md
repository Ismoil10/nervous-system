The **brachial plexus** is a network of nerves ([nerve plexus](./Nerve_plexus "Nerve plexus")) formed by the [anterior rami](./Anterior_rami "Anterior rami") of the lower four [cervical nerves](./Spinal_nerve#Cervical_nerves "Spinal nerve") and the first [thoracic nerve](./Spinal_nerve#Thoracic_nerves "Spinal nerve") ([C5](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_5 "Cervical spinal nerve 5"), [C6](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_6 "Cervical spinal nerve 6"), [C7](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_7 "Cervical spinal nerve 7"), [C8](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_8 "Cervical spinal nerve 8"), and [T1](./Thoracic_spinal_nerve_1 "Thoracic spinal nerve 1")). This plexus extends from the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), through the [cervicoaxillary canal](./Cervicoaxillary_canal "Cervicoaxillary canal") in the neck, over the first rib, and into the [armpit](./Axilla "Axilla"). It supplies [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") and [efferent nerve fibers](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") to the [chest](./Chest "Chest"), [shoulder](./Shoulder "Shoulder"), [arm](./Arm "Arm"), [forearm](./Forearm "Forearm"), and [hand](./Hand "Hand").

## Structure

The brachial plexus is divided into five *roots*, three *trunks*, six *divisions* (three anterior and three posterior), three *cords*, and five *branches*. There are five "terminal" branches and numerous other "pre-terminal" or "collateral" branches, such as the [subscapular nerve](./Subscapular_nerves "Subscapular nerves"), the [thoracodorsal nerve](./Thoracodorsal_nerve "Thoracodorsal nerve"), and the [long thoracic nerve](./Long_thoracic_nerve "Long thoracic nerve")(also known as the nerve of Bell[[1]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-1)) ,[[2]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-Brachial_Plexus_Palsy-2) that leave the plexus at various points along its length.[[3]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-Anatomy_and_Physiology-3) A common structure used to identify part of the brachial plexus in cadaver dissections is the M or W shape made by the [musculocutaneous nerve](./Musculocutaneous_nerve "Musculocutaneous nerve"), [lateral cord](./Lateral_cord "Lateral cord"), [median nerve](./Median_nerve "Median nerve"), [medial cord](./Medial_cord "Medial cord"), and [ulnar nerve](./Ulnar_nerve "Ulnar nerve").
There is a mnemonic to easily remember the structure: **R**ich (Root) **T**ourists (Trunk) **D**rink (Division) **C**old (Cord) **B**eer (Branch).

### Roots

The five **roots** are the five [anterior primary rami](./Anterior_ramus_of_spinal_nerve "Anterior ramus of spinal nerve") of the [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"), after they have given off their segmental supply to the muscles of the [neck](./Neck "Neck").
The brachial plexus emerges at five different levels: C5, C6, C7, C8, and T1. C5 and C6 merge to establish the [upper trunk](./Upper_trunk_of_the_brachial_plexus "Upper trunk of the brachial plexus"), C7 continuously forms the middle trunk, and C8 and T1 merge to establish the lower trunk.
Prefixed or postfixed formations in some cases involve C4 or T2, respectively.
The [dorsal scapular nerve](./Dorsal_scapular_nerve "Dorsal scapular nerve") comes from the superior trunk[[3]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-Anatomy_and_Physiology-3) and innervates the [rhomboid muscles](./Rhomboid_muscles "Rhomboid muscles") which retract and downwardly rotate the [scapula](./Scapula "Scapula"). The [subclavian nerve](./Subclavian_nerve "Subclavian nerve") originates in both C5 and C6 and innervates the [subclavius](./Subclavius_muscle "Subclavius muscle"), a muscle that involves lifting the first ribs during respiration. The long thoracic nerve arises from C5, C6, and C7. This nerve innervates the [serratus anterior](./Serratus_anterior_muscle "Serratus anterior muscle"), which draws the scapula laterally and is the prime mover in all forward-reaching and pushing actions.

### Trunks

These roots merge to form the **trunks**:

- "[superior](./Upper_trunk "Upper trunk")" or "upper" ([C5](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_5 "Cervical spinal nerve 5")–[C6](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_6 "Cervical spinal nerve 6"))
- "middle" ([C7](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_7 "Cervical spinal nerve 7"))
- "inferior" or "lower" ([C8](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_8 "Cervical spinal nerve 8")–[T1](./Thoracic_spinal_nerve_1 "Thoracic spinal nerve 1"))

### Divisions

Each trunk then splits in two, to form six **divisions**:

- anterior divisions of the upper, middle, and lower trunks
- posterior divisions of the upper, middle, and lower trunks
- when observing the body in the anatomical position, the anterior divisions are superficial to the posterior divisions

### Cords

These six divisions regroup to become the three **cords** or large fiber bundles. The cords are named by their position with respect to the [axillary artery](./Axillary_artery "Axillary artery").

- The *[posterior cord](./Posterior_cord "Posterior cord")* is formed from the three posterior divisions of the trunks (C5–C8, T1)
- The *[lateral cord](./Lateral_cord "Lateral cord")* is formed from the anterior divisions of the upper and middle trunks (C5–C7)
- The *[medial cord](./Medial_cord "Medial cord")* is simply a continuation of the anterior division of the lower trunk (C8, T1)

### Branches

The **branches** are listed below. Most branches arise from the cords, but a few branches (indicated in italics) arise directly from earlier structures. The five on the left are considered "terminal branches". These terminal branches are the [musculocutaneous nerve](./Musculocutaneous_nerve "Musculocutaneous nerve"), the [axillary nerve](./Axillary_nerve "Axillary nerve"), the [radial nerve](./Radial_nerve "Radial nerve"), the [median nerve](./Median_nerve "Median nerve"), and the [ulnar nerve](./Ulnar_nerve "Ulnar nerve"). Due to both emerging from the lateral cord the [musculocutaneous nerve](./Musculocutaneous_nerve "Musculocutaneous nerve") and the [median nerve](./Median_nerve "Median nerve") are well connected. The [musculocutaneous nerve](./Musculocutaneous_nerve "Musculocutaneous nerve") has even been shown to send a branch to the [median nerve](./Median_nerve "Median nerve") further connecting them.[[2]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-Brachial_Plexus_Palsy-2) There have been several variations reported in the branching pattern but these are very rare.[[4]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Brachial_plexus#cite_note-5)
