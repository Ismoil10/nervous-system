Homeostatic mechanism in the body



Flowchart showing baroreceptor reflex

The **baroreflex** or **baroreceptor reflex** is one of the body's [homeostatic](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") mechanisms that helps to maintain [blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure") at nearly constant levels. The baroreflex provides a rapid [negative feedback loop](./Negative_feedback_loop "Negative feedback loop") in which an elevated blood pressure causes the [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate") to decrease. Decreased blood pressure decreases baroreflex activation and causes heart rate to increase and to restore blood pressure levels. Their function is to sense pressure changes by responding to change in the tension of the arterial wall.[[1]](./Baroreflex#cite_note-Bär_p.-1) The baroreflex can begin to act in less than the duration of a cardiac cycle (fractions of a second) and thus baroreflex adjustments are key factors in dealing with [postural hypotension](./Orthostatic_hypotension "Orthostatic hypotension"), the tendency for blood pressure to decrease on standing due to gravity.

The system relies on specialized [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron"), known as [baroreceptors](./Baroreceptor "Baroreceptor"), chiefly in the [aortic arch](./Aortic_arch "Aortic arch") and [carotid sinuses](./Carotid_sinus "Carotid sinus"), to monitor changes in blood pressure and relay them to the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"). Baroreceptors are [stretch receptors](./Stretch_receptor "Stretch receptor") and respond to the pressure induced stretching of the blood vessel in which they are found. Baroreflex-induced changes in blood pressure are mediated by both branches of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"): the [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") and [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") nerves. Baroreceptors are active even at normal blood pressures so their activity informs the brain about both increases and decreases in blood pressure.

The body contains two other, slower-acting systems to regulate blood pressure: the heart releases [atrial natriuretic peptide](./Atrial_natriuretic_peptide "Atrial natriuretic peptide") when blood pressure is too high, and the kidneys sense and correct low blood pressure with the [renin–angiotensin system](./Renin–angiotensin_system "Renin–angiotensin system").[[2]](./Baroreflex#cite_note-Fu_Ping_Wang_Luo_p.-2)

## Anatomy

Baroreceptors are present in the [atria](./Atrium_(heart) "Atrium (heart)") of the [heart](./Heart "Heart") and [vena cavae](./Vena_cavae "Vena cavae"), but the most sensitive baroreceptors are in the [carotid sinuses](./Carotid_sinus "Carotid sinus") and [aortic arch](./Aortic_arch "Aortic arch"). While the carotid sinus [baroreceptor](./Baroreceptor "Baroreceptor") axons travel within the [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve") (CN IX), the aortic arch baroreceptor axons travel within the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") (CN X). Baroreceptor activity travels along these nerves directly into the central nervous system to excite [glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic") neurons within the [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus") (SN) in the brainstem.[[3]](./Baroreflex#cite_note-3) Baroreceptor information flows from these NSS neurons to both parasympathetic and sympathetic neurons within the brainstem.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The SN neurons send excitatory fibers ([glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic")) to the [caudal ventrolateral medulla](./Caudal_ventrolateral_medulla "Caudal ventrolateral medulla") (CVLM), activating the CVLM. The activated CVLM then sends inhibitory fibers ([GABAergic](./GABA "GABA")) to the [rostral ventrolateral medulla](./Rostral_ventrolateral_medulla "Rostral ventrolateral medulla") (RVLM), thus inhibiting the RVLM. The RVLM is the primary regulator of the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), sending excitatory fibers ([glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic")) to the [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") preganglionic neurons located in the [intermediolateral nucleus](./Intermediolateral_nucleus "Intermediolateral nucleus") of the spinal cord. Hence, when the baroreceptors are activated (by an increased blood pressure), the NTS activates the CVLM, which in turn inhibits the RVLM, thus decreasing the activity of the [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") branch of the autonomic nervous system, leading to a relative decrease in blood pressure. Likewise, low blood pressure activates baroreceptors less and causes an increase in [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") tone via "disinhibition" (less inhibition, hence activation) of the RVLM. Cardiovascular targets of the sympathetic nervous system includes both blood vessels and the heart.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Even at resting levels of blood pressure, arterial baroreceptor discharge activates SN neurons. Some of these SN neurons are tonically activated by this resting blood pressure and thus activate excitatory fibers to the [nucleus ambiguus](./Nucleus_ambiguus "Nucleus ambiguus") and [dorsal nucleus of vagus nerve](./Dorsal_nucleus_of_vagus_nerve "Dorsal nucleus of vagus nerve") to regulate the [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system"). These parasympathetic neurons send axons to the heart and parasympathetic activity slows cardiac pacemaking and thus [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"). This parasympathetic activity is further increased during conditions of elevated blood pressure. The parasympathetic nervous system is primarily directed toward the heart.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Activation

The [baroreceptors](./Baroreceptors "Baroreceptors") are [stretch](./Stretching "Stretching")-sensitive [mechanoreceptors](./Mechanoreceptor "Mechanoreceptor"). At low pressures, baroreceptors become inactive. When blood pressure rises, the carotid and aortic sinuses are distended further, resulting in increased stretch and, therefore, a greater degree of activation of the baroreceptors. At normal resting blood pressures, many baroreceptors are actively reporting blood pressure information and the baroreflex is actively modulating autonomic activity. Active baroreceptors fire [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential") ("spikes") more frequently. The greater the stretch the more rapidly baroreceptors fire action potentials. Many individual baroreceptors are inactive at normal resting pressures and only become activated when their stretch or pressure threshold is exceeded.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Baroreceptor mechanosensitivity is hypothesised to be linked to the expression of [PIEZO1](./PIEZO1 "PIEZO1") and [PIEZO2](./PIEZO2 "PIEZO2") on neurons in the [petrosal](./Inferior_ganglion_of_glossopharyngeal_nerve "Inferior ganglion of glossopharyngeal nerve") and [nodose ganglia](./Inferior_ganglion_of_vagus_nerve "Inferior ganglion of vagus nerve").

Baroreceptor action potentials are relayed to the [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus"), which uses frequency as a measure of blood pressure. Increased activation of the solitary nucleus inhibits the [vasomotor center](./Vasomotor_center "Vasomotor center") and stimulates the [vagal](./Vagal "Vagal") nuclei. The end-result of baroreceptor activation is inhibition of the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and activation of the [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system").[[4]](./Baroreflex#cite_note-4)

The [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic "Parasympathetic") branches of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") have opposing effects on blood pressure. Sympathetic activation leads to an elevation of [total peripheral resistance](./Total_peripheral_resistance "Total peripheral resistance") and [cardiac output](./Cardiac_output "Cardiac output") via increased [contractility](./Myocardial_contractility "Myocardial contractility") of the heart, [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), and arterial [vasoconstriction](./Vasoconstriction "Vasoconstriction"), which tends to increase blood pressure. Conversely, [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic "Parasympathetic") activation leads to decreased [cardiac output](./Cardiac_output "Cardiac output") via decrease in [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), resulting in a tendency to lower blood pressure.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

By coupling [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") inhibition and [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic "Parasympathetic") activation, the baroreflex maximizes blood pressure reduction. [Sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") inhibition leads to a drop in peripheral resistance, while parasympathetic activation leads to a depressed [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate") ([reflex bradycardia](./Reflex_bradycardia "Reflex bradycardia")) and [contractility](./Myocardial_contractility "Myocardial contractility"). The combined effects will dramatically decrease blood pressure. In a similar manner, [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") activation with [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic "Parasympathetic") inhibition allows the baroreflex to elevate blood pressure.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Set point and tonic activation

Baroreceptor firing has an inhibitory effect on sympathetic outflow. The sympathetic neurons fire at different rates which determines the release of norepinephrine onto cardiovascular targets. Norepinephrine constricts blood vessels to increase blood pressure. When baroreceptors are stretched (due to an increased blood pressure) their firing rate increases which in turn decreases the sympathetic outflow resulting in reduced norepinephrine and thus blood pressure.
