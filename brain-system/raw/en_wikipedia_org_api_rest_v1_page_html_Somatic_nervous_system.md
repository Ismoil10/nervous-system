The **somatic nervous system** (**SNS**), also known as **voluntary nervous system**, is a part of the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS) that links [brain](./Brain "Brain") and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") to [skeletal muscles](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") under conscious control, as well as to [sensory receptors](./Sensory_receptor "Sensory receptor") in the skin.[[1]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-2) The other part complementary to the somatic nervous system is the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") (ANS).[[3]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Barboi_Macefield_2025-3)

The somatic nervous system consists of [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve") carrying [afferent nerve fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber"), which relay sensation from the body to the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS), and nerves carrying [efferent nerve fibers](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber"), which relay motor commands from the CNS to stimulate [muscle contraction](./Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction").[[4]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Akinrodoye_2022-4) Specialized nerve fiber ends called [sensory receptors](./Sensory_receptors "Sensory receptors") are responsible for detecting information both inside and outside the body.[[3]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Barboi_Macefield_2025-3)

The *a-* of *afferent* and the *e-* of *efferent* correspond to the [prefixes](./Prefix "Prefix") *ad-* (to, toward) and *ex-* (out of).

## Structure

There are 43 segments of nerves in the human body.[[5]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Rea_2014-5) With each segment, there is a pair of sensory and motor nerves. 31 segments of nerves are in the spinal cord and 12 are in the brain stem.[[5]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Rea_2014-5) [Interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron"), also known as association neurons, are present throughout the central nervous system forming links between the sensory and motor fibres.[[6]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-SEER2024-6)
Thus the somatic nervous system consists of two parts:

- [Spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"): They are [mixed nerves](./Mixed_nerve "Mixed nerve") that carry sensory information into and motor commands out of the spinal cord.[[7]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Felten_2016-7) The spinal nerves serve as a bridge between the environment and the central nervous system (CNS). These neurons work together to transfer autonomic, sensory, and motor impulses from the spinal cord to the body's other systems. The spinal nerves are arranged into 31 pairs according to the regions of the spinal cord. To be more precise, there are eight pairs of [cervical nerves](./Cervical_nerves "Cervical nerves") (C1–C8), twelve pairs of [thoracic nerves](./Thoracic_nerves "Thoracic nerves") (T1–T12), five pairs of [lumbar nerves](./Lumbar_nerves "Lumbar nerves") (L1–L5), five pairs of [sacral nerves](./Sacral_nerves "Sacral nerves") (S1–S5), and one pair of [coccygeal nerves](./Coccygeal_nerves "Coccygeal nerves"). Peripheral nerves are included in the category of peripheral nervous system.[[8]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-8)
- [Cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves"): They are the nerve fibers that carry information into and out of the brain stem.[[5]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Rea_2014-5) They include smell, eye muscles, mouth, taste, ear, neck, shoulders, and tongue.[[7]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Felten_2016-7) Partially innervating the head and neck structures are the cranial nerves, which supply afferent and efferent functions. Neural processes connected to certain brainstem nuclei and cortical regions make up cranial nerves, in contrast to [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerves "Spinal nerves"), which have neural fibers originating from the spinal grey matter as their roots.[[9]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-9) The cranial nerves VIII (vestibulocochlear), II (optic), and I (olfactory) are regarded as entirely afferent. The exclusively efferent cranial nerves are XI (spinal accessory), XII (hypoglossal), VI (abducens), IV (trochlear), and III (oculomotor). The remaining cranial nerves, X (vagus), IX (glossopharyngeal), VII (facial), and V (trigeminal), have mixed sensory and motor functions.[[10]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-10) Anatomically, cranial nerves are numbered from I to XII, which indicates their sequential origin from the caudal to the ventral brainstem. Alternatively, they can be postulated in groups based on the developmental functions they perform (sensory, motor, mixed).[[3]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Barboi_Macefield_2025-3)

## Function

The somatic nervous system's principal function is to facilitate the organs and striated muscles of the central nervous system so that we can carry out our daily responsibilities.[[11]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-SCI.1233-24.2024-11)

The [primary motor cortex](./Primary_motor_cortex "Primary motor cortex"), or [precentral gyrus](./Precentral_gyrus "Precentral gyrus"), is home to the higher motor neurons that make up the basic motor pathway.[[12]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-12) These neurons transmit signals to the lower motor neurons in the spinal cord through axons known as the corticospinal tract. These impulses move to the [neuromuscular junction](./Neuromuscular_junction "Neuromuscular junction") (NMJ) of skeletal muscle via peripheral axons after synapsing with the [lower motor neurons](./Lower_motor_neurons "Lower motor neurons") through the ventral horn of the spinal cord. A signal that travels to the NMJ, which innervates muscles, is produced by the release of acetylcholine by upper motor neurons. [Acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine") binds to [nicotinic acetylcholine receptors](./Nicotinic_acetylcholine_receptors "Nicotinic acetylcholine receptors") of alpha-motor neurons.[[4]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Akinrodoye_2022-4)

The somatic nervous system controls all [voluntary muscular](./Voluntary_muscle "Voluntary muscle") systems within the body, and the process of voluntary [reflex arcs](./Reflex_arc "Reflex arc").[[13]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-Openstax_Anatomy_&_Physiology_attribution-13)

The basic route of [nerve signals](./Nerve_signal "Nerve signal") within the [efferent](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") somatic nervous system involves a sequence that begins in the upper [cell bodies](./Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") of [motor neurons](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron") ([upper motor neurons](./Upper_motor_neuron "Upper motor neuron")) within the [precentral gyrus](./Brodmann_area_4 "Brodmann area 4") (which approximates the [primary motor cortex](./Primary_motor_cortex "Primary motor cortex")).[[14]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-14) Stimuli from the precentral gyrus are transmitted from upper motor neurons, down the [corticospinal tract](./Corticospinal_tract "Corticospinal tract"), to [lower motor neurons](./Lower_motor_neuron "Lower motor neuron") ([alpha motor neurons](./Alpha_motor_neurons "Alpha motor neurons")) in the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") and [ventral horn](./Anterior_horn_of_spinal_cord "Anterior horn of spinal cord") of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"): upper motor neurons release a [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") called [glutamate](./Glutamate "Glutamate") from their axon terminal knobs, which is received by [glutamate receptors](./Glutamate_receptor "Glutamate receptor") on the lower motor neurons: from there, acetylcholine is released from the axon terminal knobs of alpha motor neurons and received by postsynaptic [receptors](./Receptor_(biochemistry) "Receptor (biochemistry)") ([nicotinic acetylcholine receptors](./Nicotinic_acetylcholine_receptor "Nicotinic acetylcholine receptor")) of muscles, thereby relaying the stimulus to contract muscle fibers.[[15]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-15)

### Reflex arcs

A [reflex arc](./Reflex_arc "Reflex arc") is a [neural circuit](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit") that creates a more or less automatic link between a sensory input and a specific motor output. Reflex circuits vary in complexity—the simplest spinal reflexes are mediated by a two-element chain, of which in the human body there is only one, also called a monosynaptic reflex (there is only one synapse between the two neurones taking part in the arc: sensory and motor). The singular example of a monosynaptic reflex is the [patellar reflex](./Patellar_reflex "Patellar reflex"). The next simplest reflex arc is a three-element chain, beginning with sensory neurons, which activate [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron") inside of the spinal cord, which then activate motor neurons. Some reflex responses, such as withdrawing the hand after touching a hot surface, are protective, but others, such as the patellar reflex ("knee jerk") activated by tapping the patellar tendon, contribute to ordinary behavior.[[16]](./Somatic_nervous_system#cite_note-16)
