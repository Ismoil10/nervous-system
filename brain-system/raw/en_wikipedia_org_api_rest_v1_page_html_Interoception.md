# Interoception

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/html/Interoception

---

Interoception

Sensory system that receives and integrates information from the body



Interoception is involved in many different physiological systems like the cardiorespiratory system, gastrointestinal system, nociceptive system, endocrine and immune systems.



The sensory system that allows organisms to sense internal stimuli. This includes sensing: hunger, thirst, lung expansion/contraction and many other internal stimuli.

**Interoception** is the collection of [senses](./Sense#Other_internal_sensations_and_perceptions "Sense") providing information to the organism about the internal state of the body.[[1]](./Interoception#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Interoception#cite_note-:3-2) This can be both [conscious](./Consciousness "Consciousness") and [subconscious](./Subconscious "Subconscious"). It encompasses the brain's process of integrating signals relayed from the body into specific subregions—like the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem"), [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), [insula](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex"), [somatosensory](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex "Primary somatosensory cortex"), and [anterior cingulate cortex](./Anterior_cingulate_cortex "Anterior cingulate cortex")—allowing for a complex and highly accurate representation of the physiological state of the body.[[3]](./Interoception#cite_note-:1-3)[[4]](./Interoception#cite_note-:4-4) This is important for maintaining [homeostatic](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") conditions[[5]](./Interoception#cite_note-:12-5) in the body and, potentially, facilitating [self-awareness](./Self-awareness "Self-awareness").[[6]](./Interoception#cite_note-:10-6)[[7]](./Interoception#cite_note-7)

Interoceptive signals are projected to the brain via a diversity of [neural pathways](./Neural_pathway "Neural pathway"), in particular from the lamina I of the spinal cord along the spinothalamic pathway and through the projections of the [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus"),[[8]](./Interoception#cite_note-8) that allow for the [sensory processing](./Sensory_processing "Sensory processing") and prediction of internal bodily states. Misrepresentations of internal states, or a disconnect between the body's signals and the brain's interpretation and prediction of those signals, have been suggested to underlie conditions such as [anxiety](./Anxiety_disorder "Anxiety disorder"),[[9]](./Interoception#cite_note-9) depression, [panic disorder](./Panic_disorder "Panic disorder"), [anorexia nervosa](./Anorexia_nervosa "Anorexia nervosa"),[[10]](./Interoception#cite_note-Ambrosecchia_Ardizzi_Russo_Ditaranto_p.-10) [bulimia nervosa](./Bulimia_nervosa "Bulimia nervosa"),[[11]](./Interoception#cite_note-Klabunde_Acheson_Boutelle_Matthews_2013_pp._488–492-11) [posttraumatic stress disorder](./Posttraumatic_stress_disorder "Posttraumatic stress disorder") (PTSD), [obsessive compulsive disorder](./Obsessive–compulsive_disorder "Obsessive–compulsive disorder") (OCD), [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](./Attention_Deficit_Hyperactivity_Disorder "Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder") (ADHD), [alexithymia](./Alexithymia "Alexithymia"),[[12]](./Interoception#cite_note-Shah_Hall_Catmur_Bird_2016_pp._215–220-12) [somatic symptom disorder](./Somatic_symptom_disorder "Somatic symptom disorder"), and [illness anxiety disorder](./Illness_anxiety_disorder "Illness anxiety disorder").[[13]](./Interoception#cite_note-Grossi_Longarzo_Quarantelli_Salvatore_2017_pp._22–32-13)

The contemporary definition of interoception is not synonymous with the term "visceroception".[[14]](./Interoception#cite_note-:2-14) Visceroception refers to the perception of bodily signals arising specifically from the [viscera](./Organ_(anatomy) "Organ (anatomy)"): the heart, lungs, stomach, and bladder, along with other internal organs in the trunk of the body.[[15]](./Interoception#cite_note-15) This does not include organs like the brain and skin. Interoception encompasses visceral signaling, but more broadly relates to all physiological tissues that relay a signal to the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") about the current state of the body.[[16]](./Interoception#cite_note-:5-16) Interoceptive signals are transmitted to the brain via multiple pathways including the [lamina I spinothalamic pathway](./Spinothalamic_tract "Spinothalamic tract"), the classical [viscerosensory pathway](./General_visceral_afferent_fibers "General visceral afferent fibers"), the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") and [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve"), [chemosensory pathways](./Chemoreceptor "Chemoreceptor") in the blood, and [somatosensory pathways](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system") from the skin.

Interoceptive signals arise from many different physiological systems of the body. The most commonly studied system is [cardiovascular](./Cardiovascular "Cardiovascular") interoception which is typically measured by directing attention towards the sensation of the heartbeat during various tasks.[[17]](./Interoception#cite_note-:7-17)[[18]](./Interoception#cite_note-:6-18)[[19]](./Interoception#cite_note-19) Other physiological systems integral to interoceptive processing include the [respiratory system](./Respiratory_system "Respiratory system"), [gastrointestinal](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract") and [genitourinary systems](./Genitourinary_system "Genitourinary system"), [nociceptive](./Nociception "Nociception") system, [thermoregulatory](./Thermoregulation "Thermoregulation") system, [endocrine](./Endocrine_system "Endocrine system") and [immune systems](./Immune_system "Immune system"). [Soft cutaneous touch](./Somatosensory_system#Neural_processing_of_social_touch "Somatosensory system") is another sensory signal often included within the interoceptive processing system.

## History and etymology

### Early to mid-1900s

The concept of interoception was introduced in 1906 by the Nobel Laureate [Sir Charles S. Sherrington](./Charles_Scott_Sherrington "Charles Scott Sherrington"). He did not use the noun *interoception*, but did describe as *interoceptive*[[14]](./Interoception#cite_note-:2-14) those receptors that are within the viscera—what are today called "visceroceptive"—and thus excluded all other receptors and information from the body, which he grouped as either *exteroceptive* or *[proprioceptive](./Proprioception "Proprioception")*. In Sherrington's model, exteroceptive receptors were those that received information from outward stimuli, like light, touch, sound, and odor. He classified [temperature](./Thermoception "Thermoception") and [nociception](./Nociception "Nociception") as exteroceptive sensations as well, though these are now regarded as having interoceptive qualities.[[3]](./Interoception#cite_note-:1-3)[[16]](./Interoception#cite_note-:5-16) He further divided the internal milieu of the body by its [somatic](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") and [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") functions. And proprioceptors were those found in [skeletal tissue](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") that control voluntary movement. For him, [interoceptors](./Interoceptor "Interoceptor") (a term which has lost prevalence in modern literature) were thus confined to visceral involuntary [smooth muscle](./Smooth_muscle_tissue "Smooth muscle tissue") (e.g. surrounding blood vessels).[[20]](./Interoception#cite_note-20)

Further work on interoceptive processing after Sherrington was delayed for many years owing to the influential claim by [John Newport Langley](./John_Newport_Langley "John Newport Langley") that the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") used only [efferent](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") (brain-to-body) signaling to implement its functions.[[21]](./Interoception#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Interoception#cite_note-:0-22)
By the 1950s and 1960s, many investigations of interoceptive processing had been conducted, and once it had become apparent that interoceptive receptors are present in many tissues of the body other researchers began to investigate afferent body-to-brain signals, mainly by conducting animal experiments to see if interoceptive conditioning was possible. Using principles of [Pavlovian conditioning](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning"), different physiological systems in dogs were perturbed to elicit a conditioned response to food.[[22]](./Interoception#cite_note-:0-22)

For example, in one experiment, [dogs](./Dogs "Dogs")' pelvises were distended using infusions of solution when food was presented to them. After rounds of pairing the two, salivation occurred without presenting food once the [pelvis](./Pelvis "Pelvis") was distended.[[22]](./Interoception#cite_note-:0-22) Interoceptive conditioning studies like this illustrated that interoceptive sensations may be important for learned behavior and emotion.[[22]](./Interoception#cite_note-:0-22)

### Mid-1900s to 2000

This graph shows the number of articles that reference the term "interoception" specifically from 1905 to 2015. A clear increase in the number of publications per year on the topic is seen in the 21st century.

The increased interest in interoception during the late 1950s and the 1960s, as evidenced by the number of papers published, has been referred to as the "[biofeedback](./Biofeedback "Biofeedback") blip". This was a phase during which many researchers examined humans' ability to gain control over [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") functions as a method of treatment for various conditions.[[23]](./Interoception#cite_note-23)

Interoception did not gain widespread popularity within the scientific community until the mid- to late-twentieth century. During that period, some researchers chose to use the terms *visceroceptor* and *interoceptor* interchangeably, in line with Sherrington's usage,[[22]](./Interoception#cite_note-:0-22) others combined proprioceptive and visceroceptive information into one category—interoception—based on physiological data about the lack of differences in [nerve impulses](./Nerve_impulses "Nerve impulses"),[[24]](./Interoception#cite_note-24)[[25]](./Interoception#cite_note-25) and still others proposed that interoception includes more than just [endogenous](./Endogeny_(biology) "Endogeny (biology)") (internal) stimuli.[[14]](./Interoception#cite_note-:2-14) Exactly which sensory signals could or should be classified as interoceptive remains the subject of ongoing debate.

During the 1980s, [psychophysiologists](./Psychophysiology "Psychophysiology") began to extensively examine cardiovascular interoception, introducing several different experimental tasks for studying the perception of the heartbeat: heartbeat counting,[[17]](./Interoception#cite_note-:7-17) heartbeat tapping,[[26]](./Interoception#cite_note-:8-26) and heartbeat detection.[[27]](./Interoception#cite_note-27)[[28]](./Interoception#cite_note-28) [Psychiatrists](./Psychiatrist "Psychiatrist") also began to look at the effects of pharmacological stimulation on the symptoms of [panic disorder](./Panic_disorder "Panic disorder").[[29]](./Interoception#cite_note-29) All of this further increased researchers' interest in interoception, including the development of theoretical models of the integration of interoceptive information within the body over time.[[30]](./Interoception#cite_note-30)
