The **insular cortex** (also **insula** and **insular lobe**) is a portion of the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") folded deep within the [lateral sulcus](./Lateral_sulcus "Lateral sulcus") (the fissure separating the [temporal lobe](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe") from the [parietal](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe") and [frontal lobes](./Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe")) within each [hemisphere](./Brain_hemisphere "Brain hemisphere") of the [mammalian](./Mammal "Mammal") [brain](./Brain "Brain").

The insulae are believed to be involved in [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness") and play a role in diverse functions usually linked to [emotion](./Emotion "Emotion"), [interoception](./Interoception "Interoception"), or the regulation of the body's [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis"). These functions include [compassion](./Compassion "Compassion"), [empathy](./Empathy "Empathy"), [taste](./Taste "Taste"), [perception](./Perception "Perception"), [motor control](./Motor_control "Motor control"), [self-awareness](./Self-awareness "Self-awareness"), [cognitive functioning](./Cognitive_function "Cognitive function"), [interpersonal relationships](./Interpersonal_relationship "Interpersonal relationship"), and awareness of [homeostatic emotions](./Homeostatic_emotion "Homeostatic emotion") such as [hunger](./Hunger_(physiology) "Hunger (physiology)"), [pain](./Pain "Pain") and [fatigue](./Fatigue "Fatigue"). In relation to these, it is involved in [psychopathology](./Psychopathology "Psychopathology").

The insular cortex is divided by the central sulcus of the insula, into two parts: the anterior insula and the posterior insula in which more than a dozen field areas have been identified. The cortical area overlying the insula toward the lateral surface of the brain is the [operculum](./Operculum_(brain) "Operculum (brain)") (meaning *lid*). The opercula are formed from parts of the enclosing frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes.

## Structure

The insula is divided into an anterior and a posterior part by the **central sulcus of the insula**.[[1]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-Kortz-1)

### Connections

The anterior part of the insula is shallow [sulci](./Sulcus_(neuroanatomy) "Sulcus (neuroanatomy)") divided into three or four **short gyri**.

The anterior insula receives a direct projection from the basal part of the [ventral medial nucleus](./Ventral_nuclear_group "Ventral nuclear group") of the thalamus and a particularly large input from the [central nucleus of the amygdala](./Central_nucleus_of_the_amygdala "Central nucleus of the amygdala"). In addition, the anterior insula itself projects to the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala").

One study on rhesus monkeys revealed widespread reciprocal connections between the insular cortex and almost all subnuclei of the amygdaloid complex. The posterior insula projects predominantly to the dorsal aspect of the lateral and to the central amygdaloid nuclei. In contrast, the anterior insula projects to the anterior amygdaloid area as well as the medial, the cortical, the accessory basal magnocellular, the medial basal, and the lateral amygdaloid nuclei.[[2]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-2)

The posterior part of the insula is formed by a **long gyrus**.

The posterior insula connects reciprocally with the [secondary somatosensory cortex](./Secondary_somatosensory_cortex "Secondary somatosensory cortex") and receives input from [spinothalamically](./Spinothalamic_tract "Spinothalamic tract") activated [ventral posterior lateral](./Ventral_nuclear_group "Ventral nuclear group") thalamic nuclei. It has also been shown that this region receives inputs from the ventromedial nucleus (posterior part) of the thalamus that are highly specialized to convey homeostatic information such as pain, temperature, itch, local oxygen status, and sensual touch.[[3]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-3)

A human neuroimaging study using [diffusion tensor imaging](./Diffusion_MRI "Diffusion MRI") revealed that the anterior insula is interconnected to regions in the temporal and occipital lobe, opercular and orbitofrontal cortex, triangular and opercular parts of the inferior frontal gyrus. The same study revealed differences in the anatomical connection patterns between the left and right hemisphere.[[4]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-4)

The **circular sulcus of insula** (or **sulcus of Reil**[[5]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-Sobotta's_Atlas_and_Text-book_of_human_anatomy_1909-5)) is a semicircular [sulcus](./Sulcus_(neuroanatomy) "Sulcus (neuroanatomy)") or *fissure*[[5]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-Sobotta's_Atlas_and_Text-book_of_human_anatomy_1909-5) that separates the insula from the neighboring gyri of the [operculum](./Operculum_(brain) "Operculum (brain)")[[6]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-6) in the front, above, and
behind.[[5]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-Sobotta's_Atlas_and_Text-book_of_human_anatomy_1909-5)

### Cytoarchitecture

The insular cortex has regions of variable cell structure or [cytoarchitecture](./Cytoarchitecture "Cytoarchitecture"), changing from [granular](./Granule_cell "Granule cell") in the [posterior](./Posterior_(anatomy) "Posterior (anatomy)") portion to agranular in the [anterior](./Anterior "Anterior") portion. The insula also receives differential [cortical](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") and [thalamic](./Thalamus "Thalamus") input along its length. The anterior insular cortex contains a population of [spindle neurons](./Spindle_neurons "Spindle neurons") (also called *von Economo neurons*), identified as characterising a distinctive subregion as the agranular frontal insula.[[7]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-7)

### Development

The insular cortex is considered a separate [lobe](./Lobes_of_the_brain "Lobes of the brain") of the [telencephalon](./Telencephalon "Telencephalon") by some authorities.[[8]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-8) Other sources see the insula as a part of the [temporal lobe](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe").[[9]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-9) It is also sometimes grouped with limbic structures deep in the brain into a [limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] As a paralimbic cortex, the insular cortex is considered to be a relatively old structure.

## Function

### Multi-modal sensory processing, sensory binding

Functional imaging studies show activation of the insula during audio-visual integration tasks.[[10]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-10)[[11]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-12)

### Taste

The anterior insula is part of the primary [gustatory cortex](./Gustatory_cortex "Gustatory cortex").[[13]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-14) Research in rhesus monkeys has also reported that apart from numerous taste-sensitive neurons, the insular cortex also responds to non-taste properties of oral stimuli related to the texture (viscosity, grittiness) or temperature of food.[[15]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-15)

### Speech

The sensory speech region, Wernicke's area, and the motor speech region, Broca's area, are interconnected by a large axonal fiber system known as the arcuate fasciculus which passes directly beneath the insular cortex. On account of this anatomical architecture, ischemic strokes in the insular region can disrupt the arcuate fasciculus.[[16]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-16) Functional imaging studies on the cerebral correlates of language production also suggest that the anterior insula forms part of the brain network of speech motor control.[[17]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-17) Moreover, electrical stimulation of the posterior insular can evoke speech disturbances such as speech arrest and reduced voice intensity.[[18]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-18)

Lesion of the pre-central gyrus of the insula can also cause "pure speech apraxia" (i.e. the inability to speak with no apparent aphasic or orofacial motor impairments).[[19]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-:1-19) This demonstrates that the insular cortex forms part of a critical circuit for the coordination of complex articulatory movements prior to and during the execution of the motor speech plans.[[19]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-:1-19) Importantly, this specific cortical circuit is different from those that relate to the cognitive aspects of language production (e.g., Broca's area on the inferior frontal gyrus).[[19]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-:1-19) Subvocal, or silent, speech has also been shown to activate right insular cortex, further supporting the theory that the motor control of speech proceeds from the insula.[[20]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-20)

### Interoceptive awareness

Further information: [Interoception](./Interoception "Interoception")

There is evidence that, in addition to its base functions, the insula may play a role in certain higher-level functions that operate only in humans and other [great apes](./Hominidae "Hominidae"). The spindle neurons found at a higher density in the right frontal insular cortex are also found in the [anterior cingulate cortex](./Anterior_cingulate_cortex "Anterior cingulate cortex"), which is another region that has reached a high level of specialization in great apes. It has been speculated that these neurons are involved in [cognitive](./Cognition "Cognition")-[emotional](./Emotion "Emotion") processes that are specific to primates including great apes, such as [empathy](./Empathy "Empathy") and [metacognitive](./Metacognition "Metacognition") emotional feelings. This is supported by functional imaging results showing that the structure and function of the right frontal insula is correlated with the ability to feel one's own heartbeat, or to empathize with the pain of others. It is thought that these functions are not distinct from the lower-level functions of the insula but rather arise as a consequence of the role of the insula in conveying homeostatic information to [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness").[[21]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Insular_cortex#cite_note-22) The right anterior insula is engaged in [interoceptive](./Interoception "Interoception") awareness of homeostatic emotions such as thirst, pain and fatigue,[[23]](./Insular_cortex#ci
