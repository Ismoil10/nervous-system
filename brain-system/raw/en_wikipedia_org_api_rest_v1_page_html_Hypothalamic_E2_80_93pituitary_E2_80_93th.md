# Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis

---

Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis

Part of the neuroendocrine system



Short overview of thyroid homeostasis.[[1]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-1)

The **hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis** (**HPT axis** for short, a.k.a. thyroid homeostasis or thyrotropic feedback control) is part of the [neuroendocrine system](./Neuroendocrine_system "Neuroendocrine system") responsible for the regulation of [metabolism](./Metabolism "Metabolism") and also responds to stress.

As its name suggests, it depends upon the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"), and the [thyroid gland](./Thyroid_gland "Thyroid gland").

The hypothalamus senses low circulating levels of thyroid hormone ([Triiodothyronine](./Triiodothyronine "Triiodothyronine") (T3) and [Thyroxine](./Thyroxine "Thyroxine") (T4)) and responds by releasing thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH). The TRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to produce thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH). The TSH, in turn, stimulates the thyroid to produce thyroid hormone until levels in the blood return to normal. Thyroid hormone exerts negative feedback control over the hypothalamus as well as anterior pituitary, thus controlling the release of both TRH from hypothalamus and TSH from anterior pituitary gland.[[2]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-2)

The [HPA](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis "Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis"), [HPG](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal_axis "Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis"), and HPT axes are three pathways in which the hypothalamus and pituitary direct neuroendocrine function.

## Physiology

Thyrotropic feedback control on a more detailed and quantitative level.[[3]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-3)

Thyroid homeostasis results from a multi-loop [feedback system](./Feedback_circuit "Feedback circuit") that is found in virtually all [higher vertebrates](./Higher_vertebrates "Higher vertebrates"). Proper function of thyrotropic feedback control is indispensable for [growth](./Human_development_(biology) "Human development (biology)"), [differentiation](./Cellular_differentiation "Cellular differentiation"), [reproduction](./Reproduction "Reproduction") and [intelligence](./Intelligence "Intelligence"). Very few animals (e.g. [axolotls](./Axolotl "Axolotl") and [sloths](./Sloth "Sloth")) have impaired thyroid homeostasis that exhibits a very low [set-point](./Setpoint_(control_system) "Setpoint (control system)") that is assumed to underlie the [metabolic](./Metabolism "Metabolism") and [ontogenetic](./Ontogenesis "Ontogenesis") anomalies of these animals.

The pituitary gland secretes [thyrotropin](./Thyrotropin "Thyrotropin") (TSH; Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) that stimulates the thyroid to secrete [thyroxine](./Thyroxine "Thyroxine") (T4) and, to a lesser degree, [triiodothyronine](./Triiodothyronine "Triiodothyronine") (T3). The major portion of T3, however, is produced in peripheral organs, e.g. [liver](./Liver "Liver"), [adipose tissue](./Adipose_tissue "Adipose tissue"), [glia](./Glia "Glia") and [skeletal muscle](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle") by deiodination from circulating T4. Deiodination is controlled by numerous [hormones](./Hormone "Hormone") and [nerval signals](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") including TSH, [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") and [catecholamines](./Catecholamines "Catecholamines").

Both peripheral thyroid hormones (iodothyronines) inhibit thyrotropin secretion from the pituitary ([negative feedback](./Negative_feedback "Negative feedback")). Consequently, equilibrium concentrations for all hormones are attained.

TSH secretion is also controlled by [thyrotropin releasing hormone](./Thyrotropin_releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin releasing hormone") (thyroliberin, TRH), whose secretion itself is again suppressed by plasma T4 and T3 in [CSF](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid") (long feedback, Fekete–Lechan loop).[[4]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-LechanFekete2004-4) Additional feedback loops are ultrashort feedback control of TSH secretion (Brokken-Wiersinga-Prummel loop)[[5]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-5) and linear feedback loops controlling [plasma protein](./Plasma_protein "Plasma protein") binding.

Recent research suggested the existence of an additional feedforward motif linking TSH release to deiodinase activity in humans.[[6]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-8) The existence of this TSH-T3 shunt could explain why [deiodinase activity](./Sum_activity_of_peripheral_deiodinases "Sum activity of peripheral deiodinases") is higher in hypothyroid patients and why a minor fraction of affected individuals may benefit from substitution therapy with T3.[[9]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-9)

Convergence of multiple afferent signals in the control of TSH release including but not limited to T3,[[10]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-10) cytokines[[11]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-Chatzitomaris_2017-12) and TSH receptor antibodies[[13]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-13) may be the reason for the observation that the relation between free T4 concentration and TSH levels deviates[[14]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-14)[[15]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-15)[[16]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-16)[[17]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-17) from a pure loglinear relation that has previously been proposed.[[18]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-18) Recent research suggests that ghrelin also plays a role in the stimulation of T4 production and the subsequent suppression of TSH directly and by negative feedback.[[19]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-19)

## Functional states of thyrotropic feedback control

- [Euthyroidism](./Euthyroid "Euthyroid"): Normal thyroid function
- [Hypothyroidism](./Hypothyroidism "Hypothyroidism"): Reduced thyroid function
  - primary hypothyroidism: Feedback loop interrupted by low thyroid secretory capacity, e.g. after thyroid surgery or in case of [autoimmune thyroiditis](./Autoimmune_thyroiditis "Autoimmune thyroiditis")
  - secondary hypothyroidism: Feedback loop interrupted on the level of pituitary, e.g. in anterior pituitary failure
  - tertiary hypothyroidism: Lacking stimulation by TRH, e.g. in hypothalamic failure, [Pickardt–Fahlbusch syndrome](./Pickardt-Fahlbusch-Syndrome "Pickardt-Fahlbusch-Syndrome") or [euthyroid sick syndrome](./Euthyroid_sick_syndrome "Euthyroid sick syndrome").
- [Hyperthyroidism](./Hyperthyroidism "Hyperthyroidism"): Inappropriately increased thyroid function
  - primary hyperthyroidism: Inappropriate secretion of thyroid hormones, e.g. in case of [Graves' disease](./Graves'_disease "Graves' disease").
  - secondary hyperthyroidism: Rare condition, e.g. in case of TSH producing [pituitary adenoma](./Pituitary_adenoma "Pituitary adenoma") or partial thyroid hormone resistance.
- [Thyrotoxicosis](./Thyrotoxicosis "Thyrotoxicosis"): Over-supply with thyroid hormones, e.g. by overdosed exogenously levothyroxine supplementation.
- [Low-T3 syndrome](./Low_t3_syndrome "Low t3 syndrome") and high-T3 syndrome: Consequences of step-up hypodeiodination, e.g. in [critical illness](./Critical_illness "Critical illness") as an example for type 1 [allostasis](./Allostasis "Allostasis"),[[20]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-20) or hyperdeiodination, as in type 2 [allostasis](./Allostasis "Allostasis"), including [posttraumatic stress disorder](./Posttraumatic_stress_disorder "Posttraumatic stress disorder").[[12]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-Chatzitomaris_2017-12)
- [Resistance to thyroid hormone](./Thyroid_hormone_resistance "Thyroid hormone resistance"): Feedback loop interrupted on the level of pituitary thyroid hormone receptors.

## Diagnostics

Standard procedures cover the determination of [serum](./Blood_serum "Blood serum") levels of the following hormones:

- [TSH](./Thyroid-stimulating_hormone "Thyroid-stimulating hormone") (thyrotropin, thyroid stimulating hormone)
- [Free T4](./Free_thyroxine "Free thyroxine")
- Free T3

For special conditions the following [assays](./Assay "Assay") and procedures may be required:

- Total T4
- Total T3
- [TBG](./Thyroxine-binding_globulin "Thyroxine-binding globulin")
- [TRH test](./TRH_test "TRH test")
- [Thyroid's secretory capacity (GT)](./Thyroid's_secretory_capacity "Thyroid's secretory capacity")[[21]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-dietrich2002-21)
- [Sum activity of peripheral deiodinases (GD)](./Sum_activity_of_peripheral_deiodinases "Sum activity of peripheral deiodinases")[[21]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-dietrich2002-21)
- [TSH Index (TSHI)](./Thyroid_function_tests#TSH_index "Thyroid function tests")[[22]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis#cite_note-jostel2009-22)
