The **spinal cord** is a long, thin, tubular structure made up of [nervous tissue](./Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") that extends from the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") in the lower [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") to the [lumbar](./Lumbar "Lumbar") region of the [vertebral column](./Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") (backbone) of [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") animals. The center of the spinal cord is hollow and contains a structure called the [central canal](./Central_canal "Central canal"), which contains [cerebrospinal fluid](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid"). The spinal cord is also covered by the [meninges](./Meninges "Meninges") and enclosed by the [neural arches](./Neural_arch "Neural arch"). Together, the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and spinal cord make up the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system").

In [humans](./Human "Human"), the spinal cord is a continuation of the brainstem and anatomically begins at the [occipital bone](./Occipital_bone "Occipital bone"), passing out of the [foramen magnum](./Foramen_magnum "Foramen magnum") and then enters the [spinal canal](./Spinal_canal "Spinal canal") at the beginning of the [cervical vertebrae](./Cervical_vertebrae "Cervical vertebrae"). The spinal cord extends down to between the first and second [lumbar vertebrae](./Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae"), where it tapers to become the [cauda equina](./Cauda_equina "Cauda equina"). The enclosing bony vertebral column protects the relatively shorter spinal cord. It is around 45 cm (18 in) long in adult men and around 43 cm (17 in) long in adult women. The diameter of the spinal cord ranges from 13 mm (1⁄2 in) in the cervical and lumbar regions to 6.4 mm (1⁄4 in) in the [thoracic](./Thoracic_vertebrae "Thoracic vertebrae") area.

The spinal cord functions primarily in the transmission of [nerve signals](./Action_potential "Action potential") from the [motor cortex](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex") to the body, and from the [afferent fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") of the [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") to the [sensory cortex](./Sensory_cortex "Sensory cortex"). It is also a center for coordinating many [reflexes](./Reflex "Reflex") and contains [reflex arcs](./Reflex_arc "Reflex arc") that can independently control reflexes.[[1]](./Spinal_cord#cite_note-Maton-1) It is also the location of groups of [spinal interneurons](./Spinal_interneuron "Spinal interneuron") that make up the [neural circuits](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit") known as [central pattern generators](./Central_pattern_generator "Central pattern generator"). These circuits are responsible for controlling motor instructions for rhythmic movements such as walking.[[2]](./Spinal_cord#cite_note-Guertin-2)

The spinal cord is the main pathway for information connecting the brain and [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system").[[3]](./Spinal_cord#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Spinal_cord#cite_note-Squire-4) Much shorter than its protecting [spinal column](./Vertebral_column "Vertebral column"), the human spinal cord originates in the brainstem, passes through the [foramen magnum](./Foramen_magnum "Foramen magnum"), and continues through to the [conus medullaris](./Conus_medullaris "Conus medullaris") near the second [lumbar vertebra](./Lumbar_vertebra "Lumbar vertebra") before terminating in a fibrous extension known as the [filum terminale](./Filum_terminale "Filum terminale").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The human spinal cord is an estimated 45 centimetres (18 inches) long in average-height males and about 43 cm (17 in) in average-height females.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] It is [ovoid](./Oval "Oval")-shaped and is enlarged in the cervical and lumbar regions. The cervical enlargement, stretching from the C4 to T1 vertebrae, is where sensory input comes from and motor output goes to the arms and trunk. The lumbar enlargement, located between T10 and L1, handles sensory input and motor output coming from and going to the legs.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The spinal cord is continuous with the caudal portion of the [medulla](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"), running from the base of the [skull](./Skull "Skull") to the body of the first lumbar vertebra. It does not run the full length of the vertebral column in adults. It is made of 31 segments from which branch one pair of sensory nerve roots and one pair of motor nerve roots. The nerve roots then merge into bilaterally symmetrical pairs of [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"). The peripheral nervous system is made up of these spinal roots, nerves, and [ganglia](./Ganglion "Ganglion").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The dorsal roots are [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") [fascicles](./Nerve_fascicle "Nerve fascicle"), receiving sensory information from the skin, muscles, and visceral organs to be relayed to the brain. The roots terminate in [dorsal root ganglia](./Dorsal_root_ganglia "Dorsal root ganglia"), which are composed of the cell bodies of the corresponding neurons. Ventral roots consist of [efferent fibers](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") that arise from motor neurons whose cell bodies are found in the ventral (or anterior) gray horns of the spinal cord.[[5]](./Spinal_cord#cite_note-5)

The spinal cord (and brain) are protected by three layers of tissue or membranes called [meninges](./Meninges "Meninges"), that surround the canal. The [dura mater](./Dura_mater "Dura mater") is the outermost layer, and it forms a tough protective coating. Between the dura mater and the surrounding bone of the [vertebrae](./Vertebra_(anatomy) "Vertebra (anatomy)") is a space called the [epidural space](./Epidural_space "Epidural space"). The epidural space is filled with [adipose tissue](./Adipose_tissue "Adipose tissue"), and it contains a network of [blood vessels](./Blood_vessel "Blood vessel"). The [arachnoid mater](./Arachnoid_mater "Arachnoid mater"), the middle protective layer, is named for its open, spiderweb-like appearance. The space between the arachnoid and the underlying [pia mater](./Pia_mater "Pia mater") is called the [subarachnoid space](./Subarachnoid_space "Subarachnoid space"). The subarachnoid space contains [cerebrospinal fluid](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid"), which can be sampled with a [lumbar puncture](./Lumbar_puncture "Lumbar puncture"), or "spinal tap" procedure. The delicate pia mater, the innermost protective layer, is tightly associated with the surface of the spinal cord. The cord is stabilized within the dura mater by the connecting [denticulate ligaments](./Denticulate_ligaments "Denticulate ligaments"), which extend from the enveloping pia mater laterally between the dorsal and ventral roots. The [dural sac](./Dural_sac "Dural sac") ends at the vertebral level of the second [sacral](./Sacrum "Sacrum") vertebra.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In cross-section, the peripheral region of the cord contains neuronal [white matter](./White_matter "White matter") tracts containing [sensory](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") and [motor](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron") [axons](./Axon "Axon"). Internal to this peripheral region is the [grey matter](./Grey_matter "Grey matter"), which contains the [nerve cell bodies](./Neuron "Neuron") arranged in the three [grey columns](./Grey_column "Grey column") that give the region its butterfly-shape. This central region surrounds the [central canal](./Central_canal "Central canal"), which is an extension of the [fourth ventricle](./Fourth_ventricle "Fourth ventricle") and contains cerebrospinal fluid.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The spinal cord is elliptical in cross section, being compressed dorsolaterally. Two prominent grooves, or sulci, run along its length. The [posterior median sulcus](./Posterior_median_sulcus_of_spinal_cord "Posterior median sulcus of spinal cord") is the groove in the dorsal side, and the [anterior median fissure](./Anterior_median_fissure_of_spinal_cord "Anterior median fissure of spinal cord") is the groove in the ventral side.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Segments

Diagram of the spinal cord showing segments



Spinal cord segments and [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"), spinal cord detail, and spinal [meninges](./Meninges "Meninges") and [conus medullaris](./Conus_medullaris "Conus medullaris")

The human spinal cord is divided into segments where pairs of spinal nerves (mixed; sensory and motor) form. Six to eight motor nerve rootlets branch out of right and left ventrolateral sulci in a very orderly manner. Nerve rootlets combine to form nerve roots. Likewise, sensory nerve rootlets form off right and left dorsal lateral sulci and form sensory nerve roots. The ventral (motor) and dorsal (sensory) roots combine to form [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") (mixed; motor and sensory), one on each side of the spinal cord. Spinal nerves, with the exception of C1 and C2, form inside the [intervertebral foramen](./Intervertebral_foramen "Intervertebral foramen").
