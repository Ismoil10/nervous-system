**Hirschsprung's disease** (**HD** or **HSCR**) is a [birth defect](./Birth_defect "Birth defect") in which [nerves](./Nerves "Nerves") are missing from parts of the [intestine](./Intestine "Intestine").[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1)[[3]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NORD2017-3) The most prominent symptom is [constipation](./Constipation "Constipation").[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Other symptoms may include [vomiting](./Vomiting "Vomiting"), [abdominal pain](./Abdominal_pain "Abdominal pain"), [diarrhea](./Diarrhea "Diarrhea") and [slow growth](./Failure_to_thrive "Failure to thrive").[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Most children develop signs and symptoms shortly after birth. However, others may be diagnosed later in infancy or early childhood.[[4]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-:0-4)[[5]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-5) About half of all children with Hirschsprung's disease are diagnosed in the first year of life.[[4]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-:0-4) Complications may include [enterocolitis](./Enterocolitis "Enterocolitis"), [megacolon](./Megacolon "Megacolon"), [bowel obstruction](./Bowel_obstruction "Bowel obstruction") and [intestinal perforation](./Intestinal_perforation "Intestinal perforation").[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1)[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2)

The disorder may occur by itself or in association with other [genetic disorders](./Genetic_disorders "Genetic disorders") such as [Down syndrome](./Down_syndrome "Down syndrome") or [Waardenburg syndrome](./Waardenburg_syndrome "Waardenburg syndrome").[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1)[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2) About half of isolated cases are linked to a specific genetic [mutation](./Mutation "Mutation"), and about 20% occur within families.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Some of these occur in an [autosomal dominant](./Autosomal_dominant "Autosomal dominant") manner.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) The cause of the remaining cases is unclear.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) If otherwise normal parents have one child with the condition, the next child has a 4% risk of being affected.[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2) The condition is divided into two main types, short-segment and long-segment, depending on how much of the bowel is affected.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Rarely, the [small bowel](./Small_bowel "Small bowel") may be affected, as well.[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2) Diagnosis is based on symptoms and confirmed by [biopsy](./Tissue_biopsy "Tissue biopsy").[[3]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NORD2017-3)

Treatment is generally by surgery to remove the affected section of bowel.[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2) The surgical procedure most often carried out is known as a "[pull through](./Pull-through_procedure "Pull-through procedure")".[[3]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NORD2017-3) Occasionally, an [intestinal transplantation](./Intestinal_transplantation "Intestinal transplantation") may be recommended.[[2]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-NIH2017-2) Hirschsprung's disease occurs in about one in 5,000 of newborns.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Males are more often affected than females.[[1]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-GHR2012-1) Dutch anatomist [Frederik Ruysch](./Frederik_Ruysch "Frederik Ruysch") may have described a case in 1691, although the formal description as "congenital dilatation of the colon" was by Danish physician [Harald Hirschsprung](./Harald_Hirschsprung "Harald Hirschsprung") in 1888.[[6]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-Grosfeld2007-6)[[7]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-Hir1888-7)

## Signs and symptoms

Megacolon caused by Hirschsprung's disease

Typically, Hirschsprung disease is diagnosed shortly after birth, although it may develop well into adulthood, because of the presence of [megacolon](./Megacolon "Megacolon"), or because the baby fails to pass the first stool ([meconium](./Meconium "Meconium"))[[8]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-Cecil-8) within 48 hours of delivery. Normally, 90% of babies pass their first meconium within 24 hours, and 99% within 48 hours.[[9]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-9)

Some other signs and symptoms in newborns include a swollen belly, vomiting (green or brown vomit), and flatulence. In older children, some other signs and symptoms include chronic constipation, flatulence, swollen belly, fatigue, and failure to thrive.[[10]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-10)

Other symptoms include symptoms of bowel perforation such as vomiting, constipation, poor feeding, lethargy, and diarrhea. Symptoms of bowel obstruction would include vomiting of bile and abdominal distension. Those who do not pass stools after 36 to 48 hours after birth should raise suspicion of Hirschsprung disease. Such suspicion can also be risen if there is only passage of stools after suppository, rectal exam, or enema. Children who do not respond to constipation treatment for six months should also raise suspicion of such disease. [Enterocolitis](./Enterocolitis "Enterocolitis"), an acute complication of Hirschsprung disease, is characterized by sudden onset of fever, abdominal distension, vomiting, passage of bloody stools or release of explosive gas or stools after rectal examination.[[11]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-pmid29300049-11)

Some cases are diagnosed later, into childhood, but usually before age 10.[[8]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-Cecil-8) The child may experience fecal retention, constipation, or abdominal distention.[[8]](./Hirschsprung's_disease#cite_note-Cecil-8)
