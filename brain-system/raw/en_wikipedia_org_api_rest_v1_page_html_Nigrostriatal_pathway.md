The **nigrostriatal pathway** is a bilateral [dopaminergic pathway](./Dopaminergic_pathway "Dopaminergic pathway") in the brain that connects the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra") [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta") (SNc) in the midbrain with the [dorsal striatum](./Dorsal_striatum "Dorsal striatum") (i.e., the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen")) in the forebrain. It is one of the four major [dopamine pathways](./Dopamine_pathways "Dopamine pathways") in the brain, and is critical in the production of movement as part of a system called the [basal ganglia motor loop](./Basal_ganglia#Circuit_connections "Basal ganglia"). [Dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") neurons of this pathway release dopamine from axon terminals that synapse onto [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") medium spiny neurons (MSNs), also known as spiny projection neurons (SPNs),[[1]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:02-1)[[2]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-pmid230346512-2) located in the striatum.

Degeneration of dopaminergic [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") in the SNc is one of the main pathological features of [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"),[[3]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-3) leading to a marked reduction in dopamine function and the symptomatic motor deficits of [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease") including [hypokinesia](./Hypokinesia "Hypokinesia"), [tremors](./Tremor "Tremor"), rigidity, and [postural imbalance](./Gait_abnormality "Gait abnormality").

## Anatomy

The connection between the substantia nigra pars compacta and the dorsal striatum is mediated via dopaminergic axons.

### Substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc)

The [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra") is located in the ventral [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") of each hemisphere. It has two distinct parts, the [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta") (SNc) and the [pars reticulata](./Pars_reticulata "Pars reticulata") (SNr). The pars compacta contains **dopaminergic neurons** from the [A9](./Dopaminergic_cell_groups#Cell_group_A9 "Dopaminergic cell groups") cell group that forms the nigrostriatal pathway that, by supplying dopamine to the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum"), relays information to the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia"). In contrast, the pars reticulata contains mostly **GABAergic neurons.**

The SNc is composed of a thin band of cells that overlies the SNr and is situated laterally to the [A10](./Dopaminergic_cell_groups#Cell_group_A10 "Dopaminergic cell groups") group of dopaminergic neurons in the [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area") (VTA) that forms the [mesolimbic dopamine pathway](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway"). The SNc is easily visualized in human brain sections because the dopamine neurons contain a black pigment called [neuromelanin](./Neuromelanin "Neuromelanin") which is known to accumulate with age.[[4]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-4) The dopaminergic cell bodies in the SNc are densely packed with approximately 200,000 to 420,000 dopamine cells in human SNc and 8,000 to 12,000 dopamine cells in mouse SNc.[[5]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:1-5) These dopamine cell bodies are localized to one of two chemically defined layers.[[6]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:2-6) Those in the upper layer (or **dorsal tier**) contain a binding protein called **[calbindin](./Calbindin "Calbindin")-D28K** which can buffer **calcium** levels inside the cell when it becomes too high or toxic. Dopamine cells in the lower layer (or **ventral tier**) lack this protein and are more vulnerable to the effects of [neurotoxins](./Neurotoxin "Neurotoxin") (e.g. [MPTP](./MPTP "MPTP")) that can cause Parkinson disease-like symptoms.[[7]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-8) The dorsal tier dopamine cells have [dendrites](./Dendrite "Dendrite") that radiate horizontally across the pars compacta, whereas ventral tier dopamine cells have dendrites that extend ventrally into the pars reticulata.[[6]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:2-6)[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9)

### Dopaminergic axons

The [axons](./Axons "Axons") from dopamine neurons emanate from a primary dendrite and project **ipsilaterally** (on the same side) via the **[medial forebrain bundle](./Medial_forebrain_bundle "Medial forebrain bundle")** to the dorsal striatum. There is a rough topographical correlation between the anatomical localization of the dopamine cell body within the SNc and the area of termination in the dorsal striatum. Dopaminergic cells in the lateral parts of the SNc project mainly to the lateral and caudal (posterior) parts of the striatum, whereas dopamine cells in the medial SNc project to the medial striatum.[[10]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:4-10)[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9) In addition, dopamine cells in the dorsal tier project to the ventromedial striatum, whereas the ventral tier neurons project to the dorsal caudate nucleus and putamen.[[6]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:2-6)[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9) In general, there is a greater density of dopaminergic input to the dorsolateral striatum.[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9)

Each dopamine neuron has an extremely large unmyelinated axonal arborization which can innervate up to 6% of the striatal volume in a rat.[[11]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:5-11) Although all SNc dopamine cells project to both the **[striosome](./Striosome "Striosome")** (or patch) and **matrix** neurochemical compartments of the striatum, most of the axonal territory of a dorsal tier neuron is in the matrix compartment while the majority of the axonal field of ventral tier neurons is in the striosomes.[[6]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:2-6)[[10]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:4-10)[[11]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:5-11) Nigrostriatal dopamine axons can also give rise to axon collaterals that project to other brain regions. For example, some SNc nigrostriatal dopamine axons send axon collaterals to the [pedunculopontine nucleus](./Pedunculopontine_nucleus "Pedunculopontine nucleus"), [ventral pallidum](./Ventral_pallidum "Ventral pallidum"), [subthalamic nucleus](./Subthalamic_nucleus "Subthalamic nucleus"), [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus"), [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), and [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus").[[6]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:2-6)[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9)[[12]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:6-12)

A small number of SNc dorsal tier dopamine neurons also project directly to the cortex, although most of the dopaminergic innervation of the cortex comes from the adjacent VTA dopamine neurons.[[9]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:3-9)

### Dorsal striatum

The [dorsal striatum](./Dorsal_striatum "Dorsal striatum") is located in the subcortical region of the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain"). In primates and other mammals, it is divided by the anterior limb of a [white matter](./White_matter "White matter") tract called the [internal capsule](./Internal_capsule "Internal capsule")[[13]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-13) into two parts: the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and the [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen").[[14]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-14) In rodents, the internal capsule is poorly developed such that the caudate and putamen are not separated but form one large entity called the caudate putamen (CPu).[[15]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-15)[[16]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-16) The majority (about 95%) of cells in the dorsal striatum are GABAergic medium spiny neurons (MSNs), also known as spiny projection neurons (SPNs). Approximately half of these MSNs contain [dopamine D1 receptors](./Dopamine_d1_receptors "Dopamine d1 receptors") and project directly to the substantia nigra to form the **direct pathway** of the basal ganglia, whereas the other half express [dopamine D2 receptors](./Dopamine_receptor_D2 "Dopamine receptor D2") that project indirectly to the substantia nigra via the globus pallidus and subthalamic nucleus to form the **indirect pathway** of the basal ganglia.[[17]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-17) The remaining 5% of cells are **interneurons** that are either cholinergic neurons[[18]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-18) or one of several types of GABAergic neurons.[[19]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-19) The axons and dendrites of these interneurons stay within the striatum.

The caudate nucleus and putamen receive excitatory information from all areas of the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex").[[20]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:7-20) These [glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic") inputs are generally topographically arranged such that the putamen takes information largely from the sensorimotor cortex, whereas the caudate nucleus obtains information largely from the association cortex.[[20]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:7-20) In addition, the dorsal striatum receives excitatory inputs from other brain structures like the thalamus,[[21]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-21) and minor excitatory inputs from the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") and [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala").

The dorsal striatum contains neurochemically defined compartments called striosomes (also known as patches) that exhibit dense μ-opioid receptor staining embedded within a matrix compartment that contains higher [acetylcholinesterase](./Acetylcholinesterase "Acetylcholinesterase") and calbindin-D28K.[[22]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-22)

The dopaminergic axon terminals of the nigrostriatal pathway synapse onto [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") MSNs in the dorsal striatum. They form synapses on the cell body and dendritic shaft regions but mostly on the necks of [dendritic spines](./Dendritic_spine "Dendritic spine") that also receive glutamatergic input to the heads of the same dendritic spines.[[1]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-:02-1)

## Function

The main function of the nigrostriatal pathway is to influence **voluntary movement** through basal ganglia motor loops. Along with the [mesolimbic](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway") and [mesocortical](./Mesocortical_pathway "Mesocortical pathway") dopaminergic pathways, the nigrostriatal dopamine pathway can also influence other brain functions, including **cognition**,[[23]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-23) **reward**, and **addiction**.[[24]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-24) Nigrostriatal dopaminergic neurons exhibit tonic and phasic patterns of neuronal firing activity. This can lead to different patterns of dopamine release from the axon terminals in the dorsal striatum and also from the cell body (soma) and dendrites in the SNc and SNr.[[25]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-25)[[26]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-26) As well as releasing dopamine, some axons in the nigrostriatal pathway can also co-release GABA.[[27]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-27)[[28]](./Nigrostriatal_pathway#cite_note-28)
