# Nociception

Nociception

How an organism receives and responds to painful stimuli

In [physiology](./Physiology "Physiology"), **nociception** /ˌnəʊsɪˈsɛpʃ(ə)n/, also **nocioception** (from [Latin](./Latin_language "Latin language")  *nocere* 'to [harm](./Harm "Harm")/hurt'), is the [sensory nervous system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system")'s process of encoding [noxious stimuli](./Noxious_stimulus "Noxious stimulus"). It deals with a series of events and processes required for an organism to receive a [painful](./Pain "Pain") stimulus, convert it to a molecular signal, and recognize and characterize the signal to trigger an appropriate defensive response.

In nociception, intense chemical (e.g., [capsaicin](./Capsaicin "Capsaicin") present in [chili pepper](./Chili_pepper "Chili pepper") or [cayenne pepper](./Cayenne_pepper "Cayenne pepper")), mechanical (e.g., cutting, crushing), or thermal (heat and cold) stimulation of [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") called [nociceptors](./Nociceptor "Nociceptor") produces a signal that travels along a chain of [nerve fibers](./Nerve_fiber "Nerve fiber") to the [brain](./Brain "Brain").[[1]](./Nociception#cite_note-1) Nociception triggers a variety of physiological and behavioral responses to protect the organism against an aggression, and usually results in a subjective experience, or [perception](./Perception "Perception"), of pain in [sentient](./Sentience "Sentience") beings.[[2]](./Nociception#cite_note-Bayne-2)

## Detection of noxious stimuli

Mechanism of nociception via [sensory afferents](./Sensory_afferents "Sensory afferents")

Potentially damaging mechanical, thermal, and chemical stimuli are detected by nerve endings called nociceptors, which are found in the [skin](./Skin "Skin"), on internal surfaces such as the [periosteum](./Periosteum "Periosteum"), [joint](./Joint "Joint") surfaces, and in some internal [organs](./Organ_(anatomy) "Organ (anatomy)"). Some nociceptors are unspecialized [free nerve endings](./Free_nerve_ending "Free nerve ending") that have their cell bodies outside the [spinal column](./Spinal_column "Spinal column") in [dorsal root ganglia](./Dorsal_root_ganglia "Dorsal root ganglia").[[3]](./Nociception#cite_note-3) Others are specialised structures in the skin such as nociceptive [Schwann cells](./Schwann_cells "Schwann cells").[[4]](./Nociception#cite_note-4) Nociceptors are categorized according to the [axons](./Axon "Axon") which travel from the receptors to the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") or brain. After nerve injury, it is possible for touch fibers that normally carry non-noxious stimuli to be perceived as noxious.[[5]](./Nociception#cite_note-5)

Nociceptive pain consists of an adaptive alarm system.[[6]](./Nociception#cite_note-6) Nociceptors have a certain threshold; that is, they require a minimum intensity of stimulation before they trigger a signal. Once this threshold is reached, a signal is passed along the neuron's axon into the spinal cord.

Nociceptive threshold testing deliberately applies a noxious stimulus to a human or animal subject to study pain. In animals, the technique is often used to study the efficacy of [analgesic drugs](./Analgesic_drugs "Analgesic drugs") and to establish dosing levels and periods of effect. After establishing a baseline, the drug under test is given, and the elevation in threshold is recorded at specified times. The threshold should return to the baseline (pretreatment) value when the drug wears off. In some conditions, the excitation of pain fibers increases as the pain stimulus continues, leading to a condition called [hyperalgesia](./Hyperalgesia "Hyperalgesia").

## Consequences

Nociception can also cause generalized [autonomic responses](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") before or without reaching consciousness to cause [pallor](./Pallor "Pallor"), [sweating](./Sweating "Sweating"), [tachycardia](./Tachycardia "Tachycardia"), [hypertension](./Hypertension "Hypertension"), [lightheadedness](./Lightheadedness "Lightheadedness"), [nausea](./Nausea "Nausea"), and [fainting](./Fainting "Fainting").[[7]](./Nociception#cite_note-7)

This overview discusses [proprioception](./Proprioception "Proprioception"), [thermoception](./Thermoception "Thermoception"), chemoception, and nociception, as they are all integrally connected.

### Mechanical

See also: [Neuropathic pain](./Neuropathic_pain "Neuropathic pain")

Proprioception is determined by using standard mechanoreceptors (especially [ruffini corpuscles](./Ruffini_corpuscle "Ruffini corpuscle") (stretch) and [transient receptor potential channels](./Transient_receptor_potential_channel "Transient receptor potential channel") (TRP channels). Proprioception is completely covered within the [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system"), as the brain processes them together.

Thermoception refers to stimuli of moderate temperatures 24–28 °C (75–82 °F), as anything beyond that range is considered pain and moderated by nociceptors. TRP and potassium channels [TRPM (1-8), TRPV (1-6), TRAAK, and TREK] each respond to different temperatures (among other stimuli), which create action potentials in nerves that join the mechano (touch) system in the posterolateral tract. Thermoception, like proprioception, is then covered by the somatosensory system.[[8]](./Nociception#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Nociception#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Nociception#cite_note-10)[[11]](./Nociception#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Nociception#cite_note-12)

TRP channels that detect noxious stimuli (mechanical, thermal, and chemical pain) relay that information to nociceptors that generate an action potential. Mechanical TRP channels react to depression of their cells (like touch), thermal TRPs change shape in different temperatures, and chemical TRPs act like [taste buds](./Taste_bud "Taste bud"), signalling if their receptors bond to certain elements/chemicals.

### Neural

- [Laminae 3-5](./Nucleus_proprius_of_spinal_cord "Nucleus proprius of spinal cord") make up [nucleus proprius](./Nucleus_proprius "Nucleus proprius") in spinal grey matter.
- [Lamina 2](./Rexed_lamina "Rexed lamina") makes up [substantia gelatinosa of Rolando](./Substantia_gelatinosa_of_Rolando "Substantia gelatinosa of Rolando"), unmyelinated spinal grey matter. Substantia receives input from nucleus proprius and conveys intense, poorly localized pain.
- [Lamina 1](./Marginal_nucleus_of_spinal_cord "Marginal nucleus of spinal cord") primarily project to the [parabrachial area](./Parabrachial_area "Parabrachial area") and [periaqueductal grey](./Periaqueductal_gray "Periaqueductal gray"), which begins the suppression of pain via neural and hormonal inhibition. Lamina 1 receive input from thermoreceptors via the [posterolateral tract](./Posterolateral_tract "Posterolateral tract"). Marginal nucleus of the spinal cord are the only unsuppressible pain signals.
- The [parabrachial area](./Parabrachial_area "Parabrachial area") integrates taste and pain info, then relays it. Parabrachial checks if the pain is being received in normal temperatures and if the [gustatory system](./Gustatory_system "Gustatory system") is active; if both are so the pain is assumed to be due to poison.
- [Ao fibers](./Ao_fiber?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ao fiber") synapse on laminae 1 and 5 while [Ab](./Ab_fiber "Ab fiber") synapses on 1, 3, 5, and C. [C fibers](./C_fiber "C fiber") exclusively synapse on lamina 2.[[13]](./Nociception#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Nociception#cite_note-14)
- The [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala") and [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") create and encode the memory and emotion due to pain stimuli.
- The [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") signals for the release of hormones that make pain suppression more effective; some of these are sex hormones.
- [Periaqueductal grey](./Periaqueductal_grey "Periaqueductal grey") (with hypothalamic hormone aid) hormonally signals [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation")'s [raphe nuclei](./Raphe_nucleus "Raphe nucleus") to produce [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin") that inhibits laminae pain nuclei.[[15]](./Nociception#cite_note-15)
- [Lateral spinothalamic tract](./Lateral_spinothalamic_tract "Lateral spinothalamic tract") aids in localization of pain.
- [Spinoreticular](./Spinoreticular_tract "Spinoreticular tract") and [spinotectal tracts](./Spinotectal_tract "Spinotectal tract") are merely relay tracts to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") that aid in the perception of pain and alertness towards it. Fibers cross over (left becomes right) via the spinal [anterior white commissure](./Anterior_white_commissure "Anterior white commissure").
- [Lateral lemniscus](./Lateral_lemniscus "Lateral lemniscus") is the first point of integration of sound and pain information.[[16]](./Nociception#cite_note-16)
- [Inferior colliculus](./Inferior_colliculus "Inferior colliculus") (IC) aids in sound orienting to pain stimuli.[[17]](./Nociception#cite_note-17)
- [Superior colliculus](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus") receives IC's input, integrates visual orienting info, and uses the balance topographical map to orient the body to the pain stimuli.[[18]](./Nociception#cite_note-18)[[19]](./Nociception#cite_note-19)
- [Inferior cerebellar peduncle](./Inferior_cerebellar_peduncle "Inferior cerebellar peduncle") integrates proprioceptive info and outputs to the [vestibulocerebellum](./Vestibulocerebellum "Vestibulocerebellum"). The peduncle is not part of the lateral-spinothalamic-tract-pathway; the medulla receives the info and passes it onto the peduncle from elsewhere (see [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system")).
- The [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") is where pain is thought to be brought into [perception](./Perception "Perception"); it also aids in pain suppression and modulation, acting like a [bouncer](./Bouncer_(doorman) "Bouncer (doorman)"), allowing certain intensities through to the cerebrum and rejecting others.[[20]](./Nociception#cite_note-20)
- The [somatosensory cortex](./Somatosensory_cortex "Somatosensory cortex") decodes nociceptor info to determine the exact location of pain and is where proprioception is brought into consciousness; inferior cerebellar peduncle is all unconscious proprioception.
- [Insula](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") judges the intensity of the pain and provides the ability to imagine pain.[[21]](./Nociception#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Nociception#cite_note-22)
- [Cingulate cortex](./Cingulate_cortex "Cingulate cortex") is presumed to be the memory hub for pain.[[23]](./Nociception#cite_note-23)
