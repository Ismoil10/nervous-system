The **respiratory center** is located in the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") and [pons](./Pons "Pons"), in the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem"). The respiratory center is made up of three major respiratory groups of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron"), two in the medulla and one in the pons. In the medulla they are the dorsal respiratory group, and the ventral respiratory group. In the pons, the pontine respiratory group includes two areas known as the pneumotaxic center and the apneustic center.

The respiratory center is responsible for generating and maintaining the rhythm of respiration, and also of adjusting this in [homeostatic](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") response to physiological changes. The respiratory center receives input from [chemoreceptors](./Chemoreceptors "Chemoreceptors"), [mechanoreceptors](./Mechanoreceptors "Mechanoreceptors"), the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"), and the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") in order to regulate the rate and depth of [breathing](./Breathing "Breathing"). Input is stimulated by altered levels of [oxygen](./Oxygen "Oxygen"), [carbon dioxide](./Carbon_dioxide "Carbon dioxide"), and [blood pH](./PH#pH_of_various_body_fluids "PH"), by [hormonal changes relating to stress](./Biology_of_stress "Biology of stress") and anxiety from the hypothalamus, and also by signals from the cerebral cortex to give a [conscious control](./Conscious_breathing "Conscious breathing") of respiration.

Injury to respiratory groups can cause various breathing disorders that may require [mechanical ventilation](./Mechanical_ventilation "Mechanical ventilation"), and is usually associated with a poor prognosis.

## Respiratory groups

The respiratory center is divided into three major groups, two in the medulla and one in the pons. The two groups in the medulla are the **dorsal respiratory group** and the **ventral respiratory group**. In the pons, the **pontine respiratory group** is made up of two areas – the pneumotaxic center and the apneustic center. The dorsal and ventral medullary groups control the basic rhythm of respiration.[[1]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-T_and_D-1)[[2]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-2) The groups are paired with one on each side of the brainstem.[[3]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-3)

### Dorsal respiratory group

Solitary tract nucleus in the dorsal respiratory group and nucleus ambiguous of the ventral respiratory group shown in their positions on the medulla oblongata.

The dorsal respiratory group (DRG) has the most fundamental role in the control of respiration, initiating inspiration (inhalation). The DRG is a collection of neurons forming an elongated mass that extends most of the length of the dorsal medulla. They are near to the [central canal](./Central_canal "Central canal") of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), and just behind the ventral group. They set and maintain the [rate of respiration](./Respiratory_rate "Respiratory rate").[[4]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-G_and_H-4)[[5]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Saladin-5)

Most of the neurons are located in the [nucleus of the solitary tract](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus"). Other important neurons are found in the adjacent areas including the reticular substance of the medulla. The solitary nucleus is the end-point for sensory information arriving from the pontine respiratory group, and from two [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves") – the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve"), and the [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve"). The solitary nucleus sends signals to the respiratory center from [peripheral chemoreceptors](./Peripheral_chemoreceptors "Peripheral chemoreceptors"), [baroreceptors](./Baroreceptor "Baroreceptor"), and other types of receptors in the [lungs](./Lung "Lung") in particular the [stretch receptors](./Stretch_receptor "Stretch receptor"). Thus, the dorsal respiratory group is seen as an integrating center that gives the ventral respiratory group output to modify the breathing rhythm.[[4]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-G_and_H-4)[[5]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Saladin-5)

### Ventral respiratory group

The VRG maintains a constant breathing rhythm by stimulating the diaphragm and external intercostal muscles to contract, resulting in inspiration.[[6]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-6)

In the medulla, the ventral respiratory group (VRG) consists of four groups of neurons that make up the [exhalation](./Exhalation "Exhalation") (expiratory) area of respiratory control. This area is in the ventrolateral part of the medulla, about 5 mm anterior and lateral to the dorsal respiratory group. The neurons involved include those in the [nucleus ambiguus](./Nucleus_ambiguus "Nucleus ambiguus"), the nucleus retroambiguus, and the [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron") in the [pre-Bötzinger complex](./Pre-Bötzinger_complex "Pre-Bötzinger complex").

The VRG contains both inspiratory and expiratory neurons.[[7]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Koeppen-7)[[4]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-G_and_H-4) The ventral respiratory group of neurons are active in forceful breathing and inactive during quiet, restful respirations.[[1]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-T_and_D-1) The VRG sends inhibitory impulses to the apneustic center.

### Pontine respiratory group

In the [pontine tegmentum](./Pontine_tegmentum "Pontine tegmentum") in the pons, the pontine respiratory group (PRG) includes the pneumotaxic and apneustic centers. These have connections between them, and they both form the [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus").[[8]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Song-8)

#### Pneumotaxic center

The pneumotaxic center is located in the upper part of the pons. Its nuclei are the [subparabrachial nucleus](./Parabrachial_nuclei "Parabrachial nuclei") and the [medial parabrachial nucleus](./Parabrachial_nuclei "Parabrachial nuclei").[[9]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-9) The pneumotaxic center controls both the rate and the pattern of breathing. The pneumotaxic center is considered an [antagonist](./Antagonism_(chemistry) "Antagonism (chemistry)") to the apneustic center (which produces abnormal breathing during inhalation), cyclically inhibiting inhalation. The pneumotaxic center is responsible for limiting inspiration, providing an *inspiratory off-switch* (IOS).[[10]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-IOS-10) It limits the burst of [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential") in the [phrenic nerve](./Phrenic_nerve "Phrenic nerve"), effectively decreasing the [tidal volume](./Tidal_volume "Tidal volume") and regulating the [respiratory rate](./Respiratory_rate "Respiratory rate"). Absence of the center results in an increase in depth of respiration and a decrease in respiratory rate.

The pneumotaxic center regulates the amount of air that can be taken into the body in each breath. The dorsal respiratory group has [rhythmic bursts of activity](./Neuroscience_of_rhythm "Neuroscience of rhythm") that are constant in duration and interval.[[11]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-breathing-11) When a faster rate of breathing is needed the pneumotaxic center signals the dorsal respiratory group to speed up. When longer breaths are needed the bursts of activity are elongated. All the information that the body uses to help respiration happens in the pneumotaxic center. If this was damaged or in any way harmed it would make breathing almost impossible.

One study on this subject was on anaesthetized paralyzed cats before and after bilateral [vagotomy](./Vagotomy "Vagotomy"). Ventilation was monitored in awake and anaesthetized cats breathing air or CO2. Ventilation was monitored both before and after lesions to the pneumatic center region and after subsequent bilateral vagotomy. Cats with pontine lesions had a prolonged inhalation duration.[[12]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-12) In cats, after anaesthesia and vagotomy, pontine transaction has been described as evoking a long sustained inspiratory discharges interrupted by short expiratory pauses.[*[jargon](./Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style#Technical_language "Wikipedia:Manual of Style")*] In rats on the other hand, after anaesthesia, vagotomy and pontine transaction, this breathing pattern was not observed, either in vivo or in vitro. These results suggest interspecies differences between rat and cat in the pontine influences on the medullary respiratory center.[[13]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Neuroscience_Studies-13)

#### Apneustic center

The apneustic center of the lower pons appears to promote inhalation by constant stimulation of the neurons in the medulla oblongata. The apneustic center sends signals to the dorsal group in the medulla to delay the 'switch off, the *inspiratory off switch* (IOS) signal of the inspiratory ramp provided by the pneumotaxic center. It controls the intensity of breathing, giving positive impulses to the neurons involved with inhalation. The apneustic center is inhibited by pulmonary stretch receptors and also by the pneumotaxic center. It also discharges an inhibitory impulse to the pneumotaxic center.

## Respiratory rhythm

Further information: [Central pattern generator](./Central_pattern_generator "Central pattern generator")

Breathing is the repetitive process of bringing air into the lungs and taking waste products out. The oxygen brought in from the air is a constant, on-going need of an organism to maintain life. This need is still there during sleep so that the functioning of this process has to be automatic and be part of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"). The in-breath is followed by the out-breath, giving the respiratory cycle of inhalation and exhalation. There are three phases of the respiratory cycle: inspiration, post-inspiration or passive expiration, and late or active expiration.[[14]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-M_and_D-14)[[15]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-Ramirez-15)

The number of cycles per minute is the [respiratory rate](./Respiratory_rate "Respiratory rate"). The respiratory rate is set in the respiratory center by the dorsal respiratory group, in the medulla, and these neurons are mostly concentrated in the [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus") that extends the length of the medulla.[[4]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-G_and_H-4)

The basic rhythm of respiration is that of quiet, restful breathing known as [eupnea](./Eupnea "Eupnea"). Quiet breathing only requires the activity of the dorsal group which activates the [diaphragm](./Thoracic_diaphragm "Thoracic diaphragm"), and the [external intercostal muscles](./External_intercostal_muscle "External intercostal muscle"). Exhalation is passive and relies on the [elastic recoil of the lungs](./Elastic_recoil "Elastic recoil"). When the metabolic need for oxygen increases, inspiration becomes more forceful and the neurons in the ventral group are activated to bring about [forceful exhalation](./Forceful_exhalation "Forceful exhalation").[[1]](./Respiratory_center#cite_note-T_and_D-1) [Shortness of breath](./Shortness_of_breath "Shortness of breath") is termed dyspnea – the opposite of eupnea.
