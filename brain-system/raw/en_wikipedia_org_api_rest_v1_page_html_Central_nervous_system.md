The **central nervous system** (**CNS**) is the part of the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") consisting primarily of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). The [retina](./Retina "Retina") is also technically part of the CNS.[[2]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Purves2001-2) The CNS is named so because the brain integrates the received information and coordinates and influences the activity of all parts of the bodies of [bilaterally symmetric and triploblastic animals](./Bilateria "Bilateria")—that is, all multicellular animals except [sponges](./Sponge "Sponge") and [diploblasts](./Coelenterata "Coelenterata"). It is a structure composed of [nervous tissue](./Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") positioned along the [rostral (nose end) to caudal (tail end)](./Anatomical_terms_of_location#Rostral,_cranial,_and_caudal "Anatomical terms of location") axis of the body and may have an enlarged section at the rostral end which is a brain. Only [arthropods](./Arthropods "Arthropods"), [cephalopods](./Cephalopods "Cephalopods") and [vertebrates](./Vertebrates "Vertebrates") have a true brain, though precursor structures exist in [onychophorans](./Onychophora "Onychophora"), [gastropods](./Gastropods "Gastropods") and [lancelets](./Lancelets "Lancelets").

The rest of this article exclusively discusses the vertebrate central nervous system, which is radically distinct from all other animals.

## Overview

In [vertebrates](./Vertebrates "Vertebrates"), the brain and spinal cord are both enclosed in the [meninges](./Meninges "Meninges").[[3]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Maton-3) The meninges provide [a barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier") to chemicals dissolved in the blood, protecting the brain from most [neurotoxins](./Neurotoxins "Neurotoxins") commonly found in food. Within the meninges the brain and spinal cord are bathed in [cerebral spinal fluid](./Cerebral_spinal_fluid "Cerebral spinal fluid") which replaces the [body fluid](./Extracellular_fluid "Extracellular fluid") found outside the cells of all [bilateral animals](./Bilaterian "Bilaterian").

In vertebrates, the CNS is contained within the [dorsal body cavity](./Dorsal_body_cavity "Dorsal body cavity"), while the brain is housed in the [cranial cavity](./Cranial_cavity "Cranial cavity") within the [skull](./Skull "Skull"). The spinal cord is housed in the [spinal canal](./Spinal_canal "Spinal canal") within the [vertebrae](./Vertebrae "Vertebrae").[[3]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Maton-3) Within the CNS, the interneuronal space is filled with a large amount of supporting non-nervous cells called neuroglia or [glia](./Glia "Glia") from the Greek for "glue".[[4]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-KettenmannFaissner1996-4)

In vertebrates, the CNS also includes the [retina](./Retina "Retina"),[[2]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Purves2001-2) and the [optic nerve](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") ([cranial nerve](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve") II),[[5]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Fitzgerald-6) as well as the [olfactory nerves](./Olfactory_nerves "Olfactory nerves") and [olfactory epithelium](./Olfactory_epithelium "Olfactory epithelium").[[7]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Gizurarson_S._2012_566–582-7) As parts of the CNS, they connect directly to brain neurons without intermediate [ganglia](./Ganglion "Ganglion"). The [olfactory epithelium](./Olfactory_epithelium "Olfactory epithelium") is the only central nervous tissue outside the meninges in direct contact with the environment, which opens up a pathway for [therapeutic](./Therapeutic "Therapeutic") agents which cannot otherwise cross the meninges barrier.[[7]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Gizurarson_S._2012_566–582-7)

## Structure

Main article: [Neuroanatomy](./Neuroanatomy "Neuroanatomy")

The CNS consists of two major structures: the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). The brain is encased in the skull, and protected by the cranium.[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8) The spinal cord is continuous with the brain and lies [caudally](./Anatomical_terminology "Anatomical terminology") to the brain.[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9) It is protected by the [vertebrae](./Vertebra "Vertebra").[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8) The spinal cord reaches from the base of the skull, and continues through[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8) or starting below[[10]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Huijzen-10) the [foramen magnum](./Foramen_magnum "Foramen magnum"),[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8) and terminates roughly level with the first or second [lumbar vertebra](./Lumbar_vertebra "Lumbar vertebra"),[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9)[[10]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Huijzen-10) occupying the upper sections of the [vertebral canal](./Vertebral_canal "Vertebral canal").[[6]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Fitzgerald-6)

### White and gray matter

Main articles: [Gray matter](./Gray_matter "Gray matter") and [White matter](./White_matter "White matter")

Dissection of a human brain with labels showing the clear division between white and gray matter.

Microscopically, there are differences between the neurons and tissue of the CNS and the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS).[[11]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-11) The CNS is composed of [white](./White_matter "White matter") and [gray matter](./Gray_matter "Gray matter").[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9) This can also be seen macroscopically on brain tissue. The white matter consists of [axons](./Axon "Axon") and [oligodendrocytes](./Oligodendrocyte "Oligodendrocyte"), while the gray matter consists of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") and unmyelinated fibers. Both tissues include a number of [glial](./Glia "Glia") cells (although the white matter contains more), which are often referred to as supporting cells of the CNS. Different forms of glial cells have different functions, some acting almost as scaffolding for [neuroblasts](./Neuroblast "Neuroblast") to climb during [neurogenesis](./Neurogenesis "Neurogenesis") such as [bergmann glia](./Bergmann_glia "Bergmann glia"), while others such as [microglia](./Microglia "Microglia") are a specialized form of [macrophage](./Macrophage "Macrophage"), involved in the [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system") of the brain as well as the clearance of various [metabolites](./Metabolite "Metabolite") from the [brain tissue](./Brain_tissue "Brain tissue").[[6]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Fitzgerald-6) [Astrocytes](./Astrocyte "Astrocyte") may be involved with both clearance of metabolites as well as transport of fuel and various beneficial substances to neurons from the [capillaries](./Capillary "Capillary") of the brain. Upon CNS injury astrocytes will proliferate, causing [gliosis](./Gliosis "Gliosis"), a form of neuronal scar tissue, lacking in functional neurons.[[6]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Fitzgerald-6)

The brain ([cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") as well as [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") and [hindbrain](./Hindbrain "Hindbrain")) consists of a [cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"), composed of neuron-bodies constituting gray matter, while internally there is more white matter that form [tracts](./Nerve_tract "Nerve tract") and [commissures](./Commissural_fiber "Commissural fiber"). Apart from cortical gray matter there is also [subcortical gray matter](./Subcortical_gray_matter "Subcortical gray matter") making up a large number of different [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)").[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9)

### Spinal cord

The second example shows the constriction of the pupil as a result of the activation of the Eddinger-Westphal nucleus (a cerebral ganglion).

From and to the spinal cord are projections of the peripheral nervous system in the form of [spinal](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve") (sometimes segmental nerves[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8)). The nerves connect the spinal cord to skin, joints, muscles etc. and allow for the transmission of [efferent](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") motor as well as [afferent sensory signals](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") and stimuli.[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9) This allows for voluntary and involuntary motions of muscles, as well as the perception of senses.
All in all 31 spinal nerves project from the brain stem,[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9) some forming plexa as they branch out, such as the [brachial plexa](./Brachial_plexus "Brachial plexus"), [sacral plexa](./Sacral_plexus "Sacral plexus") etc.[[8]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore's-8) Each spinal nerve will carry both sensory and motor signals, but the nerves synapse at different regions of the spinal cord, either from the periphery to sensory relay neurons that relay the information to the CNS or from the CNS to motor neurons, which relay the information out.[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9)

The spinal cord relays information up to the brain through spinal tracts through the final common pathway[[9]](./Central_nervous_system#cite_note-Kandel-9) to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") and ultimately to the cortex.
