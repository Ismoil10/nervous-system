A **motor neuron** (or **motoneuron**), also known as **efferent neuron**[[1]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-1) is a [neuron](./Neuron "Neuron") that allows for both voluntary and involuntary movements of the body through [muscles](./Muscle "Muscle") and [glands](./Gland "Gland"). Its [cell body](./Cell_body "Cell body") is located in the [motor cortex](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex"), [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") or the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), and whose [axon](./Axon "Axon") (fiber) projects to the spinal cord or outside of the spinal cord to directly or indirectly control effector organs, mainly muscles and glands.[[2]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-2) There are two types of motor neuron—[upper motor neurons](./Upper_motor_neuron "Upper motor neuron") and [lower motor neurons](./Lower_motor_neuron "Lower motor neuron"). Axons from upper motor neurons synapse onto [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron") in the spinal cord and occasionally directly onto lower motor neurons.[[3]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-OUP-3) The axons from the lower motor neurons are [efferent nerve fibers](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") that carry signals from the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") to the effectors.[[4]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-4) Types of lower motor neurons are [alpha motor neurons](./Alpha_motor_neuron "Alpha motor neuron"), [beta motor neurons](./Beta_motor_neuron "Beta motor neuron"), and [gamma motor neurons](./Gamma_motor_neuron "Gamma motor neuron").

A single motor neuron may innervate many [muscle fibres](./Myocyte "Myocyte") and a muscle fibre can undergo many [action potentials](./Action_potentials "Action potentials") in the time taken for a single [muscle twitch](./Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction"). Innervation takes place at a [neuromuscular junction](./Neuromuscular_junction "Neuromuscular junction") and twitches can become superimposed as a result of [summation](./Muscle_contraction#Gradation_of_skeletal_muscle_contractions "Muscle contraction") or a [tetanic contraction](./Tetanic_contraction "Tetanic contraction"). Individual twitches can become indistinguishable, and tension rises smoothly eventually reaching a plateau.[[5]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-Russell_2013_946-5)

Although the word "motor neuron" suggests that there is a single kind of neuron that controls movement, this is not the case. Indeed, upper and lower motor neurons—which differ greatly in their origins, synapse locations, routes, neurotransmitters, and lesion characteristics—are included in the same classification as "motor neurons". Essentially, motor neurons, also known as motoneurons, are made up of a variety of intricate, finely tuned circuits found throughout the body that innervate effector muscles and glands to enable both voluntary and involuntary motions. Two motor neurons come together to form a two-neuron circuit. While lower motor neurons start in the spinal cord and go to innervate muscles and glands all throughout the body, upper motor neurons originate in the cerebral cortex and travel to the brain stem or spinal cord. The distinctions between upper and lower motor neurons and the routes they follow are important factors in the effective detection of neuronal injuries and localisation of lesions.[[6]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-P-6)

Disorders causing selective injury to motor neurons are classified as [motor neuron diseases](./Motor_neuron_diseases "Motor neuron diseases").

## Development

A type of nerve cell that controls muscle movement by relaying signals from the spinal cord and brain

Motor neurons begin to develop early in [embryonic development](./Embryogenesis "Embryogenesis"), and motor function continues to develop well into childhood.[[7]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-7) In the [neural tube](./Neural_tube#Ventral-dorsal_patterning "Neural tube") cells are specified to either the rostral-caudal axis or ventral-dorsal axis. The [axons](./Axon "Axon") of motor neurons begin to appear in the fourth week of development from the ventral region of the ventral-dorsal axis (the [basal plate](./Basal_plate_(neural_tube) "Basal plate (neural tube)")).[[8]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-Sadler-8) This homeodomain is known as the motor neural progenitor domain (pMN). [Transcription factors](./Transcription_factor "Transcription factor") here include [Pax6](./PAX6 "PAX6"), [OLIG2](./OLIG2 "OLIG2"), [Nkx-6.1](./NKX6-1 "NKX6-1"), and [Nkx-6.2](./NKX6-2 "NKX6-2"), which are regulated by [sonic hedgehog](./Sonic_hedgehog "Sonic hedgehog") (Shh). The OLIG2 gene being the most important due to its role in promoting [Ngn2 expression](./Neurogenin#Motor_neuron_fate "Neurogenin"), a gene that causes cell cycle exiting as well as promoting further transcription factors associated with motor neuron development.[[9]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:1-9)

Further specification of motor neurons occurs when [retinoic acid](./Retinoic_acid "Retinoic acid"), [fibroblast growth factor](./Fibroblast_growth_factor "Fibroblast growth factor"), [Wnts](./Wnt_signaling_pathway "Wnt signaling pathway"), and [TGFb](./Transforming_growth_factor "Transforming growth factor"), are integrated into the various [Hox](./Hox_gene "Hox gene") transcription factors. There are 13 Hox transcription factors and along with the signals, determine whether a motor neuron will be more rostral or caudal in character. In the spinal column, Hox 4-11 sort motor neurons to one of the five motor columns.[[9]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:1-9)

### Upper motor neurons

[Upper motor neurons](./Upper_motor_neuron "Upper motor neuron") originate in the [motor cortex](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex") located in the [precentral gyrus](./Precentral_gyrus "Precentral gyrus"). The cells that make up the [primary motor cortex](./Primary_motor_cortex "Primary motor cortex") are [Betz cells](./Betz_cell "Betz cell"), which are giant [pyramidal cells](./Pyramidal_cell "Pyramidal cell"). The axons of these cells descend from the cortex to form the [corticospinal tract](./Corticospinal_tract "Corticospinal tract").[[12]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-12) [Corticomotorneurons](./Primary_motor_cortex#Corticomotorneurons "Primary motor cortex") project from the primary cortex directly onto motor neurons in the ventral horn of the spinal cord.[[13]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:02-13)[[14]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:12-14) Their axons synapse on the spinal motor neurons of multiple muscles as well as on spinal [interneurons](./Interneuron "Interneuron").[[13]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:02-13)[[14]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:12-14) They are unique to primates and it has been suggested that their function is the adaptive control of the [hands](./Hands "Hands") including the relatively independent control of individual fingers.[[14]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:12-14)[[15]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-15) Corticomotorneurons have so far only been found in the primary motor cortex and not in secondary motor areas.[[14]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:12-14)

### Nerve tracts

[Nerve tracts](./Nerve_tract "Nerve tract") are bundles of axons as [white matter](./White_matter "White matter"), that carry [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential") to their effectors. In the spinal cord these descending tracts carry impulses from different regions. These tracts also serve as the place of origin for lower motor neurons. There are seven major descending motor tracts to be found in the spinal cord:[[16]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:2-16)

- [Lateral corticospinal tract](./Lateral_corticospinal_tract "Lateral corticospinal tract")
- [Rubrospinal tract](./Rubrospinal_tract "Rubrospinal tract")
- [Lateral reticulospinal tract](./Reticular_formation#Medial_and_lateral_tracts "Reticular formation")
- [Vestibulospinal tract](./Vestibulospinal_tract "Vestibulospinal tract")
- [Medial reticulospinal tract](./Reticular_formation#Medial_and_lateral_tracts "Reticular formation")
- [Tectospinal tract](./Tectospinal_tract "Tectospinal tract")
- [Anterior corticospinal tract](./Anterior_corticospinal_tract "Anterior corticospinal tract")

### Lower motor neurons

[Lower motor neurons](./Lower_motor_neuron "Lower motor neuron") are those that originate in the spinal cord and directly or indirectly innervate effector targets. The target of these neurons varies, but in the somatic nervous system the target will be some sort of muscle fiber. There are three primary categories of lower motor neurons, which can be further divided in sub-categories.[[17]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-17)

According to their targets, motor neurons are classified into three broad categories:[[18]](./Motor_neuron#cite_note-:5-18)

- Somatic motor neurons
- Special visceral motor neurons
- General visceral motor neurons
