Proprioception

Sense of position of one's own body parts

Schematics and images of types of limb proprioceptor neurons in mammals (top) and insects (bottom)[[1]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:3-1)

**Proprioception** ([/ˌproʊpri.oʊˈsɛpʃən, -ə-/](./Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")[[2]](./Proprioception#cite_note-2)[[3]](./Proprioception#cite_note-3) [*PROH-pree-oh-SEP-shən, -⁠ə-*](./Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is the [sense](./Sense "Sense") of self-movement, force, and body position in [three-dimensional space](./Three-dimensional_space "Three-dimensional space").[[1]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:3-1)[[4]](./Proprioception#cite_note-4)

Proprioception is mediated by **proprioceptors**, [sensory receptors](./Sensory_receptor "Sensory receptor") located within [muscles](./Muscle "Muscle"), [tendons](./Tendon "Tendon"), [joints](./Joint "Joint")[[1]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:3-1) and the [vestibular organs](./Vestibular_system "Vestibular system"). Most animals possess multiple subtypes of proprioceptors, which detect distinct kinesthetic variables, such as position, movement, and force. Although all mobile animals possess proprioceptors, the structure of the sensory organs can vary across species.

Limb proprioceptive signals are transmitted to the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"), where they are integrated with information from other [sensory systems](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system"), such as [the visual system](./Visual_perception "Visual perception") and the [vestibular system](./Vestibular_system "Vestibular system"), to create an overall representation of body position, movement, and loading. In many animals, sensory feedback from proprioceptors is essential for stabilizing body posture and coordinating body movement.

## System overview

In vertebrates, limb position and velocity are encoded by [muscle spindle group Ia afferents](./Type_Ia_sensory_fiber "Type Ia sensory fiber") responding to muscle length and velocity and [muscle spindle group II afferents](./Type_II_sensory_fiber "Type II sensory fiber") responding mainly to muscle length. There is a similar division of encoding in invertebrates; different subgroups of neurons of the [chordotonal organ](./Chordotonal_organ "Chordotonal organ")[[5]](./Proprioception#cite_note-5) encode limb position and velocity.

Load on a limb is signaled by Golgi tendon organs:[[6]](./Proprioception#cite_note-6) type Ib afferents. These proprioceptors respond to the force a muscle is exerting. Similarly, invertebrates have a mechanism to determine limb load: the [campaniform sensilla](./Campaniform_sensilla "Campaniform sensilla").[[7]](./Proprioception#cite_note-7) These proprioceptors are active when a limb experiences resistance.[[1]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:3-1)

A third role for proprioceptors is to determine when a joint is at a specific position. In vertebrates, this is accomplished by [Ruffini endings](./Ruffini_ending "Ruffini ending") and [Pacinian corpuscles](./Pacinian_corpuscles "Pacinian corpuscles"). These proprioceptors are activated when the joint is at a threshold position, usually at the extremes of joint position. Invertebrates use [hair plates](./Hair_plate "Hair plate")[[8]](./Proprioception#cite_note-8) to accomplish this; a field of bristles located within joints that detects the relative movement of limb segments through the deflection of the associated cuticular hairs.

### Reflexes

The sense of proprioception is ubiquitous across mobile animals and is essential for the motor coordination of the body. Proprioceptive afferents form reflex loops with spinal alpha motor neurons to provide rapid feedback control of muscles. These [mechanosensation](./Mechanosensation "Mechanosensation") circuits are important for flexibly maintaining posture and balance, especially during locomotion. For example, consider the [stretch reflex](./Stretch_reflex "Stretch reflex"), in which stretch of a muscle is detected by proprioceptors (e.g., [muscle spindles](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle") in vertebrates, [chordotonal neurons](./Chordotonal_organ "Chordotonal organ") in invertebrates), which reflexly activate motoneurons to induce muscle contraction to oppose the stretch. During locomotion, inputs from Golgi tendon organs can reverse their reflex action on motoneurons to promote rather than oppose stretch-related orces.[[9]](./Proprioception#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Proprioception#cite_note-10)

### Conscious and nonconscious

In humans, a distinction is made between *conscious* proprioception and *nonconscious* proprioception:

- Conscious proprioception is communicated by the [dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway](./Dorsal_column-medial_lemniscus_pathway "Dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway") to the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum").[[11]](./Proprioception#cite_note-isbn0-7817-2829-0-11)
- Nonconscious proprioception is communicated primarily via the [dorsal spinocerebellar tract](./Dorsal_spinocerebellar_tract "Dorsal spinocerebellar tract")[[12]](./Proprioception#cite_note-titleChapter_7A:_Somatosensory_Systems-12) and [ventral spinocerebellar tract](./Ventral_spinocerebellar_tract "Ventral spinocerebellar tract"),[[13]](./Proprioception#cite_note-13) to the [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum").
- A nonconscious reaction is seen in the human proprioceptive reflex, or [righting reflex](./Righting_reflex "Righting reflex")—in the event that the body tilts in any direction, the person will cock their head back to level the eyes against the horizon.[[14]](./Proprioception#cite_note-14) This is seen even in infants as soon as they gain control of their neck muscles. This control comes from the [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum"), the part of the brain affecting balance.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Physiology

Proprioception is mediated by mechanically sensitive **proprioceptor neurons** distributed throughout an animal's body. Most vertebrates possess three basic types of proprioceptors: [muscle spindles](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle"), which are embedded in [skeletal muscles](./Skeletal_muscle "Skeletal muscle"), [Golgi tendon organs](./Golgi_tendon_organ "Golgi tendon organ"), which lie at the interface of muscles and tendons, and joint receptors, which are [low-threshold mechanoreceptors](./Mechanosensation#Cutaneous_Mechanoreceptors "Mechanosensation") embedded in [joint capsules](./Joint_capsule "Joint capsule"). Many invertebrates, such as insects, also possess three basic proprioceptor types with analogous functional properties: [chordotonal neurons](./Chordotonal_organ "Chordotonal organ"), [campaniform sensilla](./Campaniform_sensilla "Campaniform sensilla"), and [hair plates](./Hair_plate "Hair plate").[[1]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:3-1)

The initiation of proprioception is the activation of a proprioceptor in the periphery.[[15]](./Proprioception#cite_note-15) The proprioceptive sense is believed to be composed of information from [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") located in the [inner ear](./Labyrinth_(inner_ear) "Labyrinth (inner ear)") (motion and orientation) and in the [stretch receptors](./Stretch_receptor "Stretch receptor") located in the [muscles](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle") and the joint-supporting ligaments (stance). There are specific nerve receptors for this form of perception termed "proprioceptors", just as there are specific receptors for pressure, light, temperature, sound, and other sensory experiences.

Members of the [transient receptor potential](./Transient_receptor_potential "Transient receptor potential") family of [ion channels](./Ion_channel "Ion channel") have been found to be important for proprioception in [fruit flies](./Drosophila_melanogaster "Drosophila melanogaster"),[[16]](./Proprioception#cite_note-16) [nematode worms](./Caenorhabditis_elegans "Caenorhabditis elegans"),[[17]](./Proprioception#cite_note-17) [African clawed frogs](./African_clawed_frog "African clawed frog"),[[18]](./Proprioception#cite_note-18) and [zebrafish](./Zebrafish "Zebrafish").[[19]](./Proprioception#cite_note-19) [PIEZO2](./PIEZO2 "PIEZO2"), a nonselective cation channel, has been shown to underlie the mechanosensitivity of proprioceptors in mice.[[20]](./Proprioception#cite_note-20) Humans with loss-of-function mutations in the *PIEZO2* gene exhibit specific deficits in joint proprioception,[[a]](./Proprioception#cite_note-piezoChannels-22) as well as vibration and touch discrimination, suggesting that the PIEZO2 channel is essential for mechanosensitivity in some proprioceptors and low-threshold mechanoreceptors.[[22]](./Proprioception#cite_note-23)

Although it was known that finger kinesthesia relies on skin sensation, recent research has found that kinesthesia-based [haptic perception](./Haptic_perception "Haptic perception") relies strongly on the forces experienced during touch.[[23]](./Proprioception#cite_note-24) This research allows the creation of "virtual", illusory haptic shapes with different perceived qualities.[[24]](./Proprioception#cite_note-25)

### [Central pattern generators](./Central_pattern_generator "Central pattern generator")

[Central pattern generators](./Central_pattern_generator "Central pattern generator") are groups of neurons in the spinal cord that are responsible for generating stereotyped movement. It has been shown that in cats, rhythmic activation patterns are still observed following removal of sensory afferents and removal of the brain,[[25]](./Proprioception#cite_note-26) indicating that there is neural pattern generation in the spinal cord independent of descending signals from the brain and sensory information. It is currently understood that the spinal cord receives sensory input from proprioceptive organs and descending commands from the brain, integrates these signals, and sends activation signals to muscle through alpha motoneurons and fusimotor signals through gamma motoneurons in a coordinated and rhythmic fashion.

### Muscle spindles

The [muscle spindle](./Muscle_spindle "Muscle spindle") is a proprioceptive organ that lies embedded in the muscle. It is a 1-3mm long encapsulated, fusiform (spindle-shaped) structure comprising contractile intrafusal (inside the spindle) muscle fibers and a central non-contractile region. There are three types of intrafusal fiber: bag I, bag-II and chain, which correspond to the dynamic and static responses of the afferents they influence. Spindles relay information through primary (Group Ia) and secondary (Group II) sensory afferents. Primary afferents innervate nuclear bag and chain intrafusal muscle fibers in the central region and secondary afferents innervate nuclear chain fibers at the ends of the spindle.[[26]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:1a-27)[[27]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:2a-28)[[28]](./Proprioception#cite_note-ReferenceA-29) Spindles encode extrafusal muscle length, velocity and acceleration. Recent studies suggest that they respond to the force and yank (the first time-derivative of force) exerted on intrafusal muscle.[[27]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:2a-28)

Nonlinear features of muscle spindle responses to muscle stretch include initial bursts, history-dependence, and rate relaxation. Initial bursts occur at the onset of stretch and only last a very short time. History dependence refers to how the response of muscle spindles is affected by past stretch inputs. Rate relaxation refers to how the firing rate of muscle spindles decreases over time when held at a constant length.[[26]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:1a-27)[[27]](./Proprioception#cite_note-:2a-28)
