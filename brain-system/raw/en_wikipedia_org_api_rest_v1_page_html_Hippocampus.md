The **hippocampus** (pl.: **hippocampi**), also **hippocampus proper**, is a major component of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") of [humans](./Human "Human") and many other [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate"). In the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain") the hippocampus, the [dentate gyrus](./Dentate_gyrus "Dentate gyrus"), and the [subiculum](./Subiculum "Subiculum") are components of the [hippocampal formation](./Hippocampal_formation "Hippocampal formation") located in the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system").
The hippocampus plays important roles in the [consolidation](./Memory_consolidation "Memory consolidation") of information from [short-term memory](./Short-term_memory "Short-term memory") to [long-term memory](./Long-term_memory "Long-term memory"), and in [spatial memory](./Spatial_memory "Spatial memory") that enables [navigation](./Navigation#Navigation_in_spatial_cognition "Navigation"). In humans and other primates the hippocampus is located in the [archicortex](./Archicortex "Archicortex"), one of the three regions of [allocortex](./Allocortex "Allocortex"), in each [hemisphere](./Cerebral_hemisphere "Cerebral hemisphere") with direct neural projections to, and reciprocal indirect projections from the [neocortex](./Neocortex "Neocortex"). The hippocampus, as the [medial pallium](./Medial_pallium "Medial pallium"), is a structure found in all vertebrates.

In [Alzheimer's disease](./Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease") (and other forms of [dementia](./Dementia "Dementia")), the hippocampus is one of the first regions of the brain to be damaged; [short-term memory loss](./Short-term_memory "Short-term memory") and [disorientation](./Disorientation "Disorientation") are included among the early symptoms. Damage to the hippocampus can also result from oxygen starvation ([hypoxia](./Hypoxia_(medical) "Hypoxia (medical)")), [encephalitis](./Encephalitis "Encephalitis"), or [medial temporal lobe epilepsy](./Temporal_lobe_epilepsy#Mesial_temporal_lobe_epilepsy "Temporal lobe epilepsy"). People with extensive, bilateral hippocampal damage may experience [anterograde amnesia](./Anterograde_amnesia "Anterograde amnesia"): the inability to form and retain new [memories](./Memory "Memory").

Since different [neuronal cell types](./List_of_distinct_cell_types_in_the_adult_human_body#Nervous_system "List of distinct cell types in the adult human body") are neatly organized into layers in the hippocampus, it has frequently been used as a [model system](./Scientific_modelling "Scientific modelling") for studying [neurophysiology](./Neurophysiology "Neurophysiology"). The form of [neural plasticity](./Synaptic_plasticity "Synaptic plasticity") known as [long-term potentiation](./Long-term_potentiation "Long-term potentiation") (LTP) was initially discovered to occur in the hippocampus and has often been studied in this structure. LTP is widely believed to be one of the main neural mechanisms by which memories are stored in the brain.

Using [rodents](./Rodent "Rodent") as [model organisms](./Model_organism "Model organism"), the hippocampus has been studied extensively as part of a brain system responsible for spatial memory and navigation. Many [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") in the [rat](./Rattus_norvegicus "Rattus norvegicus") and [mouse](./Mus_musculus "Mus musculus") hippocampi respond as [place cells](./Place_cell "Place cell"): that is, they fire bursts of [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential") when the animal passes through a specific part of its environment. Hippocampal place cells interact extensively with [head direction cells](./Head_direction_cells "Head direction cells"), whose activity acts as an inertial compass, and conjecturally with [grid cells](./Grid_cells "Grid cells") in the neighboring [entorhinal cortex](./Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex").

## Name

The human hippocampus and [fornix](./Fornix_of_the_brain "Fornix of the brain") (left) compared with a [seahorse](./Seahorse "Seahorse") (right)[[1]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-1)

The earliest description of the ridge running along the floor of the [inferior horn](./Lateral_ventricles#Inferior_horn "Lateral ventricles") of the [lateral ventricle](./Lateral_ventricle "Lateral ventricle") comes from the Venetian anatomist [Julius Caesar Aranzi](./Julius_Caesar_Aranzi "Julius Caesar Aranzi") (1587), who likened it first to a [silkworm](./Bombyx_mori "Bombyx mori") and then to a [seahorse](./Seahorse "Seahorse") ([Latin](./Latin_language "Latin language") *hippocampus*, from [Greek](./Ancient_Greek "Ancient Greek") ἱππόκαμπος (latinised: *ìppókampos*) from ἵππος (*ippos*), 'horse' + κάμπος (*kámpos*), 'sea monster').[[2]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Tatu-2) The German anatomist [Johann Georg Duvernoy](./Johann_Georg_Duvernoy?action=edit&redlink=1 "Johann Georg Duvernoy") (1729), the first to illustrate the structure, also wavered between "seahorse" and "silkworm". "Ram's horn" was proposed by the Danish anatomist [Jacob Winsløw](./Jacob_B._Winslow "Jacob B. Winslow") in 1732; and a decade later his fellow Parisian, the surgeon de Garengeot, used *cornu Ammonis* – horn of [Amun](./Amun "Amun"),[[3]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Duv05-3) after the ancient Egyptian god who was often represented as having a ram's head.[[4]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Neurologia2014-4) *Ammon* is the Greek name for Amun.[[5]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-5)

The head region of the hippocampus is enlarged, and presents two or three rounded elevations or foot-like digitations, and hence it was named the **pes hippocampi** (*pes* meaning *foot*).[[6]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Anand-7) Later this part was described as *pes hippocampi major*, with an adjacent bulge in the [occipital horn of the lateral ventricle](./Lateral_ventricles#Structure "Lateral ventricles"), described as *pes hippocampi minor* later renamed as the [calcar avis](./Calcar_avis "Calcar avis").[[3]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Duv05-3)[[8]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-8) In 1786 [Félix Vicq-d'Azyr](./Félix_Vicq-d'Azyr "Félix Vicq-d'Azyr") published an authoritative description naming just the *hippocampus* but the term remained largely unused with no description of any function proposed until in the middle of the 20th century it was associated with memory.[[2]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Tatu-2)

[Mayer](./Johann_Christoph_Andreas_Mayer "Johann Christoph Andreas Mayer") mistakenly used the term [hippopotamus](./Hippopotamus "Hippopotamus") in 1779, and was followed by some other authors until [Karl Friedrich Burdach](./Karl_Friedrich_Burdach "Karl Friedrich Burdach") resolved this error in 1829. In 1861 the hippocampus minor became the center of a dispute over [human evolution](./Human_evolution "Human evolution") between [Thomas Henry Huxley](./Thomas_Henry_Huxley "Thomas Henry Huxley") and [Richard Owen](./Richard_Owen "Richard Owen"), satirized as the [Great Hippocampus Question](./Great_Hippocampus_Question "Great Hippocampus Question"). The term hippocampus minor fell from use in anatomy textbooks and was officially removed in the [Nomina Anatomica](./Nomina_Anatomica "Nomina Anatomica") of 1895.[[9]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-9) The modern term for the structure is just the hippocampus, with the term *cornu Ammonis* (that is, 'Ammon's horn') surviving in the names of the [hippocampal subfields](./Hippocampal_subfields "Hippocampal subfields") CA1–CA4.[[10]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Pang-10)[[11]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Oxford-11)[[12]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Colman-12)

## In the limbic system

Hippocampus in the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system")

The hippocampus is one of the structures of the [limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe"), first described by [Broca](./Paul_Broca "Paul Broca") in 1878, as the cortical areas that line the deep edge of the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex").[[13]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Purves2-13) The limbic lobe is the main component of the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system").[[14]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Roxo-14) The [cingulate gyrus](./Cingulate_gyrus "Cingulate gyrus"), and the [parahippocampal gyrus](./Parahippocampal_gyrus "Parahippocampal gyrus") are the two main parts of the described lobe, which had been largely associated with [olfaction](./Olfaction "Olfaction").[[13]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Purves2-13) Many studies later culminating in work by [Papez](./Papez "Papez"), and [MacLean](./Paul_D._MacLean "Paul D. MacLean"), the involvement of other interacting brain regions associated with [emotion](./Emotion "Emotion") was recognized.[[14]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Roxo-14) The hippocampus is anatomically connected to parts of the brain that are involved with emotional behavior, including the [septal area](./Septal_area#Connections "Septal area"), the [hypothalamic](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") [mammillary bodies](./Mammillary_bodies "Mammillary bodies"), and the [anterior nuclear complex in the thalamus](./Anterior_nuclei_of_thalamus "Anterior nuclei of thalamus"). MacLean proposed that the associated structures of the limbic lobe be included in what he termed as the *limbic system*.[[14]](./Hippocampus#cite_note-Roxo-14)
