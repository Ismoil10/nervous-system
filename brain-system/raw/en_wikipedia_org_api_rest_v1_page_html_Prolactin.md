**Prolactin** (**PRL**), also known as **lactotropin** and **mammotropin**, is a [protein](./Protein "Protein") best known for its role in enabling mammals to [produce milk](./Lactation "Lactation"). It is influential in over 300 separate processes in various vertebrates, including humans.[[5]](./Prolactin#cite_note-Bole-Feysot_1998-5) Prolactin is secreted from the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland") and plays an essential role in metabolism, regulation of the [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system") and pancreatic development.[[6]](./Prolactin#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Prolactin#cite_note-auto-7)

Discovered in non-human animals around 1930 by [Oscar Riddle](./Oscar_Riddle "Oscar Riddle")[[8]](./Prolactin#cite_note-Riddle_1935-8) and confirmed in humans in 1970 by [Henry Friesen](./Henry_Friesen "Henry Friesen"),[[9]](./Prolactin#cite_note-Friesen_1970-9) prolactin is a [peptide hormone](./Peptide_hormone "Peptide hormone"), encoded by the *PRL* [gene](./Gene "Gene").[[10]](./Prolactin#cite_note-pmid2567059-10)

In mammals, prolactin is associated with milk production; in fish it is thought to be related to the control of water and salt balance. Prolactin also acts in a [cytokine](./Cytokine "Cytokine")-like manner and as an important regulator of the immune system. It has important cell cycle-related functions as a growth-, differentiating- and anti-[apoptotic](./Apoptotic "Apoptotic") factor. As a growth factor, binding to cytokine-like receptors, it influences [hematopoiesis](./Hematopoiesis "Hematopoiesis") and [angiogenesis](./Angiogenesis "Angiogenesis") and is involved in the regulation of blood clotting through several pathways. The hormone acts in [endocrine](./Endocrine "Endocrine"), [autocrine](./Autocrine_signalling "Autocrine signalling"), and [paracrine](./Paracrine_signalling "Paracrine signalling") manners through the [prolactin receptor](./Prolactin_receptor "Prolactin receptor") and numerous [cytokine receptors](./Cytokine_receptor "Cytokine receptor").[[5]](./Prolactin#cite_note-Bole-Feysot_1998-5)

Pituitary prolactin secretion is regulated by endocrine neurons in the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"). The most important of these are the neurosecretory [tuberoinfundibulum](./Tuberoinfundibular_pathway "Tuberoinfundibular pathway") (TIDA) neurons of the [arcuate nucleus](./Arcuate_nucleus "Arcuate nucleus"
