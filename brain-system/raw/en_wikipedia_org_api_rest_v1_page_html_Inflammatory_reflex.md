The **inflammatory reflex** is a neural circuit that regulates the [immune response](./Immune_response "Immune response") to [injury](./Injury "Injury") and invasion. All reflexes have an [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") and [efferent](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") arc. The Inflammatory reflex has a sensory afferent arc, which is activated by [cytokines](./Cytokines "Cytokines") and a motor or efferent arc, which transmits action potentials in the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") to suppress [cytokine](./Cytokine "Cytokine") production. Increased signaling in the efferent arc inhibits inflammation and prevents organ damage.

It has also been shown that the brain can use this circuit to regulate the [immune response](./Immune_response "Immune response") and to extend the [immunological memory](./Immunological_memory "Immunological memory").[[1]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-2)

## Molecular mechanism

The molecular basis of cytokine-inhibiting signals requires the [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"), and the [Alpha-7 nicotinic receptor](./Alpha-7_nicotinic_receptor "Alpha-7 nicotinic receptor") receptor expressed on cytokine-producing cells.[[3]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-4) The release of [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine") in the [spleen](./Spleen "Spleen") suppresses the production of [TNF](./Tumor_necrosis_factor "Tumor necrosis factor") and other cytokines which causes damaging inflammation.[[5]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-5) Signaling in the efferent arc of the inflammatory reflex, termed the "[Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway](./Cholinergic_anti-inflammatory_pathway "Cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway")," provides a regulatory check on the [innate immune system](./Innate_immune_system "Innate immune system") response to invasion and injury. The action potentials that arise in the vagus nerve are transmitted to the spleen, where a subset of specialized [T cells](./T_cell "T cell") are activated to secrete acetylcholine. The net effect of the reflex is to prevent the damage caused by excessive cytokine production.[[6]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-6)

## Therapeutic potential

Evidence from experimental disease models of [arthritis](./Arthritis "Arthritis"), [colitis](./Colitis "Colitis"), [sepsis](./Sepsis "Sepsis"), [hemorrhagic shock](./Shock_(circulatory) "Shock (circulatory)"), and [congestive heart failure](./Congestive_heart_failure "Congestive heart failure") indicate that electrical stimulation of the vagus nerve can prevent or reverse these diseases.[[7]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-7) Some research suggests that it is possible to implant nerve stimulants to replace [anti-inflammatory drugs](./Anti-inflammatory_drugs "Anti-inflammatory drugs") that target cytokine activity (e.g. anti-TNF and anti-IL-1 antibodies).[[8]](./Inflammatory_reflex#cite_note-8)

[According to Dawn Steiner](https://nypost.com/2025/05/14/us-news/heres-how-a-bio-implant-stopped-long-island-womans-horrific-athritis-in-its-tracks-this-is-the-future/), a patient in [SetPoint Medical's RESET-RA clinical trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38475923/), she experienced significant relief from her rheumatoid arthritis symptoms thanks to a vitamin-sized device surgically implanted in her neck to stimulate her vagus nerve, which uses the inflammatory reflex to stop inflammation.
