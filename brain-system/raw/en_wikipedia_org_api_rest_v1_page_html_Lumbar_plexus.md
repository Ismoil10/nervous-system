The **lumbar plexus** is a web of nerves (a [nerve plexus](./Nerve_plexus "Nerve plexus")) in the [lumbar](./Lumbar "Lumbar") region of the body which forms part of the larger [lumbosacral plexus](./Lumbosacral_plexus "Lumbosacral plexus"). It is formed by the [divisions](./Ventral_ramus_of_spinal_nerve "Ventral ramus of spinal nerve") of the first four [lumbar nerves](./Lumbar_nerves "Lumbar nerves") (L1–L4) and from contributions of the [subcostal nerve](./Subcostal_nerve "Subcostal nerve") (T12), which is the last [thoracic nerve](./Thoracic_nerves "Thoracic nerves"). Additionally, the ventral rami of the fourth lumbar nerve pass communicating branches, the [lumbosacral trunk](./Lumbosacral_trunk "Lumbosacral trunk"), to the [sacral plexus](./Sacral_plexus "Sacral plexus"). The nerves of the lumbar plexus pass in front of the hip joint and mainly support the anterior part of the thigh.[[1]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-470-1)

The plexus is formed lateral to the [intervertebral foramina](./Intervertebral_foramina "Intervertebral foramina") and passes through [psoas major](./Psoas_major_muscle "Psoas major muscle"). Its smaller motor branches are distributed directly to psoas major, while the larger branches leave the muscle at various sites to run obliquely down through the pelvis to leave under the [inguinal ligament](./Inguinal_ligament "Inguinal ligament") with the exception of the [obturator nerve](./Obturator_nerve "Obturator nerve") which exits the pelvis through the [obturator foramen](./Obturator_foramen "Obturator foramen").[[1]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-470-1)

## Branches

The [iliohypogastric nerve](./Iliohypogastric_nerve "Iliohypogastric nerve") runs posterior to the [psoas major](./Psoas_major_muscle "Psoas major muscle") on its proximal lateral border to run laterally and obliquely on the anterior side of [quadratus lumborum](./Quadratus_lumborum_muscle "Quadratus lumborum muscle"). Lateral to this muscle, it pierces the [transversus abdominis](./Transversus_abdominis_muscle "Transversus abdominis muscle") to run above the [iliac crest](./Iliac_crest "Iliac crest") between that muscle and [abdominal internal oblique](./Abdominal_internal_oblique_muscle "Abdominal internal oblique muscle"). It gives off several motor branches to these muscles and a sensory branch to the skin of the lateral hip. Its terminal branch then runs parallel to the [inguinal ligament](./Inguinal_ligament "Inguinal ligament") to exit the [aponeurosis](./Aponeurosis "Aponeurosis") of the [abdominal external oblique](./Abdominal_external_oblique_muscle "Abdominal external oblique muscle") above the [external inguinal ring](./External_inguinal_ring "External inguinal ring") where it supplies the skin above the inguinal ligament (i.e. the [hypogastric region](./Hypogastrium "Hypogastrium")) with the [anterior cutaneous branch](./Anterior_cutaneous_branch_of_iliohypogastric_nerve "Anterior cutaneous branch of iliohypogastric nerve").[[2]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-472-2)

The [ilioinguinal nerve](./Ilioinguinal_nerve "Ilioinguinal nerve") closely follows the iliohypogastric nerve on the quadratus lumborum, but then passes below it to run at the level of the iliac crest. It pierces the lateral [abdominal wall](./Abdominal_wall "Abdominal wall") and runs medially at the level of the inguinal ligament where it supplies motor branches to both transversus abdominis and sensory branches through the external inguinal ring to the skin over the [pubic symphysis](./Pubic_symphysis "Pubic symphysis") and the lateral aspect of the [labia majora](./Labia_majora "Labia majora") or [scrotum](./Scrotum "Scrotum").[[2]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-472-2)

The [genitofemoral nerve](./Genitofemoral_nerve "Genitofemoral nerve") pierces psoas major anteriorly below the former two nerves to immediately split into two branches that run downward on the anterior side of the muscle. The lateral [femoral branch](./Lumboinguinal_nerve "Lumboinguinal nerve") is purely sensory. It pierces the [vascular lacuna](./Vascular_lacuna "Vascular lacuna") near the [saphenous hiatus](./Saphenous_opening "Saphenous opening") and supplies the skin below the inguinal ligament (i.e. proximal, lateral aspect of [femoral triangle](./Femoral_triangle "Femoral triangle")). The [genital branch](./Genital_branch_of_genitofemoral_nerve "Genital branch of genitofemoral nerve") differs in males and females. In males it runs in the [spermatic cord](./Spermatic_cord "Spermatic cord") and in females in the [inguinal canal](./Inguinal_canal "Inguinal canal") together with the [teres uteri ligament](./Round_ligament_of_uterus "Round ligament of uterus"). It then sends sensory branches to the scrotal skin in males and the labia majora in females. In males it supplies motor innervation to the [cremaster](./Cremaster_muscle "Cremaster muscle").[[2]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-472-2)

The [lateral cutaneous femoral nerve](./Lateral_cutaneous_nerve_of_thigh "Lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh") pierces psoas major on its lateral side and runs obliquely downward below the [iliac fascia](./Iliac_fascia "Iliac fascia"). Medial to the [anterior superior iliac spine](./Anterior_superior_iliac_spine "Anterior superior iliac spine") it leaves the pelvic area through the lateral [muscular lacuna](./Muscular_lacuna "Muscular lacuna") it enters the thigh by passing behind the lateral end of the inguinal ligament . In the thigh it briefly passes under the [fascia lata](./Fascia_lata "Fascia lata") before it breaches the fascia and supplies the skin of the anterior thigh.[[2]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-472-2)

The [obturator nerve](./Obturator_nerve "Obturator nerve") leaves the lumbar plexus and descends behind psoas major on it medial side, then follows the [linea terminalis](./Linea_terminalis "Linea terminalis") into the [lesser pelvis](./Lesser_pelvis "Lesser pelvis"), and finally leaves the pelvic area through the [obturator canal](./Obturator_canal "Obturator canal"). In the thigh, it sends motor branches to [obturator externus](./Obturator_externus_muscle "Obturator externus muscle") before dividing into an anterior and a posterior branch, both of which continues distally. These branches are separated by [adductor brevis](./Adductor_brevis_muscle "Adductor brevis muscle") and supply all thigh adductors with motor innervation: [pectineus](./Pectineus_muscle "Pectineus muscle"), [adductor longus](./Adductor_longus_muscle "Adductor longus muscle"), adductor brevis, [adductor magnus](./Adductor_magnus_muscle "Adductor magnus muscle"), [adductor minimus](./Adductor_minimus_muscle "Adductor minimus muscle"), and [gracilis](./Gracilis_muscle "Gracilis muscle"). The anterior branch contributes a terminal, sensory branch which passes along the anterior border of gracilis and supplies the skin on the medial, distal part of the thigh.[[3]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-474-3)

The [femoral nerve](./Femoral_nerve "Femoral nerve") is the largest and longest of the plexus' nerves. It gives motor innervation to [iliopsoas](./Iliopsoas_muscle "Iliopsoas muscle"), [pectineus](./Pectineus_muscle "Pectineus muscle"), [sartorius](./Sartorius_muscle "Sartorius muscle"), and [quadriceps femoris](./Quadriceps_femoris_muscle "Quadriceps femoris muscle"); and sensory innervation to the anterior thigh, posterior lower leg, and hindfoot. In the pelvic area, it runs in a groove between psoas major and iliacus giving off branches to both muscles, and exits the pelvis through the medial aspect of [muscular lacuna](./Muscular_lacuna "Muscular lacuna"). In the thigh it divides into numerous sensory and muscular branches and the [saphenous nerve](./Saphenous_nerve "Saphenous nerve"), its long sensory terminal branch which continues down to the foot.[[3]](./Lumbar_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-474-3)
