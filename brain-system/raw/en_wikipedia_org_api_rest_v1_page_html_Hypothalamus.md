The **hypothalamus** (pl.: **hypothalami**; from [Ancient Greek](./Ancient_Greek_language "Ancient Greek language") [ὑπό](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ὑπό#Ancient_Greek "wikt:ὑπό") *(*hupó*)* 'under' and [θάλαμος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/θάλαμος#Ancient_Greek "wikt:θάλαμος") *(*thálamos*)* 'chamber') is a small part of the [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") [brain](./Brain "Brain") that contains a number of [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") with a variety of functions. One of the most important functions is to link the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") to the [endocrine system](./Endocrine_system "Endocrine system") via the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"). The hypothalamus is located below the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") and is part of the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system").[[1]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-1) It forms the [basal](./Basal_(anatomy) "Basal (anatomy)") part of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon"). All vertebrate brains contain a hypothalamus.[[2]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-2) In humans, it is about the size of an [almond](./Almond#Nut "Almond").[[3]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-3)

The hypothalamus has the function of regulating certain [metabolic](./Metabolic "Metabolic") [processes](./Biological_process "Biological process") and other activities of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"). It [synthesizes](./Biosynthesis "Biosynthesis") and secretes certain [neurohormones](./Neurohormone "Neurohormone"), called [releasing hormones](./Releasing_hormone "Releasing hormone") or hypothalamic hormones, and these in turn stimulate or inhibit the secretion of [hormones](./Hormones "Hormones") from the pituitary gland. The hypothalamus controls [body temperature](./Thermoregulation "Thermoregulation"), [hunger](./Hunger_(physiology) "Hunger (physiology)"), important aspects of parenting and [maternal attachment behaviours](./Maternal_bond "Maternal bond"), [thirst](./Thirst "Thirst"),[[4]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-4) [fatigue](./Fatigue "Fatigue"), [sleep](./Sleep "Sleep"), [circadian rhythms](./Circadian_rhythm "Circadian rhythm"), and is important in certain social behaviors, such as sexual and aggressive behaviors.[[5]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-motta2009-6)

## Structure

The hypothalamus is divided into four regions (preoptic, supraoptic, tuberal, mammillary) in a parasagittal plane, indicating location anterior-posterior; and three zones (periventricular, intermediate, lateral) in the coronal plane, indicating location medial-lateral.[[7]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-7) Hypothalamic nuclei are located within these specific regions and zones.[[8]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-Singh2011-8) It is found in all vertebrate nervous systems. In mammals, [magnocellular neurosecretory cells](./Magnocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Magnocellular neurosecretory cell") in the [paraventricular nucleus](./Paraventricular_nucleus "Paraventricular nucleus") and the [supraoptic nucleus](./Supraoptic_nucleus "Supraoptic nucleus") of the hypothalamus produce [neurohypophysial hormones](./Neurohypophysial_hormone "Neurohypophysial hormone"), [oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin").[[9]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-Sukhov_1993-9) These hormones are released into the blood in the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary").[[10]](./Hypothalamus#cite_note-williams-10) Much smaller [parvocellular neurosecretory cells](./Parvocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Parvocellular neurosecretory cell"), neurons of the paraventricular nucleus, release [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") and other hormones into the [hypophyseal portal system](./Hypophyseal_portal_system "Hypophyseal portal system"), where these hormones diffuse to the [anterior pituitary](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Connections

Further information: [Lateral hypothalamus § Orexinergic projection system](./Lateral_hypothalamus#Orexinergic_projection_system "Lateral hypothalamus"), and [Tuberomammillary nucleus § Histaminergic outputs](./Tuberomammillary_nucleus#Histaminergic_outputs "Tuberomammillary nucleus")

The hypothalamus is highly interconnected with other parts of the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"), in particular the brainstem and its [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation"). As part of the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system"), it has connections to other limbic structures including the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala") and [septum](./Septum "Septum"), and is also connected with areas of the [autonomous nervous system](./Autonomous_nervous_system "Autonomous nervous system"). [*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
