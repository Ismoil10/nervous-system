Part of the brain, the **basal forebrain** structures are located in the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain") to the front of and below the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum"). They include the ventral basal ganglia (including [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and [ventral pallidum](./Ventral_pallidum "Ventral pallidum")), [nucleus basalis](./Nucleus_basalis "Nucleus basalis"), [diagonal band of Broca](./Diagonal_band_of_Broca "Diagonal band of Broca"), [substantia innominata](./Substantia_innominata "Substantia innominata"), and the [medial septal nucleus](./Medial_septal_nucleus "Medial septal nucleus"). These structures are important in the production of [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"), which is then distributed widely throughout the forebrain. The basal forebrain is considered to be the major source of [cholinergic](./Cholinergic "Cholinergic") innervation of the neocortex, hippocampus and amygdala[[1]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Goard-1) which is lost in patients with dementia.[[2]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-2) Although best known for its cholinergic projection neurons, non-cholinergic neurons, especially [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") neurons, are more numerous, at least in rodents.[[3]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-5) Non-cholinergic neurons projecting to the cortex have been found to act with the cholinergic neurons to dynamically modulate [wakefulness](./Wakefulness "Wakefulness") and cortical activity.[[6]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Lin-6)[[7]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-:0-7)

## Function

Activating [cholinergic](./Cholinergic "Cholinergic"), [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") or [glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic") neurons in the basal forebrain promotes wakefulness.[[8]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-9) Stimulating the basal forebrain gives rise to acetylcholine release, which induces [wakefulness](./Wakefulness "Wakefulness") and [REM sleep](./REM_sleep "REM sleep"), whereas inhibition of acetylcholine release in the basal forebrain by adenosine causes [slow wave sleep](./Slow_wave_sleep "Slow wave sleep").[[10]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-10) Cholinergic promotion of wakefulness depends on local cholinergic actions on non-cholinergic neurons.[[7]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-:0-7) The nucleus basalis gives widespread [cholinergic](./Cholinergic "Cholinergic") projections to the neocortex.[[11]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Gratwicke-11)[[1]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Goard-1) The nucleus basalis is an essential part of the neuromodulatory system that controls behaviour by regulating [arousal](./Arousal "Arousal") and [attention](./Attention "Attention").[[1]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Goard-1) The nucleus basalis is also seen to be a critical node in the memory circuit.[[12]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Lv-12)

The importance of non-cholinergic neurons in the basal forebrain structures has been shown in working together with the cholinergic neurons in a dynamically modulatory way. This is seen to play a significant role in cognitive functions.[[6]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Lin-6) Basal forebrain neurons which contain the calcium-binding protein, [parvalbumin](./Parvalbumin "Parvalbumin"), the majority of which are GABAergic, play important roles in controlling cortical gamma band (30–80 Hz) activity,[[13]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-14) vigilant attention,[[15]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-15) and arousals from sleep.[[16]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-16) A large GABAergic subpopulation distinct from parvalbumin neurons expresses the transcription factor [Npas1](./NPAS1 "NPAS1"), promotes wakefulness [[17]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-17) and regulates stress responsiveness.[[18]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-18) Glutamatergic neurons, expressing the [vesicular glutamate transporter 2](./Vesicular_glutamate_transporters "Vesicular glutamate transporters") (vGluT2), promote avoidance behavior and other defensive reactions to aversive stimuli.[[19]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-19)[[20]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-20)[[21]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-22)[[23]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-23) The basal forebrain is also important in olfaction. Glutamatergic basal forebrain neurons receive olfactory inputs,[[24]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-24) whereas cholinergic and GABAergic neurons project to the olfactory bulb and other olfactory areas and regulate processing of olfactory stimuli.[[25]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-25)[[26]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-26)[[27]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-27)

[Nitric oxide](./Nitric_oxide "Nitric oxide") production in the basal forebrain is both necessary and sufficient to produce [sleep](./Sleep "Sleep").[[28]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-28)

## Clinical significance

Acetylcholine affects the ability of brain cells to transmit information to one another, and also encourages [neuronal plasticity](./Neuronal_plasticity "Neuronal plasticity"), or learning. Thus, damage to the basal forebrain can reduce the amount of acetylcholine in the brain and impair learning. This may be one reason why basal forebrain damage can result in memory impairments such as [amnesia](./Amnesia "Amnesia") and [confabulation](./Confabulation "Confabulation"). One common cause of basal forebrain damage is an [aneurysm](./Aneurysm "Aneurysm") of the [anterior communicating artery](./Anterior_communicating_artery "Anterior communicating artery").[[29]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-29)

It is thought that damage to the nucleus basalis and its cortical projections are implicated in forms of [dementia](./Dementia "Dementia"), notably [Alzheimer's dementia](./Alzheimer's_dementia "Alzheimer's dementia") and [Parkinson's disease dementia](./Parkinson's_disease_dementia "Parkinson's disease dementia"). There have been studies on the use of [deep brain stimulation](./Deep_brain_stimulation "Deep brain stimulation") to the nucleus basalis, in the treatment of dementia, and while giving some positive results trials are still being undertaken.[[12]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Lv-12)[[11]](./Basal_forebrain#cite_note-Gratwicke-11)[*[may be outdated](./Portal:Current_events "Portal:Current events")*]
