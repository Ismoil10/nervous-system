The **peripheral nervous system** (**PNS**) is one of two components that make up the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") of [bilateral animals](./Bilateria "Bilateria"), with the other part being the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS). The PNS consists of [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve") and [ganglia](./Ganglia "Ganglia"), which lie outside the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[1]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-1) The main function of the PNS is to connect the CNS to the [limbs](./Limb_(anatomy) "Limb (anatomy)") and [organs](./Organ_(anatomy) "Organ (anatomy)"), essentially serving as a relay between the brain and spinal cord and the rest of the body.[[2]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-2) Unlike the CNS, the PNS is not protected by the [vertebral column](./Vertebral_column "Vertebral column") and [skull](./Skull "Skull"), which protect the CNS from physical injury or by the [blood–brain barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier"), which protects the CNS from blood-borne pathogens and [toxins](./Toxin "Toxin"), leaving the PNS more vulnerable than the CNS.[[3]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-3)

The peripheral nervous system can be divided into a [somatic division](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") and an [autonomic division](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"). Each of these can further be differentiated into a sensory and a motor sector.[[4]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-4) In the somatic nervous system, the [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve") are part of the PNS with the exceptions of the [olfactory nerve](./Olfactory_nerve "Olfactory nerve") and epithelia and the [optic nerve](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") (cranial nerve II) along with the [retina](./Retina "Retina"), which are considered parts of the central nervous system based on developmental origin. The second cranial nerve is not a true peripheral nerve but a tract of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon").[[5]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-5) [Cranial nerve ganglia](./Cranial_nerve_ganglia "Cranial nerve ganglia"), as with all [ganglia](./Ganglion "Ganglion"), are part of the PNS.[[6]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-White2008-6) The autonomic nervous system exerts involuntary control over [smooth muscle](./Smooth_muscle "Smooth muscle") and [glands](./Gland "Gland").[[7]](./Peripheral_nervous_system#cite_note-7)

## Structure

The peripheral nervous system can be divided into a somatic and an autonomic division, which are part of the [somatic nervous system](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") and the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"), respectively. The somatic nervous system is under voluntary control, and transmits signals from the brain to end organs such as [muscles](./Muscle "Muscle"). The [sensory nervous system](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system") is part of the somatic nervous system and transmits signals from senses such as [taste](./Taste "Taste") and touch (including fine touch and gross touch) to the spinal cord and brain. The autonomic nervous system is a "self-regulating" system which influences the function of organs outside voluntary control, such as the [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), or the functions of the [digestive system](./Digestion "Digestion").

The [somatic nervous system](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") includes the [sensory nervous system](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system") (ex. the [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system")) and consists of sensory nerves and somatic nerves, and many nerves which hold both functions.

In the [head](./Human_head "Human head") and [neck](./Neck "Neck"), [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve") carry somatosensory data. There are twelve cranial nerves, ten of which originate from the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem"), and mainly control the functions of the anatomic structures of the head with some exceptions. One unique cranial nerve is the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve"), which receives sensory information from organs in the [thorax](./Thorax "Thorax") and [abdomen](./Abdomen "Abdomen"). The other unique cranial nerve is the [accessory nerve](./Accessory_nerve "Accessory nerve") which is responsible for innervating the [sternocleidomastoid](./Sternocleidomastoid_muscle "Sternocleidomastoid muscle") and [trapezius muscles](./Trapezius_muscle "Trapezius muscle"), neither of which are located exclusively in the head.

For the rest of the body, [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") are responsible for somatosensory information. These arise from the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). Usually these arise as a web ("plexus") of interconnected nerves roots that arrange to form single nerves. These nerves control the functions of the rest of the body. In humans, there are 31 pairs of spinal nerves: 8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, and 1 coccygeal. These nerve roots are named according to the spinal vertebrata which they are adjacent to. In the cervical region, the spinal nerve roots come out *above* the corresponding vertebrae (i.e., nerve root between the skull and 1st cervical vertebrae is called spinal nerve C1). From the thoracic region to the coccygeal region, the spinal nerve roots come out *below* the corresponding vertebrae. This method creates a problem when naming the spinal nerve root between C7 and T1 (so it is called spinal nerve root C8). In the lumbar and sacral region, the spinal nerve roots travel within the dural sac and they travel below the level of L2 as the cauda equina.

#### Cervical spinal nerves (C1–C4)

Further information: [Cervical plexus](./Cervical_plexus "Cervical plexus")

The first 4 cervical spinal nerves, C1 through C4, split and recombine to produce a variety of nerves that serve the neck and back of head.

Spinal nerve C1 is called the [suboccipital nerve](./Suboccipital_nerve "Suboccipital nerve"), which provides motor innervation to muscles at the base of the [skull](./Skull "Skull").
C2 and C3 form many of the nerves of the neck, providing both sensory and motor control. These include the [greater occipital nerve](./Greater_occipital_nerve "Greater occipital nerve"), which provides sensation to the back of the head, the [lesser occipital nerve](./Lesser_occipital_nerve "Lesser occipital nerve"), which provides sensation to the area behind the [ears](./Ear "Ear"), the [greater auricular nerve](./Greater_auricular_nerve "Greater auricular nerve") and the [lesser auricular nerve](./Lesser_auricular_nerve "Lesser auricular nerve").

The [phrenic nerve](./Phrenic_nerve "Phrenic nerve") is a nerve essential for our survival which arises from nerve roots C3, C4 and C5. It supplies the [thoracic diaphragm](./Thoracic_diaphragm "Thoracic diaphragm"), enabling [breathing](./Breathing "Breathing"). If the spinal cord is transected above C3, then spontaneous breathing is not possible.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

#### Brachial plexus (C5–T1)

Further information: [Brachial plexus](./Brachial_plexus "Brachial plexus")

The last four cervical spinal nerves, C5 through C8, and the first thoracic spinal nerve, T1, combine to form the [brachial plexus](./Brachial_plexus "Brachial plexus"), or [plexus brachialis](./Plexus_brachialis "Plexus brachialis"), a tangled array of nerves, splitting, combining and recombining, to form the nerves that subserve the upper-limb and upper back. Although the brachial plexus may appear tangled, it is highly organized and predictable, with little variation between people. See [brachial plexus injuries](./Brachial_plexus_injuries "Brachial plexus injuries").

#### Lumbosacral plexus (L1–Co1)

The [anterior](./Anterior "Anterior") divisions of the [lumbar nerves](./Lumbar_nerves "Lumbar nerves"), [sacral nerves](./Sacral_nerves "Sacral nerves"), and [coccygeal nerve](./Coccygeal_nerve "Coccygeal nerve") form the [lumbosacral plexus](./Lumbosacral_plexus "Lumbosacral plexus"), the first lumbar nerve being frequently joined by a branch from the [twelfth thoracic](./Twelfth_thoracic "Twelfth thoracic").
