# Reward system

Reward system

Group of neural structures responsible for motivation and desire

Examples of primary rewards.[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1) Clockwise from top left: water, food, parental care, and affection.

The **reward system** (the mesocorticolimbic circuit) is a group of neural structures responsible for [incentive salience](./Incentive_salience "Incentive salience") (i.e., "wanting"; desire or craving for a reward and [motivation](./Motivation "Motivation")), [associative learning](./Associative_learning "Associative learning") (primarily [positive reinforcement](./Positive_reinforcement "Positive reinforcement") and [classical conditioning](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning")), and [positively-valenced](./Valence_(psychology) "Valence (psychology)") [emotions](./Emotion "Emotion"), particularly ones involving [pleasure](./Pleasure "Pleasure") as a core component (e.g., [joy](./Joy "Joy"), [euphoria](./Euphoria "Euphoria") and [ecstasy](./Ecstasy_(emotion) "Ecstasy (emotion)")).[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2)[[3]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Pleasure_system-3) Reward is the attractive and motivational property of a stimulus that induces appetitive behavior, also known as approach behavior, and consummatory behavior.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2) A rewarding stimulus has been described as "any stimulus, object, event, activity, or situation that has the potential to make us approach and consume it".[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2) In [operant conditioning](./Operant_conditioning "Operant conditioning"), rewarding stimuli function as [positive reinforcers](./Positive_reinforcer "Positive reinforcer");[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1) however, the converse statement also holds true: positive reinforcers are rewarding.[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1)[[4]](./Reward_system#cite_note-4) The reward system motivates animals to approach stimuli or engage in behaviour that increases fitness (sex, energy-dense foods, etc.). Survival for most animal species depends upon maximizing contact with beneficial stimuli and minimizing contact with harmful stimuli. Reward cognition serves to increase the likelihood of survival and reproduction by causing associative learning, eliciting approach and consummatory behavior, and triggering [positively-valenced](./Valence_(psychology) "Valence (psychology)") emotions.[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1) Thus, reward is a mechanism that [evolved](./Evolution "Evolution") to help increase the adaptive fitness of animals.[[5]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Behavioral_neuroscience_textbook-5) In drug [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction"), certain substances over-activate the reward circuit, leading to compulsive substance-seeking behavior resulting from synaptic plasticity in the circuit.[[6]](./Reward_system#cite_note-6)

Primary rewards are a class of rewarding stimuli which facilitate the survival of one's self and offspring, and they include homeostatic (e.g., [palatable food](./Palatability "Palatability")) and reproductive (e.g., [sexual contact](./Sexual_intercourse "Sexual intercourse") and [parental investment](./Parental_investment "Parental investment")) rewards.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2)[[7]](./Reward_system#cite_note-7) Intrinsic rewards are unconditioned rewards that are attractive and motivate behavior because they are inherently pleasurable.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2) Extrinsic rewards (e.g., money or seeing one's favorite sports team winning a game) are conditioned rewards that are attractive and motivate behavior but are not inherently pleasurable.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2)[[8]](./Reward_system#cite_note-8) Extrinsic rewards derive their motivational value as a result of a [learned association](./Associative_learning "Associative learning") (i.e., conditioning) with intrinsic rewards.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2) Extrinsic rewards may also elicit pleasure (e.g., euphoria from winning a lot of money in a lottery) after being [classically conditioned](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning") with intrinsic rewards.[[2]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid26109341-2)

## Definition

In neuroscience, the reward system is a collection of brain structures and neural pathways that are responsible for reward-related cognition, including [associative learning](./Associative_learning "Associative learning") (primarily [classical conditioning](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning") and [operant reinforcement](./Operant_reinforcement "Operant reinforcement")), [incentive salience](./Incentive_salience "Incentive salience") (i.e., motivation and "wanting", desire, or craving for a reward), and [positively-valenced](./Valence_(psychology) "Valence (psychology)") [emotions](./Emotion "Emotion"), particularly emotions that involve [pleasure](./Pleasure "Pleasure") (i.e., hedonic "liking").[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1)[[3]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Pleasure_system-3)

Reward related activities, such as feeding, exercise, sex, substance use, and social interactions play a factor in elevated levels of dopamine, ultimately altering the CNS (or the central nervous system). Dopamine is the chemical messenger that plays a role in regulating mood, motivation, reward, and pleasure.[[9]](./Reward_system#cite_note-9)

Terms that are commonly used to describe behavior related to the "wanting" or desire component of reward include appetitive behavior, approach behavior, preparatory behavior, instrumental behavior, anticipatory behavior, and seeking.[[10]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid23141060-10) Terms that are commonly used to describe behavior related to the "liking" or pleasure component of reward include consummatory behavior and taking behavior.[[10]](./Reward_system#cite_note-pmid23141060-10)

The three primary functions of rewards are their capacity to:

1. produce [associative learning](./Associative_learning "Associative learning") (i.e., [classical conditioning](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning") and [operant reinforcement](./Operant_reinforcement "Operant reinforcement"));[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1)
2. affect decision-making and induce approach behavior (via the assignment of [motivational salience](./Motivational_salience "Motivational salience") to rewarding stimuli);[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1)
3. elicit [positively-valenced](./Valence_(psychology) "Valence (psychology)") emotions, particularly pleasure.[[1]](./Reward_system#cite_note-Schultz-1)
