Schematic illustration of the structure of the sympathoadrenal system. Beginning in the sympathetic nervous system, an external stimulus affects the adrenal medulla and causes a release of catecholamines.

The **sympathoadrenal system** is a physiological connection between the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla") and is crucial in an organism's physiological response to outside stimuli.[[1]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:0-1) When the body receives sensory information, the sympathetic nervous system sends a signal to [preganglionic nerve fibers](./Preganglionic_nerve_fibers "Preganglionic nerve fibers"), which activate the adrenal medulla through [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"). Once activated, [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") and [epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine") are released directly into the blood by adrenomedullary cells where they act as the bodily mechanism for "[fight-or-flight](./Fight-or-flight_response "Fight-or-flight response")" responses. Because of this, the sympathoadrenal system plays a large role in maintaining glucose levels, sodium levels, blood pressure, and various other metabolic pathways that couple with bodily responses to the environment.[[1]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:0-1) During numerous diseased states, such as [hypoglycemia](./Hypoglycemia "Hypoglycemia") or even stress, the body's metabolic processes are skewed. The sympathoadrenal system works to return the body to [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") through the activation or inactivation of the [adrenal gland](./Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland"). However, more severe disorders of the sympathoadrenal system such as [pheochromocytoma](./Pheochromocytoma "Pheochromocytoma") (a tumor on the adrenal medulla) can affect the body's ability to maintain a homeostatic state. In these cases, curative agents such as [adrenergic agonists](./Adrenergic_agonist "Adrenergic agonist") and [antagonists](./Adrenergic_antagonist "Adrenergic antagonist") are used to modify epinephrine and norepinephrine levels released by the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla").[[2]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-2)

## Function

The normal function of the sympathoadrenal system is to help the body regulate responses to environmental stimuli. These stimuli travel through the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") by means of [preganglionic nerve fibers](./Preganglionic_nerve_fibers "Preganglionic nerve fibers") that emerge from the [thoracic spinal cord](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve").[[3]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-3) Electrical impulses carried by the sympathetic nervous system are converted to a chemical response in the adrenal gland. [Chromaffin cells](./Chromaffin_cell "Chromaffin cell") contained in the adrenal medulla act as [postganglionic nerve fibers](./Postganglionic_nerve_fibers "Postganglionic nerve fibers") that release this chemical response into the blood as a circulating messenger. The sympathoadrenal system can activate and discharge chemical messengers as a single unit to activate an organism's “fight or flight” response. This “sympathoadrenal discharge” causes an increase in heart rate, cardiac output, blood pressure, triglyceride and glucose levels. These sympathoadrenal functions show the combined responses of the central nervous system on a multitude of external stimuli.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Chemical messengers

The two main chemical messengers of the sympathoadrenal system are [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") and [epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine") (also called noradrenaline and adrenaline respectively). These chemicals are created by the [adrenal glands](./Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland") after receiving neuronal signals from the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"). The different physiological effects of these chemicals depend on the particular tissue that it innervates. As part of the sympathoadrenal system, these chemicals act rapidly and dispel quickly as opposed to the longer-lasting effect of [hormones](./Hormone "Hormone").[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Stress

Schematic illustration of the sympathoadrenal response to stress.

In the brain, reception of a signal for a stressor by the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") leads to an increase in activity of the sympathoadrenal system, essentially within the nerves that send signals to the [adrenal glands](./Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland"). This is done through the activation by the [corticotropin-releasing factor](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") (CRF), also known as the corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH).[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4) Increased activity of the adrenal nerves is done through the receptors for the corticotropin-releasing factor within the ganglia within the sympathetic nervous system.[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4) Corticotropin-releasing factors travel to the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"), where they activate the release of [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") (ACTH). The release of the adrenocorticotropic hormone is determined by the release of the corticotropin-releasing factor as the interruption of the corticotropin-releasing factor causes a weakening of the adrenocorticotropic hormone response.[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4)

Adrenocorticotropic hormones bind to ACTH receptors on the cells within the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla") and [adrenal cortex](./Adrenal_cortex "Adrenal cortex"), causing a signal cascade within the adrenomedullary cell, ultimately releasing catecholamines like epinephrine and norepinephrine.[[5]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-5) Concomitantly, adrenocortical cells secrete corticosteroids. These hormones (i.e., catecholamines and corticosteroids) affect a variety of organs like skeletal muscles along with the muscles surrounding certain bodily systems such as the [cardiovascular system](./Circulatory_system "Circulatory system") and [respiratory system](./Respiratory_system "Respiratory system"), causing an increase in force production by the skeletal muscles along with accelerated heart rate and breathing rate. [Glucocorticoids](./Glucocorticoid "Glucocorticoid") also are in effect during times of stress for the sympathoadrenal system, but provide an inhibitory function for the protection of the body from its own immune system. The glucocorticoids work to inhibit reactions produced from the [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system") during times of stress that could cause damage within the body.[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4) Glucocorticoids work to inhibit the uptake of catecholamines, like [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") and [epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine"), by the nerves.[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4) The increase in activity of synthesis of norepinephrine and epinephrine within the medulla is done from glucocorticoids through the increase in reaction rate of certain enzymes, such as: [tyrosine hydroxylase](./Tyrosine_hydroxylase "Tyrosine hydroxylase"), [aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase](./Aromatic_L-amino_acid_decarboxylase "Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase"), [dopamine-β-hydroxylase](./Dopamine_beta-hydroxylase "Dopamine beta-hydroxylase"), and [phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase](./Phenylethanolamine_N-methyltransferase "Phenylethanolamine N-methyltransferase").[[4]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:1-4)

## Hypertension and obesity

The release of [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") is usually regulated within the sympathoadrenal system as it is tasked with maintenance of [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis"); however, there are certain cases in which the levels of [adrenocorticotropic hormones](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") may be in excess, causing [hypertension](./Hypertension "Hypertension"), or even [Cushing's syndrome](./Cushing's_syndrome "Cushing's syndrome"). Hypertension, or high blood pressure, has a multitude of possible causes, one of which being the elevated levels of ACTH.[[6]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:2-6) Hypertension also causes an increase in [catecholamine](./Catecholamine "Catecholamine") release during experiments of stress-induced situations.[[7]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-7) While hypertension and Cushing's syndrome are not correlational, roughly 80% of individuals diagnosed with Cushing's syndrome also have hypertension.[[6]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:2-6) Both Cushing's syndrome, termed Cushing's disease in this case, and hypertension involve the excess production and release of [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone").[[6]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:2-6) Hypertension can also be caused by the overproduction of molecules released from the sympathoadrenal system besides ACTH, such as [mineralocorticoids](./Mineralocorticoid "Mineralocorticoid") and [glucocorticoids](./Glucocorticoid "Glucocorticoid").[[8]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:3-8) Overproduction of these molecules causes an increase in the production and release of the catecholamines, leading the cardiovascular system to become elevated in the [systolic blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure") and the [diastolic blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure"), along with the increase in the heart rate of the individual.[[8]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:3-8)

Weight gain can be accomplished through the ingestion of and storage of carbohydrates and fat. Under normal conditions, adrenal hormone receptors, type I and type II, mediate the storage of carbohydrates and fats during eating.[[9]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:4-9) In some cases, obesity in individuals is due to the overproduction of corticoids leads to the over-activation of receptor type I and type II, causing the deposition of fat and the storage of carbohydrates, respectively; furthermore, activation of either receptor causes the individual to sustain eating.[[9]](./Sympathoadrenal_system#cite_note-:4-9)

## Exercise and Metabolism

During exercise, the body undergoes an acute stress response in which more oxygen and energy is needed for physical activity. The stress induced during exercise results in an increase in the hormones, [epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine") and [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine"), which are known for the body's "fight or flight" response.
