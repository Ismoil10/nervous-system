# Mesolimbic pathway

Mesolimbic pathway

Brain pathway



"Reward center" redirects here. For the reward system, which contains this pathway, see [reward system](./Reward_system "Reward system").

The **mesolimbic pathway**, sometimes referred to as the **reward pathway**, is a [dopaminergic pathway](./Dopaminergic_pathway "Dopaminergic pathway") in the [brain](./Brain "Brain").[[1]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-pmid21205279-1) The pathway connects the [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmentum "Ventral tegmentum") in the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") to the [ventral striatum](./Ventral_striatum "Ventral striatum") of the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia") in the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain"). The ventral striatum includes the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and the [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle").[[2]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Dopaminergic_pathways_and_reward_system_review-2)

The release of dopamine from the mesolimbic pathway into the nucleus accumbens regulates [incentive salience](./Incentive_salience "Incentive salience") (e.g. motivation and desire for [rewarding stimuli](./Rewarding_stimuli "Rewarding stimuli")) and facilitates [reinforcement](./Reinforcement "Reinforcement") and reward-related motor function learning;[[3]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-NAcc_function-3)[[4]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Malenka_NAcc-4)[[5]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pleasure_system-5) it may also play a role in the subjective perception of [pleasure](./Pleasure "Pleasure").[[3]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-NAcc_function-3)[[5]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pleasure_system-5) The dysregulation of the mesolimbic pathway and its output neurons in the nucleus accumbens plays a significant role in the development and maintenance of an [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction").[[1]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-pmid21205279-1)[[6]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Nestler2-6)[[7]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-ΔFosB_reward2-7)[[8]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Natural_and_drug_addictions2-8)

## Anatomy

The mesolimbic pathway and its positioning in relation to the other [dopaminergic pathways](./Dopaminergic_pathways "Dopaminergic pathways")

The mesolimbic pathway is a collection of dopaminergic (i.e., [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine")-releasing) neurons that project from the [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area") (VTA) to the [ventral striatum](./Ventral_striatum "Ventral striatum"), which includes the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") (NAcc) and [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle").[[9]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Dopaminergic_pathways_and_reward_system_review2-9) It is one of the component pathways of the [medial forebrain bundle](./Medial_forebrain_bundle "Medial forebrain bundle"), which is a set of neural pathways that mediate [brain stimulation reward](./Brain_stimulation_reward "Brain stimulation reward").[[10]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-pmid117207862-10)

The VTA is located in the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") and consists of dopaminergic, [GABAergic](./GABA_receptor "GABA receptor"), and [glutamatergic](./Glutamic_acid "Glutamic acid") [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron").[[11]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pierce2-11) The dopaminergic neurons in this region receive stimuli from both [cholinergic neurons](./Cholinergic_neurons "Cholinergic neurons") in the [pedunculopontine nucleus](./Pedunculopontine_nucleus "Pedunculopontine nucleus") and the [laterodorsal tegmental nucleus](./Laterodorsal_tegmental_nucleus "Laterodorsal tegmental nucleus") as well as glutamatergic neurons in other regions such as the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"). The nucleus accumbens and olfactory tubercle are located in the [ventral striatum](./Ventral_striatum "Ventral striatum") and are primarily composed of [medium spiny neurons](./Medium_spiny_neuron "Medium spiny neuron").[[9]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Dopaminergic_pathways_and_reward_system_review2-9)[[12]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Zhang2-12)[[13]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Purves2-13) The nucleus accumbens is subdivided into limbic and motor subregions known as the [NAcc shell](./Nucleus_accumbens_shell "Nucleus accumbens shell") and [NAcc core](./Nucleus_accumbens_core "Nucleus accumbens core").[[11]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pierce2-11) The medium spiny neurons in the nucleus accumbens receive input from both the dopaminergic neurons of the VTA and the glutamatergic neurons of the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), and [medial prefrontal cortex](./Medial_prefrontal_cortex "Medial prefrontal cortex"). When they are activated by these inputs, the medium spiny neurons' projections release GABA onto the [ventral pallidum](./Ventral_pallidum "Ventral pallidum").[[11]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pierce2-11)

## Function

The mesolimbic pathway regulates [incentive salience](./Incentive_salience "Incentive salience"), motivation, reinforcement learning, and fear, among other cognitive processes.[[14]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-NHM_pathways2-14)[[15]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-ADHD_2008_paper2-15)[[16]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-16)

The mesolimbic pathway is involved in [motivational](./Motivation "Motivation") cognition. Depletion of dopamine in this pathway, or lesions at its site of origin, decrease the extent to which an animal is willing to go to obtain a reward (e.g. the number of lever presses for intravenous nicotine delivery in rats or time spent searching for food). Dopaminergic drugs are also able to increase the extent an animal is willing to go to obtain a reward. Moreover, the firing rate of neurons in the mesolimbic pathway increases during anticipation of reward, which may explain craving.[[17]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-17) Mesolimbic dopamine release was once thought to be the primary mediator of pleasure, but is now believed to have only a minor or secondary role in pleasure perception.[[18]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Pleasure_system2-18)[[19]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-19)

## Clinical significance

### Mechanisms of addiction

The mesolimbic pathway and a specific set of the pathway's output neurons (e.g. [D1-type](./D1-type "D1-type") [medium spiny neurons](./Medium_spiny_neurons "Medium spiny neurons") within the nucleus accumbens) play a central role in the [neurobiology](./Neurobiology "Neurobiology") of [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction").[[20]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Nestler22-20)[[21]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-ΔFosB_reward22-21)[[22]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Natural_and_drug_addictions22-22) Drug addiction is an illness caused by habitual substance use that induces chemical changes in the brain's circuitry.[[23]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-23) An addictive drug is defined as a substance that affects the mesolimbic system directly or indirectly by increasing extracellular levels of dopamine.[[24]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-:0-24)

Common addictive substances such as [cocaine](./Cocaine "Cocaine"), [alcohol](./Alcohol_(drug) "Alcohol (drug)"), and [nicotine](./Nicotine "Nicotine") have been shown to increase extracellular levels of dopamine within the mesolimbic pathway, preferentially within the nucleus accumbens. The mechanisms by which these drugs do so vary depending on the drug prototype. For example, cocaine precludes the re-uptake of synaptic dopamine through blocking the presynaptic [dopamine transporter](./Dopamine_transporter "Dopamine transporter"). Another stimulant, [amphetamine](./Amphetamine "Amphetamine"), reverses the dopamine transporter and induces the release of dopamine from synaptic vesicles. Non-stimulant drugs typically bind with [ligand-gated channels](./Ligand-gated_ion_channel "Ligand-gated ion channel") or [G protein-coupled receptors](./G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor"). Such drugs include alcohol, nicotine, and [tetrahydrocannabinol](./Tetrahydrocannabinol "Tetrahydrocannabinol") (THC).[[25]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-25)

These dopaminergic activations of the mesolimbic pathway are accompanied by the perception of reward. Repeated exposure to the drug can lead to lasting changes in the brain that gives rise to addictive behavior.[[27]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-27)[[28]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-28)

### Relation to other neurological and psychological disorders

The mesolimbic pathway is implicated in [schizophrenia](./Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia"), [depression](./Major_depressive_disorder "Major depressive disorder"),[[29]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Van2-29)[[30]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Laviolette2-30)[[31]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Diaz2-31) and [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease").[[32]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-32)[[33]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-33) It is also theorized to be implicated in [overuse of digital media](./Digital_media_use_and_mental_health "Digital media use and mental health"), although it could simply be a consequence of a [sedentary lifestyle](./Sedentary_lifestyle "Sedentary lifestyle").[[34]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-34) Each involves distinct structural changes within the mesolimbic pathway.[[29]](./Mesolimbic_pathway#cite_note-Van2-29)
