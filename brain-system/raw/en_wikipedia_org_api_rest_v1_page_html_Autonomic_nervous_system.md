The **autonomic nervous system** (**ANS**),[[1]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-1) sometimes called the **visceral nervous system** and formerly the **vegetative nervous system**, is a division of the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") that operates [internal organs](./Viscera "Viscera"), [smooth muscle](./Smooth_muscle "Smooth muscle") and [glands](./Gland "Gland").[[2]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-Dorlands-2) The autonomic nervous system is a control system that acts largely unconsciously and regulates bodily functions, such as the [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), its [force of contraction](./Myocardial_contractility "Myocardial contractility"), [digestion](./Digestion "Digestion"), [respiratory rate](./Respiratory_rate "Respiratory rate"), [pupillary response](./Pupillary_dilation "Pupillary dilation"), [urination](./Micturition "Micturition"), and [sexual arousal](./Animal_sexual_behaviour "Animal sexual behaviour").[[3]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-Human_Physiology_-_Janig-3)
The autonomic nervous system has historically been branched into three divisions based on which organs and functions are being controlled by each system. This includes the [enteric nervous system](./Enteric_nervous_system "Enteric nervous system"), which regulates the gastrointestinal tract, the [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") and the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), which are responsible for, among other things, stimulation of the "rest and digest" and the [fight-or-flight response](./Fight-or-flight_response "Fight-or-flight response") (or "acute stress response"), respectively.[[4]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-chu-5) In literature, the acronyms ENS, PSNS and SNS or SANS(where the A stands for autonomic) may be used to abbreviate the aforementioned terms.

ANS is regulated by integrated [reflexes](./Reflex "Reflex") through the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") to the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") and [organs](./Organ_(anatomy) "Organ (anatomy)"). These functions include [control of respiration](./Control_of_respiration "Control of respiration"), [cardiac regulation](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), [vasomotor](./Vasomotor "Vasomotor") activity, and certain [reflex actions](./Reflex "Reflex") such as [coughing](./Coughing "Coughing"), [sneezing](./Sneezing "Sneezing"), [swallowing](./Swallowing "Swallowing") and [vomiting](./Vomiting "Vomiting"). Those are then subdivided into other areas and are also linked to autonomic subsystems and the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system"). The [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), just above the [brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem"), acts as an integrator for autonomic functions, receiving autonomic [regulatory](./Regulatory "Regulatory") input from the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system").[[6]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-MacArthur-6)
The [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") plays a key role in the functioning of ANS, being the longest nerve of this system, and with both sensory and motor fibers, it helps with control over unconscious functions.

Although conflicting reports about its subdivisions exist in the literature, the autonomic nervous system has historically been considered a purely [motor system](./Motor_system "Motor system"), and has been divided into the aforementioned three branches of ENS, PSNS and SANS.[[7]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-Jänig08-7): 13[[8]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-9) The enteric nervous system however is a less recognized part of the autonomic nervous system.[[10]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-10) When it comes to the parasympathetic(calm, digesting, mating) amd the sympathetic(acute stress), in many cases, the two of these systems have complementary actions where one system activates a physiological response and the other inhibits it. An older simplification of the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems as "excitatory" and "inhibitory" was overturned due to the many exceptions found. A more modern characterization is that the sympathetic nervous system is a "quick response mobilizing system" and the parasympathetic is a "more slowly activated [dampening](./Dampening "Dampening") system", but even this has exceptions, such as in [sexual arousal](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal") and [orgasm](./Orgasm "Orgasm"), wherein both play a role.[[6]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-MacArthur-6)

There are [inhibitory](./Inhibitory "Inhibitory") and [excitatory](./Excitatory "Excitatory") [synapses](./Synapses "Synapses") between [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron"). A third subsystem of neurons has been named as [non-noradrenergic, non-cholinergic transmitters](./Non-noradrenergic,_non-cholinergic_transmitter "Non-noradrenergic, non-cholinergic transmitter") because they use other transmitters such as [nitric oxide](./Nitric_oxide "Nitric oxide") as a [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter"). These functions are integral in autonomic function, in particular in the [gut](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract") and the [lungs](./Lung "Lung").[[11]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-Belvisi-11)

Although the ANS is also known as the visceral nervous system and although most of its fibers carry non-somatic information to the CNS, many authors still consider it only connected with the motor side.[[12]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-brsphys-12) Most autonomous functions are involuntary but they can often work in conjunction with the [somatic nervous system](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") which provides voluntary control. Overall, the ANS ensures the maintenance of vital functions and allows the body to effectively adapt to cycles of stress and recovery.

## Structure

Autonomic nervous system, showing [splanchnic nerves](./Splanchnic_nerve "Splanchnic nerve") in middle, and the vagus nerve as "X" in blue. The heart and organs below in list to right are regarded as viscera.

The autonomic nervous system has been classically divided into the [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") and [enteric nervous systems](./Enteric_nervous_system "Enteric nervous system").[[7]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-Jänig08-7): 168[[13]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-13) More modern classifications recognize other networks that are integral to different organs, such as the [intrinsic cardiac nervous system](./Intrinsic_cardiac_nervous_system "Intrinsic cardiac nervous system").[[14]](./Autonomic_nervous_system#cite_note-14)

The sympathetic division emerges from the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") in the [thoracic](./Thoracic_vertebrae "Thoracic vertebrae") and [lumbar](./Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae") areas, terminating around L2-3. The parasympathetic division has craniosacral "outflow", meaning that the [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") begin at the [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve") (specifically the [oculomotor nerve](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve"), [facial nerve](./Facial_nerve "Facial nerve"), [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve") and [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve")) and [sacral](./Sacral_vertebrae "Sacral vertebrae") (S2-S4) spinal cord. These divisions are distinctive because they require a sequential two-neuron efferent pathway; the [preganglionic neuron](./Preganglionic_neuron "Preganglionic neuron") must [first synapse](./Synapse "Synapse") onto a [postganglionic neuron](./Postganglionic_neuron "Postganglionic neuron") before [innervating](./Innervation "Innervation") the target organ. The preganglionic, or first, neuron will have its nerve cell body in the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") and will synapse at the postganglionic, or second, neuron's cell body. The postganglionic neuron will then form junctions within the target organ.

### Sympathetic division

Main article: [Sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system")

The sympathetic nervous system consists of cells with bodies in the [lateral grey column](./Lateral_grey_column "Lateral grey column") from T1 to L2/3. These cell bodies are ["GVE" (general visceral efferent) neurons](./General_visceral_efferent_fibers "General visceral efferent fibers") and are the preganglionic neurons. There are several locations upon which [preganglionic neurons](./Preganglionic_neurons "Preganglionic neurons") can synapse for their [postganglionic neurons](./Postganglionic_neurons "Postganglionic neurons"):

- [paravertebral ganglia](./Paravertebral_ganglia "Paravertebral ganglia") (3) of the sympathetic chain (these run on either side of the vertebral bodies)

1. [cervical ganglia](./Cervical_ganglia "Cervical ganglia") (3)
2. [thoracic ganglia](./Thoracic_ganglia "Thoracic ganglia") (12) and rostral [lumbar ganglia](./Lumbar_ganglia "Lumbar ganglia") (2 or 3)
3. caudal lumbar ganglia and [sacral ganglia](./Sacral_ganglia "Sacral ganglia")

- [prevertebral ganglia](./Prevertebral_ganglia "Prevertebral ganglia") (celiac ganglion, aorticorenal ganglion, superior mesenteric ganglion, inferior mesenteric ganglion)
- [chromaffin cells](./Chromaffin_cells "Chromaffin cells") of the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla") (this is the one exception to the two-neuron pathway rule: the synapse is directly efferent onto the target cell bodies)

These ganglia provide the postganglionic neurons from which innervation of target organs follows. Examples of [splanchnic (visceral) nerves](./Splanchnic_nerves "Splanchnic nerves") are:

- cervical cardiac nerves and thoracic visceral nerves, which synapse in the [sympathetic chain](./Sympathetic_chain "Sympathetic chain")
- [thoracic splanchnic nerves](./Thoracic_splanchnic_nerves "Thoracic splanchnic nerves") (greater, lesser, least), which synapse in the prevertebral ganglia
- [lumbar splanchnic nerves](./Lumbar_splanchnic_nerves "Lumbar splanchnic nerves"), which synapse in the prevertebral ganglia
- [sacral splanchnic nerves](./Sacral_splanchnic_nerves "Sacral splanchnic nerves"), which synapse in the inferior hypogastric plexus

These all contain afferent (sensory) nerves as well, known as [GVA (general visceral afferent) neurons](./General_visceral_afferent_fibers "General visceral afferent fibers").
