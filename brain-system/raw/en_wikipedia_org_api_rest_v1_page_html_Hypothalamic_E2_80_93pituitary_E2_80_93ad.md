# Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis

Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis

Set of physiological feedback interactions



Schematic of the HPA axis (CRH, [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone"); ACTH, [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone"))



Hypothalamus, pituitary gland, and adrenal cortex

The **hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis** (**HPA axis**) is a neuroendocrine axis that controls the secretion of [corticosteroid](./Corticosteroid "Corticosteroid") stress hormones. The HPA axis has three components: the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") (a part of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") located below the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus")), the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland") (a pea-shaped structure located below the hypothalamus), and the [adrenal](./Adrenal "Adrenal") (also called "suprarenal") [glands](./Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland") (small, [conical](./Conical "Conical") organs on top of the [kidneys](./Kidneys "Kidneys")). These structures and their interactions constitute the HPA axis.

The HPA axis is a major [neuroendocrine system](./Neuroendocrine_system "Neuroendocrine system")[[1]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-NHM-Neuroendocrine_systems-1) that controls reactions to [stress](./Stress_(medicine) "Stress (medicine)") and regulates many body processes, including [digestion](./Digestion "Digestion"), [immune responses](./Immune_response "Immune response"), [mood](./Mood_(psychology) "Mood (psychology)") and [emotions](./Emotion "Emotion"), [sexual activity](./Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity"), and energy storage and expenditure. It is the common mechanism for interactions among [glands](./Gland "Gland"), [hormones](./Hormone "Hormone"), and parts of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") that mediate the [general adaptation syndrome](./General_adaptation_syndrome "General adaptation syndrome") (GAS).[[2]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-2)

While [steroid](./Steroid "Steroid") [hormones](./Hormone "Hormone") are produced mainly in [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate"), the [physiological](./Physiological "Physiological") role of the HPA axis and [corticosteroids](./Corticosteroid "Corticosteroid") in stress response is so fundamental that [analogous](./Analogous "Analogous") systems can be found in [invertebrates](./Invertebrate "Invertebrate") and [monocellular](./Unicellular_organism "Unicellular organism") organisms as well.

The HPA axis, [hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal_axis "Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis"), [hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid (HPT) axis](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid_axis "Hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis"), and the [hypothalamic–neurohypophyseal system](./Hypothalamic–neurohypophyseal_system "Hypothalamic–neurohypophyseal system") are the four major [neuroendocrine](./Neuroendocrine_cell "Neuroendocrine cell") systems through which the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") and [pituitary](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland") direct [neuroendocrine](./Neuroendocrine_cell "Neuroendocrine cell") function.[[1]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-NHM-Neuroendocrine_systems-1)

## Anatomy

The key elements of the HPA axis are:[[3]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-3)

- The [paraventricular nucleus](./Paraventricular_nucleus "Paraventricular nucleus") of the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"): It contains [neuroendocrine](./Neuroendocrine "Neuroendocrine") [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") which synthesize and secrete [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") and [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") (CRH).
- The [anterior lobe](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary") of the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"): CRH and vasopressin stimulate the anterior lobe of pituitary gland to secrete [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") (ACTH), once known as [corticotropin](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone").
- The [adrenal cortex](./Adrenal_cortex "Adrenal cortex"): It produces [glucocorticoid](./Glucocorticoid "Glucocorticoid") hormones (mainly [cortisol](./Cortisol "Cortisol") in humans) in response to stimulation by ACTH. Glucocorticoids in turn, act back on the hypothalamus and pituitary (to suppress CRH and ACTH production) in a [negative feedback](./Negative_feedback "Negative feedback") cycle[[4]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-4).

[CRH](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") are released from [neurosecretory](./Neurosecretion "Neurosecretion") nerve terminals at the [median eminence](./Median_eminence "Median eminence"). CRH is transported to the anterior pituitary through the [portal blood vessel system](./Portal_venous_system "Portal venous system") of the [hypophyseal stalk](./Hypophysial_stalk "Hypophysial stalk") and vasopressin is transported by [axonal](./Axon "Axon") transport to the [posterior pituitary gland](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary"). There, CRH and vasopressin act synergistically to stimulate the secretion of stored ACTH from corticotrope cells. ACTH is transported by the [blood](./Blood "Blood") to the [adrenal cortex](./Adrenal_cortex "Adrenal cortex") of the [adrenal gland](./Adrenal_gland "Adrenal gland"), where it rapidly stimulates the biosynthesis of [corticosteroids](./Corticosteroid "Corticosteroid") such as [cortisol](./Cortisol "Cortisol") from [cholesterol](./Cholesterol "Cholesterol"). Cortisol is a major stress hormone and has effects on many tissues in the body, including the brain. In the brain, cortisol acts on two types of receptors: [mineralocorticoid receptors](./Mineralocorticoid_receptor "Mineralocorticoid receptor") and [glucocorticoid](./Glucocorticoid "Glucocorticoid") receptors, and these are expressed by many different types of neurons. One important target of glucocorticoids is the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), which is a major controlling centre of the HPA axis.[[5]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-5)

Vasopressin can be thought of as "water conservation hormone" and is also known as "[antidiuretic](./Antidiuretic "Antidiuretic") hormone(ADH)". It is released when the body is [dehydrated](./Dehydration "Dehydration") and has potent water-conserving effects on the kidney. It is also a potent [vasoconstrictor](./Vasoconstrictor "Vasoconstrictor").[[6]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-6)

Important to the function of the HPA axis are some of the following feedback loops:

- [Cortisol](./Cortisol "Cortisol") produced in the adrenal cortex will negatively feedback to inhibit both the hypothalamus and the pituitary gland. This reduces the [secretion](./Secretion "Secretion") of CRH and vasopressin, and also directly reduces the cleavage of [proopiomelanocortin](./Proopiomelanocortin "Proopiomelanocortin") (POMC) into ACTH and β-endorphins.
- [Epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine") and [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") (E/NE) are produced by the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla") through [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") stimulation and the local effects of cortisol (upregulation enzymes to make E/NE). E/NE will positively feedback to the pituitary and increase the breakdown of POMCs into ACTH and β-endorphins.

## Function

Release of [corticotropin-releasing hormone](./Corticotropin-releasing_hormone "Corticotropin-releasing hormone") (CRH) from the hypothalamus is influenced by [stress](./Stress_(medicine) "Stress (medicine)"), physical activity, illness, by blood levels of cortisol and by the sleep/wake cycle ([circadian rhythm](./Circadian_rhythm "Circadian rhythm")). In healthy individuals, cortisol rises rapidly after wakening, reaching a peak within 30–45 minutes. It then gradually falls over the day, reaching a trough during the middle of the night. This corresponds to the rest-activity cycle of the organism.[[7]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-isbn_9780444530400-7) An abnormally flattened circadian cortisol cycle has been linked with [chronic fatigue syndrome](./Chronic_fatigue_syndrome "Chronic fatigue syndrome"),[[8]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-8) [insomnia](./Insomnia "Insomnia")[[9]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-9) and [burnout](./Occupational_burnout "Occupational burnout").[[10]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-10)

The HPA axis has a central role in regulating many [homeostatic systems](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") in the body, including the [metabolic system](./Metabolic_system "Metabolic system"), [cardiovascular system](./Cardiovascular_system "Cardiovascular system"), [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system"), [reproductive system](./Reproductive_system "Reproductive system") and [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"). The HPA axis integrates physical and [psychosocial](./Psychosocial "Psychosocial") influences in order to allow an organism to adapt effectively to its environment, use resources, and optimize survival.[[7]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-isbn_9780444530400-7)

Anatomical connections between brain areas such as the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex") and hypothalamus facilitate activation of the HPA axis.[[11]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-11) Sensory information arriving at the lateral aspect of the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala") is processed and conveyed to the amygdala's [central nucleus](./Central_nucleus "Central nucleus"), which then projects out to several parts of the brain involved in responses to fear. At the hypothalamus, fear-signaling impulses activate both the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and the modulating systems of the HPA axis.

Increased production of cortisol during stress results in an increased availability of [glucose](./Glucose "Glucose") in order to facilitate [fighting or fleeing](./Fight-or-flight_response "Fight-or-flight response"). As well as directly increasing glucose availability, cortisol also suppresses the highly demanding metabolic processes of the [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system"), resulting in further availability of glucose.[[7]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-isbn_9780444530400-7)

Glucocorticoids have many important functions, including modulation of stress reactions, but in excess they can be damaging. [Atrophy](./Atrophy "Atrophy") of the hippocampus in humans and animals exposed to severe stress is believed to be caused by prolonged exposure to high concentrations of [glucocorticoids](./Glucocorticoid "Glucocorticoid"). Deficiencies of the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") may reduce the memory resources available to help a body formulate appropriate reactions to stress.[[12]](./Hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal_axis#cite_note-12)

## Immune system

There is bi-directional communication and feedback between the HPA axis and the [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system").
