The **basal ganglia** (**BG**) or **basal nuclei** are a group of [subcortical](./Subcortical "Subcortical") [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") (cluster of cell bodies) found in the [brains](./Brain "Brain") of [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate"). Positioned at the base of the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain") and the top of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"), they have strong connections with the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"), [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") and other brain areas. The basal ganglia are associated with a variety of functions, including regulating voluntary [motor movements](./Motor_control "Motor control"), [procedural learning](./Procedural_memory "Procedural memory"), [habit formation](./Habituation "Habituation"), [conditional learning](./Conditional_learning "Conditional learning"),[[1]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-1) [eye movements](./Eye_movement "Eye movement"), [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition"),[[2]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-a1-2) and [emotion](./Emotion "Emotion").[[3]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-rapid-3)

The main functional components of the basal ganglia include the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum"), consisting of both the [dorsal striatum](./Dorsal_striatum "Dorsal striatum") ([caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") and [putamen](./Putamen "Putamen")) and the [ventral striatum](./Ventral_striatum "Ventral striatum") ([nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle")), the [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus"), the [ventral pallidum](./Ventral_pallidum "Ventral pallidum"), the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra"), and the [subthalamic nucleus](./Subthalamic_nucleus "Subthalamic nucleus").[[4]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-brs-4) Each of these components has complex internal anatomical and neurochemical structures. The largest component, the striatum (dorsal and ventral), receives input from various brain areas but only sends output to other components of the basal ganglia. The globus pallidus receives input from the striatum and sends inhibitory output to a number of motor-related areas. The substantia nigra is the source of the striatal input of the [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine"), which plays an important role in basal ganglia function. The subthalamic nucleus mainly receives input from the striatum and cerebral cortex and projects to the globus pallidus.

The basal ganglia are thought to play a key role in [action selection](./Action_selection "Action selection"), aiding in the choice of behaviors to execute. More specifically, they regulate motor and premotor cortical areas, facilitating smooth voluntary movements.[[2]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-a1-2)[[5]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-5) Experimental studies show that the basal ganglia exert an inhibitory influence on a number of [motor systems](./Motor_control "Motor control"), and that a release of this inhibition permits a motor system to become active. The "behavior switching" that takes place within the basal ganglia is influenced by signals from many parts of the brain, including the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"), which plays a key role in [executive functions](./Executive_functions "Executive functions").[[3]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-rapid-3)[[6]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-6) It has also been hypothesized that the basal ganglia are not only responsible for motor action selection, but also for the selection of more cognitive actions.[[7]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-9) Computational models of action selection in the basal ganglia incorporate this.[[10]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-10)

The basal ganglia are of major importance for normal brain function and behaviour. Their dysfunction results in a wide range of [neurological conditions](./Neurological_disorder "Neurological disorder") including disorders of behaviour control and movement, as well as cognitive deficits that are similar to those that result from damage to the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex").[[11]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-11) Those of behaviour include [Tourette syndrome](./Tourette_syndrome "Tourette syndrome"), [obsessive–compulsive disorder](./Obsessive–compulsive_disorder "Obsessive–compulsive disorder"), and [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction"). [Movement disorders](./Movement_disorders "Movement disorders") include, most notably [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"), which involves degeneration of the dopamine-producing cells in the substantia nigra; [Huntington's disease](./Huntington's_disease "Huntington's disease"), which primarily involves damage to the striatum;[[2]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-a1-2)[[4]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-brs-4) [dystonia](./Dystonia "Dystonia"); and more rarely [hemiballismus](./Hemiballismus "Hemiballismus"). The basal ganglia have a [limbic](./Limbic_system "Limbic system") sector whose components are assigned distinct names: the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens"), [ventral pallidum](./Ventral_pallidum "Ventral pallidum"), and [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area") (VTA). There is considerable evidence that this limbic part plays a central role in [reward learning](./Reinforcement "Reinforcement") as well as [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition") and [frontal lobe](./Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe") functioning, via the [mesolimbic pathway](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway") from the VTA to the nucleus accumbens that uses the neurotransmitter dopamine, and the [mesocortical pathway](./Mesocortical_pathway "Mesocortical pathway"). A number of highly addictive drugs, including [cocaine](./Cocaine "Cocaine"), [amphetamine](./Amphetamine "Amphetamine"), and [nicotine](./Nicotine "Nicotine"), are thought to work by increasing the efficacy of this dopamine signal. There is also evidence implicating overactivity of the VTA dopaminergic projection in [schizophrenia](./Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia").[[12]](./Basal_ganglia#cite_note-12)

## Structure

In terms of the [development of the nervous system in humans](./Development_of_the_nervous_system_in_humans "Development of the nervous system in humans"), the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") is often classified based on the original three primitive [brain vesicles](./Brain_vesicle "Brain vesicle"): These primary vesicles form in the normal development of the [neural tube](./Neural_tube "Neural tube") of the [embryo](./Embryo "Embryo") and initially include the [prosencephalon](./Prosencephalon "Prosencephalon"), [mesencephalon](./Mesencephalon "Mesencephalon"), and [rhombencephalon](./Rhombencephalon "Rhombencephalon"), in rostral to caudal (from head to tail) orientation. Later in development each section itself turns into smaller components.
