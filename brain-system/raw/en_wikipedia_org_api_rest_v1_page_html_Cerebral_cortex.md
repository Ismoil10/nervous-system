The **cerebral cortex**, also known as the **cerebral mantle**,[[1]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-free-1) is the outer layer of [neural tissue](./Neural_tissue "Neural tissue") of the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") in [humans](./Humans "Humans") and other [mammals](./Mammal "Mammal"). It is the largest site of [neural integration](./Neuron "Neuron") in the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"),[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2) and plays a key role in [attention](./Attention "Attention"), [perception](./Perception "Perception"), [awareness](./Awareness "Awareness"), [thought](./Thought "Thought"), [memory](./Memory "Memory"), [language](./Language "Language"), and [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness").

The six-layered [neocortex](./Neocortex "Neocortex") makes up approximately 90% of the [cortex](./Cortex_(anatomy) "Cortex (anatomy)"), with the [allocortex](./Allocortex "Allocortex") making up the remainder.[[3]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Strominger-3) The cortex is divided into left and right parts by the [longitudinal fissure](./Longitudinal_fissure "Longitudinal fissure"), which separates the two [cerebral hemispheres](./Cerebral_hemisphere "Cerebral hemisphere") that are joined beneath the cortex by the [corpus callosum](./Corpus_callosum "Corpus callosum") and other [commissural fibers](./Commissural_fibers "Commissural fibers"). In most mammals, the cerebral cortex is folded, providing a greater surface area in the confined volume of the [cranium](./Neurocranium "Neurocranium"). Apart from minimising brain and cranial volume, [cortical folding](./Gyrification "Gyrification") is crucial for the [brain circuitry](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit") and its functional organisation.[[4]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Shipp_2007-4) In mammals with small brains, there is no folding and the cortex is smooth.[[5]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-embo-5)[[6]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-ReferenceA-6)

A fold or ridge in the cortex is termed a [gyrus](./Gyrus "Gyrus") (plural gyri) and a groove is termed a [sulcus](./Sulcus_(neuroanatomy) "Sulcus (neuroanatomy)") (plural sulci). These surface convolutions appear during [fetal development](./Prenatal_development "Prenatal development") and continue to mature after birth through the process of [gyrification](./Gyrification "Gyrification"). In the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain"), the majority of the cerebral cortex is not visible from the outside, but buried in the sulci.[[7]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Principles_of_Neural_Science-7) The major sulci and gyri mark the divisions of the cerebrum into the [lobes of the brain](./Lobes_of_the_brain "Lobes of the brain"). The four major lobes are the [frontal](./Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe"), [parietal](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe"), [occipital](./Occipital_lobe "Occipital lobe") and [temporal](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe") lobes. Other lobes are the [limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe"), and the [insular cortex](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") often referred to as the *insular lobe*.

There are between 14 and 16 billion [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") in the human cerebral cortex.[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2) These are organised into horizontal cortical layers, and radially into [cortical columns](./Cortical_column "Cortical column") and [minicolumns](./Minicolumn "Minicolumn"). Cortical areas have specific functions such as movement in the [motor cortex](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex"), and sight in the [visual cortex](./Visual_cortex "Visual cortex"). The motor cortex is primarily located in the [precentral gyrus](./Primary_motor_cortex "Primary motor cortex"), and the visual cortex is located in the occipital lobe.

## Structure

Lateral view of cerebrum showing several cortices

The cerebral cortex is the outer covering of the surfaces of the cerebral hemispheres and is folded into peaks called [gyri](./Gyrus "Gyrus"), and grooves called [sulci](./Sulcus_(neuroanatomy) "Sulcus (neuroanatomy)"). In the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain"), it is between 2 and 3–4 mm. thick,[[8]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Roberts-8) and makes up 40% of the brain's mass.[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2) 90% of the cerebral cortex is the six-layered [neocortex](./Neocortex "Neocortex") whilst the other 10% is made up of the three/four-layered [allocortex](./Allocortex "Allocortex").[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2) There are between 14 and 16 billion neurons in the cortex.[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2) These cortical neurons are organized radially in [cortical columns](./Cortical_column "Cortical column"), and [minicolumns](./Cortical_minicolumn "Cortical minicolumn"), in the horizontally organized layers of the cortex.[[9]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Lodato-9)[[10]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Ansen-Wilson-10)

The neocortex is separable into different regions of cortex known in the plural as cortices, and include the [motor cortex](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex") and [visual cortex](./Visual_cortex "Visual cortex"). About two thirds of the cortical surface is buried in the sulci and the [insular cortex](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") is completely hidden. The cortex is thickest over the top of a gyrus and thinnest at the bottom of a sulcus.[[11]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Carpenter-11)

### Folds

Further information: [Gyrification](./Gyrification "Gyrification")

The cerebral cortex is folded in a way that allows a large surface area of [neural tissue](./Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") to fit within the confines of the [neurocranium](./Neurocranium "Neurocranium"). When unfolded in the human, each [hemispheric](./Cerebral_hemisphere "Cerebral hemisphere") cortex has a total surface area of about 0.12 square metres (1.3 sq ft).[[12]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-12) The folding is inward away from the surface of the brain, and is also present on the medial surface of each hemisphere within the [longitudinal fissure](./Longitudinal_fissure "Longitudinal fissure"). Most mammals have a cerebral cortex that is convoluted with the peaks known as gyri and the troughs or grooves known as sulci. Some small mammals including some small [rodents](./Rodent "Rodent") have smooth cerebral surfaces without [gyrification](./Gyrification "Gyrification").[[6]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-ReferenceA-6)

### Lobes

See also: [Lobes of the brain](./Lobes_of_the_brain "Lobes of the brain")

The larger sulci and gyri mark the divisions of the cortex of the cerebrum into the [lobes of the brain](./Lobes_of_the_brain "Lobes of the brain").[[8]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Roberts-8) There are four main lobes: the [frontal lobe](./Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe"), [parietal lobe](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe"), [temporal lobe](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe"), and [occipital lobe](./Occipital_lobe "Occipital lobe"). The [insular cortex](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") is often included as the insular lobe.[[13]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Nieuwenhuys-13) The [limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe") is a rim of cortex on the medial side of each hemisphere and is also often included.[[14]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Wiley-14) There are also three lobules of the brain described: the [paracentral lobule](./Paracentral_lobule "Paracentral lobule"), the [superior parietal lobule](./Superior_parietal_lobule "Superior parietal lobule"), and the [inferior parietal lobule](./Inferior_parietal_lobule "Inferior parietal lobule").

### Thickness

For species of mammals, larger brains (in absolute terms, not just in relation to body size) tend to have thicker cortices.[[15]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-CNSVert-15) The smallest mammals, such as [shrews](./Shrew "Shrew"), have a neocortical thickness of about 0.5 mm; the ones with the largest brains, such as humans and fin whales, have thicknesses of 2–4 mm.[[2]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Saladin-2)[[8]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-Roberts-8) There is an approximately [logarithmic](./Logarithm "Logarithm") relationship between brain weight and cortical thickness.[[15]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-CNSVert-15)
[Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain](./Magnetic_resonance_imaging_of_the_brain "Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain") (MRI) makes it possible to get a measure for the thickness of the human cerebral cortex and relate it to other measures. The thickness of different cortical areas varies but in general, sensory cortex is thinner than motor cortex.[[16]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-16) One study has found some positive association between the cortical thickness and [intelligence](./Intelligence "Intelligence").[[17]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-17)
Another study has found that the [somatosensory cortex](./Somatosensory_cortex "Somatosensory cortex") is thicker in [migraine](./Migraine "Migraine") patients, though it is not known if this is the result of migraine attacks, the cause of them or if both are the result of a shared cause.[[18]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-18)[[19]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-19)
A later study using a larger patient population reports no change in the cortical thickness in patients with migraine.[[20]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-20)
A genetic disorder of the cerebral cortex, whereby decreased folding in certain areas results in a [microgyrus](./Microgyrus "Microgyrus"), where there are four layers instead of six, is in some instances seen to be related to [dyslexia](./Dyslexia "Dyslexia").[[21]](./Cerebral_cortex#cite_note-21)
