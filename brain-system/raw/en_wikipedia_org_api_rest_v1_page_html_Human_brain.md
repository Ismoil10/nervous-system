The **human brain** is the central [organ](./Organ_(biology) "Organ (biology)") of the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system"), and with the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), comprises the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"). It consists of the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum"), the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") and the [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum"). The brain controls most of the activities of the [body](./Human_body "Human body"), processing, integrating, and coordinating the information it receives from the [sensory nervous system](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system"). The brain integrates sensory information and coordinates instructions sent to the rest of the body.

The cerebrum, the largest part of the human brain, consists of two [cerebral hemispheres](./Cerebral_hemisphere "Cerebral hemisphere"). Each hemisphere has an inner core composed of [white matter](./White_matter "White matter"), and an outer surface – the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") – composed of [grey matter](./Grey_matter "Grey matter"). The cortex has an outer layer, the [neocortex](./Neocortex "Neocortex"), and an inner [allocortex](./Allocortex "Allocortex"). The neocortex is made up of six [neuronal layers](./Cerebral_cortex#Layers_of_neocortex "Cerebral cortex"), while the allocortex has three or four. Each hemisphere is divided into four [lobes](./Lobes_of_the_brain "Lobes of the brain") – the [frontal](./Frontal_lobe "Frontal lobe"), [parietal](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe"), [temporal](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe"), and [occipital lobes](./Occipital_lobe "Occipital lobe"). The frontal lobe is associated with [executive functions](./Executive_functions "Executive functions") including [self-control](./Self-control "Self-control"), [planning](./Planning "Planning"), [reasoning](./Reason "Reason"), and [abstract thought](./Abstraction "Abstraction"), while the occipital lobe is dedicated to vision. Within each lobe, cortical areas are associated with specific functions, such as the [sensory](./Sensory_cortex "Sensory cortex"), [motor](./Motor_cortex "Motor cortex"), and [association](./Cerebral_cortex#Association_areas "Cerebral cortex") regions. Although the left and right hemispheres are broadly similar in shape and function, some functions are [associated with one side](./Lateralization_of_brain_function "Lateralization of brain function"), such as [language](./Language "Language") in the left and [visual-spatial ability](./Spatial_visualization_ability "Spatial visualization ability") in the right. The hemispheres are connected by [commissural nerve tracts](./Commissural_fiber "Commissural fiber"), the largest being the [corpus callosum](./Corpus_callosum "Corpus callosum").

The cerebrum is connected by the brainstem to the spinal cord. The brainstem consists of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"), the [pons](./Pons "Pons"), and the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"). The [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum") is connected to the brainstem by three pairs of [nerve tracts](./Nerve_tract "Nerve tract") called [cerebellar peduncles](./Cerebellar_peduncle "Cerebellar peduncle"). Within the cerebrum is the [ventricular system](./Ventricular_system "Ventricular system"), consisting of four interconnected [ventricles](./Ventricular_system#Structure "Ventricular system") in which [cerebrospinal fluid](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid") is produced and circulated. Underneath the cerebral cortex are several structures, including the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), the [epithalamus](./Epithalamus "Epithalamus"), the [pineal gland](./Pineal_gland "Pineal gland"), the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), the [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"), and the [subthalamus](./Subthalamus "Subthalamus"); the [limbic structures](./Limbic_system "Limbic system"), including the [amygdalae](./Amygdala "Amygdala") and the [hippocampi](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), the [claustrum](./Claustrum "Claustrum"), the various [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") of the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia"), the [basal forebrain](./Basal_forebrain "Basal forebrain") structures, and three [circumventricular organs](./Circumventricular_organ "Circumventricular organ"). Brain structures that are not on the midplane exist in pairs; for example, there are two hippocampi and two amygdalae.

The [cells of the brain](./Brain_cell "Brain cell") include [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") and supportive [glial cells](./Neuroglia "Neuroglia"). There are more than 86 billion neurons in the brain, and a more or less equal number of other cells. Brain activity is made possible by the interconnections of neurons and their release of [neurotransmitters](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") in response to [nerve impulses](./Action_potential "Action potential"). Neurons connect to form [neural pathways](./Neural_pathway "Neural pathway"), [neural circuits](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit"), and elaborate [network systems](./Large-scale_brain_networks "Large-scale brain networks"). The whole circuitry is driven by the process of [neurotransmission](./Neurotransmission "Neurotransmission").

The brain is protected by the [skull](./Skull#Humans "Skull"), suspended in [cerebrospinal fluid](./Cerebrospinal_fluid "Cerebrospinal fluid"), and isolated from the [bloodstream](./Circulatory_system "Circulatory system") by the [blood–brain barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier"). However, the brain is still susceptible to [damage](./Brain_damage "Brain damage"), [disease](./Central_nervous_system_disease "Central nervous system disease"), and [infection](./Infection "Infection"). Damage can be caused by [trauma](./Closed_head_injury "Closed head injury"), or a loss of blood supply known as a [stroke](./Stroke "Stroke"). The brain is susceptible to [degenerative disorders](./Neurodegeneration "Neurodegeneration"), such as [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"), [dementias](./Dementia "Dementia") including [Alzheimer's disease](./Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease"), and [multiple sclerosis](./Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis"). [Psychiatric conditions](./Psychiatric_condition "Psychiatric condition"), including [schizophrenia](./Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia") and [clinical depression](./Major_depressive_disorder "Major depressive disorder"), are thought to be associated with brain dysfunctions. The brain can also be the site of [tumours](./Brain_tumors "Brain tumors"), both [benign](./Benign_tumour "Benign tumour") and [malignant](./Cancer "Cancer"); these mostly [originate from other sites in the body](./Metastasis "Metastasis").

The study of the anatomy of the brain is [neuroanatomy](./Neuroanatomy "Neuroanatomy"), while the study of its function is [neuroscience](./Neuroscience "Neuroscience"). Numerous techniques are used to study the brain. [Specimens](./Biological_specimen "Biological specimen") from other animals, which may be [examined microscopically](./Histology "Histology"), have traditionally provided much information. [Medical imaging](./Medical_imaging "Medical imaging") technologies such as [functional neuroimaging](./Functional_neuroimaging "Functional neuroimaging"), and [electroencephalography](./Electroencephalography "Electroencephalography") (EEG) recordings are important in studying the brain. The [medical history](./Medical_history "Medical history") of people with [brain injury](./Brain_damage "Brain damage") has provided insight into the function of each part of the brain. Neuroscience research has expanded considerably, and research is ongoing.

In culture, the [philosophy of mind](./Philosophy_of_mind "Philosophy of mind") has for centuries attempted to address the question of the nature of [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness") and the [mind–body problem](./Mind–body_problem "Mind–body problem"). The [pseudoscience](./Pseudoscience "Pseudoscience") of [phrenology](./Phrenology "Phrenology") attempted to localise personality attributes to regions of the cortex in the 19th century. [In science fiction, brain transplants](./Brain_transplant#In_science_fiction "Brain transplant") are imagined in tales since the early 20th century.
