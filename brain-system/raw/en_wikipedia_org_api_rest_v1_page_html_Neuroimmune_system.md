The **neuroimmune system** is a system of structures and processes involving the [biochemical](./Biochemical "Biochemical") and [electrophysiological](./Electrophysiological "Electrophysiological") interactions between the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") and [immune system](./Immune_system "Immune system") which protect [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") from [pathogens](./Pathogen "Pathogen").[[4]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-4) It serves to protect neurons against disease by maintaining selectively permeable barriers (e.g., the [blood–brain barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier") and [blood–cerebrospinal fluid barrier](./Blood–cerebrospinal_fluid_barrier "Blood–cerebrospinal fluid barrier")), mediating [neuroinflammation](./Neuroinflammation "Neuroinflammation") and [wound healing](./Wound_healing "Wound healing") in damaged neurons, and mobilizing host defences against pathogens.[[2]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Neuroimmune_meth_toxicity-2)[[5]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Neuroimmune_receptors-5)[[6]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Astrocytes_-_abundant_neuroimmune_cells-6)

The neuroimmune system and peripheral immune system are structurally distinct. Unlike the peripheral system, the neuroimmune system is composed primarily of [glial cells](./Glial_cells "Glial cells");[[1]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Glial_tox_review_-_Ntox_diagram-1)[[6]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Astrocytes_-_abundant_neuroimmune_cells-6) among all the [hematopoietic cells](./Hematopoietic_cell "Hematopoietic cell") of the immune system, only [mast cells](./Mast_cell "Mast cell") are normally present in the neuroimmune system.[[7]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Mast_cell_neuroimmmune_system-7) However, during a neuroimmune response, certain peripheral immune cells are able to cross various blood or fluid–brain barriers in order to respond to pathogens that have entered the brain.[[2]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Neuroimmune_meth_toxicity-2) For example, there is evidence that following injury [macrophages](./Macrophage "Macrophage") and [T cells](./T_cell "T cell") of the immune system migrate into the spinal cord.[[8]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Ji-2014-8) Production of immune cells of the [complement system](./Complement_system "Complement system") have also been documented as being created directly in the central nervous system.[[9]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-9)

The key cellular components of the neuroimmune system are [glial cells](./Glial_cell "Glial cell"), including [astrocytes](./Astrocytes "Astrocytes"), [microglia](./Microglia "Microglia"), and [oligodendrocytes](./Oligodendrocytes "Oligodendrocytes").[[1]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Glial_tox_review_-_Ntox_diagram-1)[[2]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Neuroimmune_meth_toxicity-2)[[6]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Astrocytes_-_abundant_neuroimmune_cells-6) Unlike other [hematopoietic cells](./Hematopoietic_cell "Hematopoietic cell") of the peripheral immune system, [mast cells](./Mast_cell "Mast cell") naturally occur in the brain where they mediate interactions between gut microbes, the immune system, and the central nervous system as part of the [microbiota–gut–brain axis](./Gut–brain_axis "Gut–brain axis").[[7]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Mast_cell_neuroimmmune_system-7)

[G protein-coupled receptors](./G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor") that are present in both [CNS](./CNS_Neuroscience_&_Therapeutics "CNS Neuroscience & Therapeutics") and immune cell types and which are responsible for a neuroimmune signaling process include:[[5]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Neuroimmune_receptors-5)

- [Chemokine](./Chemokine "Chemokine") receptors: [CXCR4](./CXCR4 "CXCR4")
- [Cannabinoid receptors](./Cannabinoid_receptor "Cannabinoid receptor"): [CB1](./Cannabinoid_receptor_1 "Cannabinoid receptor 1"), [CB2](./Cannabinoid_receptor_2 "Cannabinoid receptor 2"), [GPR55](./GPR55 "GPR55")
- [Trace amine-associated receptors](./Trace_amine-associated_receptor "Trace amine-associated receptor"): [TAAR1](./TAAR1 "TAAR1")
- [μ-Opioid receptors](./Μ-Opioid_receptor "Μ-Opioid receptor") – all subtypes

Neuroimmunity is additionally mediated by the [enteric nervous system](./Enteric_nervous_system "Enteric nervous system"), namely the interactions of enteric neurons and glial cells. These engage with [enteroendocrine cells](./Enteroendocrine_cell "Enteroendocrine cell") and local macrophages, sensing signals from the [gut lumen](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract"), including those from the [microbiota](./Human_microbiome "Human microbiome"). These signals prompt local immune responses and transmit to the CNS through humoral and neural pathways. [Interleukins](./Interleukin_17 "Interleukin 17") and signals from immune cells can access the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") via the [neurovascular unit](./Neurovascular_unit "Neurovascular unit") or [circumventricular organs](./Circumventricular_organs "Circumventricular organs").[[10]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-10)

## Cellular physiology

The neuro-immune system, and study of, comprises an understanding of the immune and neurological systems and the cross-regulatory impacts of their functions.[[11]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-11) Cytokines regulate immune responses, possibly through activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis.[*[medical citation needed](./Wikipedia:Identifying_reliable_sources_(medicine) "Wikipedia:Identifying reliable sources (medicine)")*] Cytokines have also been implicated in the coordination between the nervous and immune systems.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) Instances of cytokine binding to neural receptors have been documented between the cytokine releasing immune cell [IL-1 β](./IL1B "IL1B") and the neural receptor [IL-1R](./Interleukin-1_receptor "Interleukin-1 receptor").[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) This binding results in an electrical impulse that creates the sensation of pain.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) Growing evidence suggests that auto-immune T-cells are involved in neurogenesis. Studies have shown that during times of adaptive immune system response, hippocampal neurogenesis is increased, and conversely that auto-immune T-cells and microglia are important for neurogenesis (and so memory and learning) in healthy adults.[[13]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-pmid16415867-13)

The neuroimmune system uses complementary processes of both [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neurons "Sensory neurons") and immune cells to detect and respond to noxious or harmful stimuli.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) For example, invading bacteria may simultaneously activate [inflammasomes](./Inflammasome "Inflammasome"), which process [interleukins](./Interleukin "Interleukin") ([IL-1 β](./IL1B "IL1B")), and depolarize sensory neurons through the secretion of [hemolysins](./Hemolysin "Hemolysin").[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12)[[14]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-McMahon-2015-14) Hemolysins create pores causing a depolarizing release of potassium ions from inside the eukaryotic cell and an influx of calcium ions.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) Together this results in an action potential in sensory neurons and the activation of inflammasomes.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12)

Injury and [necrosis](./Necrosis "Necrosis") also cause a neuroimmune response. The release of [adenosine triphosphate](./Adenosine_triphosphate "Adenosine triphosphate") (ATP) from damaged cells binds to and activates both [P2X7](./P2RX7 "P2RX7") receptors on [macrophages](./Macrophage "Macrophage") of the immune system, and [P2X3](./P2RX3 "P2RX3") receptors of [nociceptors](./Nociceptor "Nociceptor") of the nervous system.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) This causes the combined response of both a resulting action potential due to the depolarization created by the influx of calcium and potassium ions, and the activation of inflammasomes.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12) The produced action potential is also responsible for the sensation of pain, and the immune system produces IL-1 β as a result of the ATP P2X7 receptor binding.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12)

Although inflammation is typically thought of as an immune response, there is an orchestration of neural processes involved with the [inflammatory](./Inflammation "Inflammation") process of the immune system. Following injury or infection, there is a cascade of inflammatory responses such as the secretion of [cytokines](./Cytokine "Cytokine") and [chemokines](./Chemokine "Chemokine") that couple with the secretion of [neuropeptides](./Neuropeptide "Neuropeptide") (such as [substance P](./Substance_P "Substance P")) and neurotransmitters (such as [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin")).[[8]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Ji-2014-8)[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12)[[14]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-McMahon-2015-14) Together, this coupled neuroimmune response has an amplifying effect on inflammation.[[12]](./Neuroimmune_system#cite_note-Talbot-2016-12)
