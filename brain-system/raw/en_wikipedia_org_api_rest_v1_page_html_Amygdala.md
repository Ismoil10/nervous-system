The **amygdala** ([/əˈmɪɡdələ/](./Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English"); pl.: **amygdalae** [/əˈmɪɡdəli, -laɪ/](./Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") or **amygdalas**; also **corpus amygdaloideum**[[1]](./Amygdala#cite_note-dictionary.com-1)) is a paired [nuclear](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") complex present in the [cerebral hemispheres](./Cerebral_hemisphere "Cerebral hemisphere") of [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate"). It is considered part of the [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system").[[2]](./Amygdala#cite_note-amyg-cytoarchitecture-2) In [primates](./Primate "Primate"), it is located [medially](./Lateral_and_medial "Lateral and medial") within the [temporal lobes](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe").[[3]](./Amygdala#cite_note-3) It consists of many nuclei, each made up of further subnuclei. The subdivision most commonly made is into the [basolateral](./Basolateral_amygdala "Basolateral amygdala"), [central](./Central_nucleus_of_the_amygdala "Central nucleus of the amygdala"), cortical, and medial nuclei together with the [intercalated cell clusters](./Intercalated_cells_of_the_amygdala "Intercalated cells of the amygdala").[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4) The amygdala has a primary role in the processing of [memory](./Memory "Memory"), [decision-making](./Decision_making "Decision making"), and [emotional responses](./Emotions "Emotions") (including fear, anxiety, and aggression). The amygdala was first identified and named by [Karl Friedrich Burdach](./Karl_Friedrich_Burdach "Karl Friedrich Burdach") in 1822.[[5]](./Amygdala#cite_note-5)

Thirteen [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)")[[6]](./Amygdala#cite_note-6) have been identified as part of the amygdala, each with its own subdivisions and distinct connections to the rest of the brain.[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4) The chief nuclei are the [basolateral complex](./Basolateral_amygdala "Basolateral amygdala"), the [central nucleus](./Central_nucleus_of_the_amygdala "Central nucleus of the amygdala"), the cortical nucleus, the medial nucleus, and the [intercalated cell clusters](./Intercalated_cells_of_the_amygdala "Intercalated cells of the amygdala").[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4) The cortical and medial nuclei connect with the [olfactory system](./Olfactory_system "Olfactory system") and hypothalamus.[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4) The central nucleus has extensive projections to the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem").[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4)

The basolateral complex can be further subdivided into the lateral, the basal, and the accessory basal nuclei.[[2]](./Amygdala#cite_note-amyg-cytoarchitecture-2)[[7]](./Amygdala#cite_note-emotion-7)[[8]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Solano-Castiella-8) It has extensive connections with higher-order cortical areas in the [prefrontal](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"), [temporal](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe"), [insular](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") cortices, and the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus").[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4)[[9]](./Amygdala#cite_note-McDonald_2020_pp._1–38-9) The basolateral complex is surrounded by the intercalated cell net that is inhibitory and projects to a broad variety of areas in the [basal forebrain](./Basal_forebrain "Basal forebrain"), [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), and the amygdala.[[4]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Bzdok_Laird_Zilles_Fox_2013_pp._3247–3266-4)[[10]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Stern_Wilke_Root_2023_pp._ENEURO.0238–23.2023-10)

The primate amygdala contains about 32 different types of neuron.[[11]](./Amygdala#cite_note-:2-11)[[12]](./Amygdala#cite_note-12)

### Hemispheric specializations

The right and left portions of the amygdala have independent memory systems, but work together to store, encode, and interpret emotion.[[13]](./Amygdala#cite_note-13)

The right hemisphere of the amygdala is associated with negative emotion.[[14]](./Amygdala#cite_note-14)[[15]](./Amygdala#cite_note-15) It plays a role in the expression of fear and in the processing of fear-inducing stimuli. [Fear conditioning](./Fear_conditioning "Fear conditioning"), which occurs when a neutral stimulus acquires aversive properties, occurs within the right hemisphere. When an individual is presented with a conditioned, aversive stimulus, it is processed within the right amygdala, producing an unpleasant or fearful response. This emotional response conditions the individual to avoid fear-inducing stimuli and more importantly, to assess threats in the environment.

The right hemisphere is also linked to [declarative memory](./Declarative_memory "Declarative memory"), which consists of facts and information from previously experienced events and must be consciously recalled. It also plays a significant role in the retention of episodic memory. [Episodic memory](./Episodic_memory "Episodic memory") consists of the autobiographical aspects of memory, permitting recall of emotional and sensory experience of an event. This type of memory does not require conscious recall. The right amygdala plays a role in the association of time and places with emotional properties.[[16]](./Amygdala#cite_note-16)

In one study, electrical stimulations of the right amygdala induced [negative emotions](./Negative_affectivity "Negative affectivity"), especially fear and sadness. In contrast, stimulation of the left amygdala was able to induce either pleasant (happiness) or unpleasant (fear, anxiety, sadness) emotions.[[17]](./Amygdala#cite_note-amygdala_hemispheric_specialization1-17) Other evidence suggests that the left amygdala plays a role in the brain's [reward system](./Reward_system "Reward system").[[18]](./Amygdala#cite_note-amygdala_hemispheric_specialization2-18)

## Development and sex distinction

See also: [Neuroscience of sex differences](./Neuroscience_of_sex_differences "Neuroscience of sex differences")

When controlling for total brain volume or intracranial volume, there is no significant difference in the average size of the amygdala between men and women.[[19]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Marwha-19) Raw amygdala volumes are, on average, 10.6% larger in men, but men also have larger bodies and brains on average.[[19]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Marwha-19)

There is considerable growth within the first few years of structural development in both male and female amygdalae.[[20]](./Amygdala#cite_note-Development-20)[*[non-primary source needed](./Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")*] Within this early period, female limbic structures grow at a more rapid pace than the male ones. Amongst female subjects, the amygdala reaches its full growth potential approximately 1.5 years before the peak of male development. The structural development of the male amygdala occurs over a longer period than in women.[[21]](./Amygdala#cite_note-21)

There are observable developmental differences between the right and left amygdala. The left amygdala reaches its developmental peak approximately 1.5–2 years prior to the right amygdala. Despite the early growth of the left amygdala, the right increases in volume for a longer period of time. The right amygdala is associated with response to fearful stimuli as well as face recognition. For the left amygdala, it is inferred that the early development of it functions to provide infants the ability to detect danger due to its reported responds predominantly to fearful events and faces.[[22]](./Amygdala#cite_note-22)[[23]](./Amygdala#cite_note-23)[*[non-primary source needed](./Wikipedia:No_original_research#Primary,_secondary_and_tertiary_sources "Wikipedia:No original research")*] In childhood, the amygdala is found to react differently to same-sex versus opposite-sex individuals. This reactivity decreases until a person enters adolescence, where it increases dramatically at puberty.[[24]](./Amygdala#cite_note-24)

Functional and structural differences have been discovered between male and female amygdalae. The results of PET scans showed a different lateralization of the amygdala in men and women after watching an emotional film. Enhanced memory for the film was related to enhanced activity of the left, but not the right, amygdala in women, whereas it was related to enhanced activity of the right, but not the left, amygdala in men.[[25]](./Amygdala#cite_note-25)[[26]](./Amygdala#cite_note-26) Studies involving patients with amygdala damage came to a similar conclusion. In patients with unilateral amygdala damage, men with right (but not left) amygdala damage were more likely to be impaired in decision-making ability, while women with left (but not right) amygdala damage were more likely to be impaired in decision-making ability.[[27]](./Amygdala#cite_note-27)[[28]](./Amygdala#cite_note-28) One study found evidence that, on average, women tend to retain stronger memories for emotional events than men.[[29]](./Amygdala#cite_note-29)

### Connections

Variability in amygdala connectivity has been related to a variety of behaviors and outcomes such as fear recognition[[30]](./Amygdala#cite_note-30) and social network size.[[31]](./Amygdala#cite_note-ReferenceB-31) A simple view of the information processing through the amygdala follows as: the amygdala sends projections to the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), [septal nuclei](./Septal_area "Septal area") and [BNST](./Stria_terminalis "Stria terminalis") (via the [amygdalofugal tract](./Amygdalofugal_pathway "Amygdalofugal pathway")), the dorsomedial thalamus (via the amygdalothalamic tract), the nuclei of the [trigeminal nerve](./Trigeminal_nerve "Trigeminal nerve") and the [facial nerve](./Facial_nerve "Facial nerve"), the [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area"), the [locus coeruleus](./Locus_coeruleus "Locus coeruleus"), and the [laterodorsal tegmental nucleus](./Laterodorsal_tegmental_nucleus "Laterodorsal tegmental nucleus").[[7]](./Amygdala#cite_note-emotion-7)
