The **reticular formation** is a set of interconnected [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") in the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") that spans from the lower end of the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") to the upper end of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain").[[2]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Brodal-2) The neurons of the reticular formation make up a complex set of [neural networks](./Neural_network_(biology) "Neural network (biology)") in the core of the brainstem.[[3]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Purves-3) The reticular formation is made up of a diffuse net-like formation of [reticular nuclei](./Reticular_nuclei "Reticular nuclei") which is not well-defined.[[4]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Haines2018c-4) It may be seen as being made up of all the interspersed cells in the brainstem between the more compact and named structures.[[4]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Haines2018c-4)

The reticular formation is functionally divided into the **ascending reticular activating system** (**ARAS**), ascending pathways to the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"), and the **descending reticular system**, descending pathways ([reticulospinal tracts](./Reticulospinal_tract "Reticulospinal tract")) to the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[5]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Singh-5)[[6]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS-6)[[7]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Augustine-7)[[8]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Jones-8) Due to its extent along the brainstem it may be divided into different areas such as the midbrain reticular formation, the central mesencephalic reticular formation, the pontine reticular formation, the [paramedian pontine reticular formation](./Paramedian_pontine_reticular_formation "Paramedian pontine reticular formation"), the dorsolateral pontine reticular formation, and the medullary reticular formation.[[9]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Wang2017-9)

Neurons of the ARAS basically act as an on/off switch to the cerebral cortex and hence play a crucial role in regulating [wakefulness](./Wakefulness "Wakefulness"); [behavioral arousal](./Arousal "Arousal") and [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness") are functionally related in the reticular formation using a number of neurotransmitter arousal systems. The overall functions of the reticular formation are modulatory and premotor,[[A]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-10)
involving somatic motor control, cardiovascular control, pain modulation, sleep and consciousness, and habituation.[[10]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Saladin1-11) The modulatory functions are primarily found in the rostral sector of the reticular formation and the premotor functions are localized in the neurons in more caudal regions.

The reticular formation is divided into three columns: [raphe nuclei](./Raphe_nuclei "Raphe nuclei") (median), [gigantocellular reticular nuclei](./Gigantocellular_reticular_nuclei "Gigantocellular reticular nuclei") (medial zone), and [parvocellular reticular nuclei](./Parvocellular_reticular_nuclei "Parvocellular reticular nuclei") (lateral zone). The raphe nuclei are the place of synthesis of the neurotransmitter [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"), which plays an important role in mood regulation. The gigantocellular nuclei are involved in motor coordination. The parvocellular nuclei regulate [exhalation](./Exhalation "Exhalation").[[11]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-12)

The reticular formation is essential for governing some of the basic functions of higher organisms. It is [phylogenetically](./Phylogenetics "Phylogenetics") old and found in [lower vertebrates](./Lower_vertebrate "Lower vertebrate").[[2]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Brodal-2)

## Structure

A cross section of the lower part of the [pons](./Pons "Pons") showing the pontine reticular formation labeled as #9

The human reticular formation is composed of almost 100 [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") and contains many projections into the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain"), [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem"), and [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum"), among other regions.[[6]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS-6) It includes the [reticular nuclei](./Reticular_nuclei "Reticular nuclei"), reticulothalamic projection fibers, diffuse [thalamocortical projections](./Thalamocortical_radiations "Thalamocortical radiations"), ascending [cholinergic projections](./Cholinergic_neuron "Cholinergic neuron"), descending non-cholinergic projections, and descending reticulospinal projections.[[7]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Augustine-7) The reticular formation also contains two major [neural subsystems](./Systems_neuroscience "Systems neuroscience"), the ascending reticular activating system and descending reticulospinal tracts, which mediate distinct cognitive and physiological processes.[[6]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS-6)[[7]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Augustine-7) It has been functionally cleaved both [sagittally](./Sagittal "Sagittal") and [coronally](./Coronal_plane "Coronal plane").

Traditionally the reticular nuclei are divided into three columns:[[12]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-13)

- In the median column – the raphe nuclei
- In the medial column – gigantocellular nuclei (because of larger size of the cells)
- In the lateral column – parvocellular nuclei (because of smaller size of the cells)

The original functional differentiation was a division of [caudal and rostral](./Anterior "Anterior"). This was based upon the observation that the lesioning of the rostral reticular formation induces a [hypersomnia](./Hypersomnia "Hypersomnia") in the cat brain, while lesioning the caudal portion causes [insomnia](./Insomnia "Insomnia"). This study has led to the idea that the caudal portion inhibits the rostral portion of the reticular formation.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Sagittal division reveals more morphological distinctions. The [raphe nuclei](./Raphe_nuclei "Raphe nuclei") form a ridge in the middle of the reticular formation, and, directly to its periphery, there is a division called the medial reticular formation. The medial RF is large and has long ascending and descending fibers, and is surrounded by the lateral reticular formation. The lateral RF is close to the motor nuclei of the cranial nerves, and mostly mediates their function.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

### Medial and lateral reticular formation

The medial reticular formation and lateral reticular formation are two columns of [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") with ill-defined boundaries that send projections through the [medulla](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") and into the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"). The nuclei can be differentiated by function, cell type, and projections of [efferent](./Efferent_nerves "Efferent nerves") or [afferent](./Afferent_nerves "Afferent nerves") nerves. Moving caudally from the rostral [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"), at the site of the rostral [pons](./Pons "Pons") and the midbrain, the medial RF becomes less prominent, and the lateral RF becomes more prominent.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

Existing on the sides of the medial reticular formation is its [lateral](./Human_anatomical_terms#Anatomical_directions "Human anatomical terms") cousin, which is particularly pronounced in the rostral medulla and caudal pons. Out from this area spring the cranial nerves, including the very important [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve").[*[clarification needed](./Wikipedia:Please_clarify "Wikipedia:Please clarify")*] The lateral RF is known for its [ganglions](./Ganglion "Ganglion") and areas of [interneurons](./Interneurons "Interneurons") around the [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves"), which serve to mediate their characteristic reflexes and functions.

## Major subsystems

The subsystems of the reticular formation are the ascending reticular activating system, and the descending reticular system.[[7]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Augustine-7)

### Ascending reticular activating system



Ascending reticular activating system. Reticular formation labeled near center.

The ascending reticular activating system (ARAS), also known as the *extrathalamic control modulatory system* or simply the *reticular activating system* (RAS), is a set of connected [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") in the [brains](./Brain "Brain") of vertebrates that is responsible for regulating [wakefulness](./Wakefulness "Wakefulness") and [sleep-wake transitions](./Sleep-wake_cycle "Sleep-wake cycle"). The ARAS is in the midbrain reticular formation.[[13]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-Squire2-14) It is mostly composed of various nuclei in the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus")/[hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") and a number of [dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic"), [noradrenergic](./Noradrenergic "Noradrenergic"), [serotonergic](./Serotonin "Serotonin"), [histaminergic](./Histaminergic "Histaminergic"), [cholinergic](./Cholinergic "Cholinergic"), and [glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic") brain nuclei.[[6]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS-6)[[14]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-NHM_-_ascending_reticular_activating_system-15)[[15]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS_Nuclei_primary_neurotransmitters_2014_review-16)[[16]](./Reticular_formation#cite_note-ARAS,_sleep,_and_the_parafacial_zone_review-17)
