**Myelin** ([/ˈmaɪ.əlɪn/](./Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English") [*MY-ə-lin*](./Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key")) is a [lipid](./Lipid "Lipid")-rich material that in most [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") surrounds the [axons](./Axons "Axons") of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") to [insulate](./Insulator_(electricity) "Insulator (electricity)") them and increase the rate at which electrical impulses (called [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential")) pass along the axon.[[1]](./Myelin#cite_note-Bean_451–465-1)[[2]](./Myelin#cite_note-Moreell-2) The myelinated axon can be likened to an electrical wire (the axon) with insulating material (myelin) around it. However, unlike the plastic covering on an electrical wire, myelin does not form a single long sheath over the entire length of the axon. Myelin ensheaths part of an axon known as an [internodal segment](./Internodal_segment "Internodal segment"), in multiple myelin layers of a tightly regulated internodal length.

The ensheathed segments are separated at regular short unmyelinated intervals, called [nodes of Ranvier](./Node_of_Ranvier "Node of Ranvier"). Each node of Ranvier is around one [micrometre](./Micrometre "Micrometre") long. Nodes of Ranvier enable a much faster rate of [conduction](./Conduction_of_electricity "Conduction of electricity") known as [saltatory conduction](./Saltatory_conduction "Saltatory conduction") where the [action potential](./Action_potential "Action potential") recharges at each node to jump over to the next node, and so on until it reaches the [axon terminal](./Axon_terminal "Axon terminal").[[1]](./Myelin#cite_note-Bean_451–465-1)[[3]](./Myelin#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Myelin#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Myelin#cite_note-5) At the terminal the action potential provokes the release of [neurotransmitters](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") across the [synapse](./Synapse "Synapse"), which bind to [receptors](./Neurotransmitter_receptor "Neurotransmitter receptor") on the post-synaptic cell such as another neuron, [myocyte](./Myocyte "Myocyte") or [secretory cell](./Secretory_cell "Secretory cell").

Myelin is made by specialized non-neuronal [glial cells](./Glial_cells "Glial cells"), that provide insulation, and [nutritional](./Nutrition "Nutrition") and [homeostatic](./Homeostatic "Homeostatic") support, along the length of the axon. In the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"), myelination is formed by glial cells called [oligodendrocytes](./Oligodendrocyte "Oligodendrocyte"), each of which sends out [cellular extensions](./Cellular_extensions "Cellular extensions") known as [foot processes](./Foot_processes "Foot processes") to myelinate multiple nearby axons. In the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system"), myelin is formed by [Schwann cells](./Schwann_cell "Schwann cell"), which myelinate only a section of an axon. In the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS), axons carry electrical signals from one nerve cell body to another.[[6]](./Myelin#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Myelin#cite_note-7)
The "insulating" function for myelin is essential for efficient [motor function](./Motor_function "Motor function") (i.e. movement such as walking), [sensory function](./Sense "Sense") (e.g. [sight](./Sight "Sight"), [hearing](./Hearing "Hearing"), [smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction"), the feeling of [touch](./Touch "Touch") or [pain](./Pain "Pain")) and [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition") (e.g. acquiring and recalling knowledge), as demonstrated by the consequence of disorders that affect myelination, such as the genetically determined [leukodystrophies](./Leukodystrophies "Leukodystrophies");[[8]](./Myelin#cite_note-8) the acquired inflammatory [demyelinating disease](./Demyelinating_disease "Demyelinating disease"), [multiple sclerosis](./Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis");[[9]](./Myelin#cite_note-9) and the inflammatory demyelinating [peripheral neuropathies](./Peripheral_neuropathies "Peripheral neuropathies").[[10]](./Myelin#cite_note-10) Due to its high prevalence, multiple sclerosis, which specifically affects the central nervous system, is the best known demyelinating disorder.

## History

Myelin was first described as white matter fibres in the 16th century by [Vesalius](./Vesalius "Vesalius"), but the word *myelin* was coined by [Rudolf Virchow](./Rudolf_Virchow "Rudolf Virchow") in 1854. The term comes from the Greek word *myelos*, meaning bone marrow, due to the similar color and texture.[[11]](./Myelin#cite_note-Boullerne-11) Over a century later, following the development of [electron microscopy](./Electron_microscopy "Electron microscopy"), its glial cell origin, and its ultrastructure became apparent.[[11]](./Myelin#cite_note-Boullerne-11)

Myelin is found in all [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") except the [jawless fish](./Jawless_fish "Jawless fish").[[12]](./Myelin#cite_note-Daniel-12)[[13]](./Myelin#cite_note-Salzer-13) Myelin in the central nervous system differs slightly in composition and configuration from myelin in the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS), but both perform the same functions of insulation and nutritional support. Being rich in [lipid](./Lipid "Lipid"), myelin appears white, hence its earlier name of [white matter](./White_matter "White matter") of the CNS. Both CNS white matter [tracts](./Nerve_tract "Nerve tract") such as the [corpus callosum](./Corpus_callosum "Corpus callosum"), and [corticospinal tract](./Corticospinal_tract "Corticospinal tract"), and PNS [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve") such as the [sciatic nerve](./Sciatic_nerve "Sciatic nerve"), and the [auditory nerve](./Auditory_nerve "Auditory nerve"), which also appear white, comprise thousands to millions of axons, largely aligned in parallel. In the corpus callosum there are more than 200 million axons.[[14]](./Myelin#cite_note-Luders-14) Blood vessels provide the route for oxygen and energy substrates such as glucose to reach these fibre tracts, which also contain other cell types including [astrocytes](./Astrocytes "Astrocytes") and [microglia](./Microglia "Microglia") in the CNS and [macrophages](./Macrophages "Macrophages") in the PNS.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

In terms of total mass, myelin comprises approximately 40% water; the dry mass comprises between 60% and 75% [lipid](./Lipid "Lipid") and between 15% and 25% [protein](./Protein_(nutrient) "Protein (nutrient)"). Protein content includes [myelin basic protein](./Myelin_basic_protein "Myelin basic protein") (MBP),[[15]](./Myelin#cite_note-15) which is abundant in the CNS where it plays a critical, non-redundant role in formation of compact myelin; [myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein](./Myelin_oligodendrocyte_glycoprotein "Myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein") (MOG),[[16]](./Myelin#cite_note-16) which is specific to the CNS; and [proteolipid protein](./Proteolipid_protein "Proteolipid protein") (PLP),[[17]](./Myelin#cite_note-17) which is the most abundant protein in CNS myelin, but only a minor component of PNS myelin. In the PNS, [myelin protein zero](./Myelin_protein_zero "Myelin protein zero") (MPZ or P0) has a similar role to that of PLP in the CNS in that it is involved in holding together the multiple concentric layers of glial cell membrane that constitute the myelin sheath. The primary lipid of myelin is a [glycolipid](./Glycolipid "Glycolipid") called [galactocerebroside](./Galactocerebroside "Galactocerebroside"). The intertwining hydrocarbon chains of [sphingomyelin](./Sphingomyelin "Sphingomyelin") strengthen the myelin sheath. [Cholesterol](./Cholesterol "Cholesterol") is an essential lipid component of myelin, without which myelin fails to form.[[18]](./Myelin#cite_note-18)

[Myelin-associated glycoprotein](./Myelin-associated_glycoprotein "Myelin-associated glycoprotein") (MAG) is a critical protein in the formation and maintenance of myelin sheaths. MAG is localized on the inner membrane of the myelin sheath and interacts with axonal membrane proteins to attach the myelin sheath to the axon.[[19]](./Myelin#cite_note-19) Mutations to the MAG gene are implicated in demyelination diseases such as multiple sclerosis.[[20]](./Myelin#cite_note-20)

## Function

Main article: [Saltatory conduction](./Saltatory_conduction "Saltatory conduction")

Action potential propagation in myelinated neurons is faster than in unmyelinated neurons because of [saltatory conduction](./Saltatory_conduction "Saltatory conduction").

The main purpose of myelin is to increase the speed at which electrical impulses (known as [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential")) propagate along the myelinated fiber. In unmyelinated fibers, action potentials travel as continuous waves, but, in myelinated fibers, they "hop" or propagate by [saltatory conduction](./Saltatory_conduction "Saltatory conduction"). The latter is markedly faster than the former, at least for axons over a certain diameter. Myelin decreases [capacitance](./Capacitance "Capacitance") and increases [electrical resistance](./Electrical_resistance "Electrical resistance") across the axonal membrane (the [axolemma](./Axolemma "Axolemma")). It has been suggested that myelin permits larger body size by maintaining agile communication between distant body parts.[[12]](./Myelin#cite_note-Daniel-12)

Myelinated fibers lack [voltage-gated sodium channels](./Voltage-gated_sodium_channels "Voltage-gated sodium channels") along the myelinated internodes, exposing them only at the [nodes of Ranvier](./Nodes_of_Ranvier "Nodes of Ranvier"). Here, they are highly abundant and densely packed.[[21]](./Myelin#cite_note-Saladin,_Kenneth_S._2012-21) Positively charged sodium [ions](./Ion "Ion") can enter the axon through these voltage-gated channels, leading to [depolarisation](./Depolarisation "Depolarisation") of the membrane potential at the node of Ranvier. The [resting membrane potential](./Resting_membrane_potential "Resting membrane potential") is then rapidly restored due to positively charged potassium ions leaving the axon through [potassium channels](./Potassium_channels "Potassium channels").
