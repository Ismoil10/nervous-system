**Cranial nerves** are [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve") that emerge directly from the [brain](./Brain "Brain"), including the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem"), in contrast to [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve"), which emerge from segments of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[1]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Vilensky-1) They relay information between the brain and various parts of the body, primarily to the [head and neck](./Head_and_neck "Head and neck") regions and are responsible for [special senses](./Special_senses "Special senses") of [vision](./Visual_perception "Visual perception"), [taste](./Taste "Taste"), [smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction"), and [hearing](./Hearing "Hearing").[[2]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Gray's2008-2)

The cranial nerves emerge from the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") above the level of the [first vertebra](./Atlas_(anatomy) "Atlas (anatomy)") of the [vertebral column](./Vertebral_column "Vertebral column").[[3]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Kandel-3) Each cranial nerve is paired and is present on both sides.

There are conventionally twelve pairs of cranial nerves, which are described with [Roman numerals](./Roman_numerals "Roman numerals") I–XII. Some considered there to be thirteen pairs of cranial nerves, including the non-paired [terminal nerve](./Terminal_nerve "Terminal nerve"). The numbering of the cranial nerves is based on the order in which they emerge from the brain and brainstem, from front to back.[[3]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Kandel-3)

The [terminal nerves](./Terminal_nerve "Terminal nerve") (0), [olfactory nerves](./Olfactory_nerve "Olfactory nerve") (I) and [optic nerves](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") (II) emerge from the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum"), and the remaining ten pairs arise from the brainstem, which is the lower part of the brain.[[1]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Vilensky-1)

The cranial nerves are considered components of the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS),[[1]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Vilensky-1) although on a structural level the olfactory (I), optic (II), and trigeminal (V) nerves are more accurately considered part of the central nervous system (CNS).[[4]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-4)

## Anatomy

See also: [Table of cranial nerves](./Table_of_cranial_nerves "Table of cranial nerves")

Most typically, humans are considered to have twelve pairs of cranial nerves (I–XII), with the [terminal nerve](./Terminal_nerve "Terminal nerve") (0) more recently canonized.[[3]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Kandel-3)[[5]](./Cranial_nerves#cite_note-Sonne-5) The nerves are: the [olfactory nerve](./Olfactory_nerve "Olfactory nerve") (I), the [optic nerve](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") (II), [oculomotor nerve](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve") (III), [trochlear nerve](./Trochlear_nerve "Trochlear nerve") (IV), [trigeminal nerve](./Trigeminal_nerve "Trigeminal nerve") (V), [abducens nerve](./Abducens_nerve "Abducens nerve") (VI), [facial nerve](./Facial_nerve "Facial nerve") (VII), [vestibulocochlear nerve](./Vestibulocochlear_nerve "Vestibulocochlear nerve") (VIII), [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve") (IX), [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") (X), [accessory nerve](./Accessory_nerve "Accessory nerve") (XI), and the [hypoglossal nerve](./Hypoglossal_nerve "Hypoglossal nerve") (XII).
