The **limbic system**, also known as the **paleomammalian cortex**, is a set of [brain](./Brain "Brain") structures involved in emotional processing and motivation in humans and many other animals. In humans it is located on both sides of the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), immediately beneath the medial [temporal lobe](./Temporal_lobe "Temporal lobe") of the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") primarily in the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain").[[1]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Schacter,_Daniel_L_2012-1)

Its various components support a variety of functions including [emotion](./Emotion "Emotion"), [behavior](./Behavior "Behavior"), [long-term memory](./Long-term_memory "Long-term memory"), and [olfaction](./Olfaction "Olfaction").[[2]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-medlineplus-2)

The limbic system is involved in lower order emotional processing of input from sensory systems and consists of the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), [mammillary bodies](./Mammillary_body "Mammillary body"), [stria medullaris](./Stria_medullaris_of_thalamus "Stria medullaris of thalamus"), central gray and dorsal and ventral nuclei of Gudden.[[3]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Limbic_Systems-3) This processed information is often relayed to a collection of structures from the [telencephalon](./Telencephalon "Telencephalon"), [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon"), and [mesencephalon](./Mesencephalon "Mesencephalon"), including the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"), [cingulate gyrus](./Cingulate_cortex#Subdivisions "Cingulate cortex"), limbic thalamus, [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") including the [parahippocampal gyrus](./Parahippocampal_gyrus "Parahippocampal gyrus") and [subiculum](./Subiculum "Subiculum"), [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") (limbic striatum), anterior [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area"), midbrain [raphe nuclei](./Raphe_nuclei "Raphe nuclei"), [habenular commissure](./Habenular_commissure "Habenular commissure"), [entorhinal cortex](./Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex"), and [olfactory bulbs](./Olfactory_bulb "Olfactory bulb").[[3]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Limbic_Systems-3)[[4]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-5)

## Structure

Anatomical components of the limbic system

The [limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe") was originally defined by the French anatomist [Paul Broca](./Paul_Broca "Paul Broca") in 1878, as a series of [cortical](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") structures surrounding the boundary between the [cerebral hemispheres](./Cerebral_hemispheres "Cerebral hemispheres") and the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem").[[6]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Purves-6) The name "limbic" comes from the Latin word for the border, *limbus*.[[7]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-broca-7) Further studies began to associate these areas with emotional and motivational processes and linked them to [subcortical](./Subcortical "Subcortical") components that were then grouped into the limbic system.[[3]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Limbic_Systems-3)

In recent years, multiple additional limbic fiber connectivity has been revealed using [diffusion-weighted MRI](./Diffusion-weighted_MRI "Diffusion-weighted MRI"). The equivalent fiber connectivity of all these pathways has been documented by dissection studies in primates. Some of these fiber tracts include the amygdalofugal tract, amygdalothalamic tract, stria terminalis, dorsal thalamo-hypothalamic tract, cerebellohypothalamic tracts, and the parieto-occipito-hypothalamic tract.[[8]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-8)

Currently, it is not considered an isolated entity responsible for the neurological regulation of emotion, but rather one of the many parts of the brain that regulate [visceral autonomic processes](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system").[[9]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-blessing-9) Therefore, the set of anatomical structures considered part of the limbic system is controversial. The following structures are, or have been considered, part of the limbic system:[[10]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-swenson-10)[[11]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-rajmohan-11)

- Cortical areas:
  - [Limbic lobe](./Limbic_lobe "Limbic lobe")
  - [Orbitofrontal cortex](./Orbitofrontal_cortex "Orbitofrontal cortex"): a region in the frontal lobe involved in the process of decision-making
  - [Piriform cortex](./Piriform_cortex "Piriform cortex"): part of the [olfactory system](./Olfactory_system "Olfactory system")
  - [Entorhinal cortex](./Entorhinal_cortex "Entorhinal cortex"): related to memory and associative components
  - [Fornix](./Fornix_of_the_brain "Fornix of the brain"): a [white matter](./White_matter "White matter") structure connecting the hippocampus with other brain structures, particularly the [mammillary bodies](./Mammillary_bodies "Mammillary bodies") and [septal nuclei](./Septal_nuclei "Septal nuclei")
- Subcortical areas:
  - [Septal nuclei](./Septal_nuclei "Septal nuclei"): a set of structures that lie in front of the [lamina terminalis](./Lamina_terminalis "Lamina terminalis"), considered a pleasure zone
  - [Hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") and associated structures: play a central role in the consolidation of new memories
  - [Amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"): located deep within the [temporal lobes](./Temporal_lobes "Temporal lobes") and related with a number of emotional processes
  - [Nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens"): involved in reward, pleasure, and addiction
- [Diencephalic](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon") structures:
  - [Hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"): a center for the limbic system, connected with the frontal lobes, septal nuclei, and the brain stem [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation") via the [medial forebrain bundle](./Medial_forebrain_bundle "Medial forebrain bundle"), with the hippocampus via the fornix, and with the thalamus via the [mammillothalamic fasciculus](./Mammillothalamic_fasciculus "Mammillothalamic fasciculus"); regulates many autonomic processes
  - [Mammillary bodies](./Mammillary_bodies "Mammillary bodies"): part of the hypothalamus that receives signals from the hippocampus via the fornix and projects them to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus")
  - [Anterior nuclei of thalamus](./Anterior_nuclei_of_thalamus "Anterior nuclei of thalamus"): receive input from the mammillary bodies and involved in memory processing

## Function

The structures and interacting areas of the limbic system are involved in motivation, emotion, learning, and memory. The limbic system is where the subcortical structures meet the cerebral cortex.[[1]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Schacter,_Daniel_L_2012-1) The limbic system operates by influencing the [endocrine system](./Endocrine_system "Endocrine system") and the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"). It is highly interconnected with the [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens"), which plays a role in [sexual arousal](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal") and the "high" derived from certain [recreational drugs](./Recreational_drug_use "Recreational drug use"). These responses are heavily modulated by [dopaminergic](./Dopamine "Dopamine") projections from the limbic system. In 1954, [Olds](./James_Olds "James Olds") and Milner found that [rats](./Rat "Rat") with metal [electrodes](./Electrode "Electrode") implanted into their nucleus accumbens, as well as their [septal nuclei](./Septal_nuclei "Septal nuclei"), repeatedly pressed a lever activating this region.[[12]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-12)

The limbic system also interacts with the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia"). The basal ganglia are a set of subcortical structures that direct intentional movements as well as higher cognitive functions through regulating thalamocortical loops. The basal ganglia are located near the thalamus and hypothalamus. They receive input from the cerebral cortex and output to thalamic nuclei. A part of the basal ganglia called the striatum controls posture and movement. Recent studies indicate that if there is an inadequate supply of dopamine in the striatum, this can lead to the symptoms of [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease").[[1]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-Schacter,_Daniel_L_2012-1)

The limbic system is also tightly connected to the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"). This connectivity has been demonstrated to play a role in memory consolidation[[13]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-13), emotional processing[[14]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-14), and interoception-driven behavior.[[15]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-15) To cure severe emotional disorders, this connection was sometimes surgically severed, a procedure of [psychosurgery](./Psychosurgery "Psychosurgery"), called a [prefrontal lobotomy](./Leukotomy "Leukotomy") (this is actually a misnomer). Patients having undergone this procedure often became passive and lacked all motivation.[[16]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-16)

The limbic system interacts heavily with the cerebral cortex. These interactions are closely linked to olfaction, emotions, drives, autonomic regulation, memory, and pathologically to encephalopathy, epilepsy, psychotic symptoms, cognitive defects.[[17]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-17) The functional relevance of the limbic system has proven to serve many different functions such as affects/emotions, memory, sensory processing, time perception, attention, consciousness, instincts, autonomic/vegetative control, and actions/motor behavior. Some of the disorders associated with the limbic system and its interacting components are epilepsy and schizophrenia.[[18]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-18)

### Hippocampus

Location and basic anatomy of the hippocampus, as a [coronal section](./Coronal_section "Coronal section")

The [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus") is involved with various processes relating to [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition") and is one of the best understood and heavily involved limbic interacting structures.[[19]](./Limbic_system#cite_note-19)

#### Spatial memory

The first and most widely researched area concerns memory, particularly [spatial memory](./Spatial_memory "Spatial memory"). Spatial memory was found to have many sub-regions in the hippocampus, such as the [dentate gyrus](./Dentate_gyrus "Dentate gyrus") (DG) in the dorsal hippocampus, the left hippocampus, and the parahippocampal region.
