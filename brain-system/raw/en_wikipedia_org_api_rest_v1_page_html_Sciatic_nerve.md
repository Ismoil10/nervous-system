The **sciatic nerve**, also called the **ischiadic nerve**, is a large [nerve](./Nerve "Nerve") in humans and other vertebrate animals. It is the largest branch of the [sacral plexus](./Sacral_plexus "Sacral plexus") and runs alongside the hip joint and down the [lower limb](./Lower_limb "Lower limb"). It is the longest and widest single nerve in the [human body](./Human_body "Human body"), going from the top of the leg to the foot on the posterior aspect. The sciatic nerve has no cutaneous branches for the thigh. This nerve provides the connection to the nervous system for the skin of the lateral leg and the whole foot, the muscles of the back of the thigh, and those of the leg and foot. It is derived from [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") [L4](./Lumbar_spinal_nerve_4 "Lumbar spinal nerve 4") to [S3](./Sacral_spinal_nerve_3 "Sacral spinal nerve 3"). It contains [fibres](./Axon "Axon") from both the anterior and posterior divisions of the [lumbosacral plexus](./Lumbosacral_plexus "Lumbosacral plexus").

## Structure

In humans, the sciatic nerve is formed from the L4 to S3 segments of the sacral plexus, a collection of nerve fibres that emerge from the [sacral](./Sacrum "Sacrum") part of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). The [lumbosacral trunk](./Lumbosacral_trunk "Lumbosacral trunk") from the L4 and L5 roots descends between the sacral promontory and ala, and the S1 to S3 roots emerge from the ventral sacral foramina. These nerve roots unite to form a single nerve in front of the [piriformis muscle](./Piriformis_muscle "Piriformis muscle"). The nerve passes beneath the piriformis and through the [greater sciatic foramen](./Greater_sciatic_foramen "Greater sciatic foramen"), exiting the pelvis.[[1]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS20052-1): 422–4 From here, it travels down the posterior thigh to the [popliteal fossa](./Popliteal_fossa "Popliteal fossa"). The nerve travels in the [posterior compartment of the thigh](./Posterior_compartment_of_thigh "Posterior compartment of thigh") behind (superficial to) the [adductor magnus muscle](./Adductor_magnus_muscle "Adductor magnus muscle") and is itself in front of (deep to) the long head of the [biceps femoris muscle](./Biceps_femoris_muscle "Biceps femoris muscle"). At the popliteal fossa, the nerve divides into its two branches: [[1]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS20052-1): 532

- The [tibial nerve](./Tibial_nerve "Tibial nerve"), which travels down the [posterior compartment of the leg](./Posterior_compartment_of_the_leg "Posterior compartment of the leg") into the foot
- The [common fibular nerve](./Common_fibular_nerve "Common fibular nerve") (also called the [common peroneal nerve](./Common_peroneal_nerve "Common peroneal nerve")), which travels down the [anterior](./Anterior_compartment_of_leg "Anterior compartment of leg") and [lateral compartments of the leg](./Lateral_compartment_of_leg "Lateral compartment of leg") into the [foot](./Foot "Foot")

The sciatic nerve is the largest nerve in the human body.[[2]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-2): 422–4[[3]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-4)

## Function

The sciatic nerve supplies sensation to the [skin](./Skin "Skin") of the [foot](./Foot "Foot"), as well as the entire lower leg (except for its inner side). Sensation to skin to the sole of the foot is provided by the [tibial nerve](./Tibial_nerve "Tibial nerve"), and the lower leg and upper surface of the foot via the [common fibular nerve](./Common_fibular_nerve "Common fibular nerve").[[2]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-2): 422–4

The sciatic nerve also innervates muscles. In particular:[[2]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-GRAYS2005-2): 422–4

- Via the [tibial nerve](./Tibial_nerve "Tibial nerve"), the muscles in the [posterior compartment of the leg](./Posterior_compartment_of_the_leg "Posterior compartment of the leg") and sole of the foot (plantar aspect).[[a]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-5)
- Via the [common fibular nerve](./Common_fibular_nerve "Common fibular nerve"), the muscles in the [anterior](./Anterior_compartment_of_the_leg "Anterior compartment of the leg") and [lateral compartments](./Lateral_compartment_of_the_leg "Lateral compartment of the leg") of the leg.[[b]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-6)

## Clinical significance

### Sciatica

Main article: [Sciatica](./Sciatica "Sciatica")

Pain caused by compression or irritation of the sciatic nerve by a problem in the lumbar region is called [sciatica](./Sciatica "Sciatica"). Common causes of sciatica include the following lower back and hip conditions: [spinal disc herniation](./Spinal_disc_herniation "Spinal disc herniation"), [degenerative disc disease](./Degenerative_disc_disease "Degenerative disc disease"), [lumbar spinal stenosis](./Lumbar_spinal_stenosis "Lumbar spinal stenosis"), [spondylolisthesis](./Spondylolisthesis "Spondylolisthesis"), and [piriformis syndrome](./Piriformis_syndrome "Piriformis syndrome").[[5]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-7) Other acute causes of sciatica include [coughing](./Cough "Cough"), muscular [hypertension](./Hypertension "Hypertension"), and [sneezing](./Sneezing "Sneezing").[[6]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-8)

### Injury

A sciatic nerve injury occurs between 0.5% and 2.0% of the time during a [hip](./Hip_replacement "Hip replacement") replacement.[[7]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-Stiehl2-9) Sciatic nerve palsy is a complication of total hip arthroplasty with an incidence of 0.2% to 2.8% of the time, or with an incidence of 1.7% to 7.6% following revision.[[8]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-10) Following the procedure, in rare cases, a screw, broken piece of trochanteric wire, fragment of [methyl methacrylate](./Methyl_methacrylate "Methyl methacrylate") bone cement, or Burch-Schneider antiprofusio cage can impinge on the nerve; this can cause sciatic nerve [palsy](./Palsy "Palsy"), which may resolve after the fragment is removed and the nerve is freed. The nerve can be surrounded by oxidised, regenerated cellulose to prevent further scarring. Sciatic nerve palsy can also result from severe [spinal stenosis](./Spinal_stenosis "Spinal stenosis") following the procedure, which can be addressed by [spinal decompression](./Spinal_decompression "Spinal decompression") surgery.[[7]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-Stiehl2-9)[[9]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-11) It is unclear if [inversion therapy](./Inversion_therapy "Inversion therapy") is able to decompress the sacral vertebrae; it may only work on the lumbar aspects of the sciatic nerves.

A sciatic nerve injury may also occur from improperly performed injections into the buttock, and may result in sensory loss.[[10]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-Andrews-12): 66

### Other disease

Bernese periacetabular [osteotomy](./Osteotomy "Osteotomy") resulted in major nerve deficits in the sciatic or femoral nerves in 2.1% of 1760 patients, of whom approximately half experienced complete recovery within a mean of 5.5 months.[[11]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-13)

Sciatic nerve exploration can be done by [endoscopy](./Endoscopy "Endoscopy") in a [minimally invasive](./Minimally_invasive "Minimally invasive") procedure to assess lesions of the nerve.[[12]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-14) Endoscopic treatment for sciatic nerve entrapment has been investigated in [deep gluteal syndrome](./Deep_gluteal_syndrome "Deep gluteal syndrome"). Patients were treated with sciatic nerve decompression by [resection](./Segmental_resection "Segmental resection") of fibrovascular scar bands, [piriformis](./Piriformis "Piriformis") tendon release, [obturator internus](./Obturator_internus "Obturator internus"), or [quadratus femoris](./Quadratus_femoris "Quadratus femoris"), or by [hamstring](./Hamstring "Hamstring") tendon scarring.[[13]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-15)

### Anaesthetic

Signals from the sciatic nerve and its branches can be blocked, in order to interrupt the transmission of [pain signals](./Pain_signal "Pain signal") from the innervation area, by performing a [regional nerve blockade](./Local_anesthetic_nerve_block "Local anesthetic nerve block") called a [sciatic nerve block](./Sciatic_nerve_block "Sciatic nerve block").

## Society and culture

According to [Jewish law](./Jewish_law "Jewish law"), the sciatic nerve ([Hebrew](./Hebrew "Hebrew"): [Gid hanasheh](./Gid_hanasheh "Gid hanasheh")) may not be eaten by Jews to commemorate [Jacob](./Jacob "Jacob")'s injury in his struggle with an [angel](./Angel "Angel").[[14]](./Sciatic_nerve#cite_note-16)
