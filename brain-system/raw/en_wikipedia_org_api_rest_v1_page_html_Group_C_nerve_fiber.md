**Group C nerve fibers** are one of three classes of [nerve fiber](./Nerve_fiber "Nerve fiber") in the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS) and [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS). The Group C fibers are [unmyelinated](./Myelin "Myelin") and have a small diameter and low conduction velocity, whereas [Groups A](./Group_A_nerve_fiber "Group A nerve fiber") and [B](./Group_B_nerve_fiber "Group B nerve fiber") are myelinated. Group C fibers include [postganglionic fibers](./Postganglionic_fibers "Postganglionic fibers") in the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") (ANS), and nerve fibers at the [dorsal roots](./Dorsal_roots "Dorsal roots") (IV fiber). These fibers carry sensory information.

Damage or injury to nerve fibers causes [neuropathic pain](./Neuropathic_pain "Neuropathic pain"). [Capsaicin](./Capsaicin "Capsaicin") activates C fibre [vanilloid receptors](./TRPV "TRPV"), providing the burning sensation associated with [chili peppers](./Chili_pepper "Chili pepper").

## Structure and anatomy

### Location

C fibers are one class of [nerve fiber](./Nerve_fiber "Nerve fiber") found in the [nerves](./Nerves "Nerves") of the [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system").[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) They are [afferent nerve fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber"), conveying input signals from the [periphery](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") to the central nervous system.[[2]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig2002-2)

### Structure

C fibers are, unlike other nerve fibers in the central nervous system, [unmyelinated](./Myelin "Myelin").[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) That lack of myelination is the cause of their slow [conduction velocity](./Nerve_conduction_study "Nerve conduction study"), which is on the order of no more than 2 [m/s](./Metre_per_second "Metre per second").[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) C fibers are on average 0.2–1.5 [μm](./Micrometre "Micrometre") in diameter.

### Remak bundles

C fiber axons are grouped together into what is known as Remak bundles.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) These occur when a non-myelinating [Schwann cell](./Schwann_cell "Schwann cell") bundles the axons close together by surrounding them.[[4]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Fagan-4) The Schwann cell keeps them from touching each other by squeezing its [cytoplasm](./Cytoplasm "Cytoplasm") between the axons.[[4]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Fagan-4) The condition of Remak bundles varies with age.[[4]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Fagan-4) The number of C fiber axons in each Remak bundle varies with location.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) For example, in a rat model, large bundles of greater than 20 axons are found exiting the L5 [dorsal root ganglion](./Dorsal_root_ganglion "Dorsal root ganglion"), while smaller bundles of average 3 axons are found in distal nerve segments.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) Multiple neurons contribute axons to the Remak bundle with an average ratio of about 2 axons contributed per bundle.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) The cross sectional area of a Remak bundle is proportional to the number of axons found inside it.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) Remak bundles in the distal peripheral nerve are clustered with other Remak bundles.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) The Remak Schwann cells have been shown to be electrochemically responsive to [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential") of the axons contained within them.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3)

In experiments where nerve injury is caused but nearby C fibers remain intact, increased spontaneous activity in the C fibers is observed.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) This phenomenon supports the theory that damaged nerve fibers may release factors that alter the function of neighboring undamaged fibers.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) Study of Remak bundles has important implications in nerve regeneration after sustaining injury.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) Currently, recovery of distal C fiber function takes months and may still only regain incomplete function.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) This may result in abnormal sensory function or [neuropathic pain](./Neuropathy "Neuropathy").[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3) Remak bundles are thought to release certain trophic factors that promote the regeneration of the damaged axons.[[3]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Murinson-3)

## Pathway

C fibers synapse to second-order projection neurons in the spinal cord at the upper laminae of the [dorsal horn](./Posterior_horn_of_spinal_cord "Posterior horn of spinal cord") in the [substantia gelatinosa](./Substantia_gelatinosa_of_Rolando "Substantia gelatinosa of Rolando").[[5]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Baron1-5) The second-order projection neurons are of the wide dynamic range (WDR) type, which receive input from both nociceptive terminals as well as myelinated A-type fibers.[[5]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Baron1-5) There are three types of second order projection neurons in the [spinothalamic tract](./Spinothalamic_tract "Spinothalamic tract"): wide dynamic range (WDR), high threshold (HT), and low threshold (LT).[[6]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Chung-6) These classifications are based on their responses to mechanical stimuli.[[6]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Chung-6) The second-order neurons ascend to the [brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem") and [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") in the [ventrolateral](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location"), or [anterolateral](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location"), quadrant of the [contralateral](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location") half of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), forming the spinothalamic tract.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) The spinothalamic tract is the main pathway associated with pain and temperature perception, which immediately crosses the spinal cord laterally.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) This crossover feature is clinically important because it allows for identification of the location of injury.

## Function

Because of their higher conduction velocity owing to strong myelination and different activation conditions, Aδ fibers are broadly responsible for the sensation of a quick shallow pain that is specific on one area, termed as first [pain](./Pain "Pain").[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) They respond to a weaker intensity of stimulus.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) C fibers respond to stimuli which have stronger intensities and are the ones to account for the slow, lasting and spread out second pain.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) These fibers are virtually unmyelinated and their conduction velocity is, as a result, much slower which is why they presumably conduct a slower sensation of pain.[[7]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-7)

C fibers are considered polymodal because they can react to various stimuli. They react to stimuli that are thermal, or mechanical, or chemical in nature.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) C fibers respond to all kinds of physiological changes in the body.[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8) For example, they can respond to [hypoxia](./Hypoxia_(medical) "Hypoxia (medical)"), [hypoglycemia](./Hypoglycemia "Hypoglycemia"), hypo-osmolarity, the presence of muscle metabolic products, and even light or sensitive touch.[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8) C fiber receptors include:

- C fiber nociceptors
  - responsible for the second, burning pain
- C fiber warming specific receptors
  - responsible for warmth
- ultra-slow histamine-selective C fibers
  - responsible for itch
- tactile C fibers
  - sensual touch
  - includes CT fibres, also known as C low-threshold mechanoreceptors (CLTM), which are [unmyelinated](./Unmyelinated "Unmyelinated") afferents found in human hairy skin, and have a low mechanical threshold < 5 milliNewtons. They have moderate adaptation and may exhibit fatigue on repetitive stimulation and "afterdischarges" for several seconds after a stimulus.[[9]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Loken-9)
- C mechano- and metabo- receptors in muscles or joints
  - responsible for muscle exercise, burn and cramp[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8)

This variation of input signals calls for a variety of cells of the [cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") in lamina 1 to have different modality-selectiveness and morphologies.[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8) These varying neurons are responsible for the different feelings we perceive in our body and can be classified by their responses to ranges of stimuli.[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8) The brain uses the integration of these signals to maintain [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") in the body whether it is temperature related or pain related.[[8]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Craig-8)

### Vanilloid receptor

The [vanilloid receptor](./Vanilloid_receptor "Vanilloid receptor") (VR-1, TRPV1) is a [receptor](./Sensory_receptor "Sensory receptor") that is found on the free nerve endings of both C and Aδ fibers that responds to elevated levels of heat (>43 °C) and the chemical [capsaicin](./Capsaicin "Capsaicin").[[10]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Neuro-10) Capsaicin activates C fibers by opening a [ligand](./Ligand "Ligand")-gated [ion channel](./Ion_channel "Ion channel") and causing an action potential to occur.[[10]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Neuro-10) Because this receptor responds to both capsaicin and heat, chili peppers are sensed as hot.[[10]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Neuro-10) VR-1 is also able to respond to [extracellular](./Extracellular "Extracellular") acidification and can integrate simultaneous exposure to all three sensory stimuli.[[11]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Davis-11) VR1 is essential for the inflammatory sensitization to noxious thermal stimuli.[[11]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Davis-11) A second type of receptor, a [vanilloid-like receptor](./TRPV2 "TRPV2") (TRPV2,VRL-1), has a higher threshold of activation regarding heat of about 52 °C and also responds to capsaicin and low pH.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) Both types of receptors are [transmembrane receptors](./Transmembrane_receptor "Transmembrane receptor") that are closed during resting conditions.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) When open, these receptors allow for an influx of [sodium](./Sodium "Sodium") and [calcium](./Calcium "Calcium") which initiates an action potential across the fibers.[[1]](./Group_C_nerve_fiber#cite_note-Purves-1) Both receptors are part of a larger family of receptors called [transient receptor potential](./Transient_receptor_potential "Transient receptor
