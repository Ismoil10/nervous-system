Neuroendocrinology

Branch of biology studying interactions between the nervous and endocrine systems



For the journal, see [Neuroendocrinology (journal)](./Neuroendocrinology_(journal) "Neuroendocrinology (journal)").

**Neuroendocrinology** is the branch of [biology](./Biology "Biology") (specifically of [physiology](./Physiology "Physiology")) which studies the interaction between the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") and the [endocrine system](./Endocrine_system "Endocrine system"); i.e. how the [brain](./Brain "Brain") regulates the [hormonal](./Hormonal "Hormonal") activity in the body.[[1]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-1) The nervous and endocrine systems often act together in a process called [neuroendocrine integration](./Neuroendocrine "Neuroendocrine"), to regulate the [physiological](./Physiological "Physiological") processes of the human body. Neuroendocrinology arose from the recognition that the brain, especially the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), controls [secretion](./Secretion "Secretion") of [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland") hormones, and has subsequently expanded to investigate numerous interconnections of the endocrine and nervous systems.

The [endocrine](./Endocrine "Endocrine") system consists of numerous glands throughout the body that produce and secrete [hormones](./Hormones "Hormones") of diverse chemical structure, including [peptides](./Peptides "Peptides"), [steroids](./Steroids "Steroids"), and [neuroamines](./Amine "Amine"). Collectively, hormones regulate many physiological processes. The neuroendocrine system is the mechanism by which the hypothalamus maintains [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis"), regulating [reproduction](./Reproduction "Reproduction"), [metabolism](./Metabolism "Metabolism"), [eating](./Food_intake "Food intake") and drinking behaviour, [energy utilization](./Energy_homeostasis "Energy homeostasis"), [osmolarity](./Osmolarity "Osmolarity") and [blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure").

## Neuroendocrine system

### Hypothalamus

Hypothalamic interaction with the posterior and anterior pituitary glands. The hypothalamus produces the hormones oxytocin and vasopressin in its endocrine cells (left). These are released at nerve endings in the posterior pituitary gland and then secreted into the systemic circulation. The hypothalamus releases tropic hormones into the hypophyseal portal system to the anterior pituitary (right). The anterior pituitary then secretes trophic hormones into the circulation which elicit different responses from various target tissues. These responses then signal back to the hypothalamus and anterior pituitary to either stop producing or continue to produce their precursor signals.



Main article: [Hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus")

The [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") is commonly known as an integration center of the brain because of its role in integrating inputs from all areas of the brain and producing a specific response. In the neuroendocrine system, the hypothalamus receives electrical signals from different parts of the brain and translates those electrical signals into chemical signals in the form of hormones or releasing factors. These chemicals are then transported to the pituitary gland and from there to the systemic circulation.[[2]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:1-2)

### Pituitary gland

Main article: [Pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland")

The pituitary gland is divided into three lobes: the [anterior pituitary](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary"), the [intermediate pituitary lobe](./Pars_intermedia "Pars intermedia"), and the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary"). The hypothalamus controls the anterior pituitary's hormone secretion by sending releasing factors, called [tropic hormones](./Tropic_hormone "Tropic hormone"), down the hypothalamo-hypophysial portal system.[[3]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:03-3) For example, [thyrotropin-releasing hormone](./Thyrotropin-releasing_hormone "Thyrotropin-releasing hormone") released by the hypothalamus in to the portal system stimulates the secretion of [thyroid-stimulating hormone](./Thyroid-stimulating_hormone "Thyroid-stimulating hormone") by the anterior pituitary.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

The posterior pituitary is directly innervated by the hypothalamus; the hormones [oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") are synthesized by neuroendocrine cells in the hypothalamus and stored at the nerve endings in the posterior pituitary. They are secreted directly into [systemic circulation](./Systemic_circulation "Systemic circulation") by the hypothalamic neurons.[[3]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:03-3)

### Major neuroendocrine axes

[Oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") (also called anti-diuretic hormone), the two [neurohypophysial hormones](./Neurohypophysial_hormone "Neurohypophysial hormone") of the [posterior pituitary gland](./Posterior_pituitary_gland "Posterior pituitary gland") (the neurohypophysis), are secreted from the nerve endings of [magnocellular neurosecretory cells](./Magnocellular_neurosecretory_cell "Magnocellular neurosecretory cell") into the systemic circulation. The cell bodies of the oxytocin and vasopressin neurons are in the [paraventricular nucleus](./Paraventricular_nucleus "Paraventricular nucleus") and [supraoptic nucleus](./Supraoptic_nucleus "Supraoptic nucleus") of the hypothalamus, respectively,[[2]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:1-2) and the electrical activity of these neurons is regulated by [afferent synaptic inputs](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") from other brain regions.[[4]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-4)

By contrast, the hormones of the [anterior pituitary gland](./Anterior_pituitary_gland "Anterior pituitary gland") (the adenohypophysis) are secreted from endocrine cells that, in mammals, are not directly innervated, yet the secretion of these hormones ([adrenocorticotrophic hormone](./Adrenocorticotrophic_hormone "Adrenocorticotrophic hormone"), luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone, thyroid-stimulating hormone, [prolactin](./Prolactin "Prolactin"), and [growth hormone](./Growth_hormone "Growth hormone")) remains under the control of the hypothalamus. The hypothalamus controls the anterior pituitary gland via *releasing factors* and *release-inhibiting factors*; these are substances released by hypothalamic neurons into blood vessels at the base of the brain, at the [median eminence](./Median_eminence "Median eminence").[[5]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-5) These vessels, the hypothalamo-hypophysial portal vessels, carry the hypothalamic factors to the anterior pituitary, where they bind to specific receptors on the surface of the hormone-producing cells.[[3]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:03-3)

For example, the secretion of growth hormone is controlled by two neuroendocrine systems: the [growth hormone-releasing hormone](./Growth_hormone-releasing_hormone "Growth hormone-releasing hormone") (GHRH) neurons and the [somatostatin](./Somatostatin "Somatostatin") neurons, which stimulate and inhibit GH secretion, respectively.[[6]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:2-6) The GHRH neurons are located in the [arcuate nucleus](./Arcuate_nucleus "Arcuate nucleus") of the hypothalamus, whereas the somatostatin cells involved in growth hormone regulation are in the [periventricular nucleus](./Periventricular_nucleus "Periventricular nucleus"). These two neuronal systems project axons to the median eminence, where they release their [peptides](./Peptides "Peptides") into portal blood vessels for transport to the anterior pituitary. Growth hormone is secreted in pulses, which arise from alternating episodes of GHRH release and somatostatin release, which may reflect neuronal interactions between the GHRH and somatostatin cells, and negative feedback from growth hormone.[[6]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:2-6)

### Functions

The neuroendocrine systems control reproduction[[7]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-7) in all its aspects, from bonding to sexual behaviour. They control [spermatogenesis](./Spermatogenesis "Spermatogenesis") and the ovarian cycle, [parturition](./Parturition "Parturition"), [lactation](./Lactation "Lactation"), and [maternal behaviour](./Maternal_bond "Maternal bond")[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]. They control the body's response to stress[[8]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:0-8) and [infection](./Infection "Infection").[[9]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-:4-9) They regulate the body's [metabolism](./Metabolism "Metabolism"), influencing eating and drinking behaviour, and influence how energy intake is utilised, that is, how fat is metabolised.[[10]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-10) They influence and regulate mood,[[11]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-11) body fluid and electrolyte homeostasis,[[12]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-12) and [blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure").[[13]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-13)

The neurons of the neuroendocrine system are large; they are mini *factories* for producing secretory products; their nerve terminals are large and organised in coherent terminal fields; their output can often be measured easily in the blood; and what these neurons do and what stimuli they respond to are readily open to hypothesis and experiment. Hence, neuroendocrine neurons are good "model systems" for studying general questions, like "how does a neuron regulate the synthesis, packaging, and secretion of its product?" and "how is [information encoded](./Neural_coding "Neural coding") in electrical activity?"[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## History

### Pioneers

[Walter Lee Gaines](./Walter_Lee_Gaines "Walter Lee Gaines") noted the activity of the pituitary in the lactation of cows in 1915.[[14]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-14) He also noted that anaesthesia could block lactation and response to the suckling reflex.[[15]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-15)

Ernst and [Berta Scharrer](./Berta_Scharrer "Berta Scharrer"),[[16]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-16) of [LMU Munich](./LMU_Munich "LMU Munich") the [Albert Einstein College of Medicine](./Albert_Einstein_College_of_Medicine "Albert Einstein College of Medicine") are credited as co-founders the field of neuroendocrinology with their initial observations and proposals in 1945 concerning [neuropeptides](./Neuropeptide "Neuropeptide").

[Geoffrey Harris](./Geoffrey_Harris_(neuroendocrinologist) "Geoffrey Harris (neuroendocrinologist)")[[17]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-17) is considered by many to be the "father" of neuroendocrinology. Harris, the Dr. Lee's Professor of Anatomy at [Oxford University](./Oxford_University "Oxford University"), is credited with showing that the [anterior pituitary gland](./Anterior_pituitary_gland "Anterior pituitary gland") of [mammals](./Mammals "Mammals") is regulated by hormones secreted by hypothalamic [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") into the [hypothalamohypophysial](./Hypothalamohypophysial "Hypothalamohypophysial") [portal circulation](./Portal_circulation "Portal circulation"). By contrast, the hormones of the [posterior pituitary gland](./Posterior_pituitary_gland "Posterior pituitary gland") are secreted into the [systemic circulation](./Systemic_circulation "Systemic circulation") directly from the nerve endings of hypothalamic neurons. This seminal work was done in collaboration with [Dora Jacobsohn](./Dora_Jacobsohn "Dora Jacobsohn") of [Lund University](./Lund_University "Lund University").[[18]](./Neuroendocrinology#cite_note-18)
