The **dopamine transporter** (**DAT**, also **sodium-dependent dopamine transporter**) is a membrane-spanning protein coded for in humans by the *SLC6A3* [gene](./Gene "Gene") (also known as *DAT1*), that pumps the [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine") out of the [synaptic cleft](./Synaptic_cleft "Synaptic cleft") back into [cytosol](./Cytosol "Cytosol"). In the cytosol, other transporters sequester the dopamine into [vesicles](./Vesicle_(biology_and_chemistry) "Vesicle (biology and chemistry)") for storage and later release. Dopamine reuptake via DAT provides the primary mechanism through which dopamine is cleared from [synapses](./Synapse "Synapse"), although there may be an exception in the [prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex"), where evidence points to a possibly larger role of the [norepinephrine transporter](./Norepinephrine_transporter "Norepinephrine transporter").[[5]](./Dopamine_transporter#cite_note-pmid2117046-5)

DAT is implicated in a number of dopamine-related disorders, including [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](./ADHD "ADHD"), [bipolar disorder](./Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder"), [clinical depression](./Clinical_depression "Clinical depression"), [eating disorders](./Eating_disorder "Eating disorder"), and [substance use disorders](./Substance_use_disorder "Substance use disorder"). The gene that encodes the DAT protein is located on [chromosome 5](./Chromosome_5 "Chromosome 5"), consists of 15 coding [exons](./Exons "Exons"), and is roughly 64 [kbp](./Base_pair#Length_measurements "Base pair") long. Evidence for the associations between DAT and dopamine related disorders has come from a type of [genetic polymorphism](./Genetic_polymorphism "Genetic polymorphism"), known as a [variable number tandem repeat](./Variable_number_tandem_repeat "Variable number tandem repeat"), in the *SLC6A3* gene, which influences the amount of protein expressed.[[6]](./Dopamine_transporter#cite_note-Vandenbergh_DJ_1992_1104–1106-6)
