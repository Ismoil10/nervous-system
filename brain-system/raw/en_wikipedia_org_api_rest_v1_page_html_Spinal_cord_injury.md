A **spinal cord injury** (**SCI**) is damage to the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") that causes temporary or permanent changes in its function. It is a destructive neurological and pathological state that causes major motor, sensory and autonomic dysfunctions.[[3]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-3)

Symptoms of spinal cord injury may include loss of muscle function, [sensation](./Sensory_system "Sensory system"), or [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") function in the parts of the body served by the spinal cord below the level of the injury. Injury can occur at any level of the spinal cord and can be *complete*, with a total loss of sensation and muscle function at lower sacral segments, or *incomplete*, meaning some nervous signals are able to travel past the injured area of the cord up to the Sacral S4-5 spinal cord segments. Depending on the location and severity of damage, the symptoms vary, from [numbness](./Numbness "Numbness") to [paralysis](./Paralysis "Paralysis"), including bowel or bladder [incontinence](./Bladder_incontinence "Bladder incontinence"). Long term outcomes also range widely, from full recovery to permanent [tetraplegia](./Tetraplegia "Tetraplegia") (also called quadriplegia) or [paraplegia](./Paraplegia "Paraplegia"). Complications can include [muscle atrophy](./Muscle_atrophy "Muscle atrophy"), loss of voluntary motor control, [spasticity](./Spasticity "Spasticity"), [pressure sores](./Pressure_sore "Pressure sore"), [infections](./Infection "Infection"), and [breathing problems](./Breathing_problem "Breathing problem").

In the majority of cases the damage results from [physical trauma](./Physical_trauma "Physical trauma") such as [car accidents](./Car_accident "Car accident"), [gunshot wounds](./Gunshot_wound "Gunshot wound"), [falls](./Falling_(accident) "Falling (accident)"), or [sports injuries](./Sports_injuries "Sports injuries"), but it can also result from nontraumatic causes such as [infection](./Infection "Infection"), [insufficient blood flow](./Hypoperfusion "Hypoperfusion"), and [tumors](./Tumor "Tumor"). Just over half of injuries affect the [cervical spine](./Cervical_spine "Cervical spine"), while 15% occur in each of the [thoracic spine](./Thoracic_spine "Thoracic spine"), border between the thoracic and [lumbar spine](./Lumbar_spine "Lumbar spine"), and lumbar spine alone.[[1]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-ATLS2018-1) Diagnosis is typically based on symptoms and [medical imaging](./Medical_imaging "Medical imaging").[[1]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-ATLS2018-1)

Efforts to prevent SCI include individual measures such as using safety equipment, societal measures such as safety regulations in sports and traffic, and improvements to equipment. Treatment starts with [restricting further motion of the spine](./Spinal_motion_restriction "Spinal motion restriction") and maintaining adequate [blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure").[[1]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-ATLS2018-1) [Corticosteroids](./Corticosteroids "Corticosteroids") have not been found to be useful.[[1]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-ATLS2018-1) Other interventions vary depending on the location and extent of the injury, from bed rest to surgery. In many cases, spinal cord injuries require long-term [physical](./Physical_therapy "Physical therapy") and [occupational therapy](./Occupational_therapy "Occupational therapy"), especially if it interferes with [activities of daily living](./Activities_of_daily_living "Activities of daily living").

In the [United States](./United_States "United States"), about 12,000 people annually survive a spinal cord injury.[[2]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-NS2012-2) The most commonly affected group are [young adult](./Young_adult_(psychology) "Young adult (psychology)") males.[[2]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-NS2012-2) SCI has seen great improvements in its care since the middle of the 20th century. Research into potential treatments includes [stem cell](./Stem_cell "Stem cell") implantation, hypothermia, engineered materials for tissue support, [epidural](./Epidural "Epidural") [spinal stimulation](./Spinal_stimulation "Spinal stimulation"), and wearable [robotic exoskeletons](./Powered_exoskeleton "Powered exoskeleton").[[4]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-Toward_Functional_Restoration-4)

Spinal cord injury can be [traumatic](./Major_trauma "Major trauma") or nontraumatic,[[5]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-Sabapathy15-5) and can be classified into three types based on cause: mechanical forces, toxic, and [ischemic](./Ischemia "Ischemia") from lack of blood flow.[[6]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewmanFleisherFink2008348-6) The damage can also be divided into [primary and secondary injury](./Primary_and_secondary_injury "Primary and secondary injury"): the cell death that occurs immediately in the original injury, and [biochemical cascades](./Biochemical_cascade "Biochemical cascade") that are initiated by the original insult and cause further tissue damage.[[7]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewmanFleisherFink2008335-7) These secondary injury pathways include the [ischemic cascade](./Ischemic_cascade "Ischemic cascade"), [inflammation](./Inflammation "Inflammation"), [swelling](./Edema "Edema"), [cell suicide](./Apoptosis "Apoptosis"), and [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") imbalances.[[7]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTENewmanFleisherFink2008335-7) They can take place for minutes or weeks following the injury.[[8]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-Yu15-8)

At each level of the spinal column, [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") branch off from either side of the spinal cord and exit between a pair of [vertebrae](./Vertebra "Vertebra"), to innervate a specific part of the body. The area of skin innervated by a specific spinal nerve is called a [dermatome](./Dermatome_(anatomy) "Dermatome (anatomy)"), and the group of muscles innervated by a single spinal nerve is called a [myotome](./Myotome "Myotome"). The part of the spinal cord that was damaged corresponds to the spinal nerves at that level and below. Injuries can be cervical 1–8 (C1–C8), thoracic 1–12 (T1–T12), lumbar 1–5 (L1–L5),[[9]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTECifuLew2013197-9) or sacral (S1–S5).[[10]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-NINDS13-10) A person's level of injury is defined as the lowest level of full sensation and function.[[11]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerMarini2012138-11) Paraplegia occurs when the legs are affected by the spinal cord damage (in thoracic, lumbar, or sacral injuries), and tetraplegia occurs when all four limbs are affected (cervical damage).[[12]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTEField-Fote20095-12)

SCI is also classified by the degree of impairment. The *International Standards for Neurological Classification of Spinal Cord Injury* (ISNCSCI), published by the [American Spinal Injury Association](./American_Spinal_Injury_Association "American Spinal Injury Association") (ASIA), is widely used to document sensory and motor impairments following SCI.[[13]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-Marino-13) It is based on neurological responses, touch and pinprick sensations tested in each dermatome, and strength of the muscles that control key motions on both sides of the body.[[14]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-ISCOS-14)

In a "complete" spinal injury, all functions below the injured area are lost, whether or not the spinal cord is severed.[[10]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-NINDS13-10) An "incomplete" spinal cord injury involves preservation of motor or sensory function below the level of injury in the spinal cord.[[19]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-SCI_Medicine-19) To be classed as incomplete, there must be some preservation of sensation or motion in the areas innervated by S4 to S5,[[20]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-FOOTNOTESabharwal2014840-20) including voluntary [external anal sphincter](./External_anal_sphincter "External anal sphincter") contraction.[[19]](./Spinal_cord_injury#cite_note-SCI_Medicine-19) The nerves in this area are connected to the very lowest region of the spinal cord, and retaining sensation and function in these parts of the body indicates that the spinal cord is only partially damaged.
