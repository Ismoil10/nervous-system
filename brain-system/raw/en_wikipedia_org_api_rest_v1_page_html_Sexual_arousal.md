**Sexual arousal** (also known as **sexual excitement**) describes the [physiological](./Physiology "Physiology") and [psychological](./Psychological "Psychological") responses in preparation for [sexual intercourse](./Sexual_intercourse "Sexual intercourse") or when exposed to [sexual stimuli](./Sexual_stimulation "Sexual stimulation"). A number of physiological responses occur in the body and mind as preparation for sexual intercourse, and continue during intercourse. [Male arousal](./Sexual_arousal#Male_physiological_response) leads to [erection](./Erection "Erection"), [cremaster reflex](./Cremaster_reflex "Cremaster reflex") and [pre-ejaculate](./Pre-ejaculate "Pre-ejaculate"); in [female arousal](./Sexual_arousal#Female_physiological_response), the body's response is engorged sexual tissues (such as [nipples](./Erection_of_nipples "Erection of nipples"), [clitoris](./Clitoral_erection "Clitoral erection"), and [vaginal walls](./Vagina#Microanatomy "Vagina")), changes to the [cervix](./Cervix "Cervix"), and [vaginal lubrication](./Vaginal_lubrication "Vaginal lubrication").[[1]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-1)

Sexual arousal has several stages and may not lead to any actual [sexual activity](./Sexual_activity "Sexual activity") after [mental stimuli](./Stimulus_(psychology) "Stimulus (psychology)") have brought about accompanying physiological changes. [Physical stimuli](./Stimulus_(physiology) "Stimulus (physiology)"), such as touch, and the internal fluctuation of [hormones](./Hormone "Hormone") can influence sexual arousal. Given sufficient and appropriate sexual stimulation in the right context, sexual arousal intensifies to a high level and can precipitate an [orgasm](./Orgasm "Orgasm") as part of the *climax*. Mental or physical stimulation may also be pursued for their own sake, even in the absence of orgasm.

## Erotic stimuli

Main articles: [Sexual stimulation](./Sexual_stimulation "Sexual stimulation") and [Erogenous zone](./Erogenous_zone "Erogenous zone")

Depending on the situation, a person can be sexually aroused by a variety of factors, both physical and mental. A person may be sexually aroused by another person or by particular aspects of that person, or by a non-human object or scenario. The physical stimulation of an [erogenous zone](./Erogenous_zone "Erogenous zone") or acts of [foreplay](./Foreplay "Foreplay") can result in arousal, especially if it is accompanied with the anticipation of imminent sexual activity. Sexual arousal may be assisted by a [romantic](./Romance_(love) "Romance (love)") setting, music or other soothing situation. Sexual arousal can come from [porn](./Porn "Porn") or other sexual material. The potential stimuli for sexual arousal vary from person to person, and from one time to another, as does the level of arousal.

Stimuli can be classified according to the sense involved: [somatosensory](./Somatosensory "Somatosensory") (touch), visual, and [olfactory](./Olfactory "Olfactory") (scent). [Auditory stimuli](./Stimulus_(physiology)#sound "Stimulus (physiology)") are also possible, though they are generally considered secondary in role to the other three.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Erotic stimuli which can result in sexual arousal can include conversation, reading, films or images, or a smell or setting, any of which can generate erotic thoughts and memories in a person. Given the right context, these may lead to the person desiring physical contact, including [kissing](./Kissing "Kissing"), [cuddling](./Cuddling "Cuddling"), and [petting](./Petting "Petting") of an erogenous zone. This may in turn make the person desire direct sexual stimulation of the [breasts](./Breast "Breast"), [nipples](./Nipple "Nipple"), [buttocks](./Buttocks "Buttocks") or [genitals](./Genitals "Genitals"), and further [sexual activity](./Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity").

Erotic stimuli may originate from a source unrelated to the object of subsequent sexual interest. For example, many people may find [nudity](./Nudity "Nudity"), [erotica](./Erotica "Erotica") or [pornography](./Pornography "Pornography") sexually arousing.[[2]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-2) This may generate a general sexual interest that is satisfied by sexual activity. When sexual arousal is achieved by or dependent on the use of objects, it is referred to as [sexual fetishism](./Sexual_fetishism "Sexual fetishism"), or in some instances a [paraphilia](./Paraphilia "Paraphilia").

There is a common belief that women need more time to achieve arousal. However, recent scientific research has shown that there is no considerable difference for the time men and women require to become fully aroused. Scientists from [McGill University Health Centre](./McGill_University_Health_Centre "McGill University Health Centre") in [Montreal](./Montreal "Montreal") used the method of [thermal imaging](./Thermal_imaging "Thermal imaging") to record baseline [temperature](./Temperature "Temperature") change in genital area to define the time necessary for sexual arousal. Researchers studied the time required for an individual to reach the peak of sexual arousal while watching sexually explicit movies or pictures and came to the conclusion that on average women and men took almost the same time for sexual arousal – around 10 minutes.[[3]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-intro-3) The time needed for foreplay is strongly individual and varies from one occasion to the next depending on circumstances.[[3]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-intro-3)

Unlike many animals, [humans](./Human "Human") do not have a [mating season](./Mating_season "Mating season"), and both sexes are capable of sexual arousal throughout the year.

## Disorders

See also: [Sexual arousal disorder](./Sexual_arousal_disorder "Sexual arousal disorder"), [Hypoactive sexual desire disorder](./Hypoactive_sexual_desire_disorder "Hypoactive sexual desire disorder"), and [Female sexual arousal disorder](./Female_sexual_arousal_disorder "Female sexual arousal disorder")

When a person fails to be aroused in a situation that would normally produce arousal and the lack of arousal is persistent, it may indicate a [sexual arousal disorder](./Sexual_arousal_disorder "Sexual arousal disorder") or [hypoactive sexual desire disorder](./Hypoactive_sexual_desire_disorder "Hypoactive sexual desire disorder"). There are many reasons why a person fails to be aroused, including a mental disorder such as [depression](./Depression_(mood) "Depression (mood)"), [drug use](./Recreational_drug_use "Recreational drug use"), or a medical or physical condition. There may also be a lack of sexual desire generally or for the current partner. A person may always have had no or low sexual desire or the lack of desire may have started later in life.

Contrastingly, a person may be [hypersexual](./Hypersexuality "Hypersexuality"), either having high sexual desire in relation to culture or expected development, or suffering from a [persistent genital arousal disorder](./Persistent_genital_arousal_disorder "Persistent genital arousal disorder"), causing spontaneous, persistent, and uncontrollable arousal, and the physiological changes associated with arousal.

## Physiological and psychological responses

### Physiological responses

Sexual arousal causes various physical responses, most significantly in the [sex organs](./Sex_organ "Sex organ") (genital organs). Sexual arousal for a man is usually indicated by the [swelling](./Tumescence "Tumescence") and [erection](./Erection "Erection") of the [penis](./Human_penis "Human penis") when blood fills the [corpus cavernosum](./Corpus_cavernosum_penis "Corpus cavernosum penis"). This is usually the most prominent and reliable sign of sexual arousal in males. In a woman, sexual arousal leads to increased blood flow to the [clitoris](./Clitoris "Clitoris") and the rest of the [vulva](./Vulva "Vulva"), as well as vaginal transudation - the seeping of moisture through the vaginal walls, which serves as [lubrication](./Vaginal_lubrication "Vaginal lubrication"). In both sexes, pupil dilation is an involuntary physiological response to sexual arousal.[[4]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-4) However, the degree of pupil dilation varies with individuals, as does the degree of maximal pupil dilation.[[5]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-6)

#### Male

It is normal to correlate the [erection](./Erection "Erection") of the [penis](./Human_penis "Human penis") with male sexual arousal. Physical or psychological stimulation, or both, leads to [vasodilation](./Vasodilation "Vasodilation") and the increased blood flow engorges the three spongy areas that run along the length of the penis (the two [corpora cavernosa](./Corpus_cavernosum_penis "Corpus cavernosum penis") and the [corpus spongiosum](./Corpus_spongiosum "Corpus spongiosum")). The penis grows enlarged and firm, the skin of the [scrotum](./Scrotum "Scrotum") is pulled tighter, and the [testicles](./Testicle "Testicle") are pulled up against the body.[[7]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-NHS-7) However, the relationship between erection and arousal is not one-to-one (arousal non-concordance).[[8]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-8) After their mid-forties, some men report that they do not always have an erection when they are sexually aroused.[[9]](./Sexual_arousal#cite_note-Janssen-9) Equally, a male erection can occur during sleep ([nocturnal penile tumescence](./Nocturnal_penile_tumescence "Nocturnal penile tumescence")) without conscious sexual arousal or due to mechanical stimulation (e.g., rubbing against the bed sheet) alone.
