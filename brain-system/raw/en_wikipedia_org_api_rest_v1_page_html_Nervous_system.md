In [biology](./Biology "Biology"), the **nervous system** is the [highly complex](./Complex_system "Complex system") part of an [animal](./Animal "Animal") that coordinates its [actions](./Behavior "Behavior") and [sensory](./Sense "Sense") [information](./Information "Information") by transmitting [signals](./Action_potential "Action potential") to and from different parts of its body. The nervous system detects environmental changes that impact the body, then works in tandem with the [endocrine system](./Endocrine_system "Endocrine system") to respond to such events.[[1]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-1) Nervous tissue first arose in [wormlike organisms](./Ediacara_biota "Ediacara biota") about 550 to 600 million years ago. In [vertebrates](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate"), it consists of two main parts, the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS) and the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS). The CNS consists of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"). The PNS consists mainly of [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve"), which are enclosed bundles of long fibers, or [axons](./Axon "Axon"), that connect the CNS to every other part of the body. Nerves that transmit signals from the brain are called [motor nerves](./Motor_nerve "Motor nerve") (efferent), while those nerves that transmit information from the body to the CNS are called [sensory nerves](./Sensory_nerve "Sensory nerve") (afferent). The PNS is divided into two separate subsystems, the [somatic](./Somatic_nervous_system "Somatic nervous system") and [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") nervous systems. The autonomic nervous system is further subdivided into the [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") and [enteric](./Enteric_nervous_system "Enteric nervous system") nervous systems. The sympathetic nervous system is activated in cases of emergencies to mobilize energy, while the parasympathetic nervous system is activated when organisms are in a relaxed state. The enteric nervous system functions to control the [gastrointestinal system](./Gastrointestinal_system "Gastrointestinal system"). Nerves that exit from the brain are called [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve") while those exiting from the spinal cord are called [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve").

The nervous system consists of [nervous tissue](./Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") which, at a cellular level, is defined by the presence of a special type of cell, called the [neuron](./Neuron "Neuron"). Neurons have special structures that allow them to send signals rapidly and precisely to other cells. They send these signals in the form of electrochemical impulses traveling along thin fibers called [axons](./Axon "Axon"), which can be directly transmitted to neighbouring cells through [electrical synapses](./Electrical_synapse "Electrical synapse") or cause chemicals called [neurotransmitters](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") to be released at [chemical synapses](./Chemical_synapse "Chemical synapse"). A cell that receives a synaptic signal from a neuron may be [excited](./Depolarization "Depolarization"), [inhibited](./Hyperpolarization_(biology) "Hyperpolarization (biology)"), or otherwise [modulated](./Neuromodulation "Neuromodulation"). The connections between neurons can form [neural pathways](./Neural_pathway "Neural pathway"), [neural circuits](./Neural_circuit "Neural circuit"), and larger [networks](./Large-scale_brain_networks "Large-scale brain networks") that generate an organism's perception of the world and determine its behavior. Along with neurons, the nervous system contains other specialized cells called [glial cells](./Glial_cell "Glial cell") (or simply glia), which provide structural and metabolic support. Many of the cells and vasculature channels within the nervous system make up the [neurovascular unit](./Neurovascular_unit "Neurovascular unit"), which regulates cerebral blood flow in order to rapidly satisfy the high energy demands of activated neurons.[[2]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-2)

Nervous systems are found in most [multicellular animals](./Multicellular_animal "Multicellular animal"), but vary greatly in complexity.[[3]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-Columbia-3) The only multicellular animals that have no nervous system at all are [sponges](./Sponge "Sponge"), [placozoans](./Placozoa "Placozoa"), and [mesozoans](./Mesozoa "Mesozoa"), which have very simple body plans. The nervous systems of the [radially symmetric](./Radially_symmetric "Radially symmetric") organisms [ctenophores](./Ctenophores "Ctenophores") (comb jellies) and [cnidarians](./Cnidarians "Cnidarians") (which include [anemones](./Sea_anemone "Sea anemone"), [hydras](./Hydras "Hydras"), [corals](./Coral "Coral") and [jellyfish](./Jellyfish "Jellyfish")) consist of a diffuse [nerve net](./Nerve_net "Nerve net"). All other animal species, with the exception of a few types of [worm](./Worm "Worm"), have a nervous system containing a brain, a central cord (or two cords running in parallel), and nerves radiating from the brain and central cord. The size of the nervous system ranges from a few hundred cells in the simplest worms, to around 300 billion cells in [African elephants](./African_elephant "African elephant").[[4]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-4)

The central nervous system functions to send signals from one cell to others, or from one part of the body to others and to receive feedback. Malfunction of the nervous system can occur as a result of genetic defects, physical damage due to trauma or toxicity, infection, or simply [senescence](./Senescence "Senescence"). The medical specialty of [neurology](./Neurology "Neurology") studies disorders of the nervous system and looks for interventions that can prevent or treat them. In the peripheral nervous system, the most common problem is the failure of nerve conduction, which can be due to different causes including [diabetic neuropathy](./Diabetic_neuropathy "Diabetic neuropathy") and demyelinating disorders such as [multiple sclerosis](./Multiple_sclerosis "Multiple sclerosis") and [amyotrophic lateral sclerosis](./Amyotrophic_lateral_sclerosis "Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis"). [Neuroscience](./Neuroscience "Neuroscience") is the field of science that focuses on the study of the nervous system.

## Structure



The nervous system derives its name from nerves, which are cylindrical bundles of fibers (the [axons](./Axon "Axon") of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron")), that emanate from the brain and [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), and branch repeatedly to innervate every part of the body.[[5]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh2-5) Nerves are large enough to have been recognized by the ancient [Egyptians](./Egyptians "Egyptians"), [Greeks](./Greeks "Greeks"), and [Romans](./Roman_people "Roman people"),[[6]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-FingerCh1-6) but their internal structure was not understood until it became possible to examine them using a [microscope](./Microscope "Microscope").[[7]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-7) A microscopic examination shows that nerves consist primarily of axons, along with different membranes that wrap around them and segregate them into [fascicles](./Nerve_fascicle "Nerve fascicle"). The neurons that give rise to nerves do not lie entirely within the nerves themselves—their cell bodies reside within the brain, [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), or peripheral [ganglia](./Ganglion "Ganglion").[[5]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh2-5)

All animals more advanced than sponges have nervous systems. However, even [sponges](./Sponge "Sponge"), unicellular animals, and non-animals such as slime molds have cell-to-cell signalling mechanisms that are precursors to those of neurons.[[8]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-Sakarya-8) In radially symmetric animals such as the jellyfish and hydra, the nervous system consists of a [nerve net](./Nerve_net "Nerve net"), a diffuse network of isolated cells.[[9]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-Ruppert-9) In [bilaterian](./Bilateria "Bilateria") animals, which make up the great majority of existing species, the nervous system has a common structure that originated early in the [Ediacaran](./Ediacaran "Ediacaran") period, over 550 million years ago.[[10]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-Balavoine-10)[[11]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-11)

### Cells

The nervous system contains two main categories or types of cells: [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") and [glial cells](./Glial_cell "Glial cell").

#### Neurons

Structure of a typical [neuron](./Neuron "Neuron") with [Schwann cells](./Schwann_cell "Schwann cell") in the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system")

The nervous system is defined by the presence of a special type of cell—the [neuron](./Neuron "Neuron") (sometimes called "neurone" or "nerve cell").[[5]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh2-5) Neurons can be distinguished from other cells in a number of ways, but their most fundamental property is that they communicate with other cells via [synapses](./Synapse "Synapse"), which are membrane-to-membrane junctions containing molecular machinery that allows rapid transmission of signals, either electrical or chemical.[[5]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh2-5) Many types of neuron possess an [axon](./Axon "Axon"), a protoplasmic protrusion that can extend to distant parts of the body and make thousands of synaptic contacts;[[12]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh4-12) axons typically extend throughout the body in bundles called nerves.

Even in the nervous system of a single species such as humans, hundreds of different types of neurons exist, with a wide variety of morphologies and functions.[[12]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh4-12) These include [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") that transmute physical stimuli such as light and sound into neural signals, and [motor neurons](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron") that transmute neural signals into activation of muscles or glands; however in many species the great majority of neurons participate in the formation of centralized structures (the brain and ganglia) and they receive all of their input from other neurons and send their output to other neurons.[[5]](./Nervous_system#cite_note-KandelCh2-5)
