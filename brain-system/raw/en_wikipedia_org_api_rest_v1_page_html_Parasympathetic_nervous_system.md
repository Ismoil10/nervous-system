The **parasympathetic nervous system** (**PSNS**) is one of the three divisions of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"), the others being the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") and the [enteric nervous system](./Enteric_nervous_system "Enteric nervous system").[[1]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-2)

The autonomic nervous system is responsible for regulating the body's unconscious actions. The parasympathetic system is responsible for stimulation of "rest-and-digest" or "feed-and-breed"[[3]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-3) activities that occur when the body is at rest, especially after eating, including [sexual arousal](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal"), [salivation](./Salivation "Salivation"), [lacrimation](./Tears "Tears") (tears), [urination](./Urination "Urination"), [digestion](./Digestion "Digestion"), and [defecation](./Defecation "Defecation"). Its action is described as being complementary to that of the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system"), which is responsible for stimulating activities associated with the [fight-or-flight response](./Fight-or-flight_response "Fight-or-flight response"). In contrast to the sympathetic division, which predominates during acute stress responses, parasympathetic activity promotes energy conservation, metabolic storage (for example by enhancing glycogen synthesis), and the maintenance of internal [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") over longer time scales.[[4]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-4)

Beyond its role in visceral motor control, parasympathetic activity—particularly via the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve")—forms part of an "inflammatory reflex" in which efferent vagal fibers, acting through cholinergic signaling, dampen the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines and thereby modulate innate immune responses.[[5]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-pavlov-5)[[6]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-6) Non-invasive indices such as [heart rate variability](./Heart_rate_variability "Heart rate variability") (HRV), especially measures that reflect vagal modulation of the [sinoatrial node](./Sinoatrial_node "Sinoatrial node"), are widely used as surrogate markers of cardiac parasympathetic tone in research and clinical settings.[[7]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-An_Overview_of_Heart_Rate_Variabili-7)[[8]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-Heart_Rate_Variability_and_Cardiac-8)

[Nerve fibres](./Axon "Axon") of the parasympathetic nervous system arise from the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"). Specific nerves include several [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves"), specifically the [oculomotor nerve](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve"), [facial nerve](./Facial_nerve "Facial nerve"), [glossopharyngeal nerve](./Glossopharyngeal_nerve "Glossopharyngeal nerve"), and [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve"). Three [spinal nerves](./Spinal_nerve "Spinal nerve") in the [sacrum](./Sacrum "Sacrum") (S2–4), commonly referred to as the [pelvic splanchnic nerves](./Pelvic_splanchnic_nerves "Pelvic splanchnic nerves"), also act as parasympathetic nerves.[[9]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-Tindle_2022_12722–12730-9)

Because of its location, the parasympathetic system is commonly referred to as having "craniosacral outflow", which stands in contrast to the sympathetic nervous system, which is said to have "thoracolumbar outflow".[[10]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-10)

## Structure

The parasympathetic nerves are [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") or *visceral* [[11]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-11)[[12]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-12) branches of the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system") (PNS). Parasympathetic nerve supply arises through three primary areas:

1. Certain **[cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerve "Cranial nerve")** in the cranium, namely the preganglionic parasympathetic nerves (CN III, CN VII, CN IX and CN X) usually arise from specific nuclei in the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS) and synapse at one of four parasympathetic [ganglia](./Ganglion "Ganglion"): [ciliary](./Ciliary_ganglion "Ciliary ganglion"), [pterygopalatine](./Pterygopalatine_ganglion "Pterygopalatine ganglion"), [otic](./Otic_ganglion "Otic ganglion"), or [submandibular](./Submandibular_ganglion "Submandibular ganglion"). From these four ganglia the parasympathetic nerves complete their journey to target tissues via [trigeminal](./Trigeminal "Trigeminal") branches ([ophthalmic nerve](./Ophthalmic_nerve "Ophthalmic nerve"), [maxillary nerve](./Maxillary_nerve "Maxillary nerve"), [mandibular nerve](./Mandibular_nerve "Mandibular nerve")).
2. The **[vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve")** (CN X) does not participate in these cranial [ganglia](./Ganglia "Ganglia") as most of its parasympathetic fibers are destined for a broad array of ganglia on or near thoracic viscera ([esophagus](./Esophagus "Esophagus"), [trachea](./Vertebrate_trachea "Vertebrate trachea"), [heart](./Heart "Heart"), [lungs](./Lungs "Lungs")) and abdominal viscera ([stomach](./Stomach "Stomach"), [pancreas](./Pancreas "Pancreas"), [liver](./Liver "Liver"), [kidneys](./Kidneys "Kidneys"), [small intestine](./Small_intestine "Small intestine"), and about half of the [large intestine](./Large_intestine "Large intestine")). The vagus innervation ends at the junction between the midgut and hindgut, just before the [splenic flexure](./Splenic_flexure "Splenic flexure") of the [transverse colon](./Transverse_colon "Transverse colon").
3. The [**pelvic splanchnic** efferent preganglionic nerve cell bodies](./Pelvic_splanchnic_nerves "Pelvic splanchnic nerves") reside in the [intermediolateral cell column](./Intermediolateral_nucleus "Intermediolateral nucleus") (also termed the sacral parasympathetic nucleus) of the sacral spinal cord segments S2–S4, and their axons exit the vertebral column in the anterior rami of the corresponding spinal nerves before forming the pelvic splanchnic nerves.[[9]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-Tindle_2022_12722–12730-9)[[13]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-13) Their axons continue away from the CNS to synapse at autonomic ganglia that are usually in, or very close to, the wall of the organ of innervation. This differs from the sympathetic nervous system, where synapses between pre- and post-ganglionic efferent nerves in general occur at ganglia that are farther away from the target organ.

As in the sympathetic nervous system, [efferent](./Efferent_nerve "Efferent nerve") parasympathetic nerve signals are carried from the central nervous system to their targets by a system of two [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron"). The first neuron in this pathway is referred to as the [preganglionic](./Preganglionic_neuron "Preganglionic neuron") or [presynaptic neuron](./Presynaptic_neuron "Presynaptic neuron"). Its cell body sits in the central nervous system and its axon usually extends to synapse with the dendrites of a [postganglionic neuron](./Postganglionic_neuron "Postganglionic neuron") somewhere else in the body. The axons of presynaptic parasympathetic neurons are usually long, extending from the CNS into a ganglion that is either very close to or embedded in their target organ. As a result, the postsynaptic parasympathetic nerve fibers are very short.[[14]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-Moore-14): 42 In contrast to the relatively discrete end-organs of the somatic nervous system, many parasympathetic postganglionic fibers form varicosities along their length and release neurotransmitter diffusely, influencing a wider field of smooth muscle and glandular tissue.

### Cranial nerves

The [oculomotor nerve](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve") is responsible for a number of parasympathetic functions related to the eye.[[15]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-15) The oculomotor PNS fibers originate in the [Edinger–Westphal nucleus](./Edinger–Westphal_nucleus "Edinger–Westphal nucleus") in the central nervous system and travel through the [superior orbital fissure](./Superior_orbital_fissure "Superior orbital fissure") to synapse in the [ciliary ganglion](./Ciliary_ganglion "Ciliary ganglion") located just behind the orbit (eye).[[16]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-16) From the ciliary ganglion the postganglionic parasympathetic fibers leave via short ciliary nerve fibers, a continuation of the [nasociliary nerve](./Nasociliary_nerve "Nasociliary nerve") (a branch of ophthalmic division of the [trigeminal nerve](./Trigeminal_nerve "Trigeminal nerve") (CN V1)). The short ciliary nerves innervate the orbit to control the [ciliary muscle](./Ciliary_muscle "Ciliary muscle") (responsible for [accommodation](./Accommodation_(eye) "Accommodation (eye)")) and the [iris sphincter muscle](./Iris_sphincter_muscle "Iris sphincter muscle"), which is responsible for [miosis](./Miosis "Miosis") or constriction of the pupil (in response to light or accommodation). There are two motors that are part of the oculomotor nerve known as the somatic motor and visceral motor. The somatic motor is responsible for moving the eye in precise motions and for keeping the eye fixated on an object. The visceral motor helps constrict the pupil.[[17]](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system#cite_note-17)

The parasympathetic aspect of the [facial nerve](./Facial_nerve "Facial nerve") controls secretion of the sublingual and submandibular [salivary glands](./Salivary_glands "Salivary glands"), the [lacrimal gland](./Lacrimal_gland "Lacrimal gland"), and the glands associated with the [nasal cavity](./Nasal_cavity "Nasal cavity"). The preganglionic fibers originate within the CNS in the superior salivatory nucleus and leave as the [intermediate nerve](./Intermediate_nerve "Intermediate nerve") (which some consider a separate cranial nerve altogether) to connect with the facial nerve just distal (further out) to it surfacing the central nervous system.
