Sensory nervous system

Part of the nervous system



The [visual system](./Visual_system "Visual system") and the [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system") are active even during [resting state fMRI](./Resting_state_fMRI "Resting state fMRI").



Activation and response in the sensory nervous system

The **sensory nervous system** is a part of the [nervous system](./Nervous_system "Nervous system") responsible for processing [sensory](./Sense "Sense") information. A **sensory system** consists of [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") (including the sensory receptor cells), [neural pathways](./Neural_pathway "Neural pathway"), and parts of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") involved in sensory [perception](./Perception "Perception") and [interoception](./Interoception "Interoception"). Commonly recognized sensory systems are those for [vision](./Vision_(sense) "Vision (sense)"), [hearing](./Auditory_system "Auditory system"), [touch](./Somatic_sensation "Somatic sensation"), [taste](./Taste "Taste"), [smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction"), [balance](./Vestibular_system "Vestibular system") and visceral sensation. Sense organs are [transducers](./Transducers "Transducers") that convert data from the outer physical world to the realm of the mind where people interpret the information, creating their [perception](./Perception "Perception") of the world around them.[[1]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-1)

The [receptive field](./Receptive_field "Receptive field") is the area of the body or environment to which a receptor organ and receptor cells respond. For instance, the part of the world an eye can see, is its receptive field; the light that each [rod](./Rod_cell "Rod cell") or [cone](./Cone_cell "Cone cell") can see, is its receptive field.[[2]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-2) [Receptive fields](./Receptive_field "Receptive field") have been identified for the [visual system](./Visual_system "Visual system"), [auditory system](./Auditory_system "Auditory system") and [somatosensory system](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system").

## Senses and receptors

While debate exists among neurologists as to the specific number of senses due to differing definitions of what constitutes a [sense](./Sense "Sense"), [Gautama Buddha](./Gautama_Buddha "Gautama Buddha") and [Aristotle](./Aristotle "Aristotle") classified five 'traditional' human senses which have become universally accepted: [touch](./Touch "Touch"), [taste](./Taste "Taste"), [smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction"), [vision](./Visual_system "Visual system"), and [hearing](./Hearing_(sense) "Hearing (sense)"). Other senses that have been well-accepted in most mammals, including humans, include [pain](./Nociception "Nociception"), [balance](./Equilibrioception "Equilibrioception") and [temperature](./Thermoception "Thermoception"). Furthermore, some nonhuman animals have been shown to possess alternate senses, including [magnetoreception](./Magnetoception "Magnetoception") and [electroreception](./Electroreception "Electroreception").[[3]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-3)

### Receptors

Main article: [sensory receptor](./Sensory_receptor "Sensory receptor")

The initialization of sensation stems from the response of a specific receptor to a physical stimulus. The receptors which react to the stimulus and initiate the process of sensation are commonly characterized in four distinct categories: [chemoreceptors](./Chemoreceptors "Chemoreceptors"), [photoreceptors](./Photoreceptor_cell "Photoreceptor cell"), [mechanoreceptors](./Mechanoreceptors "Mechanoreceptors"), and [thermoreceptors](./Thermoreceptors "Thermoreceptors"). All receptors receive distinct physical stimuli and transduce the signal into an electrical [action potential](./Action_potential "Action potential"). This action potential then travels along [afferent neurons](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") to specific brain regions where it is processed and interpreted.[[4]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-4)

### Chemoreceptors

Main article: [Chemoreceptor](./Chemoreceptor "Chemoreceptor")

Chemoreceptors, or chemosensors, detect certain chemical stimuli and transduce that signal into an electrical action potential. The two primary types of chemoreceptors are:

1. Distance chemoreceptors are integral to receiving stimuli in [gases](./Gases "Gases") in the [olfactory system](./Olfactory_system "Olfactory system") through both olfactory receptor neurons and neurons in the [vomeronasal organ](./Vomeronasal_organ "Vomeronasal organ").
2. Direct chemoreceptors that detect stimuli in [liquids](./Liquids "Liquids") include the [taste buds](./Taste_buds "Taste buds") in the [gustatory system](./Gustatory_system "Gustatory system") as well as receptors in the [aortic bodies](./Aortic_bodies "Aortic bodies") which detect changes in [oxygen](./Oxygen "Oxygen") concentration.[[5]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-5)

### Photoreceptors

Main article: [Photoreceptor cell](./Photoreceptor_cell "Photoreceptor cell")

Photoreceptors are neuron cells and are specialized units that play the main role in initiating vision function. Photoreceptors are light-sensitive cells that capture different wavelengths of light. Different types of photoreceptors are able to respond to the varying light wavelengths in relation to color, and transduce them into electrical signals.[[6]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-6) Photoreceptors are capable of [phototransduction](./Phototransduction "Phototransduction"), a process which converts light ([electromagnetic radiation](./Electromagnetic_radiation "Electromagnetic radiation")) into, among other types of [energy](./Energy "Energy"), a [membrane potential](./Membrane_potential "Membrane potential"). There are five compartments that are present in these cells. Each compartment corresponds to differences in function and structure. The first compartment is the outer segment (OS), where it is responsible for capturing light and transducing it. The second compartment is the inner segment (IS), which includes the necessary organelles that function in cellular metabolism and biosynthesis. Mainly, these organelles include mitochondria, Golgi apparatus, and endoplasmic reticulum, among others. The third compartment is the connecting cilium (CC). As its name suggests, CC works to connect the OS and the IS regions together for the purpose of essential protein trafficking. The fourth compartment contains the nucleus and is a continuation of the IS region, known as the nuclear region. Finally, the fifth compartment is the synaptic region, where it acts as a final terminal for the signal, consisting of synaptic vesicles. In this region, glutamate neurotransmitter is transmitted from the cell to secondary neuron cells.[[7]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-8) The three primary types of photoreceptors are:
[cones](./Cone_cell "Cone cell") are photoreceptors which respond significantly to [color](./Color "Color"). In humans, the three different types of cones correspond with a primary response to short wavelength (blue), medium wavelength (green), and long wavelength (yellow/red).[[9]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2010-9)
[Rods](./Rod_cell "Rod cell") are photoreceptors which are very sensitive to the intensity of light, allowing for vision in dim lighting. The concentrations and ratio of rods to cones is strongly correlated with whether an animal is [diurnal](./Diurnality "Diurnality") or [nocturnal](./Nocturnal "Nocturnal"). In humans, rods outnumber cones by approximately 20:1, while in nocturnal animals, such as the [tawny owl](./Tawny_owl "Tawny owl"), the ratio is closer to 1000:1.[[9]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica_2010-9)
[Ganglion cells](./Retinal_ganglion_cell "Retinal ganglion cell") reside in the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla") and [retina](./Retina "Retina") where they are involved in the [sympathetic response](./Sympathetic_response "Sympathetic response"). Of the ~1.3 million ganglion cells present in the retina, 1–2% are believed to be [photosensitive ganglia](./Intrinsically_photosensitive_retinal_ganglion_cells "Intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells").[[10]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-10) These photosensitive ganglia play a role in conscious vision for some animals,[[11]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-Ecker-11) and are believed to do the same in humans.[[12]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-HoriguchiWinawer2012-12)

### Mechanoreceptors

Main article: [Mechanoreceptor](./Mechanoreceptor "Mechanoreceptor")

Mechanoreceptors are sensory receptors which respond to mechanical forces, such as [pressure](./Pressure "Pressure") or [distortion](./Distortion "Distortion").[[13]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-13) While mechanoreceptors are present in [hair cells](./Hair_cells "Hair cells") and play an integral role in the [vestibular](./Vestibular_system "Vestibular system") and [auditory systems](./Auditory_system "Auditory system"), the majority of mechanoreceptors are [cutaneous](./Cutaneous "Cutaneous") and are grouped into four categories:

1. *Slowly adapting type 1 receptors* have small receptive fields and respond to static stimulation. These receptors are primarily used in the sensations of [form](./Shape "Shape") and [roughness](./Surface_roughness "Surface roughness").
2. *Slowly adapting type 2 receptors* have large receptive fields and respond to stretch. Similarly to type 1, they produce sustained responses to a continued stimuli.
3. *Rapidly adapting receptors* have small receptive fields and underlie the perception of slip.
4. *Pacinian receptors* have large receptive fields and are the predominant receptors for high-frequency vibration.

### Thermoreceptors

Main article: [Thermoreceptor](./Thermoreceptor "Thermoreceptor")

Thermoreceptors are sensory receptors which respond to varying [temperatures](./Temperature "Temperature"). While the mechanisms through which these receptors operate is unclear, recent discoveries have shown that [mammals](./Mammal "Mammal") have at least two distinct types of thermoreceptors:[[14]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-14)

1. The end-bulb of Krause or [bulboid corpuscle](./Bulboid_corpuscle "Bulboid corpuscle") detects temperatures above body temperature.
2. [Ruffini's end organ](./Ruffini_corpuscle "Ruffini corpuscle") detects temperatures below body temperature.

TRPV1 is a heat-activated channel that acts as a small heat detecting thermometer in the membrane which begins the polarization of the neural fiber when exposed to changes in temperature. Ultimately, this allows us to detect ambient temperature in the warm/hot range. Similarly, the molecular cousin to TRPV1, TRPM8, is a cold-activated ion channel that responds to cold. Both cold and hot receptors are segregated by distinct subpopulations of sensory nerve fibers, which shows us that the information coming into the spinal cord is originally separate. Each sensory receptor has its own "labeled line" to convey a simple sensation experienced by the recipient. Ultimately, TRP channels act as thermosensors, channels that help us to detect changes in ambient temperatures.[[15]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-Thermosensor-15)

### Nociceptors

Main article: [Nociceptor](./Nociceptor "Nociceptor")

Nociceptors respond to potentially damaging stimuli by sending signals to the spinal cord and brain. This process, called [nociception](./Nociception "Nociception"), usually causes the perception of [pain](./Pain "Pain").[[16]](./Sensory_nervous_system#cite_note-16) They are found in internal organs, as well as on the surface of the body.
