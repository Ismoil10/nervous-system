In [human anatomy](./Human_anatomy "Human anatomy"), the **sacral plexus** is a [nerve plexus](./Nerve_plexus "Nerve plexus") which provides motor and sensory nerves for the posterior thigh, most of the lower leg and foot, and part of the [pelvis](./Pelvis "Pelvis"). It is part of the [lumbosacral plexus](./Lumbosacral_plexus "Lumbosacral plexus") and emerges from the [lumbar vertebrae](./Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae") and [sacral vertebrae](./Sacral_vertebrae "Sacral vertebrae") (L4-S4).[[1]](./Sacral_plexus#cite_note-Thieme-Atlas-470-1) A **sacral [plexopathy](./Plexopathy "Plexopathy")** is a disorder affecting the nerves of the sacral plexus, usually caused by trauma, nerve compression, vascular disease, or infection. Symptoms may include pain, loss of motor control, and sensory deficits.

## Structure

The sacral plexus is formed by:

- the [lumbosacral trunk](./Lumbosacral_trunk "Lumbosacral trunk")
- the [anterior division](./Anterior_division "Anterior division") of the first [sacral nerve](./Sacral_nerve "Sacral nerve")
- portions of the anterior divisions of the second and third sacral nerves

The nerves forming the sacral plexus converge toward the lower part of the [greater sciatic foramen](./Greater_sciatic_forame "Greater sciatic forame"), and unite to form a flattened band, from the anterior and posterior surfaces of which several branches arise.
The band itself is continued as the [sciatic nerve](./Sciatic_nerve "Sciatic nerve"), which splits on the back of the thigh into the [tibial nerve](./Tibial_nerve "Tibial nerve") and [common fibular nerve](./Common_fibular_nerve "Common fibular nerve"); these two nerves sometimes arise separately from the plexus, and in all cases their independence can be shown by dissection. Often, the sacral plexus and the [lumbar plexus](./Lumbar_plexus "Lumbar plexus") are considered to be one large nerve plexus, the [lumbosacral plexus](./Lumbosacral_plexus "Lumbosacral plexus"). The [lumbosacral trunk](./Lumbosacral_trunk "Lumbosacral trunk") connects the two plexuses.

### Relations

The sacral plexus lies on the back of the pelvis in front of the [piriformis](./Piriformis "Piriformis") muscle and the [pelvic fascia](./Pelvic_fascia "Pelvic fascia"). In front of it are the [internal iliac artery](./Internal_iliac_artery "Internal iliac artery"), [internal iliac vein](./Internal_iliac_vein "Internal iliac vein"), the [ureter](./Ureter "Ureter"), and the [sigmoid colon](./Sigmoid_colon "Sigmoid colon"). The [superior gluteal artery](./Superior_gluteal_artery "Superior gluteal artery") and vein run between the [lumbosacral trunk](./Lumbosacral_trunk "Lumbosacral trunk") and the first sacral nerve, and the [inferior gluteal artery](./Inferior_gluteal_artery "Inferior gluteal artery") and vein between the second and third sacral nerves.
