Orgasm

Intense physical sensation of sexual release



For other uses, see [Orgasm (disambiguation)](./Orgasm_(disambiguation) "Orgasm (disambiguation)").

**Orgasm** (from [Greek](./Greek_language "Greek language") ὀργασμός, *orgasmos*; "excitement, swelling"), **sexual climax**, or simply **climax**, is the sudden release of accumulated [sexual excitement](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal") during the [sexual response cycle](./Human_sexual_response_cycle "Human sexual response cycle"), characterized by intense sexual [pleasure](./Pleasure "Pleasure") resulting in rhythmic, involuntary [muscular contractions](./Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction") in the [pelvic](./Pelvis "Pelvis") region.[[1]](./Orgasm#cite_note-dictbiopsych-1)[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2) Orgasms are controlled by the involuntary or [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") and are experienced by both males and females; the body's response includes muscular [spasms](./Spasm "Spasm") (in multiple areas), a general [euphoric](./Euphoria "Euphoria") sensation, and, frequently, body movements and vocalizations.[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2) The period after orgasm (known as the [resolution phase](./Refractory_period_(sex) "Refractory period (sex)")) is typically a [relaxing experience](./Relaxation_(psychology) "Relaxation (psychology)") due to the release of the [neurohormones](./Neurohormone "Neurohormone") [oxytocin](./Oxytocin "Oxytocin") and [prolactin](./Prolactin "Prolactin"), as well as [endorphins](./Endorphins "Endorphins") (or "endogenous [morphine](./Morphine "Morphine")").[[3]](./Orgasm#cite_note-3)

Human orgasms usually result from physical [sexual stimulation](./Sexual_stimulation "Sexual stimulation") of the [penis](./Human_penis "Human penis") in males (typically accompanied by [ejaculation](./Ejaculation "Ejaculation")) and of the [clitoris](./Clitoris "Clitoris") (and [vagina](./Vagina "Vagina")) in females.[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2)[[4]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Weiten-4)[[5]](./Orgasm#cite_note-O'Connell-5) Sexual stimulation can be by [masturbation](./Masturbation "Masturbation") or with a [sexual partner](./Sexual_partner "Sexual partner") ([penetrative sex](./Sexual_penetration "Sexual penetration"), [non-penetrative sex](./Non-penetrative_sex "Non-penetrative sex"), or other [sexual activity](./Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity")). Physical stimulation is not a requisite, as it is possible to reach orgasm through psychological means.[[6]](./Orgasm#cite_note-6) Reaching orgasm may be difficult without a suitable [psychological state](./Psychological_state "Psychological state"). During sleep, a sex dream can trigger an orgasm and the release of sexual fluids ([nocturnal emission](./Nocturnal_emission "Nocturnal emission")).

The health effects surrounding the human orgasm are diverse. There are many physiological responses during sexual activity, including a relaxed state, as well as changes in the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"), such as a temporary decrease in the [metabolic](./Metabolism "Metabolism") activity of large parts of the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"), while there is no change or increased metabolic activity in the [limbic](./Limbic_system "Limbic system") (i.e., "bordering") areas of the brain.[[7]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Georgiadis-7) There are [sexual dysfunctions](./Sexual_dysfunction "Sexual dysfunction") involving orgasm, such as *[anorgasmia](./Anorgasmia "Anorgasmia")*. The importance of reaching orgasm for sex to be satisfying varies between individuals,[[8]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Kinsey_Institute-8) and theories about the biological and evolutionary functions of orgasm differ.[[9]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Geoffrey_Miller-9)[[10]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Wallen_K,_Lloyd_EA-10)

## Definitions

In a clinical context, orgasm is usually defined strictly by the [muscular contractions](./Muscle_contraction "Muscle contraction") involved during [sexual activity](./Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity"), along with the characteristic patterns of change in [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), [blood pressure](./Blood_pressure "Blood pressure"), and often [respiration rate](./Respiration_rate "Respiration rate") and depth.[[11]](./Orgasm#cite_note-M&J-11) This is categorized as the sudden discharge of accumulated sexual tension during the [sexual response cycle](./Human_sexual_response_cycle "Human sexual response cycle"), resulting in rhythmic muscular contractions in the [pelvic](./Pelvis "Pelvis") region.[[1]](./Orgasm#cite_note-dictbiopsych-1)[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2) Definitions of orgasm vary,[[12]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Levin_2004-12) and at least 26 definitions of orgasm are listed in the 2001 journal *[Clinical Psychology Review](./Clinical_Psychology_Review "Clinical Psychology Review")*.[[13]](./Orgasm#cite_note-mah_&_binik-13)

There is some debate about whether certain types of sexual sensations should be accurately classified as orgasms, including female orgasms caused by [G-spot](./G-spot "G-spot") stimulation alone, and the demonstration of extended or continuous orgasms lasting several minutes or even an hour.[[14]](./Orgasm#cite_note-14) The question centers around the clinical definition of orgasm, but this way of viewing orgasm is merely physiological, while there are also psychological, endocrinological, and neurological definitions of orgasm.[[12]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Levin_2004-12)[[13]](./Orgasm#cite_note-mah_&_binik-13)[[15]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Mah-15) In these and similar cases, the sensations experienced are subjective and do not necessarily involve the involuntary contractions characteristic of orgasm. In both sexes, they are extremely pleasurable and often felt throughout the body, causing a mental state that is often described as transcendental, and with [vasocongestion](./Vasocongestion "Vasocongestion") and associated pleasure comparable to that of a full-contractionary orgasm. For example, modern findings support the distinction between ejaculation and male orgasm.[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2)[[13]](./Orgasm#cite_note-mah_&_binik-13) For this reason, there are views on both sides as to whether these can be accurately defined as orgasms.[[15]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Mah-15)

## Achievement

Organs and tissues involved in triggering the female orgasm

Orgasms can be achieved during a variety of activities, including [vaginal](./Sexual_intercourse "Sexual intercourse"), [anal](./Anal_sex "Anal sex"), [oral](./Oral_sex "Oral sex"), [manual](./Non-penetrative_sex#Manual_sex "Non-penetrative sex"), and [non-penetrative sex](./Non-penetrative_sex "Non-penetrative sex"), or [masturbation](./Masturbation "Masturbation"). They may also be achieved by the use of a [sex toy](./Sex_toy "Sex toy") or an [erotic electrostimulation](./Erotic_electrostimulation "Erotic electrostimulation"). Achieving orgasm by stimulation of the [nipples](./Nipple "Nipple") or other [erogenous zones](./Erogenous_zone "Erogenous zone") is rarer.[[16]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Answer-16)[[17]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Boston-17) Multiple orgasms, i.e. orgasms that occur within a short period of one another,[[18]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Crooks_and_Baur-18) are also possible, especially in women, but uncommon.[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2)[[18]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Crooks_and_Baur-18)

In addition to physical stimulation, orgasm can be achieved from psychological arousal alone, such as during dreaming ([nocturnal emission](./Nocturnal_emission "Nocturnal emission")),[[13]](./Orgasm#cite_note-mah_&_binik-13)[[15]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Mah-15)[[19]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Komisaruk-19) or by [forced orgasm](./Forced_orgasm "Forced orgasm"). Orgasm by psychological stimulation alone was first reported among people who had [spinal cord injuries](./Spinal_cord_injury "Spinal cord injury").[[19]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Komisaruk-19) Although sexual function and [sexuality after spinal cord injury](./Sexuality_after_spinal_cord_injury "Sexuality after spinal cord injury") are very often impacted, this injury does not deprive one of sexual feelings such as [sexual arousal](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal") and erotic desires.[[19]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Komisaruk-19)

Scientific literature focuses on the psychology of female orgasm significantly more than it does on the psychology of male orgasm, which "appears to reflect the assumption that female orgasm is psychologically more complex than male orgasm", but "the limited empirical evidence available suggests that male and female orgasm may bear more similarities than differences. In one controlled study by Vance and Wagner (1976), independent raters could not differentiate written descriptions of male versus female orgasm experiences".[[15]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Mah-15)

In women, the most common way to achieve orgasm is by direct sexual stimulation of the [clitoris](./Clitoris "Clitoris") (meaning consistent [digital](./Fingering_(sexual_act) "Fingering (sexual act)"), [oral](./Cunnilingus "Cunnilingus"), or other concentrated friction against the external parts of the clitoris). General statistics indicate that 70–80 percent of women require direct clitoral stimulation to achieve orgasm,[[2]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Rosenthal-2)[[20]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Flaherty-20)[[21]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Kammerer-Doak-21) although indirect clitoral stimulation (for example, via [vaginal](./Vagina "Vagina") penetration) may also be sufficient.[[5]](./Orgasm#cite_note-O'Connell-5)[[22]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Lloyd-22) The [Mayo Clinic](./Mayo_Clinic "Mayo Clinic") states, "Orgasms vary in intensity, and women vary in the frequency of their orgasms and the amount of stimulation necessary to trigger an orgasm."[[23]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Anorgasmia-23) Clitoral orgasms are easier to achieve because the [glans of the clitoris](./Clitoris#Glans "Clitoris"), or clitoris as a whole, has more than 8,000 sensory [nerve](./Nerve "Nerve") endings, which is as many (or more in some cases) nerve endings as are present in the human penis or [glans penis](./Glans_penis "Glans penis").[[24]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Di_Marino-24)[[25]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Carroll-25)[[26]](./Orgasm#cite_note-26) As the clitoris is [homologous](./Homology_(biology) "Homology (biology)") to the penis, it is equivalent in its capacity to receive sexual stimulation.[[27]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Schünke-27)[[28]](./Orgasm#cite_note-Francoeur-28)
