# Pupillary response

Pupillary response

Physiological response that varies the size of the pupil



Dilation and constriction of the pupil

**Pupillary response** is a [physiological](./Physiology "Physiology") response that varies the [size of the pupil](./Size_of_the_pupil "Size of the pupil") between 1.5 mm and 8 mm,[[1]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-:1-1) via the [optic](./Optic_nerve "Optic nerve") and [oculomotor](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve") cranial nerve.

A **constriction response** ([miosis](./Miosis "Miosis")),[[2]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-2) is the narrowing of the pupil, which may be caused by [scleral buckles](./Scleral_buckle "Scleral buckle") or drugs such as [opiates](./Opiates "Opiates")/[opioids](./Opioids "Opioids") or [anti-hypertension medications](./Antihypertensive "Antihypertensive"). Constriction of the pupil occurs when the [circular muscle](./Iris_sphincter_muscle "Iris sphincter muscle"), controlled by the [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") (PSNS), contracts, and also to an extent when the [radial muscle](./Iris_dilator_muscle "Iris dilator muscle") relaxes.

A **dilation response** ([mydriasis](./Mydriasis "Mydriasis")), is the widening of the pupil and may be caused by [adrenaline](./Adrenaline "Adrenaline"); [anticholinergic](./Anticholinergic "Anticholinergic") agents; [stimulant](./Stimulant "Stimulant") drugs such as [MDMA](./MDMA "MDMA"), [cocaine](./Cocaine "Cocaine"), and [amphetamines](./Amphetamines "Amphetamines"); and some [hallucinogenics](./Hallucinogen "Hallucinogen") (e.g. [LSD](./LSD "LSD")).[[3]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-3) Dilation of the pupil occurs when the smooth cells of the [radial muscle](./Iris_dilator_muscle "Iris dilator muscle"), controlled by the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") (SNS), contract, and also when the cells of the iris sphincter muscle relax.

Constricted (left) and dilated (right) pupil of the same eye

The responses can have a variety of causes, from an [involuntary reflex reaction](./Pupillary_reflex "Pupillary reflex") to exposure or inexposure to light—in low light conditions a dilated pupil lets more light into the eye—or it may indicate [interest](./Interest_(emotion) "Interest (emotion)") in the subject of attention or [arousal](./Arousal "Arousal"), [sexual stimulation](./Sexual_arousal "Sexual arousal"),[[4]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-4) [uncertainty](./Uncertainty "Uncertainty"),[[5]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-:0-6)[[7]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-:2-7) [information content](./Information_content "Information content"),[[6]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-:0-6)[[7]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-:2-7) decision conflict,[[8]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-pmid29107773-8) errors,[[9]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-9) physical activity[[10]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-10) or increasing [cognitive load](./Task-invoked_pupillary_response "Task-invoked pupillary response")[[11]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-11) or cognitive demand. These relationships are investigated in the context of [task-invoked pupillary response](./Task-invoked_pupillary_response "Task-invoked pupillary response"). The responses correlate strongly with activity in the [locus coeruleus](./Locus_coeruleus "Locus coeruleus") [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") system.[[12]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-12)[[13]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-14) The pupils contract immediately before [REM sleep](./Rapid_eye_movement_sleep "Rapid eye movement sleep") begins.[[15]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-15) A pupillary response can be intentionally [conditioned](./Classical_conditioning "Classical conditioning") as a Pavlovian response to some stimuli.[[16]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-16)

Some humans have the ability to exert direct and voluntary control over their [iris sphincter muscles](./Iris_sphincter_muscle "Iris sphincter muscle") and [dilator muscles](./Iris_dilator_muscle "Iris dilator muscle"), granting them the ability to dilate and constrict their pupils on command, regardless of lighting condition and/or eye [accommodation](./Accommodation_(vertebrate_eye) "Accommodation (vertebrate eye)") state.[[17]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-17) However, this ability is very rare, and its potential use or advantages are unclear.

The *latency* of pupillary response (the time in which it takes to occur) increases with age.[[18]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-18)

In [ophthalmology](./Ophthalmology "Ophthalmology"), intensive studies of pupillary response are conducted via videopupillometry.[[19]](./Pupillary_response#cite_note-19)

[Anisocoria](./Anisocoria "Anisocoria") is the condition of one pupil being more dilated than the other.

| Cause in [pupillary light reflex](./Pupillary_light_reflex "Pupillary light reflex") | Increased light | Decreased light |
