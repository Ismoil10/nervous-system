The **olfactory system** is the [sensory system](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system") used for the [sense of smell](./Sense_of_smell "Sense of smell") (olfaction). Olfaction is one of the [special senses](./Special_sense "Special sense") directly associated with specific organs. Most mammals and reptiles have a main olfactory system and an [accessory olfactory system](./Accessory_olfactory_system "Accessory olfactory system"). The main olfactory system detects airborne substances, while the accessory system senses fluid-phase stimuli.

The senses of smell and taste ([gustatory system](./Gustatory_system "Gustatory system")) are often referred to together as the [chemosensory](./Chemosensory "Chemosensory") system, because they both give the brain information about the chemical composition of objects through a process called [transduction](./Transduction_(physiology) "Transduction (physiology)").

## Structure

This diagram linearly (unless otherwise mentioned) tracks the projections of all known structures that allow for olfaction to their relevant endpoints in the human brain.

### Peripheral

The peripheral olfactory system consists mainly of the [nostrils](./Nostril "Nostril"), [ethmoid bone](./Ethmoid_bone "Ethmoid bone"), [nasal cavity](./Nasal_cavity "Nasal cavity"), and the [olfactory epithelium](./Olfactory_epithelium "Olfactory epithelium") (layers of thin tissue covered in [mucus](./Mucus "Mucus") that line the nasal cavity). The primary components of the layers of [epithelial tissue](./Epithelial_tissue "Epithelial tissue") are the [mucous membranes](./Mucous_membranes "Mucous membranes"), [olfactory glands](./Olfactory_glands "Olfactory glands"), [olfactory neurons](./Olfactory_receptor_neurons "Olfactory receptor neurons"), and [nerve fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") of the [olfactory nerves](./Olfactory_nerves "Olfactory nerves").[[1]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-1)

Odor molecules can enter the peripheral pathway and reach the nasal cavity either through the nostrils when inhaling ([olfaction](./Olfaction "Olfaction")) or through the throat when the tongue pushes air to the back of the nasal cavity while chewing or swallowing (retro-nasal olfaction).[[2]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-Boroditsky-2) Inside the nasal cavity, mucus lining the walls of the cavity dissolves odor molecules. Mucus also covers the olfactory epithelium, which contains mucous membranes that produce and store mucus, and olfactory glands that secrete [metabolic enzymes](./Metabolic_pathway "Metabolic pathway") found in the mucus.[[3]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-Mori-3)

#### Transduction

Action potential propagated by olfactory stimuli in an axon.

[Olfactory sensory neurons](./Olfactory_receptor_neurons "Olfactory receptor neurons") in the epithelium detect odor molecules dissolved in the mucus and transmit information about the odor to the brain in a process called [sensory transduction](./Sensory_transduction "Sensory transduction").[[4]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-Rodriguez-4)[[5]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-Bushak-5) Olfactory neurons have [cilia](./Cilia "Cilia") (tiny hairs) containing [olfactory receptors](./Olfactory_receptor "Olfactory receptor") that bind to odor molecules, causing an electrical response that spreads through the [sensory neuron](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") to the [olfactory nerve fibers](./Olfactory_nerve "Olfactory nerve") at the back of the [nasal cavity](./Nasal_cavity "Nasal cavity").[[2]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-Boroditsky-2)

Olfactory nerves and fibers transmit information about odors from the peripheral olfactory system to the central olfactory system of the brain, which is separated from the epithelium by the [cribriform plate](./Cribriform_plate "Cribriform plate") of the [ethmoid bone](./Ethmoid_bone "Ethmoid bone"). Olfactory nerve fibers, which originate in the epithelium, pass through the cribriform plate, connecting the epithelium to the brain's [limbic system](./Limbic_system "Limbic system") at the [olfactory bulbs](./Olfactory_bulbs "Olfactory bulbs").[[6]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-FOOTNOTEMori2014182-6)

### Central

Details of olfaction system



Location of olfactory cortex in the human brain.

The main olfactory bulb transmits pulses to both mitral and tufted cells, which help determine odor concentration based on the time certain neuron clusters fire (called 'timing code'). These cells also note differences between highly similar odors and use that data to aid in later recognition. The cells are different with mitral having low firing-rates and being easily inhibited by neighboring cells, while tufted have high rates of firing and are more difficult to inhibit.[[7]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-8)[[9]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-10) How the bulbar neural circuit transforms odor inputs to the bulb into the bulbar responses that are sent to the olfactory cortex can be partly understood by a mathematical model.[[11]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-11)

The [uncus](./Uncus "Uncus") houses the **olfactory cortex** which includes the [piriform cortex](./Piriform_cortex "Piriform cortex") (posterior [orbitofrontal cortex](./Orbitofrontal_cortex "Orbitofrontal cortex")), [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle"), and [parahippocampal gyrus](./Parahippocampal_gyrus "Parahippocampal gyrus").

The olfactory tubercle connects to numerous areas of the amygdala, [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), [brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem"), [retina](./Retina "Retina"), [auditory cortex](./Auditory_cortex "Auditory cortex"), and olfactory system. In total it has 27 inputs and 20 outputs. An oversimplification of its role is to state that it:[[12]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-12)[[13]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-14)

- checks to ensure odor signals arose from actual odors rather than villi irritation,
- regulates motor behavior (primarily social and stereotypical) brought on by odors,
- integrates auditory and olfactory sensory info to complete the aforementioned tasks, and
- plays a role in transmitting positive signals to reward sensors (and is thus involved in addiction).

The amygdala (in olfaction) processes [pheromone](./Pheromone "Pheromone"), [allomone](./Allomone "Allomone"), and [kairomone](./Kairomone "Kairomone") (same-species, cross-species, and cross-species where the emitter is harmed and the sensor is benefited, respectively) signals. Due to [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") evolution this processing is secondary and therefore is largely unnoticed in human interactions.[[15]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-15) Allomones include flower scents, natural herbicides, and natural toxic plant chemicals. The info for these processes comes from the [vomeronasal organ](./Vomeronasal_organ "Vomeronasal organ") indirectly via the olfactory bulb.[[16]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-16) The main olfactory bulb's pulses in the amygdala are used to pair odors to names and recognize odor to odor differences.[[17]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-17)[[18]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-18)

The [bed nuclei of the stria terminalis](./Stria_terminalis#Bed_nucleus_of_the_stria_terminalis "Stria terminalis") (BNST) act as the information pathway between the amygdala and hypothalamus, as well as the hypothalamus and [pituitary gland](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland"). BNST abnormalities often lead to sexual confusion and immaturity. The BNST also connect to the septal area, rewarding sexual behavior.[[19]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-19)[[20]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-20)

Mitral pulses to the hypothalamus promote/discourage feeding, whereas accessory olfactory bulb pulses regulate reproductive and odor-related-reflex processes.

The hippocampus (although minimally connected to the main olfactory bulb) receives almost all of its olfactory information via the amygdala (either directly or via the BNST). The hippocampus forms new memories and reinforces existing ones.

Similarly, the parahippocampus encodes, recognizes and contextualizes scenes.[[21]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-21) The parahippocampal gyrus houses the topographical map for olfaction.

The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) is heavily correlated with the cingulate gyrus and septal area to act out positive/negative reinforcement. The OFC is the expectation of reward/punishment in response to stimuli. The OFC represents the emotion and reward in decision making.[[22]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-22)

The [anterior olfactory nucleus](./Anterior_olfactory_nucleus "Anterior olfactory nucleus") distributes reciprocal signals between the olfactory bulb and piriform cortex.[[23]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-23) The anterior olfactory nucleus is the memory hub for smell.[[24]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-24)

When different odor objects or components are mixed, humans and other mammals sniffing the mixture (presented by, e.g., a sniff bottle) are often unable to identify the components in the mixture even though they can recognize each individual component presented alone.[[25]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-25) This is largely because each odor sensory neuron can be excited by multiple odor components. It has been proposed that, in an olfactory environment typically composed of multiple odor components (e.g., odor of a dog entering a kitchen that contains a background coffee odor), feedback from the olfactory cortex to the olfactory bulb[[26]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-26) suppresses the pre-existing odor background (e.g., coffee) via olfactory adaptation,[[27]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-27) so that the newly arrived foreground odor (e.g., dog) can be singled out from the mixture for recognition.[[28]](./Olfactory_system#cite_note-28)

## Clinical significance

Loss of smell is known as [anosmia](./Anosmia "Anosmia"). Anosmia can occur on both sides or a single side.

Olfactory problems can be divided into different types based on their malfunction. The olfactory dysfunction can be total ([anosmia](./Anosmia "Anosmia")), incomplete (partial anosmia, [hyposmia](./Hyposmia "Hyposmia"), or microsmia), distorted ([dysosmia](./Dysosmia "Dysosmia")), or can be characterized by spontaneous sensations like [phantosmia](./Phantosmia "Phantosmia"). An inability to recognize odors despite a normally functioning olfactory system is termed olfactory [agnosia](./Agnosia "Agnosia"). [Hyperosmia](./Hyperosmia "Hyperosmia") is a rare condition typified by an abnormally heightened sense of smell.
