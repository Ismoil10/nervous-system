A multimodal (responds to multiple types of stimuli) sensory system that provides information on touch, temperature, pain and pressure to the organism.



Touch is a crucial means of receiving information. This photo shows tactile markings identifying stairs for visually impaired people.

The **somatosensory system**, or **somatic sensory system**, is a subset of the [sensory nervous system](./Sensory_nervous_system "Sensory nervous system"). The main functions of the somatosensory system are the perception of external stimuli, the perception of internal stimuli, and the regulation of body position and balance ([proprioception](./Proprioception "Proprioception")).[[1]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Wang2021-1) It is believed to act as a pathway between the different sensory modalities within the body.[[2]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-2)

As of 2024 debate continued on the underlying mechanisms,[[3]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-3) correctness and validity of the somatosensory system model,[[4]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-4) and whether it impacts emotions in the body.[[5]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-5)

The somatosensory system has been thought of as having two subdivisions;

- One for the detection of [mechanosensory](./Mechanoreceptor "Mechanoreceptor") information related to touch.[[6]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Purves-6) Mechanosensory information includes that of light touch, vibration, pressure and tension in the skin. Much of this information belongs to the sense of touch which is a general somatic sense in contrast to the [special senses](./Special_senses "Special senses") of [sight](./Sight "Sight"), [smell](./Olfaction "Olfaction"), [taste](./Taste "Taste"), [hearing](./Hearing "Hearing"), and [balance](./Sense_of_balance "Sense of balance").[[7]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Saladin1-7)
- One for the [nociception](./Nociception "Nociception") detection of pain and temperature.[[6]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Purves-6) Nociceptory information is that received from pain and temperature that is deemed as harmful (noxious). [Thermoreceptors](./Thermoreceptor "Thermoreceptor") relay temperature information in normal circumstances.[[6]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Purves-6) [Nociceptors](./Nociceptor "Nociceptor") are specialised receptors for signals of pain.[[8]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Saladin2-8)

The sense of touch in perceiving the environment uses special [sensory receptors](./Sensory_receptor "Sensory receptor") in the [skin](./Skin "Skin") called [cutaneous receptors](./Cutaneous_receptor "Cutaneous receptor"). They include [mechanoreceptors](./Mechanoreceptor "Mechanoreceptor") such as [tactile corpuscles](./Tactile_corpuscle "Tactile corpuscle") that relay information about pressure and vibration; nociceptors, and thermoreceptors for temperature perception.[[9]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-9)

Stimulation of the receptors activate [peripheral sensory neurons](./Dorsal_root_ganglion "Dorsal root ganglion") that convey signals to the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") that may drive a responsive [reflex](./Reflex "Reflex"), and may also be conveyed to the brain for conscious perception. Somatosensory information from the face and head enter the brain via [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves") such as the [trigeminal nerve](./Trigeminal_nerve "Trigeminal nerve").

The neural pathways that go to the brain are structured such that information about the location of the physical stimulus is preserved. In this way, neighboring neurons in the [somatosensory cortex](./Somatosensory_cortex "Somatosensory cortex") represent nearby locations on the skin or in the body, creating a map or [sensory homunculus](./Sensory_homunculus "Sensory homunculus").

## Touch communication

### Tactile signing

[Tactile signing](./Tactile_signing "Tactile signing") is a common means of communication used by people with [deafblindness](./Deafblindness "Deafblindness"). It is based on a sign language or another system of manual communication.

### Emotion communication

Humans can communicate specific emotions through touch alone including anger, fear, disgust, love, gratitude, and sympathy via touch at much-better-than-chance levels.[[10]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-10)

### Sensory receptors

The two different types of [mechanoreceptor](./Mechanoreceptor "Mechanoreceptor") in the skin are termed *low-threshold mechanoreceptors*, and *high threshold mechanoreceptors*.
The four mechanoreceptors in [glabrous skin](./Glabrous_skin "Glabrous skin") are low-threshold that respond to harmless stimuli. They are innervated by four different afferent fibers. High-threshold mechanoreceptors, respond to [harmful stimuli](./Noxious_stimuli "Noxious stimuli").[[1]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-Wang2021-1)

[Merkel cell](./Merkel_cell "Merkel cell") nerve endings are found in the basal [epidermis](./Epidermis "Epidermis") and [hair follicles](./Hair_follicles "Hair follicles"); they react to low vibrations (5–15 [Hz](./Hertz "Hertz")) and deep static touch such as shapes and edges. Due to having a small receptive field (extremely detailed information), they are used in areas like fingertips the most; they are not covered (shelled) and thus respond to pressures over long periods.

[Tactile corpuscles](./Tactile_corpuscle "Tactile corpuscle") react to moderate vibration (10–50 Hz) and light touch. They are located in the [dermal papillae](./Dermal_papillae "Dermal papillae"); due to their reactivity, they are primarily located in fingertips and lips. They respond in quick [action potentials](./Action_potentials "Action potentials"), unlike Merkel nerve endings. They are responsible for the ability to read Braille and feel gentle stimuli.

[Pacinian corpuscles](./Pacinian_corpuscle "Pacinian corpuscle") determine gross touch and distinguish rough and soft substances. They react in quick action potentials, especially to vibrations around 250 Hz (even up to centimeters away). They are the most sensitive to vibrations and have large receptor fields. Pacinian corpuscles react only to sudden stimuli so pressures like clothes that are always compressing their shape are quickly ignored. They have also been implicated in detecting the location of touch sensations on handheld tools.[[11]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-11)

[Bulbous corpuscles](./Bulbous_corpuscle "Bulbous corpuscle") react slowly and respond to sustained skin stretch. They are responsible for the feeling of object slippage and play a major role in the [kinesthetic](./Proprioception "Proprioception") sense and control of finger position and movement. Merkel and bulbous cells - slow-response - are [myelinated](./Myelinated "Myelinated"); the rest - fast-response - are not. All of these receptors are activated upon pressures that distort their shape causing an action potential.[[12]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-12)[[13]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-13)[[14]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-14)[[15]](./Somatosensory_system#cite_note-15)

### Somatosensory cortex

*Gray's Anatomy*, figure 759: the sensory tract, showing the pathway (blue) up the spinal cord, through the somatosensory thalamus, to S1 (Brodmann areas 3, 1, and 2), S2, and BA7



*Gray's Anatomy*, figure 717: detail showing path adjacent to the [insular cortex](./Insular_cortex "Insular cortex") (marked insula in this figure), adjacent to S1, S2, and BA7

The [postcentral gyrus](./Postcentral_gyrus "Postcentral gyrus") is in the [parietal lobe](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe") and its cortex is the [primary somatosensory cortex](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex "Primary somatosensory cortex") ([Brodmann areas](./Brodmann_area "Brodmann area") [3, 2 and 1](./Brodmann_area#BAsByAreaNumber "Brodmann area")) collectively referred to as S1.

[BA3](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex#Brodmann_areas_3,_1_and_2 "Primary somatosensory cortex") receives the densest projections from the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"). BA3a is involved with the sense of relative position of neighboring body parts and amount of effort being used during movement. BA3b is responsible for distributing somatosensory information, it projects texture information to BA1 and shape and size information to BA2.

Region S2 ([secondary somatosensory cortex](./Secondary_somatosensory_cortex "Secondary somatosensory cortex")) divides into Area S2 and parietal ventral area. Area S2 is involved with specific touch perception and is thus integrally linked with the amygdala and hippocampus to encode and reinforce memories.

Parietal ventral area is the somatosensory relay to the premotor cortex and somatosensory memory hub, BA5.

[BA5](./Brodmann_area_5 "Brodmann area 5") is the topographically organized somato memory field and association area.

[BA1](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex#Brodmann_areas_3,_1_and_2 "Primary somatosensory cortex") processes texture info while BA2 processes size and shape information.

Area S2 processes light touch, pain, visceral sensation, and tactile attention.
