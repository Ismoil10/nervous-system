In [neuroanatomy](./Neuroanatomy "Neuroanatomy"), the **postcentral gyrus** is a prominent [gyrus](./Gyrus "Gyrus") in the lateral [parietal lobe](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe") of the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain"). It is the location of the [primary somatosensory cortex](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex "Primary somatosensory cortex"), the main sensory receptive area for the [sense of touch](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system"). Like other sensory areas, there is a map of sensory space in this location, called the *[sensory homunculus](./Sensory_homunculus "Sensory homunculus")*.

The primary somatosensory cortex was initially defined from surface stimulation studies of [Wilder Penfield](./Wilder_Penfield "Wilder Penfield"), and parallel surface potential studies of Bard, Woolsey, and Marshall. Although initially defined to be roughly the same as [Brodmann areas](./Brodmann_area "Brodmann area") [3](./Brodmann_area_3 "Brodmann area 3"), [1](./Brodmann_area_1 "Brodmann area 1"), and [2](./Brodmann_area_2 "Brodmann area 2"), more recent work by [Kaas](./Jon_Kaas "Jon Kaas") has suggested that for homogeny with other sensory fields only area 3 should be referred to as "primary somatosensory cortex", as it receives the bulk of the [thalamocortical projections](./Thalamocortical_radiations "Thalamocortical radiations") from the sensory input fields[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*].

## Structure

The lateral postcentral gyrus is bounded by:

- [medial longitudinal fissure](./Medial_longitudinal_fissure "Medial longitudinal fissure") [medially](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location") (to the middle)
- [central sulcus](./Central_sulcus "Central sulcus") [rostrally](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location") (in front)
- [postcentral sulcus](./Postcentral_sulcus "Postcentral sulcus") [caudally](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location") (in back)
- [lateral sulcus](./Lateral_sulcus "Lateral sulcus") [inferiorly](./Anatomical_terms_of_location "Anatomical terms of location") (underneath)

The postcentral gyrus includes [Brodmann areas](./Brodmann_area "Brodmann area") 1, 2, and 3. Brodmann area 1 occupies the apex of the postcentral gyrus.
