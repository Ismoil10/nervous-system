**Dopamine** (**DA**, a contraction of **3,4-dihydroxyphenethylamine**) is a neuromodulatory molecule that plays several important roles in cells. It is an [organic chemical](./Organic_compound "Organic compound") of the [catecholamine](./Catecholamine "Catecholamine") and [phenethylamine](./Phenethylamine "Phenethylamine") families. It is an [amine](./Amine "Amine") synthesized by removing a [carboxyl group](./Carboxyl_group "Carboxyl group") through [decarboxylation](./Decarboxylation "Decarboxylation") from a molecule of its [precursor chemical](./Precursor_(chemistry) "Precursor (chemistry)"), [L-DOPA](./L-DOPA "L-DOPA"), which is [synthesized](./Biosynthesis "Biosynthesis") in the [brain](./Brain "Brain") and [kidneys](./Kidney "Kidney"). Dopamine is also synthesized in plants and most animals. In the brain, dopamine functions as a [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter")—a chemical released by [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") (nerve cells) to send signals to other nerve cells. The brain includes several distinct [dopamine pathways](./Dopaminergic_pathway "Dopaminergic pathway"), one of which plays a major role in the motivational component of [reward-motivated behavior](./Reward_system "Reward system"). The anticipation of most types of rewards increases the level of dopamine in the brain,[[4]](./Dopamine#cite_note-4) and many [addictive](./Addiction "Addiction") [drugs](./Psychoactive_drug "Psychoactive drug") increase dopamine release or block its [reuptake](./Reuptake "Reuptake") into neurons following release.[[5]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Wise2020-5) Other dopaminergic pathways are involved in [motor control](./Motor_system "Motor system") and controlling the release of various hormones. These pathways and [cell groups](./Dopaminergic_cell_groups "Dopaminergic cell groups") form a dopamine system which is [neuromodulatory](./Neuromodulation "Neuromodulation").[[5]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Wise2020-5)

In popular culture and media, dopamine is often portrayed as the main chemical of pleasure, but the current opinion in [pharmacology](./Pharmacology "Pharmacology") is that dopamine instead confers [motivational salience](./Motivational_salience "Motivational salience");[[6]](./Dopamine#cite_note-NAcc_function-6)[[7]](./Dopamine#cite_note-pmid24107968-7)[[8]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Aversion_neurons-8) in other words, dopamine signals the perceived motivational prominence (i.e., the desirability or aversiveness) of an outcome, which in turn directs behavior toward or away from achieving that outcome.[[8]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Aversion_neurons-8)[[9]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Motivational_salience-9)

Outside the central nervous system, dopamine functions primarily as a local [paracrine](./Paracrine "Paracrine") messenger. In blood vessels, it inhibits [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") release and acts as a [vasodilator](./Vasodilator "Vasodilator"); in the kidneys, it increases sodium excretion and urine output; in the pancreas, it reduces insulin production; in the digestive system, it hinders [gastrointestinal motility](./Gastrointestinal_physiology#Motility "Gastrointestinal physiology") and protects [intestinal mucosa](./Intestinal_mucosa "Intestinal mucosa"); and in the immune system, it reduces the activity of [lymphocytes](./Lymphocytes "Lymphocytes"). With the exception of the blood vessels, dopamine is synthesized locally within these systems and exerts its effects near the cells that release it.

Several notable diseases of the nervous system are associated with dysfunctions of the dopamine system, and some of the key medications used to treat them work by altering the effects of dopamine. [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"), a degenerative condition causing tremors and impaired motor control, is caused by a loss of dopamine-secreting neurons in the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra"). Its metabolic precursor, L-DOPA, can be manufactured; *Levodopa*, a pure form of L-DOPA, is the most widely used treatment for Parkinson's.[[10]](./Dopamine#cite_note-10) Several models of [schizophrenia](./Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia") link altered dopamine expression with the disorder; most [antipsychotic drugs](./Antipsychotic "Antipsychotic") used to treat it are [dopamine antagonists](./Dopamine_antagonist "Dopamine antagonist").[[11]](./Dopamine#cite_note-11) Similar drugs are also used as [anti-nausea agents](./Antiemetic "Antiemetic"). [Restless legs syndrome](./Restless_legs_syndrome "Restless legs syndrome") and [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](./Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder "Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder") (ADHD) are associated with decreased dopamine activity.[[12]](./Dopamine#cite_note-12)[*[better source needed](./Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] [Dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") [stimulants](./Sympathomimetic_drug "Sympathomimetic drug") can be addictive in high doses, but are used at lower doses to treat ADHD. Dopamine itself is available as a manufactured medication for [intravenous injection](./Intravenous_therapy "Intravenous therapy"). It is useful in the treatment of [severe heart failure](./Heart_failure "Heart failure") or [cardiogenic shock](./Cardiogenic_shock "Cardiogenic shock").[[13]](./Dopamine#cite_note-NHS2021-13) In newborn babies it may be used for [hypotension](./Hypotension "Hypotension") and [septic shock](./Septic_shock "Septic shock").[[14]](./Dopamine#cite_note-medscape2021-14)

## Structure

A dopamine molecule consists of a [catechol](./Catechol "Catechol") structure (a [benzene](./Benzene "Benzene") ring with two [hydroxyl](./Hydroxyl "Hydroxyl") side groups) with one amine group attached via an [ethyl](./Ethyl_group "Ethyl group") chain.[[15]](./Dopamine#cite_note-PubChem-15) Dopamine is the simplest possible catecholamine, a chemical compound class that also includes [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") and [epinephrine](./Epinephrine "Epinephrine").[[16]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Catecholamine-16) The presence of a benzene ring with this amine attachment makes it a [substituted phenethylamine](./Substituted_phenethylamine "Substituted phenethylamine"), a class that includes numerous [psychoactive drugs](./Psychoactive_drug "Psychoactive drug").[[17]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Phenethylamine-17)

Like most amines, dopamine is an organic base.[[18]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Carter-18) As a [base](./Base_(chemistry) "Base (chemistry)"), it is generally [protonated](./Protonation "Protonation") in [acidic](./Acid "Acid") environments (in an [acid-base reaction](./Acid-base_reaction "Acid-base reaction")).[[18]](./Dopamine#cite_note-Carter-18) The protonated form is highly water-soluble and relatively stable, but can become [oxidized](./Oxidation "Oxidation") if exposed to oxygen or other [oxidants](./Oxidisin
