Dopaminergic pathways

Projection neurons in the brain that synthesize and release dopamine



The main dopaminergic pathways of the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain")

**Dopaminergic pathways** (**dopamine pathways**, **dopaminergic projections**) in the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain") are involved in both physiological and behavioral processes including [movement](./Motion "Motion"), [cognition](./Cognition "Cognition"), [executive functions](./Executive_functions "Executive functions"), [reward](./Reward_system "Reward system"), [motivation](./Motivation "Motivation"), and [neuroendocrine control](./Neuroendocrine_cell "Neuroendocrine cell").[[1]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-1) Each pathway is a set of [projection neurons](./Projection_neuron "Projection neuron"), consisting of individual dopaminergic neurons.

There are more than 10 [dopaminergic cell groups](./Dopaminergic_cell_groups "Dopaminergic cell groups") and pathways. The four major dopaminergic pathways are the [mesolimbic pathway](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway"), the [mesocortical pathway](./Mesocortical_pathway "Mesocortical pathway"), the [nigrostriatal pathway](./Nigrostriatal_pathway "Nigrostriatal pathway"), and the [tuberoinfundibular pathway](./Tuberoinfundibular_pathway "Tuberoinfundibular pathway"). The mesolimbic pathway and the mesocortical pathway form the mesocorticolimbic system. Two other dopaminergic pathways to be considered are the [hypothalamospinal tract](./Hypothalamospinal_tract "Hypothalamospinal tract") and the [incertohypothalamic pathway](./Incertohypothalamic_pathway "Incertohypothalamic pathway").

[Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"), [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](./Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder "Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder") (ADHD), [substance use disorders](./Substance_use_disorder "Substance use disorder") ([addiction](./Addiction "Addiction")), and [restless legs syndrome](./Restless_legs_syndrome "Restless legs syndrome") (RLS) can be attributed to dysfunction in specific dopaminergic pathways.

The dopamine neurons of the dopaminergic pathways synthesize and release the [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine").[[2]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-urlBeyond_the_Reward_Pathway-2)[[3]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-projection-3) Enzymes [tyrosine hydroxylase](./Tyrosine_hydroxylase "Tyrosine hydroxylase") and [DOPA decarboxylase](./DOPA_decarboxylase "DOPA decarboxylase") are required for dopamine synthesis.[[4]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-Harsing_2000-4) These enzymes are both produced in the [cell bodies](./Soma_(biology) "Soma (biology)") of dopamine neurons. Dopamine is stored in the cytoplasm and vesicles in axon terminals. Dopamine release from vesicles is triggered by action potential propagation-induced membrane depolarization.[[4]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-Harsing_2000-4) The [axons](./Axon "Axon") of dopamine neurons extend the entire length of their designated pathway.

## Function

### Mesocorticolimbic system

The mesocorticolimbic pathway originates through the VTA and passes through the amygdala, nucleus accumbens, and hippocampus. These functions are relative to memory, emotional regulation, motivation, and reward.

The [mesocorticolimbic system](./Mesocorticolimbic_circuit "Mesocorticolimbic circuit") ([mesocorticolimbic circuit](./Mesocorticolimbic_circuit "Mesocorticolimbic circuit")) refers to both the [mesocortical](./Mesocortical_pathway "Mesocortical pathway") and [mesolimbic](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway") pathways.[[3]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-projection-3)[[12]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-MEDRS-quality_human_review-12) Both pathways originate at the ventral tegmental area (VTA) which is located in the midbrain. Through separate connections to the prefrontal cortex (mesocortical) and ventral striatum (mesolimbic), the mesocorticolimbic projection has a significant role in learning, motivation, reward, memory and movement.[[13]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-13) Dopamine receptor subtypes, D1 and D2 have been shown to have complementary functions in the mesocorticolimbic projection, facilitating learning in response to both positive and [negative feedback](./Negative_feedback "Negative feedback").[[14]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-14) Both pathways of the mesocorticolimbic system are associated with [ADHD](./ADHD "ADHD"), [schizophrenia](./Schizophrenia "Schizophrenia") and [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction").[[15]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-NHM-Cognitive_Control-15)[[16]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-ADHD_2008_paper-16)[[17]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-:0-17)[[18]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-18)

#### Mesocortical pathway

The [mesocortical pathway](./Mesocortical_pathway "Mesocortical pathway") projects from the ventral tegmental area to the prefrontal cortex ([VTA](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area") → [Prefrontal cortex](./Prefrontal_cortex "Prefrontal cortex")). This pathway is involved in cognition and the regulation of [executive functions](./Executive_function "Executive function") (e.g., attention, working memory, [inhibitory control](./Inhibitory_control "Inhibitory control"), planning, etc.) Dysregulation of the neurons in this pathway has been connected to ADHD.[[16]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-ADHD_2008_paper-16)

#### Mesolimbic pathway

Referred to as the reward pathway, [mesolimbic pathway](./Mesolimbic_pathway "Mesolimbic pathway") projects from the ventral tegmental area to the ventral striatum (VTA → [Ventral striatum](./Ventral_striatum "Ventral striatum") [[nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens") and [olfactory tubercle](./Olfactory_tubercle "Olfactory tubercle")]).[[17]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-:0-17) When a reward is anticipated, the firing rate of dopamine neurons in the mesolimbic pathway increases.[[19]](./Dopaminergic_pathways#cite_note-:1-19) The mesolimbic pathway is involved with [incentive salience](./Incentive_salience "Incentive salience"), [motivation](./Motivation "Motivation"), reinforcement
