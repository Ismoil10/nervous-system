The **substantia nigra** (**SN**) is a [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia") structure located in the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") that plays an important role in [reward](./Reward_system "Reward system") and [movement](./Motor_system "Motor system"). *Substantia nigra* is [Latin](./Latin "Latin") for "black substance", reflecting the fact that parts of the substantia nigra appear darker than neighboring areas due to high levels of [neuromelanin](./Neuromelanin "Neuromelanin") in [dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron").[[1]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-1) [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease") is characterized by the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta").[[2]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-2)

Although the substantia nigra appears as a continuous band in brain sections, anatomical studies have found that it actually consists of two parts with very different connections and functions: the [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta") (SNpc) and the [pars reticulata](./Pars_reticulata "Pars reticulata") (SNpr). The pars compacta serves mainly as a projection to the basal ganglia circuit, supplying the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum") with dopamine. The pars reticulata conveys signals from the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia") to numerous other brain structures.[[3]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-3)

The substantia nigra, along with four other nuclei, is part of the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia"). It is the largest [nucleus](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") in the midbrain, lying dorsal to the [cerebral peduncles](./Cerebral_peduncles "Cerebral peduncles"). Humans have two substantiae nigrae, one on each side of the midline.

The SN is divided into two parts: the [pars reticulata](./Pars_reticulata "Pars reticulata") (SNpr) and the [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta") (SNpc), which lies medial to the pars reticulata. Sometimes, a third region, the pars lateralis, is mentioned, though it is usually classified as part of the pars reticulata. The (SNpr) and the internal [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus") (GPi) are separated by the [internal capsule](./Internal_capsule "Internal capsule").[[4]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-4)

### Pars reticulata

Main article: [Pars reticulata](./Pars_reticulata "Pars reticulata")

The pars reticulata bears a strong structural and functional resemblance to the internal part of the globus pallidus. The two are sometimes considered parts of the same structure, separated by the white matter of the internal capsule. Like those of the globus pallidus, the neurons in pars reticulata are mainly [GABAergic](./Gamma-aminobutyric_acid "Gamma-aminobutyric acid").[[5]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-:0-6)

#### Afferent connections

The main input to the SNpr derives from the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum"). It comes by two routes, known as the [direct](./Direct_pathway "Direct pathway") and [indirect pathways](./Indirect_pathway "Indirect pathway"). The direct pathway consists of axons from medium spiny cells in the striatum that project directly to pars reticulata. The indirect pathway consists of three links: a projection from striatal medium spiny cells to the external part of the [globus pallidus](./Globus_pallidus "Globus pallidus"); a [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") projection from the globus pallidus to the [subthalamic nucleus](./Subthalamic_nucleus "Subthalamic nucleus"), and a [glutamatergic](./Glutamatergic "Glutamatergic") projection from the subthalamic nucleus to the pars reticulata.[[6]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-:0-6)[[7]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-7)[*[better source needed](./Wikipedia:Verifiability#Questionable_sources "Wikipedia:Verifiability")*] Thus, striatal activity via the direct pathway exerts an inhibitory effect on neurons in the (SNpr) but an excitatory effect via the indirect pathway. The direct and indirect pathways originate from different subsets of striatal medium spiny cells: They are tightly intermingled, but express different types of dopamine receptors, as well as showing other neurochemical differences.

#### Efferent connections

Significant projections occur to the thalamus (ventral lateral and ventral anterior nuclei), [superior colliculus](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus"), and other caudal nuclei from the pars reticulata (the nigrothalamic pathway),[[8]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-8) which use GABA as their neurotransmitter. In addition, these neurons form up to five collaterals that branch within both the pars compacta and pars reticulata, likely modulating dopaminergic activity in the pars compacta.[[9]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-9)

## Function

The substantia nigra is an important player in brain function, in particular, in [eye movement](./Eye_movement_(sensory) "Eye movement (sensory)"), [motor planning](./Motor_planning "Motor planning"), [reward-seeking](./Reward_system "Reward system"), [learning](./Learning "Learning"), and [addiction](./Addiction "Addiction"). Many of the substantia nigra's effects are mediated through the [striatum](./Striatum "Striatum"). The nigral [dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") input to the striatum via the [nigrostriatal pathway](./Nigrostriatal_pathway "Nigrostriatal pathway") is intimately linked with the striatum's function.[[10]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-striatum-10) The co-dependence between the striatum and substantia nigra can be seen in this way: when the substantia nigra is electrically stimulated, no movement occurs; however, the symptoms of nigral degeneration due to Parkinson's is a poignant example of the substantia nigra's influence on movement. In addition to striatum-mediated functions, the substantia nigra also serves as a major source of [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") inhibition to various brain targets.

### Pars reticulata

Main article: [Pars reticulata § Function](./Pars_reticulata#Function "Pars reticulata")

The [pars reticulata](./Pars_reticulata "Pars reticulata") of the substantia nigra is an important processing center in the basal ganglia. The GABAergic neurons in the pars reticulata convey the final processed signals of the [basal ganglia](./Basal_ganglia "Basal ganglia") to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") and [superior colliculus](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus"). In addition, the pars reticulata also inhibits dopaminergic activity in the [pars compacta](./Pars_compacta "Pars compacta") via axon collaterals, although the functional organization of these connections remains unclear.

The GABAergic neurons of the pars reticulata spontaneously fire [action potentials](./Action_potential "Action potential"). In rats, the frequency of action potentials is roughly 25 Hz.[[11]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-11) The purpose of these spontaneous action potentials is to inhibit targets of the basal ganglia, and decreases in inhibition are associated with movement.[[12]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-12) The subthalamic nucleus gives excitatory input that modulates the rate of firing of these spontaneous action potentials. However, lesion of the subthalamic nucleus leads to only a 20% decrease in pars reticulata firing rate, suggesting that the generation of action potentials in the pars reticulata is largely autonomous,[[13]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-13) as exemplified by the pars reticulata's role in [saccadic eye movement](./Saccade "Saccade"). A group of GABAergic neurons from the pars reticulata projects to the superior colliculus, exhibiting a high level of sustained inhibitory activity.[[14]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-14) Projections from the [caudate nucleus](./Caudate_nucleus "Caudate nucleus") to the superior colliculus also modulate saccadic eye movement.
Altered patterns of pars reticulata firing such as single-spike or burst firing are found in [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease")[[15]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-15) and [epilepsy](./Epilepsy "Epilepsy").[[16]](./Substantia_nigra#cite_note-16)

Main article: [Pars compacta § Function](./Pars_compacta#Function "Pars compacta")
