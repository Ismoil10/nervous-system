A **spinal nerve** is a [mixed nerve](./Mixed_nerve "Mixed nerve"), which carries [motor](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron"), [sensory](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron"), and [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") signals between the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord") and the body. In the [human body](./Human_body "Human body") there are 31 pairs of spinal [nerves](./Nerve "Nerve"), one on each side of the [vertebral column](./Vertebral_column "Vertebral column").[[1]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-Kaiser-1)[[2]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-AANS-2) These are grouped into the corresponding [cervical](./Cervical_vertebrae "Cervical vertebrae"), [thoracic](./Thoracic_vertebrae "Thoracic vertebrae"), [lumbar](./Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae"), [sacral](./Sacral_vertebrae "Sacral vertebrae") and [coccygeal](./Coccygeal_vertebrae "Coccygeal vertebrae") regions of the spine.[[1]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-Kaiser-1) There are eight pairs of **cervical nerves**, twelve pairs of **thoracic nerves**, five pairs of **lumbar nerves**, five pairs of **sacral nerves**, and one pair of **coccygeal nerves**. The spinal nerves are part of the [peripheral nervous system](./Peripheral_nervous_system "Peripheral nervous system").[[1]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-Kaiser-1)

## Structure

Spinal nerve



Typical spinal nerve location

Each spinal nerve is a [mixed nerve](./Mixed_nerve "Mixed nerve"), formed from the combination of [nerve root](./Nerve_root "Nerve root") [fibers](./Axon "Axon") from its [dorsal](./Dorsal_root_of_spinal_nerve "Dorsal root of spinal nerve") and [ventral roots](./Ventral_root_of_spinal_nerve "Ventral root of spinal nerve"). The dorsal root is the [afferent](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") sensory root and carries sensory information to the brain. The ventral root is the [efferent](./Efferent_nerve_fiber "Efferent nerve fiber") motor root and carries motor information from the brain. The spinal nerve emerges from the spinal column through an opening ([intervertebral foramen](./Intervertebral_foramen "Intervertebral foramen")) between adjacent vertebrae. This is true for all spinal nerves except for the first spinal nerve pair (C1), which emerges between the [occipital bone](./Occipital_bone "Occipital bone") and the [atlas](./Atlas_(anatomy) "Atlas (anatomy)") (the first vertebra).[[3]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-Saladin1-3) Thus the cervical nerves are numbered by the vertebra below, except spinal nerve C8, which exists below vertebra C7 and above vertebra T1. The thoracic, lumbar, and sacral nerves are then numbered by the vertebra above. In the case of a [lumbarized](./Lumbar_vertebrae#Variation "Lumbar vertebrae") S1 vertebra (also known as L6) or a [sacralized](./Lumbar_vertebrae#Variation "Lumbar vertebrae") L5 vertebra, the nerves are typically still counted to L5 and the next nerve is S1.

1. [Somatic efferent](./General_somatic_efferent_fibers "General somatic efferent fibers").

Outside the vertebral column, the nerve divides into branches. The [dorsal ramus](./Dorsal_ramus_of_spinal_nerve "Dorsal ramus of spinal nerve") contains nerves that serve the posterior portions of the trunk carrying visceral motor, somatic motor, and somatic sensory information to and from the skin and muscles of the back ([epaxial muscles](./Epaxial_muscles "Epaxial muscles")). The [ventral ramus](./Ventral_ramus "Ventral ramus") contains nerves that serve the remaining anterior parts of the trunk and the upper and lower limbs ([hypaxial muscles](./Hypaxial_muscles "Hypaxial muscles")) carrying visceral motor, somatic motor, and sensory information to and from the ventrolateral body surface, structures in the body wall, and the limbs. The [meningeal branches (recurrent meningeal or sinuvertebral nerves)](./Meningeal_branches_of_spinal_nerve "Meningeal branches of spinal nerve") branch from the spinal nerve and re-enter the intervertebral foramen to serve the ligaments, dura, blood vessels, intervertebral discs, facet joints, and periosteum of the vertebrae. The [rami communicantes](./Rami_communicantes "Rami communicantes") contain autonomic nerves that serve visceral functions carrying visceral motor and sensory information to and from the visceral organs.

Some anterior rami merge with adjacent anterior rami to form a [nerve plexus](./Nerve_plexus "Nerve plexus"), a network of interconnecting nerves. Nerves emerging from a plexus contain fibers from various spinal nerves, which are now carried together to some target location. The [spinal plexuses](./Spinal_plexus "Spinal plexus") are the [cervical plexus](./Cervical_plexus "Cervical plexus"), [brachial plexus](./Brachial_plexus "Brachial plexus"), [lumbar plexus](./Lumbar_plexus "Lumbar plexus"), the [sacral plexus](./Sacral_plexus "Sacral plexus") and the much smaller [coccygeal plexus](./Coccygeal_plexus "Coccygeal plexus").[[3]](./Spinal_nerve#cite_note-Saladin1-3)

## Regional nerves

Further information: [Spinal cord § Spinal cord segments](./Spinal_cord#Spinal_cord_segments "Spinal cord")

### Cervical nerves

Cervical nerves

The cervical nerves are the spinal nerves from the cervical vertebrae in the [cervical segment](./Spinal_cord#Spinal_cord_segments "Spinal cord") of the spinal cord. Although there are seven cervical vertebrae (C1–C7), there are eight cervical nerves [C1](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_1 "Cervical spinal nerve 1")–[C8](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_8 "Cervical spinal nerve 8"). C1–C7 emerge above their corresponding vertebrae, while C8 emerges below the C7 vertebra. Everywhere else in the spine, the nerve emerges below the vertebra with the same name.

The posterior distribution includes the [suboccipital nerve](./Suboccipital_nerve "Suboccipital nerve") (C1), the [greater occipital nerve](./Greater_occipital_nerve "Greater occipital nerve") (C2) and the [third occipital nerve](./Third_occipital_nerve "Third occipital nerve") (C3). The anterior distribution includes the [cervical plexus](./Cervical_plexus "Cervical plexus") (C1–C4) and [brachial plexus](./Brachial_plexus "Brachial plexus") (C5–T1).

The cervical nerves innervate the [sternohyoid](./Sternohyoid_muscle "Sternohyoid muscle"), [sternothyroid](./Sternothyroid_muscle "Sternothyroid muscle") and [omohyoid muscles](./Omohyoid_muscle "Omohyoid muscle").

A loop of nerves called [ansa cervicalis](./Ansa_cervicalis "Ansa cervicalis") is part of the cervical plexus.

### Thoracic nerves

The thoracic nerves are the twelve spinal nerves emerging from the thoracic vertebrae. Each thoracic nerve T1–T12 originates from below each corresponding [thoracic vertebra](./Thoracic_vertebra "Thoracic vertebra"). Branches also exit the spine and go directly to the [paravertebral ganglia](./Paravertebral_ganglia "Paravertebral ganglia") of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") where they are involved in the functions of organs and glands in the head, neck, thorax and abdomen.

#### Anterior divisions

The [intercostal nerves](./Intercostal_nerves "Intercostal nerves") come from thoracic nerves T1–T11, and run between the ribs. At T2 and T3, further branches form the [intercostobrachial nerve](./Intercostobrachial_nerve "Intercostobrachial nerve"). The [subcostal nerve](./Subcostal_nerve "Subcostal nerve") comes from nerve T12, and runs below the twelfth rib.

#### Posterior divisions

The medial branches (ramus medialis) of the posterior branches of the upper six thoracic nerves run between the [semispinalis dorsi](./Semispinalis_dorsi "Semispinalis dorsi") and [multifidus](./Multifidus "Multifidus"), which they supply; they then pierce the [rhomboid](./Rhomboid_muscle "Rhomboid muscle") and [trapezius muscles](./Trapezius_muscle "Trapezius muscle"), and reach the skin by the sides of the spinous processes. This sensitive branch is called the medial cutaneous ramus.

The medial branches of the lower six are distributed chiefly to the multifidus and [longissimus dorsi](./Longissimus_dorsi "Longissimus dorsi"), occasionally they give off filaments to the skin near the middle line. This sensitive branch is called the posterior cutaneous ramus.

### Lumbar nerves

Lumbar plexus and branches

The [lumbar nerves](./Lumbar_nerves "Lumbar nerves") are the five spinal nerves emerging from the lumbar vertebrae. They are divided into posterior and anterior divisions.

#### Posterior divisions

The medial branches of the posterior divisions of the lumbar nerves run close to the articular processes of the vertebrae and end in the [multifidus muscle](./Multifidus_muscle "Multifidus muscle").

The laterals supply the [erector spinae muscles](./Erector_spinae_muscles "Erector spinae muscles").

The upper three give off cutaneous nerves which pierce the aponeurosis of the [latissimus dorsi](./Latissimus_dorsi "Latissimus dorsi") at the lateral border of the erector spinae muscles, and descend across the posterior part of the [iliac crest](./Iliac_crest "Iliac crest") to the skin of the [buttock](./Buttock "Buttock"), some of their twigs running as far as the level of the [greater trochanter](./Greater_trochanter "Greater trochanter").

#### Anterior divisions

The anterior divisions of the lumbar nerves (rami anteriores) increase in size from above downward. They are joined, near their origins, by [gray rami communicantes](./Gray_rami_communicantes "Gray rami communicantes") from the [lumbar ganglia](./Lumbar_ganglia "Lumbar ganglia") of the [sympathetic trunk](./Sympathetic_trunk "Sympathetic trunk"). These rami consist of long, slender branches which accompany the [lumbar arteries](./Lumbar_arteries "Lumbar arteries") around the sides of the vertebral bodies, beneath the [psoas major](./Psoas_major "Psoas major"). Their arrangement is somewhat irregular: one ganglion may give rami to two lumbar nerves, or one lumbar nerve may receive rami from two [ganglia](./Ganglia "Ganglia").

The first and second, and sometimes the third and fourth lumbar nerves are each connected with the lumbar part of the sympathetic trunk by a [white ramus communicans](./White_ramus_communicans "White ramus communicans").
