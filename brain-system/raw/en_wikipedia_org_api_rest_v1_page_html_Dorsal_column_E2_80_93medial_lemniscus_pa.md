The **dorsal column–medial lemniscus pathway** (**DCML**) (also known as the **posterior column-medial lemniscus pathway** (**PCML**) is the major [sensory pathway](./Sensory_system "Sensory system") of the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") that conveys [sensations](./Somatosensory_system "Somatosensory system") of [fine touch](./Fine_touch "Fine touch"), [vibration](./Vibration "Vibration"), [two-point discrimination](./Two-point_discrimination "Two-point discrimination"), and [proprioception](./Proprioception "Proprioception") (body position) from the skin and joints. It transmits this information to the [somatosensory cortex](./Somatosensory_cortex "Somatosensory cortex") of the [postcentral gyrus](./Postcentral_gyrus "Postcentral gyrus") in the [parietal lobe](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe") of the brain.[[1]](./Dorsal_column–medial_lemniscus_pathway#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Dorsal_column–medial_lemniscus_pathway#cite_note-OS-2) The pathway receives information from sensory receptors throughout the body, and carries this in the [gracile fasciculus](./Gracile_fasciculus "Gracile fasciculus") and the [cuneate fasciculus](./Cuneate_fasciculus "Cuneate fasciculus"), tracts that make up the white matter [dorsal columns](./Dorsal_columns "Dorsal columns") (also known as the posterior [funiculi](./Funiculus_(neuroanatomy) "Funiculus (neuroanatomy)")) of the spinal cord. At the level of the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"), the fibers of the tracts [decussate](./Sensory_decussation "Sensory decussation") and are continued in the [medial lemniscus](./Medial_lemniscus "Medial lemniscus"), on to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") and relayed from there through the [internal capsule](./Internal_capsule "Internal capsule") and transmitted to the somatosensory cortex. The name dorsal-column medial lemniscus comes from the two structures that carry the sensory information: the dorsal columns of the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), and the medial lemniscus in the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem").

There are three groupings of neurons that are involved in the pathway: [first-order neurons](./First-order_neuron "First-order neuron"), [second-order neurons](./Second-order_neuron "Second-order neuron"), and [third-order neurons](./Third-order_neuron "Third-order neuron"). The first-order neurons are [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") located in the [dorsal root ganglia](./Dorsal_root_ganglia "Dorsal root ganglia"), that send their [afferent fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") through the two dorsal columns.[[3]](./Dorsal_column–medial_lemniscus_pathway#cite_note-:0-3) The first-order [axons](./Axon "Axon") [make contact with](./Synapse "Synapse") second-order neurons of the [dorsal column nuclei](./Dorsal_column_nuclei "Dorsal column nuclei") (the [gracile nucleus](./Gracile_nucleus "Gracile nucleus") and the [cuneate nucleus](./Cuneate_nucleus "Cuneate nucleus")) in the lower [medulla](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"). The second-order neurons send their axons to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"). The third-order neurons are in the [ventral posterolateral nucleus](./Ventral_posterolateral_nucleus "Ventral posterolateral nucleus") in the thalamus and fibres from these ascend to the postcentral gyrus.

Sensory information from the upper half of the body is received at the [cervical](./Cervical_vertebrae "Cervical vertebrae") level of the spinal cord and carried in the cuneate tract, and information from the lower body is received at the [lumbar](./Lumbar_vertebrae "Lumbar vertebrae") level and carried in the gracile tract. The gracile tract is medial to the more lateral cuneate tract.

The axons of second-order neurons of the gracile and cuneate nuclei are known as the [internal arcuate fibers](./Internal_arcuate_fibers "Internal arcuate fibers") and when they cross over the midline, at the [sensory decussation](./Sensory_decussation "Sensory decussation") in the medulla, they form the medial lemniscus which connects with the thalamus; the axons synapse on neurons in the ventral posterolateral nucleus which then send axons to the [primary somatosensory cortex](./Primary_somatosensory_cortex "Primary somatosensory cortex") in the postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe. All of the axons in the DCML pathway are rapidly conducting, large, [myelinated](./Myelin "Myelin") fibers.[[2]](./Dorsal_column–medial_lemniscus_pathway#cite_note-OS-2)

## Structure

Spinal cord tracts - tracts of the DCML pathway shown upper right.

The DCML pathway is made up of the axons of first, second, and third-order [sensory neurons](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron"), beginning in the [dorsal root ganglia](./Dorsal_root_ganglion "Dorsal root ganglion"). The axons from the [first-order neurons](./First-order_neuron "First-order neuron") form the ascending tracts of the **gracile fasciculus**, and the **cuneate fasciculus** (the [dorsal columns](./Dorsal_columns "Dorsal columns")) which synapse on the second-order neurons in the [gracile nucleus](./Gracile_nucleus "Gracile nucleus") and the [cuneate nucleus](./Cuneate_nucleus "Cuneate nucleus") known together as the [dorsal column nuclei](./Dorsal_column_nuclei "Dorsal column nuclei"); axons from these neurons ascend as the [internal arcuate fibers](./Internal_arcuate_fibers "Internal arcuate fibers"); the fibers cross over at the [sensory decussation](./Sensory_decussation "Sensory decussation") and form the [medial lemniscus](./Medial_lemniscus "Medial lemniscus") which connects with the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"); the axons synapse on neurons in the [ventral posterolateral nucleus](./Ventral_posterolateral_nucleus "Ventral posterolateral nucleus") of the thalamus, which then send axons to the [postcentral gyrus](./Postcentral_gyrus "Postcentral gyrus") in the [parietal lobe](./Parietal_lobe "Parietal lobe").

The gracile fasciculus carries sensory information from the lower half of the body, i.e. the nerves entering the spinal cord below [T6](./Thoracic_vertebrae#Sixth_thoracic_vertebra "Thoracic vertebrae"). The cuneate fasciculus carries sensory information from the upper half of the body (upper limbs, trunk, neck, and the [posterior third of the scalp](./Cervical_spinal_nerve_2 "Cervical spinal nerve 2")), i.e. the nerves entering the spinal cord at or above [T6](./Thoracic_vertebrae#Sixth_thoracic_vertebra "Thoracic vertebrae").[[4]](./Dorsal_column–medial_lemniscus_pathway#cite_note-Purves-4) Note that sensory information from the face and anterior 2/3 of the scalp is not carried by the dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway but by the [trigeminal lemniscus](./Trigeminal_lemniscus "Trigeminal lemniscus") tract.

The gracile fasciculus is wedge-shaped on transverse section and lies next to the posterior median septum. Its base is at the surface of the spinal cord, and its apex directed toward the posterior gray commissure. The gracile fasciculus increases in size from inferior to superior.

The cuneate fasciculus is triangular on transverse section and lies between the gracile fasciculus and the posterior column, its base corresponding with the surface of the spinal cord.
Its fibers, larger than those of the gracile fasciculus, are mostly derived from the same source, viz., the posterior nerve roots.
Some ascend for only a short distance in the tract, and, entering the gray matter, come into close relationship with the cells of the dorsal nucleus, while others can be traced as far as the medulla oblongata, where they end in the gracile nucleus and cuneate nucleus.

The two ascending tracts meet at the level of the [sixth thoracic vertebra](./Sixth_thoracic_vertebra "Sixth thoracic vertebra") (T6). Ascending tracts typically have three levels of neurons, namely [first-order](./First-order_neuron "First-order neuron"), [second-order](./Second-order_neuron "Second-order neuron"), and [third-order neurons](./Third-order_neuron "Third-order neuron"), that relay information from the physical point of reception to the actual point of interpretation in the brain.
