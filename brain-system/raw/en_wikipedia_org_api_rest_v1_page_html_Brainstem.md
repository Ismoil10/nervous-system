The **brainstem** (or **brain stem**) is the posterior stalk-like part of the [brain](./Brain "Brain") that connects the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") with the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord").[[1]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Singh-1) In the [human brain](./Human_brain "Human brain"), the brainstem is composed of the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"), the [pons](./Pons "Pons"), and the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata").[[1]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Singh-1) The midbrain is continuous with the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus") of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon") through the [tentorial notch](./Tentorial_notch "Tentorial notch"), and sometimes the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon") is included in the brainstem.[[2]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Haines-2)

The brainstem is very small, making up around only 2.6 percent of the brain's total weight.[[3]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Haines1-3) It has the critical roles of regulating [heart](./Heart "Heart") and [respiratory](./Respiratory_system "Respiratory system") function, helping to control [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate") and [breathing rate](./Breathing_rate "Breathing rate").[[4]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-4) It also provides the main motor and sensory nerve supply to the [face](./Face "Face") and [neck](./Neck "Neck") via the [cranial nerves](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves"). Ten pairs of cranial nerves come from the brainstem.[[5]](./Brainstem#cite_note-5) Other roles include the regulation of the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") and the body's [sleep cycle](./Sleep_cycle "Sleep cycle").[[4]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-4) It is also of prime importance in the conveyance of motor and sensory pathways from the rest of the brain to the body, and from the body back to the brain.[[4]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Encyclopedia_Britannica-4) These pathways include the [corticospinal tract](./Corticospinal_tract "Corticospinal tract") (motor function), the [dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway](./Dorsal_column-medial_lemniscus_pathway "Dorsal column-medial lemniscus pathway") ([fine touch](./Fine_touch#Fine_touch_and_crude_touch "Fine touch"), [vibration sensation](./Cutaneous_receptor#Modalities "Cutaneous receptor"), and [proprioception](./Proprioception "Proprioception")), and the [spinothalamic tract](./Spinothalamic_tract "Spinothalamic tract") ([pain](./Pain "Pain"), temperature, itch, and [crude touch](./Somatosensory_system#Fine_touch_and_crude_touch "Somatosensory system")).[[6]](./Brainstem#cite_note-6)

## Structure

The parts of the brainstem are the midbrain, the pons, and the medulla oblongata; the diencephalon is sometimes considered part of the brainstem.[[2]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Haines-2)

The brainstem extends from just above the [tentorial notch](./Tentorial_notch "Tentorial notch") superiorly to the [first cervical vertebra](./First_cervical_vertebra "First cervical vertebra") below the [foramen magnum](./Foramen_magnum "Foramen magnum") inferiorly.[[7]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Sinnatamby-2011-7)

### Midbrain

Main article: [Midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain")

Diagram showing the position of the colliculi. Superior shown in green and inferior in orange.



View of the midbrain showing covering tectum and tegmental floor

The [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") is further subdivided into three parts: [tectum](./Tectum "Tectum"), [tegmentum](./Tegmentum "Tegmentum"), and the [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area"). The [tectum](./Tectum "Tectum") forms the ceiling. The tectum comprises the paired structure of the [superior](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus") and [inferior colliculi](./Inferior_colliculus "Inferior colliculus") and is the dorsal covering of the [cerebral aqueduct](./Cerebral_aqueduct "Cerebral aqueduct"). The [inferior colliculus](./Inferior_colliculus "Inferior colliculus") is the principal midbrain nucleus of the auditory pathway and receives input from several peripheral brainstem nuclei, as well as inputs from the auditory cortex. Its inferior brachium (arm-like process) reaches to the [medial geniculate nucleus](./Medial_geniculate_nucleus "Medial geniculate nucleus") of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon"). The [superior colliculus](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus") is positioned above the inferior colliculus, and marks the rostral midbrain. It is involved in the special sense of vision and sends its superior brachium to the [lateral geniculate body](./Lateral_geniculate_body "Lateral geniculate body") of the diencephalon.

The [tegmentum](./Tegmentum "Tegmentum") which forms the floor of the midbrain, is ventral to the [cerebral aqueduct](./Cerebral_aqueduct "Cerebral aqueduct"). Several [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)"), [tracts](./Neural_tract "Neural tract"), and the [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation") are contained here.

The [ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area") (VTA) is composed of paired [cerebral peduncles](./Cerebral_peduncle "Cerebral peduncle"). These transmit axons of [upper motor neurons](./Upper_motor_neuron "Upper motor neuron").

#### Midbrain nuclei

The midbrain consists of:

- [Periaqueductal gray](./Periaqueductal_gray "Periaqueductal gray"): The [gray matter](./Gray_matter "Gray matter") around the [cerebral aqueduct](./Cerebral_aqueduct "Cerebral aqueduct") contains neurons involved in the [pain](./Pain "Pain") desensitization pathway. Neurons synapse here. When stimulated by a signal, the synaptic connections activate neurons in the [nucleus raphe magnus](./Nucleus_raphe_magnus "Nucleus raphe magnus"). The pathway then projects down into the [posterior grey column](./Posterior_grey_column "Posterior grey column") of the spinal cord, inhibiting pain sensation transmission.
- [Oculomotor nerve](./Oculomotor_nerve "Oculomotor nerve") nucleus: This is the third [cranial nerve nucleus](./Cranial_nerve_nucleus "Cranial nerve nucleus").
- [Trochlear nerve](./Trochlear_nerve "Trochlear nerve") nucleus: This is the fourth cranial nerve.
- [Red nucleus](./Red_nucleus "Red nucleus"): This is a motor nucleus that sends a descending tract to the [lower motor neurons](./Lower_motor_neuron "Lower motor neuron").
- [Substantia nigra pars compacta](./Substantia_nigra_pars_compacta "Substantia nigra pars compacta"): This is a concentration of neurons in the ventral portion of the midbrain that uses [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine") as its [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") and is involved in both motor function and emotion. Its dysfunction is implicated in [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease").
- [Reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation"): This is a large area in the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain") that is involved in various important functions of the midbrain. In particular, it contains lower motor neurons, is involved in the pain desensitization pathway, is involved in the arousal and consciousness systems, and contains the [locus coeruleus](./Locus_coeruleus "Locus coeruleus"), which is involved in intensive [alertness](./Alertness "Alertness") modulation and in [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") reflexes.
- [Central tegmental tract](./Central_tegmental_tract "Central tegmental tract"): Directly anterior to the floor of the [fourth ventricle](./Fourth_ventricle "Fourth ventricle"), this is a pathway by which many tracts project up to the cortex and down to the spinal cord.
- [Ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area"): A [dopaminergic](./Dopaminergic "Dopaminergic") [nucleus](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)"), known as [group A10 cells](./Dopaminergic_cell_groups#Cell_group_A10 "Dopaminergic cell groups")[[8]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Hegarty-8) is located close to the midline on the floor of the midbrain.
- [Rostromedial tegmental nucleus](./Rostromedial_tegmental_nucleus "Rostromedial tegmental nucleus"): A [GABAergic](./GABAergic "GABAergic") nucleus located adjacent to the ventral tegmental area.

### Pons

Main article: [Pons](./Pons "Pons")

The pons lies between the midbrain and the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata"). It is separated from the midbrain by the [superior pontine sulcus](./Superior_pontine_sulcus "Superior pontine sulcus"), and from the medulla by the [inferior pontine sulcus](./Inferior_pontine_sulcus "Inferior pontine sulcus").[[9]](./Brainstem#cite_note-Carpenter-9) It contains tracts that carry signals from the [cerebrum](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") to the medulla and to the [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum") and also tracts that carry sensory signals to the [thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus"). The pons is connected to the cerebellum by the [cerebellar peduncles](./Cerebellar_peduncles "Cerebellar peduncles"). The pons houses the respiratory [pneumotaxic center](./Pneumotaxic_center "Pneumotaxic center") and [apneustic center](./Apneustic_center "Apneustic center") that make up the pontine respiratory group in the [respiratory center](./Respiratory_center "Respiratory center"). The pons co-ordinates activities of the [cerebellar hemispheres](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum").[[10]](./Brainstem#cite_note-10)
The [pons](./Pons "Pons") and [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") are parts of the [hindbrain](./Hindbrain "Hindbrain") that form much of the brainstem.
