The **enteric nervous system** (**ENS**) is one of the three divisions of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") (ANS), the others being the [sympathetic nervous system](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") (SNS) and [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") (PSNS). The ENS is formed from the [myenteric plexus](./Myenteric_plexus "Myenteric plexus"), and the [submucosal plexus](./Submucosal_plexus "Submucosal plexus"), and consists of a mesh-like system of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") that governs the functions of the [gastrointestinal tract](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract"),[[1]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-NIH2025-1)[[2]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Furness2008-2) including [motility](./Gut_motility "Gut motility") and secretion, and is often referred to as the "second brain".[[3]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Avetisyan2015-3)[[4]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Spencer2020-4) It is derived from [neural crest](./Neural_crest "Neural crest") cells.[[5]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-pmid18385256-5)[[6]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-pmid16961690-6)

The enteric nervous system is capable of operating independently of the brain and spinal cord,[[7]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Gershon-7) but is thought to rely on innervation from the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") and [prevertebral ganglia](./Prevertebral_ganglia "Prevertebral ganglia") in healthy subjects. However, studies have shown that the system is operable with a severed vagus nerve.[[8]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-amjphysgastro-8) The neurons of the enteric nervous system control the motor functions of the system, in addition to the secretion of gastrointestinal enzymes. These neurons communicate through many [neurotransmitters](./Neurotransmitters "Neurotransmitters") similar to the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS), including [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"), [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine"), and [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"). The large presence of serotonin and dopamine in the [intestines](./Intestines "Intestines") are key areas of research for [neurogastroenterology](./Neurogastroenterology "Neurogastroenterology").[[9]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-10)[[11]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-:0-11)

## Structure

Layers of the [gastrointestinal tract](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract"). The wall of the [gastrointestinal wall](./Gastrointestinal_wall "Gastrointestinal wall") has four basic tissue layers: the mucosa, submucosa, muscular layer, and serosa.

The enteric nervous system in humans consists of some 500 million [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron")[[12]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-12) (including the various types of [Dogiel cells](./Dogiel_cells "Dogiel cells")),[[2]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Furness2008-2)[[13]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-13) 0.5% of the number of neurons in the [brain](./Human_brain "Human brain"), five times as many as the one hundred million neurons in the human spinal cord,[[14]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Saunders_Elsevier-14) and about 2⁄3 as many as in the [whole nervous system of a cat](./List_of_animals_by_number_of_neurons#Whole_nervous_system "List of animals by number of neurons"). The enteric nervous system is embedded in the lining of the [gastrointestinal system](./Gastrointestinal_system "Gastrointestinal system"), beginning in the [esophagus](./Esophagus "Esophagus") and extending down to the anus.[[14]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Saunders_Elsevier-14)

The neurons of the ENS are collected into two types of [ganglia](./Ganglia "Ganglia"): [myenteric (Auerbach's)](./Myenteric_plexus "Myenteric plexus") and [submucosal (Meissner's) plexuses](./Submucosal_plexus "Submucosal plexus").[[15]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-urlThe_Enteric_Nervous_System-15) Myenteric plexuses are located between the inner and outer layers of the [muscularis externa](./Muscularis_externa "Muscularis externa"), while submucosal plexuses are located in the [submucosa](./Submucosa "Submucosa").

### Myenteric plexus

Main article: [Myenteric plexus](./Myenteric_plexus "Myenteric plexus")

The myenteric plexus, also known as *Auerbach's plexus*, is a collection of fibers and postganglionic autonomic cell bodies that lie between the circular and longitudinal layers of the [muscular layer](./Muscular_layer "Muscular layer") in the gastrointestinal tract.[[1]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-NIH2025-1) It was discovered and first named by German neuropathologist [Leopold Auerbach](./Leopold_Auerbach "Leopold Auerbach"). These neurons provide motor inputs to both layers of the muscular layer and provide both parasympathetic and sympathetic input. The anatomy of the plexus is similar to the anatomy of the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system"). The plexus includes sensory receptors, such as [chemoreceptors](./Chemoreceptors "Chemoreceptors") and [mechanoreceptors](./Mechanoreceptors "Mechanoreceptors"), that are used to provide sensory input to the interneurons in the enteric nervous system.

### Submucosal plexus

Main article: [Submucosal plexus](./Submucosal_plexus "Submucosal plexus")

The submucosal plexus (also known as Meissner's plexus) is found in the submucosal layer of the [gastrointestinal tract](./Gastrointestinal_tract "Gastrointestinal tract").[[16]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-16) It was discovered and named by German physiologist [Georg Meissner](./Georg_Meissner "Georg Meissner"). It functions as a pathway for the innervation in the mucosa layer of the gastrointestinal wall.

## Function

The ENS is capable of autonomous functions[[17]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-17) like the coordination of [reflexes](./Reflex "Reflex"); although it receives considerable innervation from the autonomic nervous system, it can and does operate independently of the brain and the spinal cord.[[18]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGershon199817-18) Its study is the focus of **neurogastroenterology**.

### Complexity

The enteric nervous system is a complex part of the nervous system. The enteric nervous system can operate autonomously. It normally communicates with the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS) through the [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") (e.g., via the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve")) and [sympathetic](./Sympathetic_nervous_system "Sympathetic nervous system") (e.g., via the [prevertebral ganglia](./Prevertebral_ganglia "Prevertebral ganglia")) nervous systems. However, [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") studies show that when the [vagus nerve](./Vagus_nerve "Vagus nerve") is severed, the enteric nervous system continues to function.[[8]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-amjphysgastro-8)

In vertebrates, the enteric nervous system includes [efferent neurons](./Efferent_neurons "Efferent neurons"), [afferent neurons](./Afferent_neuron "Afferent neuron"), and [interneurons](./Interneurons "Interneurons"), all of which make the enteric nervous system capable of carrying reflexes and acting as an [integrating center](./Integrating_center?action=edit&redlink=1 "Integrating center") in the absence of CNS input. The sensory neurons report on mechanical and chemical conditions. Through intestinal muscles, the motor neurons control [peristalsis](./Peristalsis "Peristalsis") and churning of intestinal contents. Other neurons control the secretion of [enzymes](./Enzymes "Enzymes"). The enteric nervous system also makes use of more than 30 [neurotransmitters](./Neurotransmitters "Neurotransmitters"), most of which are identical to the ones found in CNS, such as [acetylcholine](./Acetylcholine "Acetylcholine"), [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine"), and [serotonin](./Serotonin "Serotonin"). More than 90% of the body's serotonin lies in the gut, as well as about 50% of the body's dopamine, which is currently being studied to further our understanding of its utility in the brain.[[19]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-19)[[20]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-20)[[11]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-:0-11)

The enteric nervous system has the capacity to alter its response depending on such factors as bulk and nutrient composition.[[21]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Neunlist_Schemann_pp._2959–2965-21) In addition, the ENS contains support cells which are similar to [astroglia](./Astroglia "Astroglia") of the brain and a diffusion barrier around the capillaries surrounding ganglia which is similar to the [blood–brain barrier](./Blood–brain_barrier "Blood–brain barrier") of [cerebral](./Cerebrum "Cerebrum") blood vessels.[[22]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-22)

### Peristalsis

A simplified image showing peristalsis



Main article: [Peristalsis](./Peristalsis "Peristalsis")

Peristalsis is a series of radially symmetrical contractions and relaxations of muscles which propagate down a muscular tube. In humans and other mammals, peristalsis is found in the smooth muscles of the digestive tract to propel contents through the digestive system. The word is derived from New Latin and comes from the Greek peristallein, "to wrap around," from peri-, "around" + stellein, "draw in, bring together; set in order". Peristalsis was discovered in 1899 by the work of physiologists [William Bayliss](./William_Bayliss "William Bayliss") and [Ernest Starling](./Ernest_Starling "Ernest Starling"). Working on the [small intestines](./Small_intestines "Small intestines") of dogs, they found that the response of increasing the pressure in the intestine caused the contraction of the muscle wall above the point of stimulation and the relaxation of the muscle wall below the point of stimulation.[[23]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-23)[[7]](./Enteric_nervous_system#cite_note-Gershon-7)

### Segmentation

Main article: [Segmentation contractions](./Segmentation_contractions "Segmentation contractions")

Segmentation contractions are the contractions in intestines carried out by the smooth muscle walls. Unlike peristalsis, which involves the contraction and relaxation of muscles in one direction, segmentation occurs simultaneously in both directions as the circular muscles alternatively contract. This allows for thorough mixing of intestinal contents, known as [chyme](./Chyme "Chyme"), to allow greater absorption.
