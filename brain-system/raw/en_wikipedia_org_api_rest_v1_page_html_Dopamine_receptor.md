Dopamine receptor

Class of G protein-coupled receptors

[Dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine")

**Dopamine receptors** are a class of [G protein-coupled receptors](./G_protein-coupled_receptor "G protein-coupled receptor") that are prominent in the [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system") (CNS). Dopamine receptors activate different effectors through not only G-protein coupling, but also signalling through different protein (dopamine receptor-interacting proteins) interactions.[[1]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-1) The [neurotransmitter](./Neurotransmitter "Neurotransmitter") [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine") is the primary [endogenous](./Endogenous "Endogenous") [ligand](./Ligand_(biochemistry) "Ligand (biochemistry)") for dopamine receptors.

Dopamine receptors are implicated in many neurological processes, including motivational and incentive salience, cognition, memory, learning, and fine motor control, as well as modulation of [neuroendocrine](./Neuroendocrine "Neuroendocrine") signalling. Abnormal dopamine receptor signalling and dopaminergic nerve function is implicated in several neuropsychiatric disorders.[[2]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Girault,_2004-2) Thus, dopamine receptors are common neurologic drug targets; [antipsychotics](./Antipsychotics "Antipsychotics") are often dopamine [receptor antagonists](./Receptor_antagonist "Receptor antagonist") while [psychostimulants](./Psychostimulant "Psychostimulant") are typically [indirect agonists](./Indirect_agonist "Indirect agonist") of dopamine receptors.

The existence of multiple types of receptors for dopamine was first proposed in 1976.[[3]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-4) There are at least five subtypes of dopamine receptors, D1, D2, D3, D4, and D5. The D1 and D5 receptors are members of the **[D1-like family](./D1-like_receptor "D1-like receptor")** of dopamine receptors, whereas the D2, D3, and D4 receptors are members of the **[D2-like family](./D2-like_receptor "D2-like receptor")**. There is also some evidence that suggests the existence of possible D6 and D7 dopamine receptors, but such receptors have not been conclusively identified.[[5]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Contreras,_2002-5)

At a global level, D1 receptors have widespread expression throughout the brain. The relative amount of DA receptors is in the following order: D1 > D2 > D3 > D5 > D4.[[6]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-6) D1-2 receptor subtypes are found at 10–100 times the levels of the D3-5 subtypes.[[7]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Hurley,_2006-7)

### D1-like family

The [D1-like family receptors](./D1-like_receptor "D1-like receptor") are coupled to the [G protein](./G_protein "G protein") [Gsα](./G(s)alpha "G(s)alpha"). D1 is also coupled to [Golf](./G(olf)?action=edit&redlink=1 "G(olf)").

[Gsα](./G(s)alpha "G(s)alpha") subsequently activates [adenylyl cyclase](./Adenylyl_cyclase "Adenylyl cyclase"), increasing the intracellular concentration of the [second messenger](./Second_messenger_system "Second messenger system") [cyclic adenosine monophosphate](./Cyclic_adenosine_monophosphate "Cyclic adenosine monophosphate") (cAMP).[[8]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Dopamine_receptor_signaling-8)

- **D1** is encoded by the [Dopamine receptor D1](./Dopamine_receptor_D1 "Dopamine receptor D1") gene (*[DRD1](https://www.genenames.org/tools/search/#!/genes?query=DRD1)*).
- **D5** is encoded by the [Dopamine receptor D5](./Dopamine_receptor_D5 "Dopamine receptor D5") gene (*[DRD5](https://www.genenames.org/tools/search/#!/genes?query=DRD5)*).

### D2-like family

The [D2-like family receptors](./D2-like_receptor "D2-like receptor") are coupled to the [G protein](./G_protein "G protein") [Giα](./Gi_alpha_subunit "Gi alpha subunit"), which directly inhibits the formation of [cAMP](./Cyclic_AMP "Cyclic AMP") by inhibiting the enzyme adenylyl cyclase.[[9]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Neves_2002_Gprotein-9)

- **D2** is encoded by the [Dopamine receptor D2](./Dopamine_receptor_D2 "Dopamine receptor D2") gene (*[DRD2](https://www.genenames.org/tools/search/#!/genes?query=DRD2)*), of which there are two forms: D2Sh (short) and D2Lh (long):
  - The [*D2Sh*](./D2sh "D2sh") form is pre-synaptically situated, having modulatory functions (viz., [autoreceptors](./Autoreceptor "Autoreceptor"), which regulate neurotransmission via [feedback mechanisms](./Feedback_mechanism "Feedback mechanism"). It affects synthesis, storage, and release of dopamine into the [synaptic cleft](./Synaptic_cleft "Synaptic cleft")).[[10]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-D2sh-10)
  - The *D2Lh* form may function as a classical post-synaptic receptor, i.e., transmit information (in either an excitatory or an inhibitory fashion) unless blocked by a [receptor antagonist](./Receptor_antagonist "Receptor antagonist") or a synthetic partial [agonist](./Agonist "Agonist").[[10]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-D2sh-10)
- **D3** is encoded by the [Dopamine receptor D3](./Dopamine_receptor_D3 "Dopamine receptor D3") gene (*[DRD3](https://www.genenames.org/tools/search/#!/genes?query=DRD3)*). Maximum expression of dopamine D3 receptors is noted in the [islands of Calleja](./Islands_of_Calleja "Islands of Calleja") and [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens").[[11]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Suzuki_1998_D3-11)
- **D4** is encoded by the [Dopamine receptor D4](./Dopamine_receptor_D4 "Dopamine receptor D4") gene (*[DRD4](https://www.genenames.org/tools/search/#!/genes?query=DRD4)*). The D4 receptor gene displays polymorphisms that differ in a [variable number tandem repeat](./Variable_number_tandem_repeat "Variable number tandem repeat") present within the [coding sequence](./Coding_sequence "Coding sequence") of [exon](./Exon "Exon") 3.[[12]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-12) Some of these alleles are associated with greater incidence of certain disorders. For example, the D4.7 alleles have an established association with [attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder](./Attention-deficit_hyperactivity_disorder "Attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder").[[13]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-pmid12192625-13)[[14]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-pmid14702261-14)[[15]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-pmid14699430-15)

- D1–D3
- D2–D3
- D2–D4
- D2–D5

Non-isoreceptors

- D1–[adenosine A1](./Adenosine_A1_receptor "Adenosine A1 receptor")
- D2–[adenosine A2A](./Adenosine_A2A_receptor "Adenosine A2A receptor")
- D2–[ghrelin receptor](./Ghrelin_receptor "Ghrelin receptor")
- [D2sh](./D2sh "D2sh")–[TAAR1](./TAAR1 "TAAR1") (an [autoreceptor](./Autoreceptor "Autoreceptor") heteromer)
- D4–[adrenoceptor α1B](./Alpha-1B_adrenergic_receptor "Alpha-1B adrenergic receptor")
- D4–[adrenoceptor β1](./Beta-1_adrenergic_receptor "Beta-1 adrenergic receptor")

## Signalling mechanism

[Dopamine receptor D1](./Dopamine_receptor_D1 "Dopamine receptor D1") and [Dopamine receptor D5](./Dopamine_receptor_D5 "Dopamine receptor D5") are [Gs](./Gs_alpha_subunit "Gs alpha subunit") coupled receptors that stimulate [adenylyl cyclase](./Adenylyl_cyclase "Adenylyl cyclase") to produce [cAMP](./Cyclic_adenosine_monophosphate "Cyclic adenosine monophosphate"), which in turn increases intracellular calcium and mediates a number of other functions. The D2 class of receptors produce the opposite effect, as they are [Gαi](./Gi_alpha_subunit "Gi alpha subunit") and/or [Gαo](./Go_alpha_subunit?action=edit&redlink=1 "Go alpha subunit") coupled receptors, which blocks the activity of adenylyl cyclase. cAMP mediated [protein kinase A](./Protein_kinase_A "Protein kinase A") activity also results in the phosphorylation of [DARPP-32](./DARPP-32 "DARPP-32"), an inhibitor of [protein phosphatase 1](./Protein_phosphatase_1 "Protein phosphatase 1"). Sustained D1 receptor activity is kept in check by [Cyclin-dependent kinase 5](./Cyclin-dependent_kinase_5 "Cyclin-dependent kinase 5"). Dopamine receptor activation of [Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II](./Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent_protein_kinase_II "Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II") can be cAMP dependent or independent.[[19]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-receptor_mechanism-19)

The cAMP mediated pathway results in amplification of PKA phosphorylation activity, which is normally kept in equilibrium by PP1. The DARPP-32 mediated PP1 inhibition amplifies PKA phosphorylation of AMPA, NMDA, and inward rectifying potassium channels, increasing AMPA and NMDA currents while decreasing potassium conductance.[[8]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-Dopamine_receptor_signaling-8)

### cAMP independent

D1 receptor agonism and D2 receptor blockade also increases mRNA translation by phosphorylating [ribosomal protein s6](./Ribosomal_protein_s6 "Ribosomal protein s6"), resulting in activation of mTOR. The behavioural implications are unknown. Dopamine receptors may also regulate [ion channels](./Ion_channel "Ion channel") and [BDNF](./BDNF "BDNF") independent of cAMP, possibly through direct interactions. There is evidence that D1 receptor agonism regulates [phospholipase C](./Phospholipase_C "Phospholipase C") independent of cAMP, however implications and mechanisms remain poorly understood. D2 receptor signalling may mediate [protein kinase B](./Protein_kinase_B "Protein kinase B"), [arrestin beta 2](./Arrestin_beta_2 "Arrestin beta 2"), and [GSK-3](./GSK-3 "GSK-3") activity, and inhibition of these proteins results in stunting of the hyperlocomotion in [amphetamine](./Amphetamine "Amphetamine") treated rats. Dopamine receptors can also transactivate [Receptor tyrosine kinases](./Receptor_tyrosine_kinase "Receptor tyrosine kinase").[[19]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-receptor_mechanism-19)

Beta Arrestin recruitment is mediated by G-protein kinases that phosphorylate and inactivate dopamine receptors after stimulation. While beta arrestin plays a role in receptor desensitization, it may also be critical in mediating downstream effects of dopamine receptors. Beta arrestin has been shown to form complexes with MAP kinase, leading to activation of [extracellular signal-regulated kinases](./Extracellular_signal-regulated_kinases "Extracellular signal-regulated kinases"). Furthermore, this pathway has been demonstrated to be involved in the locomotor response mediated by dopamine receptor D1. Dopamine receptor D2 stimulation results in the formation of an Akt/Beta-arrestin/[PP2A](./PP2A "PP2A") protein complex that inhibits Akt through PP2A phosphorylation, therefore disinhibiting GSK-3.[[20]](./Dopamine_receptor#cite_note-20)
