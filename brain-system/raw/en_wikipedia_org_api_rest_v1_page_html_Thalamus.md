The **thalamus** (pl.: **thalami**; from [Greek](./Greek_language "Greek language") [θάλαμος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/θάλαμος "wikt:θάλαμος"), "chamber") is a large mass of [gray matter](./Gray_matter "Gray matter") on the lateral wall of the [third ventricle](./Third_ventricle "Third ventricle") forming the [dorsal](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/dorsal "wikt:dorsal") part of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon") (a division of the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain")). [Nerve fibers](./Nerve_fibers "Nerve fibers") project out of the thalamus to the [cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex") in all directions, known as the [thalamocortical radiations](./Thalamocortical_radiations "Thalamocortical radiations"), allowing [hub-like](./Hub_(network_science) "Hub (network science)") exchanges of information. It has several functions, such as the relaying of [sensory](./Sensory_neuron "Sensory neuron") and [motor](./Motor_neuron "Motor neuron") signals to the cerebral cortex[[1]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Poetics-1)[[2]](./Thalamus#cite_note-2) and the regulation of [consciousness](./Consciousness "Consciousness"), [sleep](./Sleep "Sleep"), and [alertness](./Alertness "Alertness").[[3]](./Thalamus#cite_note-3)[[4]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Whyte2024-4)

Anatomically, the thalami are paramedian symmetrical structures (left and right), within the [vertebrate](./Vertebrate "Vertebrate") brain, situated between the cerebral cortex and the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"). It forms during [embryonic development](./Embryonic_development "Embryonic development") as the main product of the diencephalon, as first recognized by the Swiss [embryologist](./Embryologist "Embryologist") and anatomist [Wilhelm His Sr.](./Wilhelm_His_Sr. "Wilhelm His Sr.") in 1893.[[5]](./Thalamus#cite_note-5)

## Anatomy

The thalami are paired structures of gray matter about four centimetres long and ovoid in appearance,[[6]](./Thalamus#cite_note-thalamus-6) located in the [forebrain](./Forebrain "Forebrain") which is superior to the [midbrain](./Midbrain "Midbrain"), near the center of the brain with nerve fibers projecting out to the cerebral cortex in all directions. In fact, almost all thalamic neurons (with the notable exception of the [thalamic reticular nucleus](./Thalamic_reticular_nucleus "Thalamic reticular nucleus")[[7]](./Thalamus#cite_note-FOOTNOTEJones1985218-7)) project to the cerebral cortex, and every region of the cortex so far studied has been found to innervate the thalamus.[[8]](./Thalamus#cite_note-8)

Each of the thalami may be subdivided into at least 30 [nuclei](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)"), giving a total of at least 60 for the 'whole thalamus'.[[4]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Whyte2024-4)[[9]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Sheridan2023-9)

The medial surface of the thalamus constitutes the upper part of the lateral wall of the [third ventricle](./Third_ventricle "Third ventricle"), and is connected to the corresponding surface of the opposite thalamus by a flattened gray band, the [interthalamic adhesion](./Interthalamic_adhesion "Interthalamic adhesion").

The lateral part of the thalamus is the [neothalamus](./Neothalamus "Neothalamus"), the [phylogenetically](./Phylogenesis "Phylogenesis") newest part of the thalamus, which includes the [lateral nuclei](./Lateral_nuclear_group "Lateral nuclear group"), the [pulvinar nuclei](./Pulvinar_nuclei "Pulvinar nuclei") and the [medial](./Medial_geniculate_nucleus "Medial geniculate nucleus") and [lateral geniculate nuclei](./Lateral_geniculate_nucleus "Lateral geniculate nucleus").[[12]](./Thalamus#cite_note-merriam-webster-12)[[13]](./Thalamus#cite_note-13)

The surface of the thalamus is covered by two layers of [white matter](./White_matter "White matter"), the *stratum zonale* covers the dorsal surface, and the [external medullary lamina](./Medullary_laminae_of_thalamus "Medullary laminae of thalamus") covers the lateral surface. (This stratum zonale should not be confused with the [stratum zonale](./Superior_colliculus#Structure "Superior colliculus") of the [superior colliculus](./Superior_colliculus "Superior colliculus").) The grey matter of thalamus is partitioned by a Y shaped [internal medullary lamina](./Internal_medullary_lamina "Internal medullary lamina") which divides the nuclei into anterior, medial, and lateral groups.[[14]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Tortora-14)[[15]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Georgescu-15)[[6]](./Thalamus#cite_note-thalamus-6)

Derivatives of the [diencephalon](./Diencephalon "Diencephalon") include the dorsally located [epithalamus](./Epithalamus "Epithalamus") (essentially the [habenula](./Habenula "Habenula") and annexes) and the peri-thalamus (pre-thalamus) containing the [zona incerta](./Zona_incerta "Zona incerta") and the thalamic reticular nucleus. Due to their different [ontogenetic](./Ontogenetic "Ontogenetic") origins, the epithalamus and the peri-thalamus are formally distinguished from the thalamus proper. The **metathalamus** is made up of the lateral geniculate and medial geniculate nuclei.[[16]](./Thalamus#cite_note-NN351hier-16)[[17]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Kiwitz_2022-17)

The thalamus comprises a system of [lamellae](./Medullary_laminae_of_thalamus "Medullary laminae of thalamus") (made up of [myelinated](./Myelin "Myelin") [fibers](./Axon "Axon")) that separate different thalamic subparts. Other areas are defined by distinct clusters of [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron"), such as the [periventricular nucleus](./Periventricular_nucleus "Periventricular nucleus"), the intralaminar elements, the "nucleus limitans", and others.[[18]](./Thalamus#cite_note-18) These latter structures, different in structure from the major part of the thalamus, have been grouped together into the *[allothalamus](./Allothalamus "Allothalamus")* as opposed to the *[isothalamus](./Isothalamus "Isothalamus")*.[[19]](./Thalamus#cite_note-19) This distinction simplifies the global description of the thalamus.

The principal subdivision of the thalamus into nucleus groups is the trisection of each thalamus (left and right) by a Y-shaped [internal medullary lamina](./Medullary_laminae_of_thalamus "Medullary laminae of thalamus"). This trisection divides each thalamus into [anterior](./Anterior_nuclei_of_thalamus "Anterior nuclei of thalamus"), [medial](./Medial_nuclear_group "Medial nuclear group") and [lateral groups](./Lateral_nuclear_group "Lateral nuclear group") of nuclei.[[9]](./Thalamus#cite_note-Sheridan2023-9) The medial group is subdivided into the [medial dorsal nucleus](./Medial_dorsal_nucleus "Medial dorsal nucleus") and [midline group](./Midline_nuclear_group "Midline nuclear group"). The lateral group is subdivided into [ventral](./Ventral_nuclear_group "Ventral nuclear group"), [pulvinar](./Pulvinar_nuclei "Pulvinar nuclei"), [lateral dorsal](./Lateral_dorsal_nucleus "Lateral dorsal nucleus"), [lateral posterior](./Lateral_posterior_nucleus "Lateral posterior nucleus") and metathalamus. The ventral group is further subdivided into [ventral anterior](./Ventral_anterior_nucleus "Ventral anterior nucleus"), [ventral lateral](./Ventral_lateral_nucleus "Ventral lateral nucleus") and [ventral posterior](./Ventral_posterior_nucleus "Ventral posterior nucleus").

The interior medullary lamina is subdivided into [intralaminar nuclei](./Intralaminar_thalamic_nuclei "Intralaminar thalamic nuclei"). Additional structures are the reticular nucleus (which envelops the lateral thalamus), the stratum zonale,[[20]](./Thalamus#cite_note-NN374-20) and the [interthalamic adhesion](./Interthalamic_adhesion "Interthalamic adhesion").[[21]](./Thalamus#cite_note-NN301-21)
