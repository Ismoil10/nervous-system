The **vagus nerve**, also known as the **tenth [cranial nerve](./Cranial_nerves "Cranial nerves")** (CN X), plays a crucial role in the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system"), which is responsible for regulating involuntary functions within the human body. This nerve carries both sensory and motor fibers and serves as a major pathway that connects the brain to various organs, including the heart, lungs, and digestive tract. As a key part of the [parasympathetic nervous system](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system"), the vagus nerve helps regulate essential involuntary functions like [heart rate](./Heart_rate "Heart rate"), [breathing](./Breathing "Breathing"), and [digestion](./Digestion "Digestion"). By controlling these processes, the vagus nerve contributes to the body's "rest and digest" response, helping to calm the body after stress, lower heart rate, improve digestion, and maintain [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis").

There are two separate vagus nerves: the right vagus and the left vagus. In the neck, the right vagus nerve contains on average approximately 105,000 fibers, while the left vagus nerve has about 87,000 fibers, according to one source. Other sources report different figures, with around 25,000 fibers in the right vagus nerve and 23,000 fibers in the left.[[1]](./Vagus_nerve#cite_note-1)[[2]](./Vagus_nerve#cite_note-2)

The vagus nerve is the longest nerve of the [autonomic nervous system](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") in the human body, consisting of both sensory – the majority – and some motor fibers, both sympathetic and parasympathetic. The sensory fibers originate from the [jugular](./Jugular_ganglion "Jugular ganglion") and [nodose](./Nodose_ganglion "Nodose ganglion") ganglia, while the motor fibers are derived from neurons in the [dorsal nucleus of the vagus](./Dorsal_nucleus_of_vagus_nerve "Dorsal nucleus of vagus nerve") and the [nucleus ambiguus](./Nucleus_ambiguus "Nucleus ambiguus").[[3]](./Vagus_nerve#cite_note-Walker-3) Although historically the vagus nerve was also known as the pneumogastric nerve, reflecting its role in regulating both the lungs and digestive system, its role in regulating cardiac function is fundamental.

## Structure

Upon leaving the [medulla oblongata](./Medulla_oblongata "Medulla oblongata") between the [olive](./Olivary_body "Olivary body") and the [inferior cerebellar peduncle](./Inferior_cerebellar_peduncle "Inferior cerebellar peduncle"), the vagus nerve extends through the [jugular foramen](./Jugular_foramen "Jugular foramen"), then passes into the [carotid sheath](./Carotid_sheath "Carotid sheath") between the [internal carotid artery](./Internal_carotid_artery "Internal carotid artery") and the [internal jugular vein](./Internal_jugular_vein "Internal jugular vein") down to the [neck](./Neck "Neck"), [chest](./Chest "Chest"), and [abdomen](./Abdomen "Abdomen"), where it contributes to the innervation of the [viscera](./Viscera "Viscera"), reaching all the way to the [colon](./Large_intestine "Large intestine"). Besides giving some output to various organs, the vagus nerve comprises between 80% and 90% of [afferent nerve fibers](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") conveying [sensory](./Sense "Sense") information about the state of the body's organs to the [central nervous system](./Central_nervous_system "Central nervous system").[[4]](./Vagus_nerve#cite_note-4)

The right and left vagus nerves descend from the cranial vault through the jugular foramina,[[5]](./Vagus_nerve#cite_note-5) penetrating the carotid sheath between the internal and external carotid arteries, then passing posterolateral to the common carotid artery. The cell bodies of [visceral afferent](./General_visceral_afferent_fibers "General visceral afferent fibers") fibers of the vagus nerve are located bilaterally in the [inferior ganglion of the vagus nerve](./Inferior_ganglion_of_vagus_nerve "Inferior ganglion of vagus nerve") (nodose ganglia). The vagus runs parallel to the common carotid artery and internal jugular vein inside the carotid sheath.

**Right Vagus Nerve:** The right vagus nerve gives rise to the right [recurrent laryngeal nerve](./Recurrent_laryngeal_nerve "Recurrent laryngeal nerve"), which hooks around the right [subclavian artery](./Subclavian_artery "Subclavian artery") and ascends into the neck between the [trachea](./Trachea "Trachea") and [esophagus](./Esophagus "Esophagus"). The right vagus then crosses anterior to the right subclavian artery, runs posterior to the [superior vena cava](./Superior_vena_cava "Superior vena cava"), descends posterior to the [right main bronchus](./Right_main_bronchus "Right main bronchus"), and contributes to [cardiac](./Cardiac_plexus "Cardiac plexus"), [pulmonary](./Pulmonary_plexus "Pulmonary plexus"), and [esophageal plexuses](./Esophageal_plexus "Esophageal plexus"). It forms the [posterior vagal trunk](./Posterior_vagal_trunk "Posterior vagal trunk") at the lower part of the esophagus and passes through the [diaphragm](./Thoracic_diaphragm "Thoracic diaphragm") to enter the abdomen through the [esophageal hiatus](./Esophageal_hiatus "Esophageal hiatus").

**Left Vagus Nerve:** The left vagus nerve enters the [thorax](./Thorax "Thorax") between [left common carotid artery](./Left_common_carotid_artery "Left common carotid artery") and left [subclavian artery](./Subclavian_artery "Subclavian artery") and descends on the [aortic arch](./Aortic_arch "Aortic arch"). It gives rise to the left [recurrent laryngeal nerve](./Recurrent_laryngeal_nerve "Recurrent laryngeal nerve"), which hooks around the aortic arch to the left of the [ligamentum arteriosum](./Ligamentum_arteriosum "Ligamentum arteriosum") and ascends between the trachea and esophagus. The left vagus further gives off thoracic cardiac branches, breaks up into the pulmonary plexus, continues into the esophageal plexus, and enters the abdomen as the [anterior vagal trunk](./Anterior_vagal_trunk "Anterior vagal trunk") by way of the esophageal hiatus of the diaphragm.

### Nuclei

The vagus nerve includes [axons](./Axon "Axon") which emerge from or converge onto four [nuclei](./Cranial_nerve_nucleus "Cranial nerve nucleus") of the medulla:

1. The [dorsal nucleus of vagus nerve](./Dorsal_nucleus_of_vagus_nerve "Dorsal nucleus of vagus nerve") – sends [parasympathetic](./Parasympathetic_nervous_system "Parasympathetic nervous system") output to the viscera, especially the intestines
2. The [nucleus ambiguus](./Nucleus_ambiguus "Nucleus ambiguus") – gives rise to the branchial efferent motor fibers of the vagus nerve and preganglionic parasympathetic neurons that innervate the heart
3. The [solitary nucleus](./Solitary_nucleus "Solitary nucleus") – receives afferent taste information and primary afferents from visceral organs
4. The [spinal trigeminal nucleus](./Spinal_trigeminal_nucleus "Spinal trigeminal nucleus") – receives information about deep/crude touch, pain, and temperature of the outer ear, the [dura](./Dura_mater "Dura mater") of the [posterior cranial fossa](./Posterior_cranial_fossa "Posterior cranial fossa") and the mucosa of the larynx
