Female (left) and male (right) adult human bodies photographed in ventral (above) and dorsal (below) perspectives. Naturally occurring [pubic hair](./Pubic_hair "Pubic hair"), [body hair](./Body_hair "Body hair"), and [facial hair](./Facial_hair "Facial hair") have been [deliberately removed](./Hair_removal "Hair removal") to show anatomy.

The **[human](./Human "Human") [body](./Body_(biology) "Body (biology)")** is composed of many different types of [cells](./Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)") that together create [tissues](./Tissue_(biology) "Tissue (biology)") and subsequently [organs](./Organ_(biology) "Organ (biology)") and then [organ systems](./Organ_system "Organ system").

The external human body consists of a [head](./Human_head "Human head"), [hair](./Hair "Hair"), [neck](./Neck "Neck"), [torso](./Torso "Torso") (which includes the [thorax](./Thorax "Thorax") and [abdomen](./Abdomen "Abdomen")), [genitals](./Sex_organ "Sex organ"), [arms](./Arm "Arm"), [hands](./Hand "Hand"), [legs](./Human_leg "Human leg"), and [feet](./Foot "Foot"). The internal human body includes organs, [teeth](./Human_tooth "Human tooth"), [bones](./Bone "Bone"), [muscles](./Muscle "Muscle"), [tendons](./Tendon "Tendon"), [ligaments](./Ligament "Ligament"), [blood vessels](./Blood_vessel "Blood vessel") and [blood](./Blood "Blood"), [lymphatic vessels](./Lymphatic_vessel "Lymphatic vessel") and [lymph](./Lymph "Lymph").

The study of the human body includes [anatomy](./Anatomy "Anatomy"), [physiology](./Physiology "Physiology"), [histology](./Histology "Histology"), and [embryology](./Embryology "Embryology"). The body [varies anatomically](./Anatomical_variation "Anatomical variation") in known ways. Physiology focuses on the systems and organs of the human body and their functions. Many systems and mechanisms interact in order to maintain [homeostasis](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis"), with safe levels of substances such as [sugar](./Sugar "Sugar"), [iron](./Iron "Iron"), and [oxygen](./Oxygen "Oxygen") in the blood.

The human body is studied by [health professionals](./Health_professional "Health professional"), physiologists, anatomists, and artists to assist them in their work.

The [human body is composed of](./Composition_of_the_human_body "Composition of the human body") [elements](./Chemical_element "Chemical element") including [hydrogen](./Hydrogen "Hydrogen"), [oxygen](./Oxygen "Oxygen"), [carbon](./Carbon "Carbon"), [calcium](./Calcium "Calcium") and [phosphorus](./Phosphorus "Phosphorus"). These elements reside in trillions of cells and non-cellular components of the body.

The adult male body is about 60% [total body water](./Body_water "Body water") content of some 42 litres (9.2 imp gal; 11 US gal). This is made up of about 19 litres (4.2 imp gal; 5.0 US gal) of [extracellular fluid](./Extracellular_fluid "Extracellular fluid") including about 3.2 litres (0.70 imp gal; 0.85 US gal) of [blood plasma](./Blood_plasma "Blood plasma") and about 8.4 litres (1.8 imp gal; 2.2 US gal) of [interstitial fluid](./Extracellular_fluid "Extracellular fluid"), and about 23 litres (5.1 imp gal; 6.1 US gal) of fluid inside cells.[[1]](./Human_body#cite_note-1) The content, [acidity](./PH "PH") and composition of the water inside and outside cells is carefully maintained. The main [electrolytes](./Electrolyte "Electrolyte") in body water outside cells are [sodium](./Sodium "Sodium") and [chloride](./Chloride "Chloride"), whereas within cells it is [potassium](./Potassium "Potassium") and other [phosphates](./Phosphate "Phosphate").[[2]](./Human_body#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGanong's20165-2)

### Cells

See also: [List of distinct cell types in the adult human body](./List_of_distinct_cell_types_in_the_adult_human_body "List of distinct cell types in the adult human body")

The body contains trillions of [cells](./Cell_(biology) "Cell (biology)"), the fundamental unit of life. At maturity, there are roughly 30 trillion cells, and 38 trillion bacteria in the body,[[3]](./Human_body#cite_note-sender-et-al-3)[[4]](./Human_body#cite_note-4) an estimate arrived at by totaling the cell numbers of all the [organs](./Organ_(biology) "Organ (biology)") of the body and [cell types](./List_of_human_cell_types "List of human cell types"). The skin of the body is also host to billions of commensal organisms as well as immune cells.[[5]](./Human_body#cite_note-Sfriso-5) Not all parts of the body are made from cells. Cells sit in an [extracellular matrix](./Extracellular_matrix "Extracellular matrix") that consists of [proteins](./Protein "Protein") such as [collagen](./Collagen "Collagen"), surrounded by extracellular fluids.

Each of the cells of the human body experiences, on average, tens of thousands of [DNA damages](./DNA_damage_(naturally_occurring) "DNA damage (naturally occurring)") per day.[[6]](./Human_body#cite_note-Jackson2009-6) These damages can block [genome](./Human_genome "Human genome") replication or genome transcription, and if they are not [repaired](./DNA_repair "DNA repair") or are repaired incorrectly, they may lead to [mutations](./Mutation "Mutation"), or other genome alterations that threaten cell viability.[[6]](./Human_body#cite_note-Jackson2009-6)

#### Genome

Main article: [Genome](./Genome "Genome")

See also: [Genetics](./Genetics "Genetics")

[Genome](./Genome "Genome")

Cells in the body function because of [DNA](./DNA "DNA"). DNA sits within the [nucleus of a cell](./Cell_nucleus "Cell nucleus"). Here, parts of DNA are [copied](./Transcription_(biology) "Transcription (biology)") and sent to the body of the cell via [RNA](./RNA "RNA").[[7]](./Human_body#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGanong's201616-7) The RNA is then used to [create](./Translation_(biology) "Translation (biology)") [proteins](./Protein "Protein"), which form the basis for cells, their activity, and their products. Proteins dictate cell function and gene expression, a cell is able to self-regulate by the amount of proteins produced.[[8]](./Human_body#cite_note-8) However, not all cells have DNA; some cells such as mature [red blood cells](./Red_blood_cell "Red blood cell") lose their nucleus as they mature.

### Tissues

Diagram of the different types of [soft tissue](./Soft_tissue "Soft tissue") in the body

The body consists of many different types of [tissue](./Tissue_(biology) "Tissue (biology)"), defined as cells that act with a specialised function.[[9]](./Human_body#cite_note-9) The study of tissues is called [histology](./Histology "Histology") and is often done with a [microscope](./Microscope "Microscope"). The body consists of four main types of tissues. These are lining cells ([epithelia](./Epithelium "Epithelium")), [connective tissue](./Connective_tissue "Connective tissue"), [nerve tissue](./Nervous_tissue "Nervous tissue") and [muscle tissue](./Muscle "Muscle").[[10]](./Human_body#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGray's_Anatomy200827-10)

#### Cells

Cells that line surfaces exposed to the outside world or gastrointestinal tract ([epithelia](./Epithelium "Epithelium")) or internal cavities ([endothelium](./Endothelium "Endothelium")) come in numerous shapes and forms – from [single layers of flat cells](./Epithelium "Epithelium"), to cells with small beating hair-like [cilia](./Cilium "Cilium") in the lungs, to column-like cells that line the [stomach](./Stomach "Stomach"). Endothelial cells are cells that line internal cavities including blood vessels and glands. Lining cells regulate what can and cannot pass through them, protect internal structures, and function as sensory surfaces.[[10]](./Human_body#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGray's_Anatomy200827-10)
