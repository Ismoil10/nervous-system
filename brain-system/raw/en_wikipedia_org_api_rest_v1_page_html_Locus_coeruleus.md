The **locus coeruleus** ([/sɪˈruːliəs/](./Help:IPA/English "Help:IPA/English")) (**LC**), also spelled **locus caeruleus** or **locus ceruleus**,[[1]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-1) is a [nucleus](./Nucleus_(neuroanatomy) "Nucleus (neuroanatomy)") in the [pons](./Pons "Pons") of the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem") involved with [physiological](./Physiology "Physiology") responses to [stress](./Stress_(medicine) "Stress (medicine)") and [panic](./Panic "Panic").[[2]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-2) It is a part of the [reticular activating system](./Reticular_activating_system "Reticular activating system") in the [reticular formation](./Reticular_formation "Reticular formation").[[3]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Khroud2022-3)

The locus coeruleus, which in Latin means "blue spot", is the principal site for brain synthesis of [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") (noradrenaline). The locus coeruleus and the areas of the body affected by the norepinephrine it produces are described collectively as the **locus coeruleus-noradrenergic system** or **LC-NA system**.[[4]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Mehler-4) Norepinephrine may also be released directly into the blood from the [adrenal medulla](./Adrenal_medulla "Adrenal medulla").

## Anatomy

The locus coeruleus (LC) is located in the posterior area of the rostral [pons](./Pons "Pons") in the lateral floor of the [fourth ventricle](./Fourth_ventricle "Fourth ventricle"). It is composed of mostly medium-size [neurons](./Neurons "Neurons"). [Melanin](./Melanin "Melanin") granules inside the neurons contribute to its blue colour. Thus, it is also known as the *blue nucleus*, or the *nucleus pigmentosus pontis* (heavily pigmented pontine nucleus).[[5]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-NIH2025-5) The [neuromelanin](./Neuromelanin "Neuromelanin") is formed by the [polymerization](./Polymerization "Polymerization") of norepinephrine and is analogous to the black [dopamine](./Dopamine "Dopamine")-based neuromelanin in the [substantia nigra](./Substantia_nigra "Substantia nigra").

In adult human males,[[a]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-6) the locus coeruleus has 22,000 to 51,000 total pigmented neurons that range in volume from 31,000 to 60,000 μm3.[[6]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-7)

## Connections

The projections of this nucleus reach far and wide. For example, they innervate the [spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord"), the brain stem, [cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum"), [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus"), the [hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus"), [the thalamic relay nuclei](./Thalamus "Thalamus"), the [amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala"), the basal [telencephalon](./Telencephalon "Telencephalon"), and the [cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex"). The norepinephrine from the LC has an excitatory effect on most of the brain, mediating [arousal](./Arousal "Arousal") and priming the brain's neurons to be activated by stimuli.

As an important [homeostatic](./Homeostasis "Homeostasis") control center of the body, the locus coeruleus receives [afferents](./Afferent_nerve_fiber "Afferent nerve fiber") from the hypothalamus. The [cingulate gyrus](./Cingulate_gyrus "Cingulate gyrus") and the amygdala also innervate the LC, allowing emotional pain and stressors to trigger noradrenergic responses. The cerebellum and afferents from the [raphe nuclei](./Raphe_nuclei "Raphe nuclei") also project to the LC, in particular the [pontine raphe nucleus](./Pontine_raphe_nucleus "Pontine raphe nucleus") and [dorsal raphe nucleus](./Dorsal_raphe_nucleus "Dorsal raphe nucleus").

### Inputs

The locus coeruleus receives inputs from a number of other brain regions, primarily:

- The [medial prefrontal cortex](./Medial_prefrontal_cortex "Medial prefrontal cortex"), whose connection is constant, excitatory, and increases in strength with raised activity levels in the subject
- The [nucleus paragigantocellularis](./Nucleus_paragigantocellularis "Nucleus paragigantocellularis"), which integrates [autonomic](./Autonomic_nervous_system "Autonomic nervous system") and [environmental](./Natural_environment "Natural environment") stimuli
- The [nucleus prepositus](./Nucleus_prepositus "Nucleus prepositus"), which is involved in gaze
- The lateral hypothalamus, which releases [orexin](./Orexin "Orexin"), which, as well as its other functions, is excitatory in the locus coeruleus.

### Outputs

The projections from the locus coeruleus consist of neurons that utilize norepinephrine as their primary neurotransmitter.[[7]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-VTA_inputs-8)[[8]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Noradrenergic_pathways-9) These projections include the following connections:[[7]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-VTA_inputs-8)[[8]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Noradrenergic_pathways-9)

:   - LC → [Amygdala](./Amygdala "Amygdala") and [Hippocampus](./Hippocampus "Hippocampus")
    - LC → [Brain stem](./Brain_stem "Brain stem") and [Spinal cord](./Spinal_cord "Spinal cord")
    - LC → [Cerebellum](./Cerebellum "Cerebellum")
    - LC → [Cerebral cortex](./Cerebral_cortex "Cerebral cortex")
    - LC → [Hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus")
    - LC → [Tectum](./Tectum "Tectum")
    - LC → [Thalamus](./Thalamus "Thalamus")
    - LC → [Ventral tegmental area](./Ventral_tegmental_area "Ventral tegmental area")

## Function

It is related to many functions via its widespread projections. The LC-NA system modulates cortical, subcortical, cerebellar, brainstem, and spinal cord circuits. Some of the most important functions influenced by this system are:[[9]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-pmid19917994-10)[[10]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Malenka_pathways-11)

- [Arousal](./Arousal "Arousal") and [sleep-wake cycle](./Sleep-wake_cycle "Sleep-wake cycle")
- [Attention](./Attention "Attention")[[11]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-12) and [memory](./Memory "Memory")
- Behavioral and [cognitive flexibility](./Cognitive_flexibility "Cognitive flexibility"),[[12]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-13) [creativity](./Creativity "Creativity"),[[13]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-14) [personality](./Personality "Personality"),[[14]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-15) behavioral inhibition and [stress (psychological)](./Stress_(psychological) "Stress (psychological)")
- [Cognitive control](./Cognitive_control "Cognitive control")
- [Decision making](./Decision-making "Decision-making") and utility maximization[[15]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-16)
- [Emotions](./Emotions "Emotions")
- [Neuroplasticity](./Neuroplasticity "Neuroplasticity")
- Posture and [balance](./Balance_(ability) "Balance (ability)")

The locus coeruleus is a part of the [reticular activating system](./Reticular_activating_system "Reticular activating system"), and is almost completely inactivated in [rapid eye movement sleep](./Rapid_eye_movement_sleep "Rapid eye movement sleep").[[16]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Schwartz_2008-17)

## Pathophysiology

The locus coeruleus may figure in [clinical depression](./Clinical_depression "Clinical depression"), [panic disorder](./Panic_disorder "Panic disorder"), [Parkinson's disease](./Parkinson's_disease "Parkinson's disease"), [Alzheimer's disease](./Alzheimer's_disease "Alzheimer's disease")[[17]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-Heneka2010-18) and [anxiety](./Anxiety "Anxiety"). Some medications including [norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors](./Norepinephrine_reuptake_inhibitor "Norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor") ([reboxetine](./Reboxetine "Reboxetine"), [atomoxetine](./Atomoxetine "Atomoxetine")), [serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors](./Serotonin-norepinephrine_reuptake_inhibitor "Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor") ([venlafaxine](./Venlafaxine "Venlafaxine"), [duloxetine](./Duloxetine "Duloxetine")), and [norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitors](./Norepinephrine-dopamine_reuptake_inhibitor "Norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor") ([bupropion](./Bupropion "Bupropion")) are believed to show efficacy by acting upon [neurons](./Neuron "Neuron") in this area.

Research continues to reveal that [norepinephrine](./Norepinephrine "Norepinephrine") (NE) is a critical regulator of numerous activities from stress response, the formation of memory to attention and arousal. Many neuropsychiatric disorders precipitate from alterations to NE modulated neurocircuitry: disorders of affect, anxiety disorders, PTSD, ADHD and Alzheimer's disease. Alterations in the locus coeruleus (LC) accompany dysregulation of NE function and likely play a key role in the pathophysiology of these neuropsychiatric disorders.[[18]](./Locus_coeruleus#cite_note-19)

### In stress

The locus coeruleus is responsible for mediating many of the sympathetic effects during stress. The locus coeruleus is activated by stress, and will respond by increasing norepinephrine secretion, which in turn will alter cognitive function (through the prefrontal cortex), increase motivation (through [nucleus accumbens](./Nucleus_accumbens "Nucleus accumbens")), activate the [hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis](./Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal_axis "Hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis"), and increase the sympathetic discharge/inhibit parasympathetic tone (through the [brainstem](./Brainstem "Brainstem")). Specific to the activation of the hypothalamic-pituitary adrenal axis, norepinephrine will stimulate the secretion of [corticotropin-releasing factor](./Corticotropin-releasing_factor "Corticotropin-releasing factor") from the hypothalamus, that induces [adrenocorticotropic hormone](./Adrenocorticotropic_hormone "Adrenocorticotropic hormone") release from the [anterior pituitary](./Anterior_pituitary "Anterior pituitary") and subsequent cortisol synthesis in the [adrenal glands](./Adrenal_glands "Adrenal glands").
