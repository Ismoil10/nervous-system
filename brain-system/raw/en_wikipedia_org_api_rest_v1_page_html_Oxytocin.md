**Oxytocin** is a [peptide hormone](./Peptide_hormone "Peptide hormone") and [neuropeptide](./Neuropeptide "Neuropeptide") normally produced in the [hypothalamus](./Hypothalamus "Hypothalamus") and released by the [posterior pituitary](./Posterior_pituitary "Posterior pituitary").[[3]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-3) Present in animals since early stages of evolution, in humans it plays roles in behavior that include [social bonding](./Human_bonding "Human bonding"), [love](./Love "Love"), [reproduction](./Reproduction "Reproduction"), [childbirth](./Childbirth "Childbirth"), and the [period after childbirth](./Postpartum_period "Postpartum period").[[4]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-4)[[5]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-5)[[6]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid12000539-6)[[7]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid11573971-7) Oxytocin is released into the bloodstream as a hormone in response to [sexual activity](./Human_sexual_activity "Human sexual activity") and during childbirth.[[8]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-Rogers-8)[[9]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-Chi2012-9) It is also available in [pharmaceutical form](./Oxytocin_(medication) "Oxytocin (medication)"). In either form, oxytocin stimulates uterine contractions to speed up the process of childbirth.

In its natural form, it also plays a role in [maternal bonding](./Maternal_bonding "Maternal bonding") and [milk production](./Lactation "Lactation").[[9]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-Chi2012-9)[[10]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-10) Production and secretion of oxytocin is controlled by a [positive feedback](./Positive_feedback "Positive feedback") mechanism, where its initial release stimulates production and release of further oxytocin. For example, when oxytocin is released during a uterine contraction at the start of childbirth, this stimulates the production and release of more oxytocin and an increase in the intensity and frequency of contractions. This process intensifies in frequency and continues until the triggering activity ceases. A similar process takes place during lactation and during sexual activity.

Oxytocin is derived by [enzymatic](./Enzymatic "Enzymatic") splitting from the [peptide precursor](./Peptide_precursor "Peptide precursor") encoded by the human OXT [gene](./Gene "Gene"). The deduced structure of the active nonapeptide is:

[Cys](./Cysteine "Cysteine") – [Tyr](./Tyrosine "Tyrosine") – [Ile](./Isoleucine "Isoleucine") – [Gln](./Glutamine "Glutamine") – [Asn](./Asparagine "Asparagine") – Cys – [Pro](./Proline "Proline") – [Leu](./Leucine "Leucine") – [Gly](./Glycine "Glycine") – NH2, or CYIQNCPLG-NH2.[*[citation needed](./Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]

## Etymology

The term "oxytocin" derives from the Greek [ὀξυτόκιον](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ὀξυτόκιον "wikt:ὀξυτόκιον") (*oxytokion*), based on [ὀξύς](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ὀξύς "wikt:ὀξύς") (*oxús*), meaning "sharp" or "swift", and [τόκος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/τόκος "wikt:τόκος") (*tókos*), meaning "childbirth".[[11]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-Ding-11) The adjective form is "oxytocic", which refers to medicines that stimulate uterine contractions, to speed up the process of childbirth. Colloquially, it has been referred to as the "cuddle hormone," "hug hormone," or "love hormone" because of extensive evidence of its involvement in mating and social behavior. It is involved in mating behavior in animals as low as [C. elegans](./C._elegans "C. elegans").

## History

The uterine-contracting properties of the principle that would later be named oxytocin were discovered by British pharmacologist [Henry Hallett Dale](./Henry_Hallett_Dale "Henry Hallett Dale") in 1906,[[12]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid16992821-12)[[13]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid19482229-13) and its milk ejection property was described by Ott and Scott in 1910[[14]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-14) and by Schafer and Mackenzie in 1911.[[15]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-Schafer_Mackenzie_1911-15) In 1909 the first clinical use of oxytocin was performed by [William Blair-Bell](./William_Blair-Bell "William Blair-Bell") to induce childbirth in patients with complications.[[16]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-16)[[17]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-17)

By the 1920s, oxytocin and [vasopressin](./Vasopressin "Vasopressin") had been isolated from [pituitary](./Pituitary_gland "Pituitary gland") tissue and given their current names. Oxytocin's molecular structure was determined in 1952.[[18]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-18) In the early 1950s, American biochemist [Vincent du Vigneaud](./Vincent_du_Vigneaud "Vincent du Vigneaud") found that oxytocin is made up of nine [amino acids](./Amino_acid "Amino acid"). He identified its amino acid sequence, the first [polypeptide](./Peptide "Peptide") hormone to be sequenced.[[19]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid13129273-19) In 1953, du Vigneaud carried out the synthesis of oxytocin, the first polypeptide [hormone](./Hormone "Hormone") to be synthesized.[[20]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-20)[[21]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-21)[[22]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-du_Vigneaud_1954-22) Du Vigneaud was awarded the [Nobel Prize in Chemistry](./Nobel_Prize_in_Chemistry "Nobel Prize in Chemistry") in 1955 for his work.[[23]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-pmid13324123-23) Further work on different synthetic routes for oxytocin, as well as the preparation of analogues of the hormone (*e.g.* 4-deamido-oxytocin) was performed in the following decade by [Iphigenia Photaki](./Iphigenia_Photaki "Iphigenia Photaki").[[24]](./Oxytocin#cite_note-:2-24)
