Stellar classification

Classification of stars based on spectral properties



"Star classification" redirects here. For the ranking system, e.g. "four out of five stars", see [Star (classification)](./Star_(classification) "Star (classification)").

In [astronomy](./Astronomy "Astronomy"), **stellar classification** is the classification of [stars](./Star "Star") based on their [spectral](./Stellar_spectrum "Stellar spectrum") characteristics. [Electromagnetic radiation](./Electromagnetic_radiation "Electromagnetic radiation") from the star is analyzed by splitting it with a [prism](./Prism_(optics) "Prism (optics)") or [diffraction grating](./Diffraction_grating "Diffraction grating") into a [spectrum](./Spectrum "Spectrum") exhibiting the [rainbow](./Continuum_(spectrum) "Continuum (spectrum)") of colors interspersed with [spectral lines](./Spectral_line "Spectral line"). Each line indicates a particular [chemical element](./Chemical_element "Chemical element") or [molecule](./Molecule "Molecule"), with the line strength indicating the abundance of that element. The strengths of the different spectral lines vary mainly due to the temperature of the [photosphere](./Photosphere "Photosphere"), although in some cases there are true abundance differences. The *spectral class* of a star is a short code primarily summarizing the [ionization](./Ionization "Ionization") state, giving an objective measure of the photosphere's temperature.

Most stars are currently classified under the Morgan–Keenan (MK) system using the letters *O*, *B*, *A*, *F*, *G*, *K*, and *M*, a sequence from the hottest ([*O*-type](./O-type_star "O-type star")) to the coolest ([*M*-type](./M-type_dwarf "M-type dwarf")). Each letter class is then subdivided using a numeric digit with *0* being hottest and *9* being coolest (e.g., A8, A9, F0, and F1 form a sequence from hotter to cooler). The sequence has been expanded with three classes for other stars that do not fit in the classical system: *[W](./W-type_star "W-type star")*, *[S](./S-type_star "S-type star")* and *[C](./C-type_star "C-type star")*. Some [stellar remnants](./Compact_object "Compact object") or objects of deviating mass have also been assigned letters: *[D](./Stellar_classification#White_dwarf_classifications)* for [white dwarfs](./White_dwarf "White dwarf") and *[L](./L_dwarf "L dwarf")*, *[T](./T_dwarf "T dwarf")* and *[Y](./Y_Dwarf "Y Dwarf")* for [brown dwarfs](./Brown_dwarfs "Brown dwarfs") (and [exoplanets](./Exoplanet "Exoplanet")).

In the MK system, a [luminosity class](./Luminosity_class "Luminosity class") is added to the spectral class using [Roman numerals](./Roman_numerals "Roman numerals"). This is based on the width of certain absorption lines in the star's spectrum, which vary with the density of the atmosphere and so distinguish giant stars from dwarfs. Luminosity class *0* or *Ia+* is used for *[hypergiants](./Hypergiant "Hypergiant")*, class *I* for *[supergiants](./Supergiant "Supergiant")*, class *II* for bright *[giants](./Giant_star "Giant star")*, class *III* for regular *giants*, class *IV* for *[subgiants](./Subgiant "Subgiant")*, class *V* for *[main-sequence](./Main-sequence "Main-sequence") stars*, class *sd* (or *VI*) for *[subdwarfs](./Subdwarf "Subdwarf")*, and class *D* (or *VII*) for *[white dwarfs](./White_dwarf "White dwarf")*. The full spectral class for the [Sun](./Sun "Sun") is then G2V, indicating a main-sequence star with a surface temperature around 5,800 K.

## Conventional colour description

The conventional colour description takes into account only the peak of the stellar spectrum. In actuality, however, stars radiate in all parts of the spectrum. Because all spectral colours combined appear white, the actual apparent colours the human eye would observe are far lighter than the conventional colour descriptions would suggest. This characteristic of 'lightness' indicates that the simplified assignment of colours within the spectrum can be misleading. Excluding colour-contrast effects in dim light, and white dwarfs that have strong absorption by metallic elements in their atmospheres,[[1]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-1) in typical viewing conditions there are no green, cyan, indigo, or violet stars. ["Yellow" dwarfs](./Yellow_dwarf_star "Yellow dwarf star") such as the [Sun](./Sun "Sun") are white, ["red" dwarfs](./Red_dwarf "Red dwarf") are a deep shade of yellow/orange, and ["brown" dwarfs](./Brown_dwarf "Brown dwarf") do not literally appear brown, but hypothetically would appear dim red or grey/black to a nearby observer.

## Modern classification

The modern classification system is known as the *Morgan–Keenan* (MK) classification. Each star is assigned a spectral class (from the older Harvard spectral classification, which did not include luminosity[[2]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-2)) and a luminosity class using Roman numerals as explained below, forming the star's spectral type.

Other modern [stellar classification systems](./Photometric_system "Photometric system"), such as the [UBV system](./UBV_photometric_system "UBV photometric system"), are based on [color indices](./Color_index "Color index")—the measured differences in three or more [color magnitudes](./Magnitude_(astronomy) "Magnitude (astronomy)").[[3]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-3) Those numbers are given labels such as "U−V" or "B−V", which represent the colors passed by two standard filters (e.g. *U*ltraviolet, *B*lue and *V*isual).

### Harvard spectral classification

The *Harvard system* is a one-dimensional classification scheme by the astronomer [Annie Jump Cannon](./Annie_Jump_Cannon "Annie Jump Cannon"), who re-ordered and simplified Draper's alphabetical system (see [History](./Stellar_classification#History)). Stars are grouped according to their spectral characteristics by single letters of the alphabet, optionally with numeric subdivisions. Main-sequence stars vary in surface temperature from approximately 2,000 to 50,000 [K](./Kelvin "Kelvin"), whereas more-evolved stars – in particular, newly-formed white dwarfs – can have surface temperatures above 100,000 K.[[4]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-Jeffery2023-4) Physically, the classes indicate the temperature of the star's atmosphere and are normally listed from hottest to coldest.

The traditional [mnemonic](./Mnemonic "Mnemonic") for remembering the order of the spectral type letters, from hottest to coolest, is "Oh, Be A Fine Girl/Guy: Kiss Me!".[[13]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-mnem-16) Many alternative mnemonics have been proposed, in contests held by astronomy courses and organizations, but the traditional mnemonic remains the most popular.[[14]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-17)[[15]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-18)

The spectral classes O through M, as well as other more specialized classes discussed later, are subdivided by [Arabic numerals](./Arabic_numerals "Arabic numerals") (0–9), where 0 denotes the hottest stars of a given class. For example, A0 denotes the hottest stars in class A and A9 denotes the coolest ones. Fractional numbers are allowed; for example, the star [Mu Normae](./Mu_Normae "Mu Normae") is classified as O9.7.[[16]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-UpsOriType-19) The [Sun](./Sun "Sun") is classified as G2.[[17]](./Stellar_classification#cite_note-SunSpectrum-20)
