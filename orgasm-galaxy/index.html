What this animation shows

A barred spiral galaxy, built from real astrophysics, stands in for a model of the physiology and neurology of the human orgasm. Nothing in the image depicts a body: each physiological or neural signal drives one real galactic phenomenon.

The response cycle

Arousal builds through excitement into a plateau. A short point of inevitability (about 2–3 s) follows, after which the response can no longer be stopped. Orgasm is a train of rhythmic muscle contractions about 0.8 s apart, growing weaker and further apart. In resolution the body returns to baseline, and during the refractory period further stimulation has little or no effect.

Two clocks

Every screen second carries two labels: human time, which the physiology runs on, and astrophysical time, which the galaxy runs on. During the build, one screen second is about 10 s of human time and 4.8 million years for the galaxy. At the climax the galaxy clock slows smoothly to about 1,000 years per second while human time runs in real time, so the 0.8 s rhythm is shown as it happens and the disk appears frozen. Afterwards both clocks speed up again.

Documented compromises

  • The jet is drawn about 10× brighter than real optical jets so that it stays visible.
  • The flush analog fades centre-first because galaxies quench inside-out, unlike the human flush, which fades in the reverse order of its spread.
  • The numbers are illustrative typical values from the literature, not measurements of anyone. Bodily signs correlate only weakly with felt intensity, and the cause of the refractory period is still debated.
  • Profiles are response patterns available to any body. Differences between groups are overlapping tendencies, not categories.
