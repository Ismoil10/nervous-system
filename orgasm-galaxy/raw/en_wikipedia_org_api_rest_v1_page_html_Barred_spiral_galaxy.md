# Barred spiral galaxy

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/html/Barred_spiral_galaxy

Barred spiral galaxy

Spiral galaxy with a central bar-shaped structure composed of stars



[NGC 1300](./NGC_1300 "NGC 1300"), viewed nearly face-on; [Hubble Space Telescope](./Hubble_Space_Telescope "Hubble Space Telescope") image

A **barred spiral galaxy** is a [spiral galaxy](./Spiral_galaxy "Spiral galaxy") with a central bar-shaped structure composed of [stars](./Star "Star").[[1]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-1) Bars are found in about two thirds of all spiral galaxies in the local universe,[[2]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-eskridge-2) and generally affect both the motions of stars and [interstellar gas](./Interstellar_gas "Interstellar gas") within spiral galaxies and can affect [spiral arms](./Spiral_arm "Spiral arm") as well.[[3]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-mihalas1968-3)[[4]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-timothy1998-4) The [Milky Way Galaxy](./Milky_Way_Galaxy "Milky Way Galaxy"), where the [Solar System](./Solar_System "Solar System") is located, is classified as a barred spiral galaxy.[[5]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-5)

[Edwin Hubble](./Edwin_Hubble "Edwin Hubble") classified spiral galaxies of this type as "SB" (spiral, barred) in his [Hubble sequence](./Hubble_sequence "Hubble sequence") and arranged them into sub-categories based on how open the arms of the spiral are. SBa types feature tightly bound arms, while SBc types are at the other extreme and have loosely bound arms. SBb-type galaxies lie in between the two. SB0 is a barred [lenticular galaxy](./Lenticular_galaxy "Lenticular galaxy"). A new type, SBm, was subsequently created to describe somewhat [irregular barred spirals](./Barred_irregular_galaxy "Barred irregular galaxy"), such as the [Magellanic Clouds](./Magellanic_Clouds "Magellanic Clouds"), which were once classified as irregular galaxies, but have since been found to contain barred spiral structures. Among other types in Hubble's classifications for the galaxies are the spiral galaxy, [elliptical galaxy](./Elliptical_galaxy "Elliptical galaxy") and irregular galaxy.

Although theoretical models of galaxy formation and evolution had not previously expected galaxies becoming stable enough to host bars very early in the universe's history, evidence has recently emerged of the existence of numerous spiral galaxies in the early universe.[[6]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-6)[[7]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-7)[[8]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-8)

Barred spiral galaxy [IC 5201](./IC_5201 "IC 5201"), located more than 40 million light-years from Earth. It was discovered by [Joseph Lunt](./Joseph_Lunt?action=edit&redlink=1 "Joseph Lunt").[[9]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-9)[[10]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-10)

## Bars

Further information: [Density wave theory § Galactic spiral arms](./Density_wave_theory#Galactic_spiral_arms "Density wave theory")

Milky Way Galaxy spiral arms – based on [WISE](./Wide-field_Infrared_Survey_Explorer "Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer") data.

Barred galaxies are apparently predominant, with surveys showing that up to two-thirds of all spiral galaxies develop a bar.[[2]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-eskridge-2) The creation of the bar is generally thought to be the result of a [density wave](./Density_wave_theory "Density wave theory") radiating from the center of the galaxy whose effects reshape the orbits of the inner stars. This effect builds over time to stars orbiting farther out, which creates a self-perpetuating bar structure.[[11]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-11)

The bar structure is believed to act as a type of [stellar nursery](./Stellar_nursery "Stellar nursery"), channeling [gas](./Interstellar_gas "Interstellar gas") inwards from the spiral arms through [orbital resonance](./Orbital_resonance "Orbital resonance"), fueling [star birth](./Stellar_evolution#Birth "Stellar evolution") in the vicinity of its center.[[12]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-12) This process is also thought to explain why many barred spiral galaxies have [active galactic nuclei](./Active_galactic_nuclei "Active galactic nuclei"), such as that seen in the [Southern Pinwheel Galaxy](./Southern_Pinwheel_Galaxy "Southern Pinwheel Galaxy").

Bars are thought to be temporary phenomena in the lives of spiral galaxies; the bar structures decay over time, transforming galaxies from barred spirals to more "regular" spiral patterns. Past a certain size the accumulated mass of the bar compromises the stability of the overall bar structure. Simulations show that many bars likely experience a "buckling" event in which a disturbance in the orbital resonances of stars in the bar structure leads to an inward collapse in which the bar becomes thicker and shorter, though the exact mechanism behind this buckling instability remains hotly debated.[[13]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-13) Barred spiral galaxies with high mass accumulated in their center thus tend to have short, stubby bars.[[14]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-14) Such buckling phenomena are significantly suppressed and delayed by the presence of a [supermassive black hole](./Supermassive_black_hole "Supermassive black hole") in the galactic center but occur nonetheless.[[15]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-15)

Since so many spiral galaxies have bar structures, it is likely that they are recurring phenomena in spiral galaxy development. The oscillating evolutionary cycle from spiral galaxy to barred spiral galaxy is thought to take on average about two billion years.[[16]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-16)

Recent studies have confirmed the idea that bars are a sign of galaxies reaching full maturity as the "formative years" end. A 2008 investigation found that only 20 percent of the spiral galaxies in the distant past possessed bars, compared with about 65 percent of their local counterparts.[[17]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-ShethElmegreen2008-17)

## Grades

[NGC 7640](./NGC_7640 "NGC 7640") is a barred spiral galaxy in the [Andromeda](./Andromeda_(constellation) "Andromeda (constellation)") constellation.[[18]](./Barred_spiral_galaxy#cite_note-18)

The general classification is "SB" (spiral barred). The sub-categories are based on how open or tight the arms of the spiral are. SBa types feature tightly bound arms. SBc types are at the other extreme and have loosely bound arms. SBb galaxies lie in between. SBm describes somewhat irregular barred spirals. SB0 is a [barred lenticular galaxy](./Barred_lenticular_galaxy "Barred lenticular galaxy").
